## Phase 2 — original unaided reconstruction

Written immediately after that single read, without reopening the source. This section is permanent first-read evidence and will not be revised to match subsequent knowledge.

What happened, remembered order: Justice removes Kailan from ordinary life while Va-Sheva waits. His family will be given a repeatable formula, not a clear account of his fate. His name is sealed, though the wrist knot stays. They go through low private passages. Balance supplies a spare pack because he may fail. An unfamiliar Faith woman blesses his departure without the expected answering scripture. The door opens into unbounded cold. Paul interrupts to insist on the entire road and mentions his brother's boy, whom he has promised to teach to swim. Something is recording five recurring knocks at eleven-minute intervals while Kailan speaks.

They cross desert in night stages, shelter by day; Va-Sheva refuses Sarah's name and says Shield lies to its children by promising training can save those they love. Kailan hears an engine and must trust Va-Sheva about its danger. A dry well conceals Horizon. Electric bulbs astonish him. Jean-Charles meets them and gives Va-Sheva pistachio-and-rose sweets; her face changes and she hides that change. He is affectionate and watchful. Elise receives the body and documentation, renames Kailan Kylian, and makes him a provisional Candidate. Clear spectacles conceal him. She takes the dark cord but leaves the Veil knot. Horizon doesn't fit the five offices. Sarah commands it.

Kailan changes clothes; Va-Sheva begins a salute but cannot finish it. Their farewell hurts: he accuses her of choosing his continued breathing, not him; she concedes it, tells him to believe slowly that he is alive, and leaves. Later in a vehicle mirror he sees her crying with Jean-Charles; she puts her hand on his chest. He retreats from Jean-Charles when the man gets in. The aircraft is Jamaican diplomatic, Captain Billy is warm and offers all sorts of future Jamaican pleasures. Elise steers them back to Geneva. Kailan doesn't yet know English, so Jean-Charles translates. The flight is terrifying; while Elise is out of the cabin Jean-Charles asks about the escape route and tells him not to trouble her with it. Helicopter, lake house, windows that open, switches and taps. Eleven silent days. Sarah finally receives him, older than the photo, thumb trembling, wearing a cross. She is cruelly amusing and doesn't give up much. He remains confined, cannot contact anyone or keep paper, cannot enter churches. She wants his account of what Light saw. She tells Jean-Charles to teach him but reserves recordkeeping to Elise. Outside there is a joke about not shooting anyone; he laughs.

Breakfast without prayer. Jean-Charles makes a joke of Mountain words over the water, sees that it hurts, and apologises. Elise supplies a translated optics manual. Kailan wants to refuse but touches the cover. One diagram shows light entering water. She says tomorrow; nobody has offered him tomorrow since judgment. Alone he looks at the place on his wrist where Va-Sheva's cord was, then the manual. Across the lake a lamp's path reaches his window until dawn. Back in the room he claims he has given everything. Paul says the agreement is fulfilled but the machine will not let them rest. Kailan distinguishes the continuous knocks from the two ceremonies: five and a later answer there, this continuous recurrence here. Paul orders Urs to keep the drum running.

Who wanted what: Kailan wanted his mother's knowledge clarified, then understanding, then some acknowledgment from Va-Sheva that he—not merely a surviving body—had been chosen. He wanted not to need these outsiders. Va-Sheva wanted him alive within a containment she could justify and wanted to keep some feeling private. Elise wanted compliance and control of what entered the record. Jean-Charles wanted access to him and his route, but the kindness did not feel necessarily false. Sarah wanted what he knew. Paul wanted the road completed and a home life outside this room, and at the end could not let the practical situation end with the contract.

Things that remain: a name sealed away; mother handed words she can repeat; still water without the anticipated verse; stars with no ceiling; pistachio-and-rose sweets and Va-Sheva's transformed face; the failed salute; her tears in a mirror; an airplane tail's colours (I cannot reconstruct exact design); Billy's lobster/snorkelling/weed offer; Sarah's trembling thumb and cross; a forbidden knife beside a quartered apple; lens and light-in-water diagrams; a lamp path over a lake. I remember 'Believe it slowly' and 'No one had spoken to me of tomorrow' more exactly than much operational instruction.

High and low: greatest emotional pressure was Va-Sheva's concession that choosing breathing was not choosing him, followed by her unwitnessed-for-her tears. The low was the institutional renaming, which felt a second long denial after the opening. The future briefly opened with the manual and tomorrow; the repeated five closed the chapter back into urgency.

Expected next: the source of the repeated signal must force Kailan and Paul to remain engaged despite the fulfilled agreement. Jean-Charles's private questions will matter. The manual will convert a sacred uncertainty into an optical fact. Sarah will prove less outside the Mountain than Kailan supposes.

Unresolved: Sarah's full relationship to Mountain, the exact reach of Horizon, Jean-Charles's purpose, what Kailan's family has been told, whether Va-Sheva's relation with Jean-Charles can coexist with what she means to Kailan, who is producing the current signal. I did not retain the precise sequence of each office's legal consequence or the specific paperwork sheets; those absences did not prevent the grief.

Felt certain without being proved: Jean-Charles is collecting something he should not collect; Va-Sheva loves or depends on him in a way Kailan did not know; Sarah is very powerful but physically fragile. I read Horizon as the Mountain extending confinement, though I cannot infer all of its loyalties. I briefly assumed Va-Sheva had actually departed before the mirror showed her again; the second departure corrected this but required a moment's readjustment.

First remembered: the sealed name and the mother with a lawful formula. Last remembered: the drum kept running after the account had technically ended. The most persistent middle memory is the hand on Jean-Charles's chest seen in a mirror.
