\typesetchapter{Chapter 16}{Horizon}

\ornament

The white-haired Justice witness came down to the sealed route beneath the hearing chamber. Five witnesses had heard the statement above, she told me; no one from my family had been admitted. Shield was preparing the transfer. I stood beside my pack while she read the words that would accompany me.\par

``Va-Kailan of Light ceases ordinary standing.''\par

She kept her eyes on the tablet.\par

``Office consequence passes to Light under Keeper management. Household consequence passes to Faith under sealed wording. Material consequence passes to Balance. Movement consequence passes to Shield. Sequence consequence remains with Justice. The name is held from common use.''\par

Her finger moved down the tablet, stopping at each office. Va-Sheva stood three paces from me. She wore no visible weapon. Below the Shield cloth covering my white Veil knot was the dark cord she had tied around my wrist. I put a finger beneath it.\par

``Will my mother be told I am dead?''\par

The witness turned a line on the tablet. ``Faith will give the household a form sufficient for survival.''\par

``What form?''\par

She looked at me. ``One they can repeat without committing breach.''\par

I tried to remember my mother's voice when she had spoken of someone dead. My father's came more readily, asking for a date, a witness, the place where a thing had happened. I looked at Va-Sheva. She waited until I lifted the pack.\par

We passed no public gate. Shield took me below the old water lines and through seams I had never seen on a map. In one of them Va-Sheva bent her head and kept it bent for so long that my neck began to hurt from watching her. The Justice witness followed us.\par

Balance had packed dried fruit, water skins, sand wraps, and a list of measures tied with green thread. Another pack rode on a warden's back.\par

``Is that mine too?'' I asked.\par

Va-Sheva answered without turning. ``That is what Shield carries because Balance assumes you may become useless.''\par

I almost smiled. The pack caught on stone and I worked it free.\par

At the last interior threshold a Faith attendant waited with a bowl of water. Her face was covered. I did not know her voice; above her thumb was a small scar. I prepared the answering verse while she raised the bowl.\par

``What leaves under seal leaves under God.''\par

The verse I had prepared began differently. I lowered my head. ``What returns under seal returns only if God permits return.''\par

She touched water to my forehead, the cloth over my Veil knot, and the dark cord below it.\par

``What is held from the people is not hidden from God.''\par

She drew back her hands. I waited, then Va-Sheva moved past me toward the door.\par

It opened outward. Cold air lifted the cloth across my mouth. Beyond it there was no wall.\par

(Kylian): --- Mark the hour it began, Paul, and hold it against what I am about to give you.

Beyond the door the cold reached through my wraps. The air smelled of mineral, dry scrub, something faintly animal. I stepped beneath the Mountain's shadow for the second time in my life, this time without prayer access. The Justice witness came out behind me and the door closed.\par

Stars filled the sky. I looked up until my head fell back, with nothing against which to rest it. When I lowered my eyes, Va-Sheva was waiting on the path.\par

We moved before dawn: she led to the first ridge, a wrapped warden beyond it, two more wardens following with the witness. For a while the Mountain stood behind us, blacker than the night. Then the path folded and it disappeared. I turned back.\par

``Move,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

My feet knew stair heights and level stone. Loose ground turned my ankles; on each ridge I lifted my head and saw another to cross. When daylight came, Shield put us beneath a low cut in the rock. We lay beside our packs while a desert-coloured cloth was pinned across the mouth. Va-Sheva watched the remaining gap. Dust lay along her cheek and in the creases beside her eyes.\par

``Did you know,'' I asked, ``before I showed you the scrap?''\par

``I knew there were outer holdings.''\par

``Sarah Blackwood?''\par

One finger tightened against the stone. ``You may not say that name again on this route.''\par

``But you know it.''\par

She kept her eyes on the gap. I pressed my tongue against the split inside my lip.\par

``I thought knowing would make things clearer.''\par

``Light lies to its children. Shield lies differently.''\par

``How?''\par

``We tell ours that if they train enough, they will see danger before it harms what they love.''\par

I waited. She turned her face toward me.\par

``It is true often enough to destroy them.''\par

At dusk we moved again.\par

On the second night we lay flat while an engine passed beyond a ridge. Its sound moved without hooves and was too large for any animal I knew. Va-Sheva closed her hand in warning. I held still until she opened it again. Later she gave me the word: vehicle.\par

``Outsider?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``Did it see us?''\par

``No.''\par

I wanted to ask how she knew. She was already following the warden over the ridge, and I followed her.\par

Before the third dawn we reached a dry well. Its lip was cracked, stones had fallen inward, and a dead thorn tree leaned above it. Shield circled the place. Va-Sheva ran two fingers along the inner wall and pressed at something I could not see. A hollow sound came from below. We waited with our packs on.\par

A voice inside the well spoke in a strange language. One of the wardens answered it. The stone at the base shifted.\par

I had carried the word \textit{Horizon} across two nights. Va-Sheva lowered herself beneath the broken lip, and I followed.\par

The passage below was cool and wide enough for us to stand. Metal ribs supported smooth white panels; the seals between them smelled of rubber. Small glass bulbs shone overhead. I stopped under one and searched it for the flame.\par

``No lamp,'' I said.\par

A man farther along the passage laughed softly. ``Not in your sense.''\par

He spoke Mountain speech with an unfamiliar bend to the words. His face was lined by sun; he looked in his early forties. A short blade hung beside metal tools and black objects at his belt. Even the pockets of his clothes fastened shut. His eyes moved from the wardens to Va-Sheva.\par

``Jean-Charles,'' she said.\par

He crossed before the wardens had settled around us, drew a small flat box from his coat and held it out. A pink ribbon circled it.\par

``Pistachio and rose. They travelled badly. Eat them tonight or they die.''\par

She took the box, and her face opened. Her shoulders lowered. I had stood before her through years of instruction; I watched her say his name again, softly, with the box held between both hands. A warden shifted beside her. She put it inside her coat and clasped her hands behind her back.\par

Jean-Charles looked at me. His next words came more slowly. ``So this is him.''\par

``This is the transfer,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

``Of course.'' He stepped aside. ``Welcome to the part of the world that pretends it is not also a cave.''\par

He glanced over my hands, my feet, the covered wrist and the place at my side where a blade could hang. His palm touched my shoulder and turned me toward the passage. It was gone before I had decided what to do with it.\par

``He is under Exception,'' Va-Sheva said. ``Do not test him for sport.''\par

``Never only for sport.''\par

The witness carried her sealed packet into the first inner chamber. A pale-haired woman waited beside a metal table, wearing a jacket and trousers in grey outsider cloth. Her hair was cut short. At her throat hung a narrow cord without a Kahir sign.\par

``Elise,'' Jean-Charles said.\par

She took the packet without looking at him.\par

``Va-Elise,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

Under the witness's eyes, Va-Elise broke the outer seal, read the first page and left the second folded. She signed a smooth white sheet with ink flowing from a tube.\par

``Body received under Shield-Horizon transfer. Speech restricted. Return unpresumed. Name held. Public standing ceased.''\par

``Sequence delivered,'' the witness answered.\par

Va-Elise put down the tube. ``Your old name is not for ordinary use here. Va-Kailan remains in the Mountain record. Outside you will be Kylian. Within Horizon, under instruction, you are Sa-Kylian.''\par

``Candidate?''\par

``Provisional Candidate.''\par

``I was Veil of Light.''\par

``You will have instruction in doors, telephones, passports and money. Until then you will ask before touching anything that has been closed, fastened or switched off.''\par

On the table lay folded clothes, shoes, keys, a coloured map and spectacles with clear lenses. I reached for the spectacles and stopped.\par

``Those,'' Va-Elise said, ``you may touch.''\par

``My sight is good.''\par

``They do not change it. They change the way people look at you.''\par

Jean-Charles nodded gravely. ``Sadly true.''\par

Va-Sheva took my wrist. She untied the dark cord and unwound it. The Shield cloth over the white knot stayed in place. She gave the cord to Va-Elise, who enclosed it in an envelope and wrote the time.\par

``Am I still under Shield?''\par

Va-Elise answered. ``Horizon has received you.''\par

``What is Horizon?''\par

``We keep certain Mountain facts from dying inside it, and certain outside facts from entering uncontained.''\par

``Another Kahir?''\par

``Do not try to fit it into five hands,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

I looked from her to the woman with the envelope. ``Who commands it?''\par

``For now, Ka-Sarah.''\par

I lowered the spectacles to the table.\par

Va-Elise gathered the packet. ``You will meet her when you are clean, dressed, fed, and no longer looking as if every object in the room is a theological insult.''\par

``They are.''\par

Jean-Charles laughed. Va-Sheva looked at me and briefly raised one corner of her mouth.\par

\ornament

They gave me water, food and clothes. The shirt carried no office colour. It closed with small discs pushed through cuts in the cloth; I fastened one in the wrong cut and had to begin again. The trousers showed the separate shape of each leg. I turned twice before the metal wall, looking for an outer wrapping. Va-Sheva had stayed away while I changed.\par

When I returned, Jean-Charles looked me over. ``Almost human. In the outside sense.''\par

``That does not improve it.''\par

Va-Elise handed me the spectacles. Small reflections of the bulbs shone at the edges of the clear lenses. Va-Sheva entered while I was fitting the arms behind my ears. She stopped. Her hand began the Shield salute and halted below her breast.\par

``You look like an Outsider scholar.''\par

``I have been called worse today.''\par

``Yes.'' She came closer. ``This is where Shield leaves you.''\par

I waited for her to use my name.\par

``Do you return to the Mountain?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``Will you tell them I reached Horizon?''\par

``The report goes through sequence. I cannot carry messages.''\par

``To Ka-Raedin?''\par

``To anyone.''\par

``My family?''\par

She did not answer again. I looked down at the shoes they had given me. The right one pressed against a toe.\par

``You chose this,'' I said.\par

``I chose the only containment that left you breathing.''\par

``That is not the same as choosing me.''\par

Va-Elise kept her eyes lowered. Va-Sheva stood without moving.\par

``No,'' she said quietly.\par

I nodded. ``Then thank you for choosing breathing.''\par

Her lower lip pressed white against the upper. ``You are alive.''\par

``So I keep being told.''\par

``Believe it slowly.''\par

She moved her hand toward the covered knot and stopped before touching it. I held my arm where it was while she stepped back. The door shut behind her. Va-Elise waited beside the table; I lowered my arm.\par

Instruction came before Ka-Sarah. Va-Elise sat me in a room with a telephone, a camera, a map of the world so large that I searched it for the edge, and a passport with a blank space for my image. She pointed to things and required me to give back their names. Jean-Charles explained what I had failed to understand. I was still working at the fastenings on my borrowed clothes when they told me to prepare to leave.\par

Before dawn they put me in the back of a vehicle. A small mirror on the door showed part of the road behind it. Through that mirror I saw Va-Sheva step away from the wardens with Jean-Charles.\par

I could not hear them. Her hands moved as she spoke, and he stood still. Then she bowed her head and wiped one cheek with the heel of her hand. I had seen blood on that hand, sand under its nails. She wiped the cheek again. Jean-Charles said something without reaching for her. She put her palm flat on his chest and held it there through one breath, then another. When she took it back, she turned toward the desert without looking at the vehicle.\par

He watched her go. I followed her in the mirror until he opened the door beside me. I moved closer to the opposite door.\par

The engine began to shake the seat. Jean-Charles looked at my hands gripping it.\par

``You are safer than you feel.''\par

``I have felt safe. It felt like stone on four sides.''\par

``Good answer.''\par

We crossed dark land, then places where lights multiplied, with doors opening straight onto the road and no wardens beside them. At the airfield Va-Elise handed papers through a small window. A man came out and opened a gate. Our vehicle stopped beside a white aircraft with a gold cross dividing green and black on its tail.\par

``Jamaican diplomatic flight,'' Va-Elise said. ``To Geneva. Stay with me.''\par

The captain came down the steps to meet us, a lean, dark-skinned man with his hair tied high behind his head and gold bars on the shoulders of his white shirt. He shook Jean-Charles's hand and kept it while they laughed. Then he offered me his.\par

``Capt'n Billy.''\par

I gave him the name Va-Elise had told me to use. He repeated it, then led us up. Wide leather seats faced one another across a table. Va-Elise put her papers on it. Billy rested a hand on the back of my seat while Jean-Charles translated between us.\par

``Next time you come to Jamaica. You wanna go snorkelling? You wanna eat lobster? You wanna smoke weed? You can do all dees tings. Just let Capt'n Billy know.''\par

Jean-Charles stopped twice to laugh. He repeated the English slowly before giving me each part, with Billy waiting beside my chair. Va-Elise looked up from her papers. ``Geneva first, Billy.''\par

``Yes, ma'am.'' He pulled once on my fastened belt, then went forward to the other pilot and shut the door.\par

The floor tilted beneath my feet; pressure closed my ears. The earth withdrew below the window. Jean-Charles said, ``Do not look down until I say.'' I stared at the place where his hand lay on the table. My tongue hurt from pressing it against the roof of my mouth. Va-Elise read her papers while he spoke to me.\par

Somewhere over the water Va-Elise went forward to speak to Billy and closed the door behind her. Jean-Charles talked to me. I watched him while he asked how the well had closed behind us, whether Shield had cut the desert route for me alone, which warden had answered the voice below. I answered what little I knew. When I could look through the window again, there was water as far as I could see.\par

``No need to trouble Elise with any of that,'' he said. ``She writes everything down, and a man should keep some of his own weather.''\par

I nodded. His reflection remained in the glass.\par

We came to Switzerland. Jean-Charles gave me the name from a newspaper, pointing to the printed word so that I could find it again. At Geneva a vehicle met us beside the aircraft and took us to a helicopter.\par

``The family's,'' Va-Elise said.\par

From it I saw mountains with roads cut along their sides, houses beneath the open sky and water spread between them. Church towers rose above roofs where I could follow people walking. When we landed, the air smelled of water, metal, pine, bread and strangers.\par

They placed me in a house above a lake. Its windows opened. I stood beside one with my fingers around the handle until Va-Elise told me to shut it; the room was growing cold. Water came by turning another metal handle. Light came when she touched a switch. I watched her do it twice.\par

On the night we arrived I listened to the walls. An electrical hum continued through them with a steadiness I distrusted. When a car passed on the road below, I could hear the hum beneath it and after it had gone.\par

For eleven days I barely slept and did not speak. I ate when Va-Elise brought food. When Jean-Charles asked questions, I wrote the shortest answer I could on a slate and rubbed it away under his eyes. He had to wait for me to clean it before he could ask the next. On the twelfth day Va-Elise came without the slate and took me to Ka-Sarah.\par

She was older than the photograph. I stood in the doorway looking at her until Va-Elise said my new name. Her white hair was gathered at the back of her head; dark wool hung from shoulders narrowed by age. Blue veins showed through the skin of her folded hands. Her right thumb trembled against the left, and she closed the left hand over it. A small gold cross lay at her throat.\par

Her eyes were blue-grey and steady. She sat with her back partly to the open window above the lake. Jean-Charles went to stand beside it. Va-Elise stayed by the door.\par

I could find no greeting for her.\par

``An ex-Veil,'' Ka-Sarah said. ``It is not every decade that Horizon receives one of those. How extravagant of Shield.''\par

``I was Veil of Light.''\par

``Yes. Elise has put it in the file. Sit down.''\par

``You are Sarah Blackwood.''\par

``I was Sarah Blackwood before most of the men who feared me learned how to spell it. Here, you may call me Ka-Sarah.''\par

``Keeper of Horizon?''\par

``Among other inconveniences.''\par

I sat in the chair before her. She studied me over her folded hands, and I tried to keep from looking at the thumb she had hidden.\par

``You have seen a room you were not meant to see, survived a trial that had excellent reasons to kill you, crossed the desert, refused speech for eleven days and acquired spectacles intended to make you look harmless. An eventful fortnight.''\par

``Why am I here?''\par

``Because dead boys tell us less.''\par

I looked at Jean-Charles. He was looking through the window.\par

Ka-Sarah opened the file. I could see photographs and copied Mountain marks among the pages.\par

``You are under Exception. You will not leave this property without permission. You will not attempt to contact your family, Ka-Raedin, Va-Sheva or any Mountain office. You will not use telephones. You will not keep private writing materials until we decide whether your mind can hold paper responsibly.''\par

Air escaped through my nose.\par

``And you will not enter churches.''\par

``Why?''\par

``Because you are full of broken scripture, and churches arrange memory in public. You are not ready to stand inside one without worshipping it, condemning it, or stealing from it.''\par

``You wear a cross.''\par

She touched it. ``Yes.''\par

``Are you an Outsider?''\par

``My dear boy, from where you sit, everyone is.''\par

Beyond her a boat crossed the open water. I watched it pass behind her shoulder and appear beside the other one. She waited until I looked back.\par

``Horizon has custody of you now. You are alive, confined and unassigned.''\par

``And if I refuse it?''\par

``Then Shield was right and Justice was merciful for too long.''\par

She spoke in the same tone she had used for telephones and paper.\par

``What do you want from me?''\par

``For now, to survive the outside without turning every object into theology.''\par

``And after that?''\par

``Perhaps you will tell us what Light saw before Shield buried you.''\par

``You already know.''\par

She laid her hand on the open file. ``We know what Shield could afford to transfer. We know what Justice could lawfully write. We know what Light is probably hiding from itself. We do not know what you saw.''\par

I looked at the photographs again. From my chair I could not make out what they showed. The top page lifted in the air from the window; she held it down and waited. I let my weight settle against the back of the chair.\par

``Good,'' she said. ``You are learning.''\par

Ka-Raedin had given the word that same downward weight. My fingers tightened on the chair.\par

She closed the file. ``A final warning, Sa-Kylian. The Mountain will feel purer from here. It is the distance. Do not trust it.''\par

Then she turned toward Jean-Charles. ``He is yours to teach the world to. He is not yours to write down. Elise keeps the record.''\par

He inclined his head. His right hand remained in his pocket.\par

Va-Elise opened the door. I followed her out with Jean-Charles, leaving Ka-Sarah in the chair above the water. In the passage he said, ``Well. That went beautifully. She did not have anyone shot, which in this house counts as hospitality.''\par

``Do not make him think that is a joke,'' Va-Elise said.\par

``Half a joke,'' he allowed.\par

I made a sound before I recognised it as a laugh. Both of them looked at me. I looked down and followed Va-Elise into the room where they had set out food.\par

There was bread with a crust that broke beneath my hand, cheese sharp enough to tighten my jaw, and an apple cut into quarters. Va-Elise moved the small knife away from my plate before she sat. The water glass was so clear that I put out my finger to find its edge.\par

I waited for the prayer. Jean-Charles had already torn himself a piece of bread. He lifted his glass.\par

``Measured,'' he said, grinning.\par

Va-Elise looked at him. ``Do not.''\par

``Witnessed.'' He set the glass against the table.\par

At home those words went over the first cup and the sealed jar. My bread stopped halfway to my mouth. Jean-Charles put down the piece he was holding, and the grin left his face.\par

``Sorry. Bad joke. I did not know it still had teeth in it.''\par

He pushed the loaf toward me. ``Eat. We make no ceremony of breakfast here; it is the one mercy of the place.''\par

I took more bread. He did not use either word again.\par

After the meal, Va-Elise set a narrow grey book before me. The title appeared twice on its cover, in outside letters I could not read and in careful Mountain hand.\par

\textit{On Light, Lenses, and the Conduct of Images.}\par

I read it twice with both hands on the table.\par

``For me?''\par

``An optics manual,'' she said. ``It has been translated into your language where translation was possible. Where it was not possible, we left the outside term and added notes.''\par

``Am I permitted to read it?''\par

``Yes. The lessons will be supervised. You were Veil of Light. We intend to know what you make of a book that explains what Light has not yet named.''\par

I wanted to refuse it. My fingers had reached the edge of the cover. I lifted it and found a lens drawn with dimensions, labels and notes along its curve. There was no office seal on the page. I followed the curve with one finger, leaving room for the label beneath it.\par

Va-Elise remained beside the table. ``You are protected here.''\par

I looked toward the closed door.\par

``The locks are part of that answer,'' she said.\par

I turned a page. Light entered water in the next drawing. A line ran across the sheet; above and below it the path changed. I kept my finger on the edge and looked from one part to the other.\par

``We will begin there tomorrow.''\par

``Tomorrow?''\par

``After breakfast. Bring the book.''\par

I shut it carefully. When she stood aside for me to leave, I carried it under my arm.\par

\ornament

That night the bed gave beneath every part of me. I lay awake listening for the pipe murmur, the distant gate chain, the passage of feet against stone. A car travelled along the road below. Inside the walls an electrical hum continued after it had passed.\par

I pushed back my sleeve. The Shield cloth still covered the white Veil knot. Beneath it was the bare strip of skin where Va-Sheva's cord had lain. I rubbed the strip with my thumb, then slid the sleeve down again.\par

Kailan, Va-Kailan, Kylian, Sa-Kylian. I said them under my breath. In the mirror she had turned toward the desert with her hand empty at her side. I tried to count the ridges between the well and the Mountain. At the place where we had lain down for the vehicle, I lost the path.\par

The optics manual lay on the bedside table. Va-Elise had left the curtains open. ``First learn what windows do,'' she had said when I reached for them. Across the lake, houses stood along the far shore with their lights exposed. I sat up and put my hand against the glass. My own lamp showed behind me.\par

I opened the manual on my knees and found the drawing of light entering water. Across the lake, one house lamp laid a narrow, trembling path over the black surface to my window. I watched it reach me until dawn thinned it away.\par
