\typesetchapter{Chapter 16}{Horizon}

\ornament

The white-haired Justice witness read my name in the sealed route beneath the hearing chamber. Shield had already covered the white knot at my wrist. Below the cloth, Va-Sheva's dark cord pressed into the skin.\par

Five witnesses had heard the statement above, the woman told me. My family had been excluded. She held her tablet where the lamp reached it and began again for me.\par

``Va-Kailan of Light ceases ordinary standing.''\par

Her finger moved to the next line.\par

``Office consequence passes to Light under Keeper management. Household consequence passes to Faith under sealed wording. Material consequence passes to Balance. Movement consequence passes to Shield. Sequence consequence remains with Justice. The name is held from common use.''\par

``Will my mother be told I am dead?''\par

The witness turned one line on the tablet. ``Faith will give the household a form sufficient for survival.''\par

``What form?''\par

``One they can repeat without committing breach.''\par

I waited while she covered the tablet. I could hear the fastening scrape against its rim. Va-Sheva stood three paces from me with her hands empty, and I looked at her until the witness said that Shield could proceed.\par

We passed no public gate. The route descended beneath the hearing chamber and the old water lines, through seams I had never seen on a map. At one low place Va-Sheva bent and kept a hand above her head; I followed with my pack against my chest until I could straighten.\par

Balance had supplied dried fruit, water skins and sand wraps, with a list of measures tied to the pack by green thread. A warden carried a second pack almost as large as mine.\par

``Is that mine too?'' I asked.\par

``No,'' Va-Sheva said. ``That is what Shield carries because Balance assumes you may become useless.''\par

I almost smiled. The strap slipped from my shoulder and she waited while I put it back.\par

At the last interior threshold a Faith attendant stood with a bowl in both hands. Her face was covered; a small scar crossed the skin above her thumb. I did not know her voice. I had prepared the answering scripture, but she said, ``What leaves under seal leaves under God.''\par

I lowered my head. ``What returns under seal returns only if God permits return.''\par

She touched water to my forehead, then to the covered knot and the dark cord beneath it. A drop stayed between the strands.\par

``What is held from the people is not hidden from God.''\par

She withdrew the bowl. I stood with my head lowered until Va-Sheva touched my shoulder.\par

The final Shield door opened outward. Cold air lifted the cloth over my mouth. Beyond it there was no wall.\par

\begin{room}

--- They have finished with you. Then give me the road out: all of it, in order. The desert, the well, the water. I have kept you from your bed four nights for this account, and myself from a summer I mean to get back to. There is a boy at home, my brother's son, that I have promised two years running to teach to swim, and I should like to keep it this year while the water is still warm. I would not stop this one door short of where it ends.

--- It ends badly.

--- Most roads out of a home do. Then walk it, and do not spare the turns.

--- \ldots{} There. Eleven minutes, and five again. Does Madame's machine ever tire?

--- It has drawn the same five without a pause since it began, and it will go on drawing them while you talk. Urs and Elise are counting the gaps in the next room and I have stopped asking them for the tally. Walk your road, Kailan.

--- I will walk it. But mark the hour it began, Paul, and hold it against what I am about to give you.

\end{room}

Outside, the cold went through my wraps. I smelled mineral dust, dry scrub, something animal beyond the prepared route. Stars filled the sky over the Mountain's shadow. I lifted my face until the pack pulled across my shoulders and Va-Sheva told me to watch my feet.\par

She led to the first ridge, then let a wrapped warden pass her. Two more came behind with the Justice witness. For a while I could see the Mountain whenever I turned; after a bend I lost it. I stopped and turned farther.\par

``Move,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

The loose ground rolled beneath my soles. I had strength for stair after stair, but by dawn my ankles shook when I put weight on them. Under an overhang Shield spread a desert-coloured cloth across the opening and pinned its lower edge with stones. We lay with our packs under our heads. The witness slept with one hand tucked inside the cloth over her tablet.\par

Va-Sheva watched the narrow gap beside the covering. Dust lay in the creases near her eyes. I worked my tongue against the split inside my lip and waited until the others had settled.\par

``Did you know, before I showed you the scrap?''\par

``I knew there were outer holdings.''\par

``Sarah Blackwood?''\par

Her finger tightened on the stone beside her. ``You may not say that name again on this route.''\par

I reached for the water skin. The measure was tied around its neck, and I held it a while without opening it.\par

``I thought knowing would make things clearer,'' I said.\par

``Light lies to its children. Shield lies differently.''\par

``How does Shield lie?''\par

``We tell ours that if they train enough, they will see danger before it harms what they love.''\par

She looked through the gap again. I loosened the stopper, drank my measure and secured it. A grain of sand had caught under the binding; I picked it out before I put the skin down.\par

We moved at dusk. During the second night an engine passed beyond a ridge. Va-Sheva closed her hand and we went flat. I lay with a stone under my hip while the sound grew, held and dwindled; the warden ahead of us stayed down long after I could hear it.\par

``Vehicle,'' Va-Sheva said when we rose.\par

``Outsider?''\par

``Yes. Keep below the ridge.''\par

I followed the next pair of heels. Each time their owner stopped, I had to stop; when he moved, I took up the weight again.\par

Before the third dawn we reached a dry well. Its lip was cracked, the rope gone. A dead thorn tree leaned over fallen stones. Shield circled it before approaching. Va-Sheva lowered herself far enough to run two fingers along the inner wall, found a place and pressed. A hollow sound answered below.\par

We waited beside the well until a voice came up in an unfamiliar language. One warden answered. Stone shifted at the base.\par

I had carried the word \textit{Horizon} across two nights. Now I had to pass my pack down through a hole and follow it.\par

The passage below was cool and high enough to stand in. Metal ribs supported smooth white panels; a black seal around an inner door smelled of rubber. Small glass bulbs burned steadily along the wall. I stopped beside one and held my palm near it.\par

``No lamp,'' I said.\par

``Not in your sense.''\par

The man spoke Mountain speech with unfamiliar stresses. He was in his early forties, sun-lined, with a short blade at his belt beside tools of metal and black material. His pockets all closed. He looked past me at the wardens.\par

``Jean-Charles,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

He came across before they had finished setting down the loads. From his coat he took a bright, flat box tied with pink ribbon and offered it to her.\par

``Pistachio and rose. They travelled badly. Eat them tonight or they die.''\par

She took it with both hands. Her shoulders lowered, and she said his name again. I could see a crushed corner under the ribbon. Then she looked at the wardens, put the box inside her coat and set her hands behind her back.\par

Jean-Charles turned to me. ``So this is him.''\par

``This is the transfer,'' Va-Sheva said. ``He is under Exception.''\par

``I know.'' His eyes passed over my shoes, my hands and the covered wrist. He touched my shoulder lightly to direct me down the passage.\par

``Do not test him for sport.''\par

``Never only for sport.''\par

In an inner chamber a woman waited beside a metal table. Her pale hair was cut short, her grey jacket and trousers bore no office sign, and a narrow cord disappeared into her collar. The Justice witness set a sealed packet in front of her.\par

``Elise,'' Jean-Charles said.\par

Va-Sheva addressed her as Va-Elise. She broke the outer seal under the witness's eyes, read the first page and left the second folded. With a thin tube she signed a smooth white sheet.\par

``Body received under Shield-Horizon transfer. Speech restricted. Return unpresumed. Name held. Public standing ceased.''\par

``Sequence delivered,'' the witness answered.\par

Va-Elise turned the sheet toward me. ``Your old name is held in the Mountain record. Outside you will use Kylian. Here, while you are under instruction, Sa-Kylian.''\par

``Candidate?''\par

``Provisional Candidate.''\par

``I was Veil of Light.''\par

``And we need you alive long enough to tell us about Light. Until you know how a door opens here, what a telephone gives away, what to show at a border, you will follow instructions.''\par

She placed the tube of ink across the paper. On the rest of the table lay folded clothes, dark shoes, keys, a coloured map and a pair of clear spectacles. I picked up the spectacles and looked through them at my own hand.\par

``I do not need correction.''\par

``They have none,'' Va-Elise said. ``People look differently at a man wearing them. Put them beside the clothes.''\par

Va-Sheva came to my wrist. She worked a nail under the dark cord and loosened it, drawing it away without disturbing the cloth over the white knot. A strand caught. She freed it, wound the cord once around two fingers and handed it across the table.\par

Va-Elise put it in a small envelope and marked the time.\par

``Am I still under Shield?''\par

``No. Horizon has received you.''\par

``Where does it stand among the five?''\par

Va-Sheva said, ``Do not try to fit it into five hands.''\par

Va-Elise laid the envelope on the packet. ``We keep certain Mountain facts from dying inside it, and certain outside facts from entering uncontained. Ka-Sarah commands here.''\par

I watched her slide the packet out of my reach. She had said the name in front of every person at the table.\par

``You will meet her after you have washed, eaten and slept,'' she said. ``Jean-Charles, show him.''\par

\ornament

They gave me water in a room where I could close the door. I washed the dust from my face and arms. It gathered in the bottom of the basin; when Jean-Charles showed me the release, it went away through a hole too small for my smallest finger.\par

The shirt closed with discs pushed through slits in the cloth. I missed one and had to undo the others to put the edges level. The trousers showed each leg separately. I turned twice before the metal wall, searching the folded things for an outer wrapping. Va-Sheva remained away while I changed.\par

When I returned Jean-Charles looked me over. ``Almost human. In the outside sense.''\par

``That does not improve it.''\par

He pulled the back of my collar free where I had folded it under. Va-Elise handed me the spectacles. Their lenses caught the bulbs and placed two small lights at the edge of my sight.\par

Va-Sheva entered and stopped. Her hand began the Shield salute, rose a little and fell to her side.\par

``You look like an Outsider scholar,'' she said.\par

I touched the frame. ``I have been called worse today.''\par

She came closer. ``This is where Shield leaves you.''\par

``Will they be told I reached Horizon?''\par

``Ka-Raedin will receive what sequence permits.''\par

``And my family?''\par

She said nothing. I looked at the dark shoes. Inside one of them I could feel sand pressed against the skin beside a nail.\par

``You chose this for me,'' I said.\par

``I chose the only containment that left you breathing.''\par

``That is not the same as choosing me.''\par

Va-Elise kept her eyes on the table. Va-Sheva's lower lip pressed white beneath the upper; she took a breath before she answered.\par

``No.''\par

``Then thank you for choosing breathing.''\par

Her hand moved toward the covered knot and stopped short of it. ``You are alive. Believe it slowly.''\par

She stepped back and left. When the door closed I kept my arm where her hand had nearly reached it. Jean-Charles waited beside the clothes I had taken off.\par

\begin{room}

--- You know them. You told me what you are; so you know these names.

--- I know them. Jean-Charles is exactly as tiresome as you found him. Elise has not warmed once in all the years I have worked beside her.

--- And her. Ka-Sarah.

--- The account first. Finish the road: bring yourself into the house above the water, and give me the first time you saw her.

--- \ldots{} Five.

--- I heard it. Go on.

\end{room}

\ornament

Va-Elise taught me in a room with a telephone, a camera and a map whose oceans occupied most of the wall. A passport lay open on the table, with an empty space for my image. She made me say the name on it, then say it again while Jean-Charles placed the camera at the height of my face.\par

The door handle had to be pressed down. I tried it while she watched. She showed me what to carry, where to keep my hands, when to let her speak. Jean-Charles supplied Mountain words for the objects she used; for some he had to leave the outside word and show me the thing twice.\par

Before dawn they put me in the back of a vehicle and left the door open while they finished loading. A small mirror on the door held a portion of the road behind. In it I saw Va-Sheva and Jean-Charles step away from the vehicles and wardens.\par

I could not hear them. Her hands moved as she spoke. Then she bowed her head and wiped her cheek with the heel of one hand. Jean-Charles stayed still. He said something, and she put her palm flat against his chest. She left it there through one breath, then another. When she took it away she turned toward the desert. He watched her go.\par

I moved closer to the opposite door before he climbed in beside me. He reached across and closed mine.\par

The engine began beneath us. I gripped the seat until the seams hurt my fingers.\par

``You are safer than you feel,'' Jean-Charles said.\par

``I have felt safe. It felt like stone on four sides.''\par

``Good answer.''\par

The lights outside multiplied as we drove. At the airfield Va-Elise passed papers through a small window. A man came out to open a gate, and we drove to a white aircraft with a gold cross dividing green and black on its tail.\par

``Jamaican diplomatic flight,'' Va-Elise said. ``To Geneva. Stay with me.''\par

The captain came down the steps, a lean, dark-skinned man with his hair tied high behind his head and gold bars on the shoulders of his white shirt. He shook Jean-Charles's hand and kept it while they laughed, then offered me his.\par

``Capt'n Billy.''\par

I gave him the name from the passport. He repeated it and led us up. Wide leather seats faced one another across a table, where Va-Elise put her papers. Billy rested a hand on the back of my seat. Jean-Charles translated between us.\par

``Next time you come to Jamaica. You wanna go snorkelling? You wanna eat lobster? You wanna smoke weed? You can do all dees tings. Just let Capt'n Billy know.''\par

Jean-Charles stopped twice to laugh before finishing the translation. I asked what snorkelling was. He put two fingers beside his eyes, pointed downward and said that I could breathe while I looked under the sea. Billy watched the demonstration and nodded.\par

Va-Elise looked up. ``Geneva first, Billy.''\par

``Yes, ma'am.'' He pulled once on my fastened belt, then went forward to the other pilot and shut the door.\par

The floor tilted. Pressure closed my ears, and the earth withdrew below the window. Jean-Charles told me to keep my eyes on him. I watched his mouth as he spoke until he said I could look outside. The belt lay tight across my hips; I tested it with both hands, as Billy had done.\par

Somewhere over the water Va-Elise went forward to speak to the captain and closed the door behind her. Jean-Charles asked about the well: how it had closed, whether Shield had cut the route for me alone, which warden had answered the voice from below. I could supply little, but he kept talking and I kept my face turned toward him.\par

``No need to trouble Elise with any of that,'' he said. ``She writes everything down, and a man should keep some of his own weather.''\par

I nodded. His reflection lay over the dark water in the window. He had asked for no names I had been forbidden to give. I tried to remember whether the warden who answered the voice had also carried the spare pack.\par

At Geneva a vehicle met us beside the aircraft and took us to a helicopter. ``The family's,'' Va-Elise said. The turning blades beat through the closed cabin. I sat with my hands on the fastenings and watched mountains pass beneath us, their roads cut across exposed slopes. Houses stood beside a lake. Church towers rose above the roofs; I could see paths leading to their doors.\par

When we landed I smelled pine, bread and water. Va-Elise kept my passport with her papers. We entered a house above the lake, and she showed me a room where the window opened. I put a hand through it and withdrew it. No voice called me back.\par

Water came when I turned a metal handle. I closed it before the basin filled and stood waiting with wet fingers. The light obeyed a small switch by the door. That night I got out of bed to try it again, with my free hand resting on the wall.\par

For eleven days I barely slept and did not speak. Va-Elise put food before me and waited until I ate. When Jean-Charles asked a question, I wrote a short answer on a slate. He watched me erase it before the next question. I worked the wet cloth across each line until the marks had gone.\par

On the twelfth day Va-Elise took me to Ka-Sarah.\par

\ornament

She sat with her back partly to an open window. Her hair was white, gathered behind her head, and dark wool hung from shoulders narrowed by age. A thumb trembled against her other hand until she covered it. At her throat lay a small gold cross. I looked from the cross to her face and back to the cross; she waited.\par

I knew the face from the photograph. In front of me the skin over her hands was thin enough to show the blue veins.\par

Va-Elise stood by the door and Jean-Charles near the window. I remained standing with my arms at my sides.\par

``An ex-Veil,'' Ka-Sarah said. ``It is not every decade that Horizon receives a Candidate.''\par

``I was Veil of Light.''\par

``Yes. Sit down, Sa-Kylian.''\par

I sat in the chair she indicated. ``You are Sarah Blackwood.''\par

``Here you may call me Ka-Sarah.''\par

``Keeper of Horizon?''\par

``Among other inconveniences.''\par

I looked at Va-Elise. There was no office sign on either woman.\par

``You may use the grammar you have,'' Ka-Sarah said. ``We have more urgent things to teach you.''\par

She drew a file across the table. Under its cover were photographs, outside writing and copied Mountain marks. I leaned forward at the marks. She held the page flat beneath one hand.\par

``You have survived a trial, crossed the desert, flown here and refused to speak for eleven days. Elise tells me you eat. Do you sleep?''\par

``A little.''\par

``Tell her when you cannot. She cannot help you with something she does not know.''\par

The hand on the page shook. She moved it to the edge of the file and closed her fingers around the cover.\par

``You are under Exception,'' she said. ``That means you are alive by procedure. You will not leave this property without permission. You will not attempt to contact your family, Ka-Raedin, Va-Sheva or any Mountain office. You will not use telephones. You will not keep private writing materials until we decide whether your mind can hold paper responsibly.''\par

I pressed my fingers together in my lap.\par

``And you will not enter churches.''\par

``Why?''\par

``You have seen the towers from the helicopter. Sooner or later you will ask to see inside. Churches arrange memory in public. I need to know what you will do with yours before I let you into one.''\par

``You wear a cross.''\par

She touched it. ``Yes.''\par

``Are you one of them? An Outsider?''\par

``My dear boy, from where you sit, everyone is.''\par

The lake moved behind her shoulder. A boat crossed the opening between the frame and her head. I watched until it disappeared behind her, then looked at the file.\par

``Why am I here?''\par

``To survive, for the present. Horizon has custody of you. Alive, confined and unassigned.''\par

``And if I refuse?''\par

``Then Shield was right and Justice was merciful for too long.''\par

She said it in the same voice she had used for telephones. I put my back against the chair.\par

``What do you want from me?''\par

``What Light saw before Shield buried you.''\par

``You have the papers.''\par

``We have what Shield could transfer and Justice could write. We do not have your account.'' She released the cover. ``That can wait until you can sleep.''\par

I disliked the ease with which she could leave the question there. The photograph had given me nothing to say to this woman. I pulled at the sleeve above my covered wrist.\par

``A final warning,'' she said. ``The Mountain will feel purer from here. It is the distance. Do not trust it.''\par

She turned toward Jean-Charles. ``He is yours to teach the world to. He is not yours to write down. Elise keeps the record.''\par

He inclined his head. His right hand stayed in his pocket.\par

Va-Elise opened the door and we followed her out. In the passage Jean-Charles said, ``Well. That went beautifully. She did not have anyone shot, which in this house counts as hospitality.''\par

``Do not make him think that is a joke,'' Va-Elise said.\par

``Half a joke.''\par

I laughed. The sound hurt my dry throat and I swallowed before I could do it again.\par

They fed me bread whose crust broke under my thumb, a sharp cheese, an apple and water in a clear glass. Va-Elise cut the apple into quarters. When she had finished, she laid the knife on her own side of the table.\par

I waited for the prayer. Jean-Charles took his glass and raised it a little.\par

``Measured.''\par

Va-Elise looked at him. ``Do not.''\par

``Witnessed,'' he added, and set it down.\par

The bread stopped halfway to my mouth. These were the words for the first cup and the sealed jar. I held the broken crust against my fingers until a piece fell onto the table.\par

His grin went. ``Sorry. Bad joke. I did not know it still had teeth in it.''\par

He moved the bread within my reach. I ate the piece I was holding, then picked up the fallen crust. He drank without speaking again.\par

After the meal Va-Elise cleared a place beside my glass and set a narrow grey book there. Its title had been written twice: in outside letters I could not read and in careful Mountain hand.\par

\textit{On Light, Lenses, and the Conduct of Images.}\par

``An optics manual,'' she said. ``It has been translated into your language where translation was possible. Where it was not possible, we left the outside term and added notes.''\par

I kept my hands beside the plate. ``Why give it to me?''\par

``You were Veil of Light. We intend to know what you make of it.''\par

``Am I permitted to read it?''\par

``Yes. The lessons will be supervised.''\par

I brought one hand to the cover. It had a shallow crease near the spine and opened easily. On the page a lens was drawn in section, with dimensions and labels along its curve. I ran my finger below the drawing. There was no office seal.\par

Some labels had the Mountain words written close above the outside ones. At a place where the space was too narrow, a line led into the margin and the explanation continued there. I turned the page carefully, keeping my thumb clear of a drop beside the glass.\par

``You are protected here,'' Va-Elise said. ``You must follow the rules I give you.''\par

The next drawing showed light entering water. My hand stopped on the corner of the page.\par

``We will begin there tomorrow.''\par

She took the plates. I stayed at the table with the book open until Jean-Charles said he would show me back to my room.\par

The bed gave beneath my hips when I lay down. I turned, pressed it flat with my hand and tried again. Through the open window I heard a car on the road below; within the wall something hummed with a steadiness I distrusted. I listened for the pipe murmur and gate chains until the sound of the car had gone.\par

I pushed back the shirt sleeve. The white Veil knot remained under Shield cloth. Below it the skin was bare where Va-Sheva's dark cord had rested. I rubbed the narrow band with my thumb, then drew the sleeve down.\par

The book lay on the table beside the bed. Va-Elise had left the curtains open. ``First learn what windows do,'' she had said when I reached for them. Across the lake there were lights at different heights along the shore; some disappeared while I watched. My room shone in the window around my reflection.\par

I sat up and opened the manual on my knees at the drawing of light entering water. One lamp across the lake laid a narrow, trembling path over the black surface toward the house. I watched until dawn thinned the path away.\par

\begin{room}

(Kylian): --- That is the end of it. There is no more Mountain to give you. From the well to this house is the whole of the road, and the rest you have watched with your own eyes.

(Paul): --- \ldots{} No. There is not. You have given a good account, all of it, and by the letter of what you signed you are free of us now. \ldots{} And I would send you up to your bed, if that drum in the next room would let any of us rest.

(Kylian): --- It has not stopped since the early second stroke. Eleven minutes, each time. At both ceremonies I counted five, with longer waits, and then the slow answer. This keeps coming. I cannot tell you who is making it.

(Sarah): --- Keep the drum running, Urs.

\end{room}
