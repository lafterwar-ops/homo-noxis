\typesetchapter{Chapter 16}{Horizon}

\ornament

The Justice witness waited in the sealed route beneath the hearing chamber. Her white hair was braided against her skull; she carried the tablet she had held beside my bindings. Va-Sheva stopped me before her and stood three paces away while two wardens prepared the packs.\par

``Five witnesses heard the statement above,'' the woman said. ``No member of your household was admitted.''\par

She set one finger beside the first line.\par

``Va-Kailan of Light ceases ordinary standing. Office consequence passes to Light under Keeper management. Household consequence passes to Faith under sealed wording. Material consequence passes to Balance. Movement consequence passes to Shield. Sequence consequence remains with Justice. The name is held from common use.''\par

I watched her finger descend. At the bottom she turned the tablet towards Va-Sheva, who examined it and gave it back.\par

``What will my mother be permitted to say?'' I asked.\par

``Faith will give the household a form sufficient for survival.''\par

``I know about the Labyrinth. I know the day. I am asking about the words.''\par

She looked up from the tablet. ``Words she can repeat without committing breach.''\par

I had heard my mother correct a blessing while she worked, keeping the cloth between her fingers and making me begin again. I tried to hear the new words in her voice. The witness waited until I lowered my head. Then she covered the tablet.\par

Va-Sheva checked the dark cord she had tied beneath the covered white knot at my wrist. The Veil mark remained under Shield cloth. She put a pack into my hands: dried fruit, water skins, sand wraps, a list of measures tied on with green thread. A warden lifted a second pack.\par

``Is that mine too?''\par

``No,'' Va-Sheva said. ``That is what Shield carries because Balance assumes you may become useless.''\par

I almost smiled. She tightened the strap across my shoulder and turned towards the descent.\par

We passed no public gate. Below the old water lines the stone narrowed until even Va-Sheva had to bend. I knew none of the turns. The witness followed behind the wardens; whenever we stopped, I could hear the tablet knock softly against her leg.\par

At the last interior threshold a Faith attendant waited with a bowl of water. Her face was covered. I knew neither her voice nor the small scar above her thumb.\par

``What leaves under seal leaves under God.''\par

I lowered my head. ``What returns under seal returns only if God permits return.''\par

She touched water to my forehead, to the covered knot and then to the dark cord below it. A drop ran from the cord onto her finger.\par

``What is held from the people is not hidden from God.''\par

I remained before her with my head lowered. She stepped aside. The final Shield door opened outward, and cold air moved the cloth across my mouth.\par

The stars stood clear above the Mountain's shoulder. I found the northern star, then the three points by which I had set a bearing through the vent. Va-Sheva was already moving in another direction. I followed her feet until we reached the ridge.\par

A wrapped warden took the lead there. Two more walked behind us with the Justice witness. For a while I could turn and see the Mountain, blacker than the sky; the path folded between two shoulders, and at the next turn I could find no edge of it. I stopped.\par

``Move,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

My body knew stone stairs and the distance between one permitted rest and the next. The sand rolled under my heels. On the slope beyond it the pack pulled me backwards, and I climbed bent almost double, watching Va-Sheva's soles appear beneath the hem of her wrapping. When she stopped, I had to put both hands on my knees before I could drink.\par

By day we lay beneath a cut in the rock, with desert-coloured cloth pinned across its mouth. The wardens opened the spare pack and passed food along the row. Va-Sheva stayed by the gap. Dust had settled in the creases beside her eyes.\par

``Did you know before I brought you the scrap?''\par

``I knew there were outer holdings.''\par

``Sarah Blackwood?''\par

Her finger tightened against the stone. ``Do not say that name again on this route.''\par

I pressed my tongue against the split inside my lip. One of the wardens had stopped chewing. I waited until he looked away.\par

``I thought knowing would make things clearer.''\par

Va-Sheva kept her face towards the gap. ``I have trained for this road since before I was Veil. I still have to keep looking out of this hole.''\par

She lifted one corner of the cloth, held it while the warden beside her looked out, then pinned it down again.\par

I lay back with the pack under my head. Sand had entered the fold beneath my collar and scratched when I swallowed. At dusk she touched my ankle with her foot. We rose and went on.\par

During the second night an engine sounded beyond a ridge. Va-Sheva closed her hand, and all of us went down. I lay with my face beside a small stone until the sound had crossed from one ear to the other and faded. Her hand remained closed. The witness's pack rested against my heel; I could feel her breathing through it.\par

We waited after the sound was gone. Then the leading warden rose.\par

``Vehicle,'' Va-Sheva said when I could walk beside her again.\par

``Did it see us?''\par

``Keep below the ridge.''\par

Before the third dawn we reached a dry well. Its lip had cracked and fallen inward; a dead thorn tree leaned over the opening. Shield circled it before approaching. Va-Sheva ran two fingers along the inner wall and pressed. Something answered beneath us, hollow and distant.\par

I leaned forward. She caught the pack at my shoulder and brought me back.\par

``Wait.''\par

At last a voice came from below in a language I could not follow. A warden answered. Stone moved at the bottom. We descended by holds cut into the wall and entered a passage wide enough for two people to walk together. Metal ribs crossed its ceiling. Smooth white panels lined the walls, with rubber-smelling strips at their joins. Glass bulbs gave a steady light.\par

I stopped beneath one and looked for the oil feed.\par

``No lamp,'' I said.\par

``Not in your sense.''\par

The man who answered spoke our language with its stresses displaced. His face was lined by sun, his clothing full of closed pockets. A short blade rode at his belt beside tools I could not name. He looked past me.\par

``Jean-Charles,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

He crossed between the wardens before they had finished taking their places. From inside his coat he drew a small flat box tied with pink ribbon.\par

``Pistachio and rose. They travelled badly. Eat them tonight or they die.''\par

She took it. Her shoulders lowered and her mouth opened into a smile. She said his name again, softly, with the box held between them. I watched her thumb pass beneath the ribbon. Then a warden moved beside me. She put the box inside her coat and set her hands behind her back.\par

Jean-Charles turned to me. ``So this is him.''\par

``This is the transfer,'' she said.\par

He stepped aside. ``Come through. Mind that edge. It has already taken one of my knees.''\par

As I passed, his eyes went over my hands, feet and covered wrist. His palm touched my shoulder and turned me towards the inner passage; it was gone before I decided whether to resist it.\par

``He is under Exception,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

``I know.''\par

``Do not test him for sport.''\par

He looked back at her, then walked ahead of me without answering.\par

\ornament

A woman waited beside a metal table. Her pale hair was cut short, her grey jacket and trousers without office colour. She took the packet the Justice witness held out and placed it beneath the table's light.\par

``Elise,'' Jean-Charles said.\par

``Va-Elise,'' Va-Sheva said, formally.\par

The woman broke the outer seal under the witness's eyes. She read the first page and left the second folded. Then she signed a smooth white sheet with ink from a tube.\par

``Body received under Shield-Horizon transfer. Speech restricted. Return unpresumed. Name held. Public standing ceased.''\par

``Sequence delivered,'' the witness answered.\par

Va-Elise looked at me. ``Here you answer to Kylian. For people within Horizon, Sa-Kylian. You are under instruction, not command.''\par

``Candidate?''\par

``Provisional Candidate.''\par

``I was Veil of Light.''\par

``The packet records that. You will need to change before we leave. Through that door when I have finished.''\par

She showed me a flat card. The door had a metal handle, a small dark square above it and no visible bolt. She held the card against the square until something clicked inside, then pressed the handle.\par

``It will lock when it closes. Call if you need to come back.''\par

On the table lay folded clothes, dark shoes, a coloured map, keys and a pair of spectacles. I picked up the spectacles by one arm.\par

``My sight is good.''\par

``The lenses are clear. We want people to remember an ordinary young man with glasses.''\par

Jean-Charles took them from me and opened the other arm. ``Both ears. That part is important.''\par

Va-Sheva came to my side. She untied the dark cord below the covered white knot and drew it free. A pale ring remained in the dusty skin. She gave the cord to Va-Elise, who placed it in an envelope and marked the time.\par

``Am I still under Shield?''\par

``Horizon has received you,'' Va-Elise said.\par

``What is Horizon?''\par

``We keep certain Mountain facts from dying inside it and certain outside facts from entering uncontained. You will learn your part here.''\par

``Another Kahir?''\par

Va-Sheva said, ``Do not try to fit it into five hands.''\par

I looked from her bare wrists to the envelope. ``Who commands it?''\par

``Ka-Sarah,'' Va-Elise said. ``For now.''\par

The Justice witness received her copy of the signed sheet. She examined the mark, folded it around her tablet and went out with two wardens. I heard their feet diminish towards the well. Va-Sheva remained.\par

They brought me water and food. Afterwards I changed behind a metal partition, trying each fastening before putting the unfamiliar garment on. The shirt closed with small discs pushed through slits. The trousers showed the separate shape of each leg. I searched the remaining clothes for a wrapping to go over them and found none.\par

When I returned, Jean-Charles asked me to turn round. He pulled the back of the shirt clear where I had caught it beneath the belt.\par

Va-Elise gave me the spectacles. The bulbs caught their edges and set small false lights beside my sight. Va-Sheva entered, saw me and stopped. Her hand began the Shield salute and fell before it reached her breast.\par

``You look like an Outsider scholar,'' she said.\par

``I have been called worse today.''\par

She came closer. ``This is where Shield leaves you.''\par

``You are going back.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``Will Ka-Raedin know I reached this place?''\par

``Through sequence.''\par

I looked down at the shoes. They had a raised seam across each toe, and I could not stop feeling it through the cloth around my feet.\par

``You chose this,'' I said.\par

``I chose the containment that left you breathing.''\par

``I wanted you to choose me.''\par

She kept her eyes on mine. Behind her, Va-Elise gathered the remaining clothes from the table.\par

``You are alive, Kay.''\par

``So I keep being told.''\par

Her hand moved towards the covered knot and stopped. I waited for it to come the rest of the way. She lowered it.\par

``Believe it slowly,'' she said.\par

Then she went through the door. I stood where she had left me until Va-Elise called the new name. She had to call it twice.\par

\ornament

Before departure Va-Elise sat me before a camera. She moved my chin, told me to look at the small opening and stopped me when I straightened into the ceremonial posture. Jean-Charles stood behind her and let his own shoulders sag until I laughed. The light flashed.\par

She showed me the passport in which my face would go. On a separate sheet she wrote Kylian in the outside hand. I copied the letters until she covered her version and I could make them without it. Then she opened the passport and pointed to the name, the photograph space and the numbers beside them.\par

``Someone may take this from you and look at your face. Let him. Stay beside me. If he speaks, wait for me to answer.''\par

She put a telephone on the table and showed me which surfaces were to remain untouched. She spread a world map and named Switzerland, Geneva, the lake. I followed the route of her finger across the printed water. The Mountain occupied no marked place on the sheet.\par

They set me in the back of a vehicle while it was still dark. Through the small mirror on the door I saw Va-Sheva and Jean-Charles walk away from the wardens. I could not hear them. Her hands moved while she spoke. He stood still. Then she bowed her head and wiped one cheek with the heel of her hand.\par

Jean-Charles spoke. He did not reach for her. She set her palm flat against his chest and left it there through one breath, then another. When she took it back, she turned towards the desert. He watched her go and came to the vehicle. I moved nearer the opposite door.\par

The engine made the seat tremble. I gripped its edge.\par

``You are safer than you feel,'' he said.\par

``I have felt safe. It felt like stone on four sides.''\par

He showed me the belt and waited while I fastened it. We crossed dark ground, then roads lined with outside lights. People stood in open doorways. A man pushed a wheeled bin past our window and never looked up. I turned to watch him until the vehicle carried us round a corner.\par

At the airfield Va-Elise passed papers through a small window. A man came out and opened the gate; we drove to a white aircraft with a gold cross dividing green and black on its tail.\par

``Jamaican diplomatic flight,'' Va-Elise said. ``To Geneva. Stay with me.''\par

The captain came down the steps to meet us, a lean, dark-skinned man with his hair tied high behind his head and gold bars on the shoulders of his white shirt. He shook Jean-Charles's hand and kept it while they laughed. Then he offered me his.\par

``Capt'n Billy.''\par

I gave him the name Va-Elise had taught me. He repeated it, then led us up. Wide leather seats faced one another across a table. Va-Elise put her papers on it. Billy rested a hand on the back of my seat while Jean-Charles translated between us.\par

``Next time you come to Jamaica. You wanna go snorkelling? You wanna eat lobster? You wanna smoke weed? You can do all dees tings. Just let Capt'n Billy know.''\par

Jean-Charles stopped twice to laugh before he finished translating. Va-Elise looked up from her papers. ``Geneva first, Billy.''\par

``Yes, ma'am.'' He pulled once on my fastened belt, then went forward to the other pilot and shut the door.\par

I asked Jean-Charles where Jamaica lay. He drew it with a finger on the table, too small for the width of his nail.\par

``He would be pleased to see you there,'' he said.\par

The aircraft began to move. I had another question about the island, but the movement pressed me into the seat and kept it in my mouth. The floor tilted, pressure closed my ears, and the ground withdrew below the window.\par

``Do not look down until I say,'' Jean-Charles told me.\par

I watched the fastener of my belt. A loose end of the strap lifted each time I breathed. When I looked up, the line of land beyond his shoulder lay far below us. Water reached from it to the edge of the window.\par

Somewhere over that water Va-Elise went forward to speak to Billy and closed the door behind her. Jean-Charles sat opposite me. He asked how the well had closed behind us, whether Shield had cut the desert route for me alone, which warden had answered the voice below. I answered what little I knew, stopping when the aircraft moved beneath us. He waited each time and asked the next question after my hands loosened.\par

Near the end he said, ``No need to trouble Elise with any of that. She writes everything down, and a man should keep some of his own weather.''\par

I nodded and watched his reflection in the dark window.\par

When Va-Elise returned, she put a cup beside my hand and sat with her papers again. I waited for the drink to spill. Its surface trembled, settled, and stayed almost level while the land passed slowly below us. I drank without taking my eyes from the rim. Jean-Charles had closed his own eyes; his belt remained fastened, his hands folded in his lap. After a while I put the cup down and let go of the seat.\par

At Geneva a vehicle met us beside the aircraft and took us to a helicopter. ``The family's,'' Va-Elise said. She held my sleeve against its beating wind until I reached the door.\par

From the helicopter I saw mountains with roads cut across their faces, houses standing on the slopes and water gathered between them. Church towers rose above open roofs. A boat crossed the lake below us, leaving two lines that spread until I could no longer distinguish them. We landed near a house above the water. The air smelled of pine, metal and bread.\par

The room they gave me had windows that opened. Water came when I turned a metal handle; light came when I touched the switch. Va-Elise made me repeat both actions, then left me with the light burning and a towel folded beside the basin.\par

For eleven days I barely slept and did not speak. I ate when she put food before me. When Jean-Charles asked a question I wrote the shortest answer I could on a slate and erased it while he watched. Each morning the spectacles lay beside my bed. I put them on when footsteps stopped outside the door.\par

On the twelfth day Va-Elise came for me and brought me to Ka-Sarah.\par

\ornament

She sat with her back partly to the open window. Her white hair was gathered behind her head; dark wool hung from shoulders narrowed by age. A small gold cross lay at her throat. Her right thumb trembled against the left until she closed the left hand over it. Above those hands her eyes were blue-grey and steady.\par

The photograph had shown her standing beside Amastan. I had carried it against my ribs. I stopped inside the doorway until Va-Elise touched my arm.\par

Jean-Charles stood near the window. Ka-Sarah pointed to a chair.\par

``Sit, Sa-Kylian. Elise tells me you have been keeping your answers very short.''\par

I sat.\par

``You are Sarah Blackwood.''\par

``Yes. Here you may call me Ka-Sarah.''\par

``Keeper of Horizon.''\par

``Among other inconveniences.''\par

I looked at the cross. She let it lie against the wool and opened a file on the table. My new photograph was clipped inside the cover. Behind it were copied Mountain marks.\par

``Why am I here?''\par

``The five sent a living man. I intend to keep him capable of answering a question.''\par

``I answered theirs.''\par

``And you will give us yours. We have the transfer record. It tells us what Justice could write and Shield could send. It does not tell us what you saw through a lens, or what you touched, or what you decided to leave where it lay.''\par

I kept my hands on the arms of the chair.\par

``You have the account of my breach.''\par

``I have its charges. Were all the objects you handled named in them?''\par

``No.''\par

``Then there is work to do. I want the order, including what seems unimportant to you. If you do not remember, say so. No one benefits from a description you make to please me.''\par

She turned the page. I saw columns running beneath my name, some filled, some empty.\par

``First the terms. You will not leave this property without permission. You will not attempt private contact with your family, Ka-Raedin, Va-Sheva or any Mountain office. The report the five ordered is to travel by its appointed channel; Elise will arrange its passage. You will not use telephones. You will not keep private writing materials until we decide what may safely remain in your room.''\par

``You brought me out to put me in another room.''\par

``I brought you to this one. You will have others when you can use them without exposing yourself or the people who brought you here.''\par

She waited. Through the window I could hear a boat engine, small and sustained.\par

``And you will not enter churches.''\par

I looked again at her cross. ``Why?''\par

``You have strong opinions about our sacred objects and no way to explain them to a policeman. For now, we keep those encounters here.''\par

``Are you one of them?''\par

``A Christian?''\par

``An Outsider.''\par

``Yes.''\par

The engine outside diminished. She closed the file and kept one hand upon it.\par

``I can have you taught what you need to move among us. I can keep the men who moved you from asking you to repay their curiosity. I cannot give you your old place back. You may dislike the terms. You still have to live under them.''\par

``And if I refuse?''\par

``Then Shield was right and Justice was merciful for too long.''\par

She spoke in the tone she had used for telephones and paper. I looked towards Va-Elise. She remained beside the door with her hands empty.\par

Beyond the window a white sail leaned over, stood upright, then leaned the other way.\par

``I do not want to give you my Mountain.''\par

``Then give me what you saw. Keep the inference apart. If you saw an object and guessed its use, I want the object before the guess.''\par

``Light taught me that.''\par

``Good. There is something I do not have to pay to teach you. You will be given a listener, and time. I want you capable of disagreeing with him.''\par

I drew a breath and had to draw another before it reached the bottom of my chest. She waited.\par

``And the questions I ask?''\par

``Will be answered where we can answer them. Elise has arranged the first lessons.''\par

She turned towards Jean-Charles. ``He is yours to teach the world to. He is not yours to write down. Elise keeps the record.''\par

He inclined his head. His right hand remained in his pocket.\par

Va-Elise opened the door. I rose and took one step towards it.\par

``Sa-Kylian.''\par

I turned back.\par

``The Mountain will feel purer from here. It is the distance. Do not trust it.''\par

Jean-Charles followed me out. In the corridor he said, ``That went beautifully. She did not have anyone shot, which in this house counts as hospitality.''\par

``Do not make him think that is a joke,'' Va-Elise said.\par

``Half a joke.''\par

I laughed before I could stop myself. The sound hurt my throat.\par

\ornament

They gave me bread with a crust that broke under my hand, sharp cheese, water in a glass and an apple cut into quarters. The small knife remained beside Va-Elise's plate. I waited while Jean-Charles poured more water.\par

``Measured,'' he said, grinning.\par

Va-Elise looked at him. ``Do not.''\par

``Witnessed.'' He set the glass against the table.\par

I held the bread halfway to my mouth. His grin went.\par

``Sorry,'' he said. ``Bad joke. Eat.''\par

He pushed the bread nearer. Neither word came again. I took another piece and chewed until I could swallow it. Va-Elise cut the remaining apple and put half on my plate.\par

After the meal she laid a narrow grey book before me. Its title was written twice, once in an outside language and once in careful Mountain hand.\par

\textit{On Light, Lenses, and the Conduct of Images.}\par

I kept both hands beside the plate. ``Am I permitted to read it?''\par

``Yes. It has been translated into your language where translation was possible. Where it was not possible, we left the outside term and added notes. The lessons will be supervised.''\par

``Then you need someone who can read both.''\par

``You will have someone beside you. We need what you can read. If a drawing is wrong, show us.''\par

I put my hand on the cover. The first page held a lens with dimensions and lines printed along its curve. No office seal restricted it. I followed one of the lines, turned the page and found light entering water.\par

The drawing used a line my teachers had left unnamed. I read the note beneath it twice. The sentence ran into an outside word, repeated in small letters beside the angle.\par

I put one finger on the word and another on the place where the line entered the water. There were two possible readings of the note. One agreed with the drawing. I tried the other, tracing its path over the printed page with my nail, then went back to the sentence. Va-Elise sat down opposite me.\par

``A pencil,'' I said.\par

``Tomorrow. With the lesson.''\par

I took a loose thread from the edge of my cuff and laid it between the pages. Va-Elise watched me close the book upon it.\par

``We will begin there,'' she said.\par

``May I keep it tonight?''\par

``Yes.''\par

I carried it upstairs myself.\par

\ornament

That night I lay in a bed too soft and listened to the house. A car passed on the road below. An electrical system hummed inside the walls; I could hear no change in it when the car went away. I pushed back the ordinary sleeve. The white Veil knot remained beneath Shield cloth. Below it the skin was bare where Va-Sheva's cord had been.\par

The book lay on the table. I reached for it and the spectacles, then sat with both in my lap. The curtains remained open. Va-Elise had told me to leave them so. Across the lake, houses showed their light towards the water, and the glass beside my bed returned the room behind me.\par

I opened the manual at the thread. One house lamp laid a narrow, trembling path across the black lake towards my window. I moved my head along the pillow, and the path moved with me. The book stayed open on my knees while I watched. Near dawn the far shore appeared, and the path thinned until I could no longer find it.\par
