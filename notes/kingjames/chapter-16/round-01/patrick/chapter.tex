\typesetchapter{Chapter 16}{Horizon}

\ornament

The Justice witness stood before me in the sealed route beneath the hearing chamber. She was white-haired, and she held the tablet high enough to read without bending her neck. Five witnesses had heard the statement above, she told me. No one from my family had been admitted. Va-Sheva waited three paces away while the witness read the words that would travel with me.\par

``Va-Kailan of Light ceases ordinary standing.''\par

I looked at Va-Sheva. She kept her hands at her sides.\par

``Office consequence passes to Light under Keeper management. Household consequence passes to Faith under sealed wording. Material consequence passes to Balance. Movement consequence passes to Shield. Sequence consequence remains with Justice. The name is held from common use.''\par

The witness touched the tablet at each line. Below the white knot hidden under Shield cloth, Va-Sheva's dark cord pressed into my wrist. I put a finger beneath it.\par

``Will my mother be told I am dead?''\par

``Faith will give the household a form sufficient for survival.''\par

``Give me the words. What will she be told?''\par

The witness lowered the tablet. ``One they can repeat without committing breach. That is all I am permitted to give you.''\par

I waited. Behind her, a warden lifted a pack and passed his arm through its strap. Va-Sheva turned toward the passage. I took my finger from under the cord and followed.\par

We passed no public gate. Shield took me below the hearing chamber, below the old water lines, through seams I had never walked. In one low place Va-Sheva bent almost double. The Justice witness handed her tablet ahead and went through on her knees.\par

Balance had prepared dried fruit, water skins and sand wraps, with a list of measures tied to the pack in green thread. A warden carried a second pack.\par

``Is that mine too?'' I asked.\par

``No,'' Va-Sheva said. ``That is what Shield carries because Balance assumes you may become useless.''\par

She pulled my shoulder strap clear of a projecting stone. I almost smiled.\par

At the last interior threshold, a veiled attendant waited with a bowl of water. I did not recognise her voice or the small scar above her thumb. She held the bowl level while the wardens passed her.\par

``What leaves under seal leaves under God.''\par

I lowered my head. ``What returns under seal returns only if God permits return.''\par

Her wet fingers touched my forehead, the covered knot and the dark cord beneath it. I waited for another verse.\par

``What is held from the people is not hidden from God.''\par

She drew the bowl against her body and stepped aside. The final Shield door opened outward. Cold air moved the cloth over my mouth. Beyond the threshold there was no wall.\par

The desert entered as cold against my cheeks and the smell of mineral, dry scrub, something faintly animal beyond the prepared route. Above the Mountain's shadow the sky was full of stars. I tilted my head back until the cloth at my throat pulled tight, then lowered my eyes to Va-Sheva's feet.\par

We moved before dawn. She led to the first ridge, where a wrapped warden passed her. Two more walked behind me with the Justice witness. For a while the Mountain stood black against the night; then the path folded. I turned and took two steps back.\par

``Move,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

I could see the ridge we had crossed. I turned again and followed her. The loose ground rolled beneath my feet. Where the path narrowed, the warden ahead braced a foot against a stone and reached back. I gave him my hand. He held it until I had found a place for both feet, then went on without looking at me. I kept my eyes on the places where he stepped. By the time Shield put us under stone for the day, both ankles ached.\par

The cut was barely deep enough for bodies and packs. A desert-coloured cloth covered its mouth, pinned at the corners, with a gap beside the rock where Va-Sheva watched. Dust lay along her cheek and in the creases beside her eyes. I crawled near enough to speak without raising my voice.\par

``Did you know, before I showed you the scrap?''\par

``I knew there were outer holdings.''\par

``Sarah Blackwood?''\par

Her finger tightened against the rock. ``You may not say that name again on this route.''\par

``But you know it.''\par

She turned her face toward me. ``You heard me.''\par

I pressed my tongue against the split inside my lip. Behind us someone shifted a pack beneath his head. Va-Sheva returned to the gap.\par

``I thought knowing would make things clearer,'' I said.\par

``Light lies to its children. Shield lies differently.''\par

``How does Shield lie?''\par

She kept her eyes outside. ``We tell ours that if they train enough, they will see danger before it harms what they love.''\par

``And that is false?''\par

``It is true often enough to destroy them.''\par

I stayed beside her until she told me to lie down. At dusk she shook my shoulder and put the pack within reach.\par

The second night carried us across ground I could no longer measure against the Mountain. Once a warden dropped flat and Va-Sheva pulled me down beside him. An engine passed beyond the ridge, a sound without hooves, too large for any animal I knew. Her closed hand stayed in front of my face until it had receded.\par

``Vehicle,'' she said when we were walking again.\par

``Outsider? Did it see us?''\par

``No. Keep behind me.''\par

Before the third dawn we reached a dry well. Its lip was cracked, several stones had fallen, and a dead thorn tree leaned across it. Shield circled the place before approaching. Va-Sheva felt along the inner wall with two fingers and pressed. A hollow sound came from below. We waited beside the stones.\par

A voice spoke inside the well in a language I could not understand. One of the wardens answered. Farther down, stone shifted. The warden went first; Va-Sheva sent me after him and followed close enough that I heard her feet above my head.\par

Below the well the passage widened. Metal ribs supported smooth white panels, and the joints smelled of rubber. Small glass bulbs shone along the wall. I stopped under one and held my hand beneath it.\par

``No lamp,'' I said.\par

``Not in your sense.''\par

A man stood farther along the passage. His Mountain speech bent the words at their ends. He was in his early forties, with a sun-lined face and clothes cut close at wrist and ankle. A short blade rode beside metal tools and black objects I could not name. Every pocket closed.\par

``Jean-Charles,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

He crossed before the wardens had taken their places. From inside his coat he brought a bright flat box tied with pink ribbon and held it out to her.\par

``Pistachio and rose. They travelled badly. Eat them tonight or they die.''\par

She took it in both hands. Her shoulders lowered and she smiled at him.\par

``Jean-Charles.''\par

He kept his hand under the box for a moment after she had received it. A warden set down his pack. She looked toward the sound, put the box inside her coat and clasped her hands behind her back.\par

Jean-Charles turned to me. ``So this is him.''\par

``This is the transfer,'' she said.\par

``Of course.'' He moved aside. ``Welcome. Mind your head there; we have brought you a considerable distance to knock it off now.''\par

His eyes passed over my hands, my feet, the covered wrist and the place at my belt where a blade could have hung. He put a light hand on my shoulder and turned me toward the inner passage. It left before I had decided whether to pull away.\par

``He is under Exception,'' Va-Sheva said. ``Do not test him for sport.''\par

``Never only for sport.''\par

She waited until he was ahead of us before she let me pass.\par

In the first inner chamber a woman stood beside a metal table. Her pale hair was cut above her collar. Grey jacket, trousers, boots; at her throat a narrow cord bore no Kahir sign. On the table lay folded clothes, dark shoes, keys, a coloured map and a pair of spectacles.\par

``Elise,'' Jean-Charles said.\par

Va-Sheva addressed her formally. ``Va-Elise.''\par

The Justice witness brought the packet to her. Va-Elise broke the outer seal while the witness watched, read the first page and left the second folded. She signed a smooth white sheet with a tube that carried its own ink.\par

``Body received under Shield-Horizon transfer. Speech restricted. Return unpresumed. Name held. Public standing ceased.''\par

``Sequence delivered,'' the witness answered.\par

Va-Elise put down the tube. ``What name will you use here?''\par

``Va-Kailan.''\par

``That name remains in the Mountain record. You will travel as Kylian. Under Horizon classification you are Sa-Kylian, a Provisional Candidate.''\par

``I was Veil of Light.''\par

``Here you need instruction. Can you use a telephone? Show a passport? Open an outside door without attracting attention?''\par

I looked at the keys.\par

``A telephone can betray where you are,'' she said. ``The name on the passport must agree with the name you give the man who asks for it. He may be very pleasant. You will still give him the name we have taught you.''\par

``And if he asks about Light?''\par

``You will stay beside me until you can hear such a question without answering it.''\par

``We begin with Kylian,'' she said. ``Say it.''\par

I said it. She made me repeat the sound, then wrote it on the white sheet.\par

Jean-Charles leaned against the table. ``She likes to welcome people warmly.''\par

``Stand clear,'' Va-Elise told him.\par

Va-Sheva took my wrist. She loosened the dark cord below the Shield cloth, drew it free and passed it across the table. Va-Elise put it in an envelope and marked the time. I pulled my hand back against my chest. The white knot remained covered.\par

``Am I still under Shield?''\par

``No,'' Va-Elise said.\par

``What is Horizon?''\par

``We keep certain Mountain facts from dying inside it, and certain outside facts from entering uncontained. You are in our custody.''\par

``Another Kahir?''\par

Va-Sheva said, ``Do not try to fit it into five hands.''\par

``Who commands it?''\par

Va-Elise closed the packet. ``For now, Ka-Sarah.''\par

I looked at Va-Sheva. She held my gaze without speaking.\par

``You will meet her,'' Va-Elise said. ``First you will wash, eat and change. Jean-Charles will show you where.''\par

The shirt closed with small discs pushed through cuts in the cloth. I fastened one into the wrong hole and had to begin again. The trousers showed the shape of each leg. Before the polished metal wall I turned twice, looking for an outer wrapping. Jean-Charles called through the door to ask whether I had drowned.\par

``There is not enough water,'' I said.\par

``Excellent. We can reduce the provision tomorrow.''\par

When I came out, Va-Elise gave me the spectacles.\par

``I can see,'' I said.\par

``The lenses are clear. They are part of the clothes. People look differently at men with spectacles. We want them to look at you and pass on.''\par

She opened the arms and held them toward me. I took them from her.\par

Electric bulbs set small false lights at the edge of my sight. I adjusted the arms behind my ears as Va-Sheva came in. Her hand began the Shield salute and stopped below her breast.\par

``You look like an Outsider scholar.''\par

``Is that what I am to be?''\par

She came closer. ``This is where Shield leaves you.''\par

``You are going back.''\par

``Yes.''\par

I took off the spectacles. One arm caught in my hair, and she waited while I freed it.\par

``Tell them I reached Horizon. If you cannot tell my family, tell Ka-Raedin. Let someone know I came through.''\par

``He will receive it through sequence. I cannot carry a message for you.''\par

``My mother will ask.''\par

Va-Sheva said nothing. I folded the spectacles and opened them again.\par

``You chose this,'' I said.\par

``I chose the only containment that left you breathing.''\par

``That is not the same as choosing me.''\par

Va-Elise kept her eyes on the table. Va-Sheva's lower lip pressed white against the upper.\par

``No,'' she said.\par

I put the spectacles back on. ``Then thank you for choosing breathing.''\par

``You are alive.''\par

``So I keep being told.''\par

``Believe it slowly.''\par

Her hand moved toward the covered knot at my wrist and stopped short of it. I held my arm where it was. She stepped back and left. The door closed behind her, and Va-Elise waited until I turned from it.\par

Before I met Ka-Sarah, Va-Elise sat me in front of a camera. She moved my chin with two fingers, took away the spectacles and made me hold still. On the table were a telephone, a map of the world spread wider than my arms and a passport waiting for my image. She taught me the name I must answer to and the sounds I must leave to her. Jean-Charles opened and closed the door until I could work its handle. When I pushed while the catch was still engaged, he kept his hand off it and waited. I lifted my eyes to his.\par

``Again,'' he said. ``Slowly. Nobody is chasing you here.''\par

I turned the handle until the catch drew back. Through the open doorway I could see the table where Va-Elise was putting the photograph against the passport. She gave me back the spectacles when she had finished.\par

They put me in the back of a vehicle before dawn and left me alone. A small mirror on the door showed the road behind. In it I saw Va-Sheva and Jean-Charles step away from the wardens.\par

I could not hear them. Her hands moved as she spoke. He stood still. Then she bowed her head and wiped one cheek with the heel of her hand. He did not reach for her. His mouth moved, and she set her palm flat against his chest. She left it there through one breath, then another. When she took it back she turned toward the desert without looking at the vehicle. He watched her go, then came to the door beside me. I moved closer to the opposite door before he climbed in.\par

The engine shook the seat. I gripped its edge.\par

``It does that,'' Jean-Charles said. ``Keep your feet on the floor. You are safer than you feel.''\par

``I have felt safe. It felt like stone on four sides.''\par

He looked at my hands. ``You can let go when you are ready.''\par

We crossed dark land, then streets where outside lights multiplied, doors everywhere and no wardens at them. At the airfield Va-Elise handed papers through a small window. A man came out and opened a gate; our vehicle stopped beside a white aircraft with a gold cross dividing green and black on its tail.\par

``Jamaican diplomatic flight,'' Va-Elise said. ``To Geneva. Stay with me.''\par

The captain came down the steps, a lean, dark-skinned man with his hair tied high behind his head and gold bars on the shoulders of his white shirt. He shook Jean-Charles's hand and kept it while they laughed. Then he offered me his.\par

``Capt'n Billy.''\par

I gave him the name Va-Elise had told me to use. He repeated it and led us up. Wide leather seats faced one another across a table. Va-Elise put her papers on it. Billy rested a hand on my seat while Jean-Charles translated between us, Billy's English into Mountain speech, my answers back to him.\par

``Next time you come to Jamaica. You wanna go snorkelling? You wanna eat lobster? You wanna smoke weed? You can do all dees tings. Just let Capt'n Billy know.''\par

Jean-Charles stopped twice to laugh before he finished translating. Va-Elise looked up from her papers. ``Geneva first, Billy.''\par

``Yes, ma'am.'' He pulled once on my fastened belt, then went forward to the other pilot and shut the door. Jean-Charles gave me those words too. I looked down at the belt Billy had checked and left my hand beside its fastening.\par

The floor tilted beneath my feet. Pressure closed my ears, and the earth withdrew below the window.\par

``Do not look down until I say,'' Jean-Charles told me.\par

I kept my eyes on him. Somewhere over water Va-Elise went forward to speak to Billy and closed the door behind her. Jean-Charles loosened his belt.\par

``How did they close the well behind you?''\par

``I was in the passage. I did not see.''\par

``But you heard it?''\par

``Stone moving.''\par

``And the desert route? Cut for you?''\par

I looked toward Va-Elise's empty seat. ``There were old water lines. Below them I do not know.''\par

``Which warden answered the voice?''\par

``The one ahead of me. He was wrapped.''\par

Jean-Charles nodded. ``No need to trouble Elise with any of that. She writes everything down, and a man should keep some of his own weather.''\par

I nodded too. In the dark window I could watch his reflection without turning my head.\par

He showed me Switzerland on a newspaper map and said the country's name. At Geneva the air was cold enough to hurt my teeth. A vehicle met us beside the aircraft and took us to a helicopter.\par

``The family's,'' Va-Elise said.\par

From it I saw mountains with roads across their open faces, water stretching far below us, houses standing apart beneath the sky. Church towers rose above their roofs. When we landed, the air smelled of pine, water, metal and baking bread.\par

They placed me in a house above a lake. The windows opened. Water came by turning a metal handle; light, by touching a switch. Va-Elise made me operate both before she left me in the room.\par

For eleven days I barely slept and did not speak. I ate what she brought. When Jean-Charles asked a question, I wrote the shortest answer I could on a slate and erased it under his eyes. He began putting his hand on the slate before I finished. I waited until he took it away.\par

On the twelfth day, Ka-Sarah received me.\par

She was older than the photograph. I stopped inside the door with my hand still on the handle. White hair was gathered at the back of her head; dark wool hung from narrow shoulders. Her right thumb trembled against her left hand until she closed that hand over it. A small gold cross lay at her throat. Her eyes were blue-grey and steady.\par

She sat with her back partly toward the open window. Va-Elise remained at the door, Jean-Charles by the window. Ka-Sarah did not rise.\par

``An ex-Veil,'' she said. ``It is not every decade Horizon receives one of those. Sit down, Sa-Kylian.''\par

``You are Sarah Blackwood.''\par

``I am. Here you may call me Ka-Sarah.''\par

``Keeper of Horizon?''\par

``You may use that if it helps. The chair is for you.''\par

I sat on its forward edge. She looked at my spectacles, then at my hands.\par

``Elise tells me you have refused speech for eleven days. Have you finished?''\par

``Why am I here?''\par

``Because dead boys tell us less.''\par

``I am not a boy.''\par

``Then ask what you need to ask.''\par

She opened the file on the table between us. I could see paper, photographs and copied Mountain marks. I leaned forward, and her hand settled across the upper sheet.\par

``You are under Exception. You will not leave this property without permission. You will not attempt to contact your family, Ka-Raedin, Va-Sheva or any Mountain office. You will not use telephones. Private writing materials are withheld until we decide you can keep them.''\par

``You have given me a slate.''\par

``Which Elise supervises. You are alive by procedure, Sa-Kylian. Do not mistake that for freedom.''\par

I sat back.\par

``And you will not enter churches.''\par

``Why?''\par

``You are full of broken scripture. Before I let you among people praying in public, I want to know you can leave them alone.''\par

``You wear a cross.''\par

She touched it. ``Yes.''\par

``Are you an Outsider?''\par

``My dear boy, from where you sit, everyone is.''\par

She left her hand on the cross until I looked back at her face.\par

``Horizon has custody of you now. You are alive, confined and unassigned. Can you obey those restrictions?''\par

``And if I refuse?''\par

``Then Shield was right and Justice was merciful for too long.''\par

She spoke in the same tone she had used for the telephones. Beyond her shoulder a boat crossed the lake. Its white wake spread until another boat cut through it. I watched the crossing, then looked at the file beneath her hand.\par

``What do you want from me?''\par

``For now I want you to eat what you are given, sleep when you can, and let Elise teach you how to leave this house without giving yourself away. She cannot do that while you erase every answer from the slate.''\par

``And after that?''\par

``You will tell us what Light saw before Shield buried you.''\par

I pointed to the file. ``You already know.''\par

She lifted the upper sheet. ``What Shield could transfer. What Justice could lawfully write. This is what reached my desk. Your eyes did not come with it.''\par

She put the sheet down where I could see the copied marks. I could not read them from the chair.\par

``We do not know what you saw,'' she said.\par

I let the chair take my weight.\par

``Good. You are learning.''\par

Ka-Raedin had put the same downward weight on the word. My fingers tightened against the chair. She closed the file.\par

``A final warning, Sa-Kylian. The Mountain will feel purer from here. It is the distance. Do not trust it.''\par

Then she turned to Jean-Charles. ``He is yours to teach the world to. He is not yours to write down. Elise keeps the record.''\par

Jean-Charles inclined his head. His right hand stayed in his pocket. Va-Elise opened the door and I rose. Ka-Sarah remained beside the window while we went out.\par

``Well,'' Jean-Charles said in the passage. ``She did not have anyone shot. In this house that counts as hospitality.''\par

``Do not make him think that is a joke,'' Va-Elise said.\par

``Half a joke.''\par

I laughed. The sound hurt my dry throat, and he waited while I swallowed.\par

They gave me bread with a crust that broke under the hand, sharp cheese, an apple and a clear glass of water. Va-Elise cut the apple into quarters and put the small knife beyond my reach. I waited with my hands beside the plate.\par

Jean-Charles lifted his glass. ``Measured,'' he said, grinning.\par

Va-Elise looked at him. ``Do not.''\par

``Witnessed.'' He set the glass against the table.\par

I had lifted the bread. It stopped halfway to my mouth. Those words belonged over the first cup and the sealed jar. Jean-Charles lowered his eyes to my hand, and his grin went.\par

``Sorry. Bad joke.''\par

He moved the bread closer. ``Eat. We make no ceremony of breakfast here.''\par

Va-Elise began her own meal. I put the bread in my mouth. Jean-Charles did not use either word again.\par

Afterward she set a narrow grey book on the cleared table. Its title was written twice, in an outside language I could not read and in careful Mountain hand.\par

\textit{On Light, Lenses, and the Conduct of Images.}\par

``For your lessons,'' she said.\par

I left my hands in my lap. ``Who permits this?''\par

``Ka-Sarah. We have translated it into your language where we could. Where a term would not translate, we have kept it and added notes.''\par

``I may open it?''\par

``Yes. The lessons will be supervised. We intend to know what you make of it. You were Veil of Light.''\par

I reached across the table. The cover lifted stiffly. A lens filled the first page, with dimensions, labels and notes printed along its curve. No office seal crossed the diagram. I followed the curve with one finger. At a word I could not read, I stopped and moved my finger down to the small Mountain writing beneath it. Va-Elise sat beside me without pulling the book toward herself. I read the note twice.\par

``You are protected here,'' Va-Elise said.\par

``The doors are locked.''\par

``Yes. There will be someone to open them when it is time for your lesson.''\par

I turned the page. Light entered water in the next drawing. My finger stopped at the surface line. Va-Elise drew the chair beside mine away from the table and stood.\par

``We will begin there tomorrow.''\par

I kept the page open after she had gone.\par

\ornament

That night the bed sank beneath my hips. I lay listening for the pipe murmur, the stone heartbeat and the distant gate chain. A car passed on the road below. Something hummed steadily inside the wall.\par

I pushed back my sleeve. The white Veil knot remained hidden beneath Shield cloth; below it the skin circled by Va-Sheva's dark cord was bare. I put two fingers there and held them against the pulse.\par

On the table lay the optics manual. Va-Elise had left the curtains open. ``First learn what windows do,'' she had said. I sat up. Houses stood along the far shore with their lamps exposed. One light went out while I watched; farther along, two stayed bright above the water. My own lamp stood beside the window.\par

I opened the manual on my knees and found the drawing of light entering water. Across the lake, one house lamp laid a narrow, trembling path over the black surface to my window. I watched it reach me until dawn thinned it away.\par
