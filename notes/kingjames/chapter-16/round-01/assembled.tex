\typesetchapter{Chapter 16}{Horizon}

\ornament

The white-haired Justice witness read my name in the sealed route beneath the hearing chamber. Shield had already covered the white knot at my wrist. Below the cloth, Va-Sheva's dark cord pressed into the skin.\par

Five witnesses had heard the statement above, the woman told me. My family had been excluded. She held her tablet where the lamp reached it and began again for me.\par

``Va-Kailan of Light ceases ordinary standing.''\par

Her finger moved to the next line.\par

``Office consequence passes to Light under Keeper management. Household consequence passes to Faith under sealed wording. Material consequence passes to Balance. Movement consequence passes to Shield. Sequence consequence remains with Justice. The name is held from common use.''\par

``What will my mother be permitted to say?''\par

``Faith will give the household a form sufficient for survival.''\par

``I know about the Labyrinth. I know the day. I am asking about the words.''\par

The witness looked up. ``Words she can repeat without committing breach.''\par

I watched her cover the tablet. The fastening scraped against its rim. Va-Sheva stood three paces from me with her hands empty, and I looked at her until the witness said that Shield could proceed.\par

We passed no public gate. The route descended beneath the hearing chamber and the old water lines, through seams I had never seen on a map. At one low place Va-Sheva bent and kept a hand above her head; I followed with my pack against my chest until I could straighten.\par

Balance had supplied dried fruit, water skins and sand wraps, with a list of measures tied to the pack by green thread. A warden carried a second pack almost as large as mine.\par

``Is that mine too?'' I asked.\par

``No,'' Va-Sheva said. ``That is what Shield carries because Balance assumes you may become useless.''\par

I almost smiled. The strap slipped from my shoulder and she waited while I put it back.\par

At the last interior threshold a Faith attendant stood with a bowl in both hands. Her face was covered; a small scar crossed the skin above her thumb. I did not know her voice. I had prepared the answering scripture, but she said, ``What leaves under seal leaves under God.''\par

I lowered my head. ``What returns under seal returns only if God permits return.''\par

She touched water to my forehead, then to the covered knot and the dark cord beneath it. A drop stayed between the strands.\par

``What is held from the people is not hidden from God.''\par

She withdrew the bowl. I stood with my head lowered until Va-Sheva touched my shoulder. The final Shield door opened outward. Cold air lifted the cloth over my mouth. Beyond it there was no wall.\par

Outside, the cold went through my wraps. I smelled mineral dust, dry scrub, something animal beyond the prepared route. Stars filled the sky over the Mountain's shadow. I lifted my face until the pack pulled across my shoulders and Va-Sheva told me to watch my feet.\par

She led to the first ridge, then let a wrapped warden pass her. Two more came behind with the Justice witness. For a while I could see the Mountain whenever I turned; after a bend I lost it. I stopped and turned farther.\par

``Move,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

The loose ground rolled beneath my soles. Where the path narrowed, the warden ahead braced a foot against a stone and reached back. I gave him my hand. He held it until I had found a place for both feet, then went on without looking at me. I kept my eyes on the places where he stepped. By dawn my ankles shook when I put weight on them.\par

Under an overhang Shield spread a desert-coloured cloth across the opening and pinned its lower edge with stones. We lay with our packs under our heads. The witness tucked one hand inside the cloth over her tablet and closed her eyes.\par

Va-Sheva watched the narrow gap beside the covering. Dust lay in the creases near her eyes. I worked my tongue against the split inside my lip and waited until the others had settled.\par

``Did you know, before I showed you the scrap?''\par

``I knew there were outer holdings.''\par

``Sarah Blackwood?''\par

Her finger tightened on the stone beside her. ``You may not say that name again on this route.''\par

I reached for the water skin. The measure was tied around its neck, and I held it a while without opening it.\par

``I thought knowing would make things clearer.''\par

Va-Sheva kept her face towards the gap. ``I have trained for this road since before I was Veil. I still have to keep looking out of this hole.''\par

She lifted one corner of the cloth, held it while the warden beside her looked out, then pinned it down again. I loosened the stopper, drank my measure and secured it. A grain of sand had caught under the binding; I picked it out before I put the skin down.\par

We moved at dusk. During the second night an engine passed beyond a ridge. Va-Sheva closed her hand and we went flat. I lay with a stone under my hip while the sound grew, held and dwindled; the warden ahead stayed down long after I could hear it.\par

``Vehicle,'' Va-Sheva said when we rose.\par

``Outsider?''\par

``Yes. Keep below the ridge.''\par

I followed the next pair of heels. Each time their owner stopped, I had to stop; when he moved, I took up the weight again.\par

Before the third dawn we reached a dry well. Its lip was cracked, the rope gone. A dead thorn tree leaned over fallen stones. Shield circled it before approaching. Va-Sheva lowered herself far enough to run two fingers along the inner wall, found a place and pressed. A hollow sound answered below.\par

We waited beside the well until a voice came up in an unfamiliar language. One warden answered. Stone shifted at the base. I passed my pack down through the opening and followed it.\par

The passage below was cool and high enough to stand in. Metal ribs supported smooth white panels; a black seal around an inner door smelled of rubber. Small glass bulbs shone steadily along the wall. I stopped beside one and held my palm near it.\par

``No lamp,'' I said.\par

``Not in your sense.''\par

The man spoke Mountain speech with unfamiliar stresses. His face was sun-lined, his clothes cut close at wrist and ankle. A short blade rode at his belt beside tools of metal and black material. Every pocket closed. He looked past me at the wardens.\par

``Jean-Charles,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

He came across before they had finished setting down the loads. From his coat he took a bright, flat box tied with pink ribbon and offered it to her.\par

``Pistachio and rose. They travelled badly. Eat them tonight or they die.''\par

She took it with both hands. Her shoulders lowered and she smiled at him. She said his name again, softly. I could see a crushed corner under the ribbon. Then a warden set down his pack. She put the box inside her coat and set her hands behind her back.\par

Jean-Charles turned to me. ``So this is him.''\par

``This is the transfer,'' she said.\par

He stepped aside. ``Come through. Mind that edge. It has already taken one of my knees.''\par

As I passed, his eyes went over my hands, feet and covered wrist. His palm touched my shoulder and turned me towards the inner passage; it was gone before I decided whether to resist it.\par

``He is under Exception,'' Va-Sheva said. ``Do not test him for sport.''\par

He looked back at her, then walked ahead of me without answering.\par

\ornament

In an inner chamber a woman waited beside a metal table. Her pale hair was cut short, her grey jacket and trousers bore no office sign, and a narrow cord disappeared into her collar. The Justice witness set a sealed packet in front of her.\par

``Elise,'' Jean-Charles said.\par

Va-Sheva addressed her formally. ``Va-Elise.''\par

Va-Elise broke the outer seal under the witness's eyes, read the first page and left the second folded. With a thin tube she signed a smooth white sheet.\par

``Body received under Shield-Horizon transfer. Speech restricted. Return unpresumed. Name held. Public standing ceased.''\par

``Sequence delivered,'' the witness answered.\par

Va-Elise turned the sheet towards me. ``Your old name is held in the Mountain record. Outside you will use Kylian. Here, while you are under instruction, Sa-Kylian.''\par

``Candidate?''\par

``Provisional Candidate.''\par

``I was Veil of Light.''\par

``The packet records that. Here you will need instruction in doors, telephones, passports and money. We begin with Kylian. Say it.''\par

I said it. She made me repeat the sound, then wrote it on the sheet. On the rest of the table lay folded clothes, dark shoes, keys, a coloured map and a pair of spectacles. I picked them up and looked through them at my own hand.\par

``My sight is good.''\par

``The lenses are clear. We want people to remember an ordinary young man with glasses. Put them beside the clothes.''\par

Va-Sheva took my wrist. She worked a nail under the dark cord and loosened it, drawing it away without disturbing the cloth over the white knot. A strand caught. She freed it, wound the cord once around two fingers and handed it across the table. Va-Elise put it in an envelope and marked the time.\par

``Am I still under Shield?''\par

``Horizon has received you.''\par

``What is Horizon?''\par

``We keep certain Mountain facts from dying inside it, and certain outside facts from entering uncontained.''\par

``Another Kahir?''\par

``Do not try to fit it into five hands,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

I looked at the envelope. ``Who commands it?''\par

``For now, Ka-Sarah.''\par

Va-Elise slid the packet out of my reach. The Justice witness received a copy of the signed sheet, examined the mark and folded it around her tablet.\par

``You will meet her after you have washed, eaten and slept,'' Va-Elise said. ``Jean-Charles, show him.''\par

\ornament

They gave me water in a room where I could close the door. I washed the dust from my face and arms. It gathered in the bottom of the basin; when Jean-Charles showed me the release, it went away through a hole too small for my smallest finger.\par

The shirt closed with discs pushed through slits in the cloth. I missed one and had to undo the others to put the edges level. The trousers showed each leg separately. I turned twice before the metal wall, searching the folded things for an outer wrapping.\par

When I returned, Jean-Charles pulled the back of my collar free where I had folded it under. Va-Elise handed me the spectacles. Their lenses caught the bulbs and placed two small lights at the edge of my sight.\par

Va-Sheva entered and stopped. Her hand began the Shield salute, rose a little and fell to her side.\par

``You look like an Outsider scholar.''\par

I touched the frame. ``I have been called worse today.''\par

She came closer. ``This is where Shield leaves you.''\par

``You are going back.''\par

``Yes.''\par

I took off the spectacles. One arm caught in my hair, and she waited while I freed it.\par

``Will Ka-Raedin know I reached this place?''\par

``Through sequence.''\par

I folded the spectacles and opened them again. ``And my family will hear nothing.''\par

She kept her eyes on mine.\par

``You chose this,'' I said.\par

``I chose the only containment that left you breathing.''\par

``I wanted you to choose me.''\par

Va-Elise gathered the remaining clothes from the table. I put the spectacles back on. Va-Sheva's lower lip pressed white against the upper.\par

``You are alive, Kay.''\par

``So I keep being told.''\par

Her hand moved towards the covered knot and stopped. I waited for it to come the rest of the way. She lowered it.\par

``Believe it slowly.''\par

She stepped back and went through the door. I stood where she had left me until Va-Elise called the new name. She had to call it twice.\par

\ornament

Before departure Va-Elise sat me in front of a camera. She moved my chin with two fingers, took away the spectacles and made me hold still. On the table were a telephone, a world map spread wider than my arms and a passport waiting for my image.\par

She wrote Kylian in the outside hand. I copied the letters until she covered her version and I could make them without it. Then she pointed to the name, the photograph space and the numbers beside them.\par

``Someone may take this from you and look at your face. Let him. Stay beside me. If he speaks, wait for me to answer.''\par

Jean-Charles opened and closed the door until I could work its handle. When I pushed while the catch was still engaged, he kept his hand off it and waited. I lifted my eyes to his.\par

``Again,'' he said. ``Slowly. Nobody is chasing you here.''\par

I turned the handle until the catch drew back. Through the doorway Va-Elise was putting the photograph against the passport. She gave me back the spectacles when she had finished, then named the places on the map: Switzerland, Geneva, the lake. I followed her finger across the printed water.\par

Before dawn they put me in the back of a vehicle. A small mirror on the door showed part of the road behind it. Through that mirror I saw Va-Sheva step away from the wardens with Jean-Charles.\par

I could not hear them. Her hands moved as she spoke, and he stood still. Then she bowed her head and wiped one cheek with the heel of her hand. I had seen blood on that hand, sand under its nails. She wiped the cheek again. Jean-Charles said something without reaching for her. She put her palm flat on his chest and held it there through one breath, then another. When she took it back, she turned towards the desert without looking at the vehicle.\par

He watched her go. I followed her in the mirror until he opened the door beside me. I moved closer to the opposite door.\par

The engine made the seat tremble. I gripped its edge.\par

``You are safer than you feel,'' he said.\par

``I have felt safe. It felt like stone on four sides.''\par

He showed me the belt and waited while I fastened it. We crossed dark ground, then roads lined with outside lights. People stood in open doorways. A man pushed a wheeled bin past our window and never looked up. I turned to watch him until the vehicle carried us round a corner.\par

At the airfield Va-Elise handed papers through a small window. A man came out and opened a gate; our vehicle stopped beside a white aircraft with a gold cross dividing green and black on its tail.\par

``Jamaican diplomatic flight,'' Va-Elise said. ``To Geneva. Stay with me.''\par

The captain came down the steps to meet us, a lean, dark-skinned man with his hair tied high behind his head and gold bars on the shoulders of his white shirt. He shook Jean-Charles's hand and kept it while they laughed. Then he offered me his.\par

``Capt'n Billy.''\par

I gave him the name Va-Elise had told me to use. He repeated it, then led us up. Wide leather seats faced one another across a table. Va-Elise put her papers on it. Billy rested a hand on the back of my seat while Jean-Charles translated between us, Billy's English into Mountain speech, my answers back to him.\par

``Next time you come to Jamaica. You wanna go snorkelling? You wanna eat lobster? You wanna smoke weed? You can do all dees tings. Just let Capt'n Billy know.''\par

Jean-Charles stopped twice to laugh. He repeated the English slowly before giving me each part. I asked what snorkelling was. He put two fingers beside his eyes, pointed downward and said that I could breathe while I looked under the sea. Billy watched the demonstration and nodded.\par

Va-Elise looked up from her papers. ``Geneva first, Billy.''\par

``Yes, ma'am.'' He pulled once on my fastened belt, then went forward to the other pilot and shut the door.\par

The floor tilted beneath my feet; pressure closed my ears. The earth withdrew below the window.\par

``Do not look down until I say,'' Jean-Charles told me.\par

I watched the fastener of my belt. A loose end of the strap lifted each time I breathed. When I looked up, the line of land beyond his shoulder lay far below us. Water reached from it to the edge of the window. He talked while Va-Elise read her papers; I kept my eyes on him.\par

Somewhere over that water Va-Elise went forward to speak to Billy and closed the door behind her. Jean-Charles sat opposite me. He asked how the well had closed behind us, whether Shield had cut the desert route for me alone, which warden had answered the voice below. I answered what little I knew, stopping when the aircraft moved beneath us. He waited each time and asked the next question after my hands loosened.\par

Near the end he said, ``No need to trouble Elise with any of that. She writes everything down, and a man should keep some of his own weather.''\par

I nodded and watched his reflection in the dark window.\par

When Va-Elise returned, she put a cup beside my hand and sat with her papers again. I waited for the drink to spill. Its surface trembled, settled, and stayed almost level while the land passed slowly below us. I drank without taking my eyes from the rim. Jean-Charles had closed his eyes; his belt remained fastened, his hands folded in his lap. After a while I put the cup down and let go of the seat.\par

At Geneva a vehicle met us beside the aircraft and took us to a helicopter. ``The family's,'' Va-Elise said. She held my sleeve against its beating wind until I reached the door.\par

From it I saw mountains with roads cut across their faces, houses standing on the slopes and water gathered between them. Church towers rose above the roofs. A boat crossed the lake below us, leaving two lines that spread until I could no longer distinguish them. We landed near a house above the water. The air smelled of pine, metal and bread.\par

The room they gave me had windows that opened. I stood beside one with my fingers around the handle until Va-Elise told me to shut it; the room was growing cold. Water came when I turned another metal handle; light came when I touched a switch. She made me repeat both actions before leaving.\par

On the night we arrived I listened to the walls. An electrical hum continued through them with a steadiness I distrusted. When a car passed on the road below, I could hear the hum beneath it and after it had gone.\par

For eleven days I barely slept and did not speak. I ate when Va-Elise put food before me and drank when she placed the cup in my hands. When Jean-Charles asked a question, I wrote the shortest answer I could on a slate. He watched me erase it before the next question. I worked the wet cloth across each line until the marks had gone.\par

On the twelfth day Va-Elise came without the slate and took me to Ka-Sarah.\par

\ornament

She sat with her back partly to an open window. Her white hair was gathered behind her head; dark wool hung from shoulders narrowed by age. A small gold cross lay at her throat. Her right thumb trembled against the left until she closed the left hand over it. Above those hands her eyes were blue-grey and steady.\par

The photograph had shown her standing beside Amastan. I had carried it against my ribs. I stopped inside the doorway until Va-Elise touched my arm.\par

Jean-Charles stood near the window. Ka-Sarah pointed to a chair.\par

``Sit, Sa-Kylian. Elise tells me you have been keeping your answers very short.''\par

I sat.\par

``You are Sarah Blackwood.''\par

``Yes. Here you may call me Ka-Sarah.''\par

``Keeper of Horizon.''\par

``Among other inconveniences.''\par

I looked at the cross. She let it lie against the wool and opened a file on the table. My new photograph was clipped inside the cover. Behind it were copied Mountain marks.\par

``Elise tells me you eat. Do you sleep?''\par

``A little.''\par

``Tell her when you cannot. She cannot help you with something she does not know.''\par

``Why am I here?''\par

``The five sent a living man. I intend to keep him capable of answering a question.''\par

``I answered theirs.''\par

``We have the transfer record. It tells us what Justice could write and Shield could send. It does not tell us everything you saw through a lens, or touched, or decided to leave where it lay.''\par

I kept my hands on the arms of the chair.\par

``You have the account of my breach.''\par

``I have its charges. Were all the objects you handled named in them?''\par

``No.''\par

``Then there is work to do. I want the order, including what seems unimportant to you. If you do not remember, say so.''\par

She turned the page. Columns ran beneath my name, some filled, some empty.\par

``First the terms. You will not leave this property without permission. You will not attempt private contact with your family, Ka-Raedin, Va-Sheva or any Mountain office. The report the five ordered still goes through Va-Sheva; Elise will arrange its passage. You will not use telephones. You will not keep private writing materials until we decide what may safely remain in your room.''\par

``You brought me out to put me in another room.''\par

``I brought you to this one. You will have others when you can use them without exposing yourself or the people who brought you here.''\par

She waited. Through the window I could hear a boat engine, small and sustained.\par

``And you will not enter churches.''\par

I looked again at her cross. ``Why?''\par

``You have strong opinions about our sacred objects and no way to explain them to a policeman. For now, we keep those encounters here.''\par

``Are you one of them?''\par

``A Christian?''\par

``An Outsider.''\par

``Yes.''\par

The engine outside diminished. She closed the file and kept one hand upon it.\par

``I can have you taught what you need to move among us. I cannot give you your old place back. You may dislike the terms. You still have to live under them.''\par

``And if I refuse?''\par

``Then Shield was right and Justice was merciful for too long.''\par

She spoke in the tone she had used for telephones and paper. I looked towards Va-Elise. She remained beside the door with her hands empty. Beyond the window a white sail leaned over, stood upright, then leaned the other way.\par

``I do not want to give you my Mountain.''\par

``Then give me what you saw. Keep the inference apart. If you saw an object and guessed its use, I want the object before the guess.''\par

``Light taught me that.''\par

``Good. There is something I do not have to pay to teach you. You will be given a listener, and time. I want you capable of disagreeing with him.''\par

I drew a breath and had to draw another before it reached the bottom of my chest. She waited.\par

``And the questions I ask?''\par

``Will be answered where we can answer them. Elise has arranged the first lessons.''\par

She turned towards Jean-Charles. ``He is yours to teach the world to. He is not yours to write down. Elise keeps the record.''\par

He inclined his head. His right hand remained in his pocket. Va-Elise opened the door. I rose and took one step towards it.\par

``Sa-Kylian.''\par

I turned back.\par

``The Mountain will feel purer from here. It is the distance. Do not trust it.''\par

Jean-Charles followed me out. In the passage he said, ``Well. That went beautifully. She did not have anyone shot, which in this house counts as hospitality.''\par

``Do not make him think that is a joke,'' Va-Elise said.\par

``Half a joke.''\par

I laughed. The sound hurt my dry throat and I swallowed before I could do it again.\par

\ornament

They fed me bread whose crust broke under my thumb, a sharp cheese, an apple and water in a clear glass. Va-Elise cut the apple into quarters. When she had finished, she laid the knife on her own side of the table.\par

I waited for the prayer. Jean-Charles took his glass and raised it a little.\par

``Measured,'' he said, grinning.\par

Va-Elise looked at him. ``Do not.''\par

``Witnessed,'' he added, and set it down.\par

The bread stopped halfway to my mouth. These were the words for the first cup and the sealed jar. I held the broken crust against my fingers until a piece fell onto the table.\par

His grin went. ``Sorry. Bad joke.''\par

He moved the bread within my reach. I ate the piece I was holding, then picked up the fallen crust. He drank without speaking again.\par

After the meal Va-Elise cleared a place beside my glass and set a narrow grey book there. Its title had been written twice: in outside letters I could not read and in careful Mountain hand.\par

\textit{On Light, Lenses, and the Conduct of Images.}\par

I kept my hands beside the plate. ``Am I permitted to read it?''\par

``Yes. It has been translated into your language where translation was possible. Where it was not possible, we left the outside term and added notes. The lessons will be supervised.''\par

``Then you need someone who can read both.''\par

``You will have someone beside you. We need what you can read. If a drawing is wrong, show us.''\par

I put my hand on the cover. The first page held a lens with dimensions and lines printed along its curve. No office seal restricted it. Some labels had Mountain words written close above the outside ones. Where the space was too narrow, a line led into the margin and the explanation continued there.\par

I turned the page and found light entering water. The note beneath it ran into an outside word, repeated in small letters beside the angle. I put one finger on the word and another on the place where the line entered the water.\par

There were two possible readings of the note. One agreed with the drawing. I tried the other, tracing its path over the printed page with my nail, then went back to the sentence. Va-Elise sat down opposite me.\par

``A pencil,'' I said.\par

``Tomorrow. With the lesson.''\par

I took a loose thread from the edge of my cuff and laid it between the pages. Va-Elise watched me close the book upon it.\par

``We will begin there,'' she said.\par

``May I keep it tonight?''\par

``Yes.''\par

I carried it upstairs myself.\par

\ornament

The bed gave beneath my hips when I lay down. I turned, pressed it flat with my hand and tried again. A car passed on the road below. Inside the walls the electrical hum continued. I listened for the pipe murmur and gate chains until the sound of the car had gone.\par

I pushed back the shirt sleeve. The white Veil knot remained under Shield cloth. Below it the skin was bare where Va-Sheva's dark cord had rested. I rubbed the narrow band with my thumb, then drew the sleeve down.\par

The book lay on the table beside the bed. Va-Elise had left the curtains open. ``First learn what windows do,'' she had said when I reached for them. Across the lake there were lights at different heights along the shore; some disappeared while I watched. My room shone in the window around my reflection.\par

I sat up and opened the manual on my knees at the thread. One lamp across the lake laid a narrow, trembling path over the black surface towards my window. I watched until dawn thinned the path away.\par
