## Governing diagnosis

Chapter 1 already contains the book's strongest opening-scale ritual engine: the Pendulum, the Bowl, Kailan's naming, the closed gates, the five evenly spaced strokes, the delayed answer, and Sarah's counter-reading all belong in the chapter. Its weakness is not insufficient invention but an incomplete causal closure and too much retrospective explanation around the strongest physical events.

The central hard defect is procedural. The chapter sends the five Keepers through their office arches, but the public certification of the answer is given by Va-Maera, an unsuccessful Veil, and Shield then releases the exits without showing the Keepers' return. This contradicts the established readiness sequence and erases the intended significance of Ka-Elun's first true act as Keeper of Faith. The repair is to make the Faith arch open first, have Ka-Elun return and certify the answer, then return the other four Keepers before Shield opens the Hall.

The governing style defect is over-interpretation. Kailan repeatedly names the significance, social meaning, or inner plurality of actions that are already visible: the narration calls out laws, power, shame, cheering, and several simultaneous selves instead of letting bodily decisions carry those meanings. The opening interview also spends too long establishing Paul's qualifications before the ritual begins, while Sarah's closing inference occasionally exceeds the evidence and risks sounding like the book's answer key.

Two local continuity defects require correction: the Hall contains five thousand people but an early thermal description counts only a thousand kneeling backs; and the final release must follow the Keepers' certified return rather than the answer alone.

The chapter's soul is the fifteen-year-old Kailan's divided hunger: he wants the public name, Va-Sheva's recognition, and the hidden shape behind the rite at the same instant. Revision must preserve that adolescent mixture through physical attention and choice, not flatten it into procedural exposition.

The chapter's ending remains right: Paul extracts the reproducible count and interval; Sarah recognizes the Reggane pattern, reclassifies Kailan as a strategic source, and orders Paul to gain his trust without telling him why. That reversal is the chapter's irreversible external turn.

### Fundamental dramatic question

What does it cost a curious fifteen-year-old to be named into a sacred order at the exact moment that order reveals evidence of a hidden planetary work?

### Causal spine

1. Paul asks Kailan for the beginning and establishes a recorded, sceptical inquiry.
2. Kailan gives the day of his Ascension under the Pendulum.
3. The Bowl demonstrates that the Mountain can both receive and answer.
4. Kailan chooses the unscripted answer and is blood-bound as Sa-Kailan.
5. Shield withholds release; each Keeper enters a separate office passage.
6. The congregation waits while five sources sound at exact intervals.
7. A distinct answer returns after the same interval.
8. Ka-Elun returns through Faith and certifies the answer; the other Keepers return; Shield releases the Hall.
9. Paul extracts count, interval, distinction, and recurrence.
10. Sarah recognizes the Reggane signature and converts testimony into an operation founded on concealed intent.

### Structural asymmetry and end value

The ritual begins as Kailan's initiation and ends as Sarah's acquisition. Kailan leaves the remembered Hall with a name; Paul leaves the interview with a waveform; Sarah leaves the monitoring room with a human route into the Mountain. The reader should finish ahead of Kailan but without being handed the complete mechanism.
