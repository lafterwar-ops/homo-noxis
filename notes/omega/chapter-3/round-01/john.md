## Governing diagnosis

Chapter 3 works. Its soul is not the information that an underground people possess
an oasis or a cognate creation story. Its soul is Kailan's first encounter with an
open world that is at once beautiful, useful, sexual, doctrinally hostile, and
operationally dangerous. The chapter makes one governing idea physical: light never
arrives innocent. A film is chosen light; the Oasis is controlled light; scripture
places an interpretation over what Kailan sees; a flash may be signal, mirage, or
projection; Shield reacts by collecting traces before choosing a story. That unity
lets the chapter carry substantial exposition without becoming a tour.

The dramatic question is: **when Kailan is finally permitted to see outside, will
seeing free him, recruit him more deeply into the Mountain's account, or teach him
how evidence can be controlled?** The answer is productively mixed. He discovers
that the sacred image is a working ecosystem, notices the omissions in its official
representation, is personally reawakened by Va-Sheva, and then watches the Mountain
turn uncertainty into procedure. He returns grateful for the roof and certain that
the desert addressed him personally. The older Kailan can hear the vanity in that
certainty; the boy could not. That is a genuine reversal rather than a simple
revelation.

The chapter's material defects are local:

1. The opening hinge says that Light gave Kailan a beam and would not let him learn
   it, then moves directly to the Oasis permit. Chapter 4 allows the chronology to
   be recovered—assignment first, Oasis interruption, tracing resumed after—but a
   first-time reader has to reconstruct the hinge after the fact.
2. The access-party enumeration momentarily obscures the crucial arithmetic. The
   prose announces seven, then names Kailan, three older women, a boy attached to
   one of them, and two workers in a grammatical shape that can be read as six.
   Because the later correction from seven to six drives the breach sequence, the
   original seven should be instantly countable.
3. One conclusion, “Nothing was missing from the work,” is recoverable from the
   aloud Balance tally, but its present form briefly sounds like an external summary
   rather than Kailan's report of what the tally established.
4. The Oasis geography contains one heavily loaded orientation sentence after Paul
   has already forced a useful size estimate. Its hiding logic is important, but the
   accumulation of crescent, second height, farther ridge, south-east closure,
   south-west dunes, terraces, palms, crops, and hypothetical desert observer asks
   the reader to hold more map than the subsequent action uses.
5. Kailan and Paul are sharply differentiated in knowledge and motive, but both
   occasionally arrive at polished antitheses in the same house rhythm. The best
   lines earn that compression; adjacent interpretation should not repeat it.

None of these defects justifies a new scene, a changed outcome, a new observer, or a
new causal event.

## Reading-experience and architecture map

### Causal spine

1. Modern instruction exposes Kailan to deep time, testable claims, and the chosen
   frame of moving images.
2. His demand for direct testing produces the swimming/microscope bargain and makes
   Paul's limits of authority visible.
3. Paul's question about the morning after the Mirror returns the account to the
   first outside permission and the beam that permission postpones.
4. Permission, counters, washing, gate orders, and body counts establish that the
   Oasis is not freedom; it is controlled access through an organism of offices.
5. The first sky and inhabited Oasis overturn the simplified Genesis gravure while
   preserving Kailan's awe.
6. The Book of Blood explains the five offices and manufactures the Outsider as a
   hereditary moral threat; visible labour undercuts and supports the scripture at
   once.
7. Paul's comparison with his own Genesis story moves the conflict from “true or
   false scripture” to rival tellers, transmission, and agency.
8. Va-Sheva converts the outside from doctrine into desire, personal history, and
   professional vigilance.
9. Flash, turned marker, strand, and missing body trigger a Shield response that
   refuses premature separation of coincidence from attack.
10. The return rite restores enclosure. The boy misreads ambiguous light as a
    private sign; the interview's close lets the older teller expose that error
    without erasing its emotional truth.

### Emotional movement

Confinement and suspicion → bargaining agency → ceremonial privilege → sensory
overwhelm → intellectual recognition → attraction and remembered intimacy → alarm →
humiliation by the body's count → relief under stone → retrospective tenderness
toward the believing boy.

### Proof structure

The chapter deliberately offers nested grades of proof rather than one verdict:

- fossils and DNA arrive through a film and tutor;
- Paul admits that moving images can persuade and that he did not witness the lesson;
- the Oasis supplies Kailan's direct sight but only from a controlled marker;
- scripture supplies a named preserved source whose historical authority remains
  disputed;
- the flash is direct perception without explanation;
- Shield collects marker orientation, strand, body count, and custody rather than
  naming a cause;
- the boy nevertheless chooses a private supernatural meaning.

This proof ladder is load-bearing and should be preserved.

## Teller, source, room, and omniscience audit

The chapter obeys the permanent narration rule. There is no external authorial
camera.

- Every `room` passage is an identified exchange between Kailan and Paul.
- The prose after the room ending is adult Kailan's sustained first-person testimony
  to Paul about his own perception, memory, action, and contemporaneous inference.
- The permission strip, Book of Blood, and Threshold verse are explicitly identified

  motives belong to those sources, not to an omniscient narrator.
- The Oasis labour, Va-Sheva's gestures, the marker, the strand, Mara, the counts,
  and the return washing remain within Kailan's field of sight or hearing.
- Kailan's counterfactual desert observer is marked as inference (“would”), not
  invisible factual access.

  I know of” and by his admission that he was absent.
- The one provenance edge worth repairing is the work-tally conclusion. Anchor the
  result explicitly to the called tally. Do not add Va-Sheva's thoughts, an unseen
  watcher on the ridge, an objective explanation of the flash, or institutional
  intentions that no teller can recover.

## Self-arbitrated intervention package

This is the only compatible package John recommends for production:

1. **Opening hinge, sentence scale:** make clear that Kailan had been assigned his
   first beam, but the Oasis permission prevented him from beginning its route that
   morning. Preserve Paul's question, the order of disclosure, and Chapter 4's full
   tracing scene. The indispensable concept is “assigned, not yet walked”; no new
   event is needed.
2. **Count legibility, sentence scale:** make the seven access bodies grammatically
   countable on first reading. Preserve every existing person and preserve Mara as
   one of the older women, so her absence still changes seven to six.
3. **Source anchor, clause scale:** render the clean work count as what Kailan heard
   the Balance tally establish, not as a view from outside his account.
4. **Payment pass, paragraph scale:** in the post-size-interruption geography, remove
   only duplicated orientation. Preserve the Mountain's northern shadow, near rock,
   farther ridge/dunes as concealment, cultivated terraces, and goat-driven pot
   wheel. Those details establish why the Oasis exists, how it is hidden, and how it
   is sustained.
5. **House-rhythm pass, at most a few phrases:** where a physical image, an aphoristic
   interpretation, and an explanatory gloss all make the same point, keep the image
   and whichever single interpretation carries character. Do not flatten Kailan's
   optical/religious voice or Paul's evidentiary/dry voice.
