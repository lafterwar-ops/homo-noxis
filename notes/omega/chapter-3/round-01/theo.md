## What happened, in remembered order

The interview begins with Paul asking about Kailan's first lessons. Kailan remembers the film or projected lesson on life on Earth: immense geological time, fossils in rock, DNA, and the disturbing freedom of a world that treats scripture as the beginning of inquiry rather than its end. A Basquiat-like crowned beast in the corridor becomes newly legible to him after the fossils. He wants to use the microscope himself and distrusts the curated light-image because someone chose what to show and what to leave dark. Paul denies that anyone watched the lesson, admits that moving images can persuade nations, and cannot promise access to the microscope.

Paul raises the exercise Kailan refused. Kailan rejects being dressed up for games, then accepts swimming because the film showed a diver among sea creatures. He turns the imposed regimen into a request: learn to swim and dive, obtain the microscope, and test the pond rather than trusting the film.

Paul asks what happened after the Mirror. The remembered narrative opens on the boy receiving rare permission to go outside to the Oasis. His mother gives ritual warnings; his father denies that outside is a reward, and the boy insists that an open eye is not the danger. The route outward is a chain of counted permissions, washing, counters, gates, Shield orders, and lowered eyes. Then uncontrolled daylight and the endless sky break the Mountain's scale. The Oasis overwhelms him: water, leaves, bees, crops, paper or fibre work, fish, dew, and a hidden agricultural system whose abundance extends beyond the holy picture he knew.

At the water prayer, scripture names the five Keepers and gives each an office. It then tells of Shield's two sons: the weaker or cunning brother murders the stronger and carries away a daughter of Faith; their descendants become the Outsiders. In the interview frame Paul recognizes a reversed or opposite-facing Adam-and-Eve/Cain tradition. Their disagreement sharpens around the woman: Paul's version blames her for crossing a threshold; Kailan's makes her passive plunder. Kailan calls Paul a child of the murderer who has remembered and found the Mountain.

After the prayer Kailan sees Va-Sheva on perimeter duty and is struck by her hair in the light. Their old familiarity survives inside her rank and his new prefix. He is awkwardly attracted to her; she catches his gaze, teases him, and keeps returning her attention to the perimeter. He asks whether the Outsider story and the Beast are real. She will not answer directly, but says Shield does not guard empty gates, and that the desert is larger than the Oasis.

Kailan sees a momentary flash on a distant ridge. Va-Sheva stops him pointing and answers with scripture about desert mirage, which he does not believe. She signals a security response. A boundary marker has been turned; something like a pale strand is lifted from the ground and sealed. A count is wrong because an old woman, Mara, has strayed to gather or hold a dried herb. The wardens isolate what she carried or wore, recount every person, animal, and tool, and keep the group from re-entering until the breach is contained. Va-Sheva says the flash, marker, and woman are probably unrelated, but that at the Threshold Shield joins signs first and separates them later.

The group returns through surrender of the counter, washing, and a blessing that brings what touched the open back under shadow. The low ceiling produces involuntary relief. The boy sleeps convinced the desert flashed for him alone. In the frame Paul laughs at the vanity of boys, then softens it: perhaps it harms no one to have believed that once. He ends the interview to sit with what he heard.
