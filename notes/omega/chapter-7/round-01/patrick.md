## 3. Spoken performance and testimony

### Sa-Kailan

The frame voice alternates between literal inquiry, argumentative acceleration, and dry retaliation. “What is all this for?” is clean, answerable speech. His long Homer passage is not a detachable essay: he is trying to compel two listeners by accumulation. The repeated pauses are breath stations and moments in which he watches them fail to interrupt. Its sequence should remain whole. The claim becomes fully playable at “Do you not see it, Paul?” because he assigns Paul the obligation to answer and then assigns Urs the war parallel.

The younger remembered voice has a different breath. Under title and hierarchy it contracts into “Yes, Keeper,” “No, Keeper,” and short questions. When an apparatus appears, the sentences lengthen into observation: “Thick wall. Curved base. The glaze distorts...” When embarrassed, language collapses to “Oh” and the body takes over. When hungry for forbidden knowledge, he begins stacking abstractions; Ka-Syphiron and Va-Raedin repeatedly cut that expansion down. This is a distinct and playable voice, not retrospective omniscience.

### Paul

Paul uses administrative calm and methodological refusal. He can be funny without chasing Urs's rhythm. His Homer answer is longer and syntactically balanced because his tactic is to reverse an argument carefully, not to win a joke. It gives Sa-Kailan a genuine opponent and should not be shortened into a slogan. In the Martinez exchange the same tactic returns in compressed form: “Perhaps” and “without cheating” enact the standard he previously demanded. Sa-Kailan's reminder about Homer is therefore an earned answer, not reader-facing commentary.

### Urs

Urs's vocabulary arrives in nicknames, mock titles, and verdicts: “cider boy,” “His Absconditusness,” “For the win,” “Harsh Paul.” He accelerates transitions rather than develops explanations. His function is playable because he repeatedly makes someone choose, answer, or stop. “Jesus, Paul. Let him eat” also prevents Paul's experiment from becoming socially frictionless.

### Ka-Syphiron

Ka-Syphiron changes pressure by colliding official diction with appetite and impropriety. His imperatives are short—close, clear, look, drink, laugh, be careful—while his longer warnings appear only after he has won the body's attention with a touch or object. The dangerous temptation is to treat his polished sayings as isolated aphorisms. In this chapter they remain attached to acts: the clean-hands warning follows the assignment of work; the mystery/secret distinction follows the sudden stillness around the Beast; the ludic warning follows the closed Justice box; the Hall instruction follows dismissal.

His tonal turn at “No” after the Beast question must be performed without ornamental menace. The prior laughter stops, both older men fail to move, and his source is explicitly personal: “I am one of five.” That is enough.

### Va-Raedin

Va-Raedin's instrument is refusal. “I served,” “No,” “Absolutely not,” “He is nineteen,” “It was not for tonight,” and “You will still be corrected” keep him from sharing Ka-Syphiron's abundance. Because his speech is spare, the rare admissions carry force: “Yes” to the toast, “Resonance,” “You did well,” and the final statement that Sa-Kailan is no longer only his student. His mouth, shoulders, thumb on bread, closed eyes, delayed drink, and eventual bite do not illustrate his lines; they show what the lines cost him.

### Silence

The consequential silences already work and should stay:

- the phone ellipses make Sa-Kailan wait outside an English exchange;
- Sa-Kailan's failed answer to praise lets the smile speak;
- Ka-Syphiron's absence behind the curtain permits Va-Raedin's shoulders to lower;
- the pause after the Va-Sheva warning lets future cost enter the joke;
- neither older man moving after “the Beast” changes the room before doctrine does;
- Sa-Kailan's refusal to manufacture a worthy farewell protects the evening;
- the closing latch cuts off information exactly at the limit of his hearing;
- the cut before the Ascension makes the next morning an intentionally held performance, not missing exposition.

## 5. Bounded failed-beat interventions

These are the only interventions supported by the native pass. They are proposals for later production, not manuscript edits in this brainstorm.

### Intervention 1 — Carry the paired-food evidence through hands

**Failed element:** the test's objective and dialogue work, but the stamps/dates arrive as disembodied verification. Urs says “Eleventh of April on both” before the prose has shown who holds or turns either egg. Helga's delivery is audible but the transfer from tray to experiment is not.

**Bounded repair:** within the existing food-test unit, give one existing participant one action that places the paired items, and let Urs turn or compare the already existing stamps before saying the dates. Sa-Kailan's smell reaction can then follow the toast reaching him. Do not add a new explanation of the test, do not decide more about Helga than the scene currently does, and do not alter any preference or result.

**Preserve exactly in function:** the delayed purpose, Marmite disgust, equal teas, blue-stamped egg preference, Urs's verdict, and Paul's return to the record.

### Intervention 2 — Remove the unestablished seating assumption

**Failed element:** the room establishes one low stool occupied by Ka-Syphiron. Later all three “stopped sitting straight”; Ka-Syphiron is newly “on his feet”; Sa-Kailan “rose.” A stage production must invent two seats or silently relocate bodies.

**Bounded repair:** keep the named stool and the room unchanged. Revise only the three dispersed posture verbs so the feast can be performed by bodies around the table without asserting unintroduced furniture: let the men stop **holding themselves** straight; let Ka-Syphiron push away from or leave the table rather than newly acquire feet; let Sa-Kailan step back from the table rather than rise from an unnamed seat. This is one blocking correction across a continuous action unit, not a room redesign.

**Preserve exactly in function:** the progressive loosening of formality, Ka-Syphiron's departure through the curtain, and Sa-Kailan's final bow and exit.

### Intervention 3 — Reopen the prism to light

**Failed element:** Sa-Kailan closes his fingers around the prism. Soon afterward Ka-Syphiron turns his wrist until the lamp strikes the prism more narrowly. A closed fist with a thumb-length prism inside does not offer a reliable lit face.

**Bounded repair:** at the existing wrist turn, have Sa-Kailan's fingers open, or have Ka-Syphiron turn the hand and expose the prism. One physical clause is enough. Do not add optical explanation; the visible wound of colour and Va-Raedin's question already carry the lesson.

**Preserve exactly in function:** “For after,” the warning against believing a whole beam too quickly, Ka-Syphiron looking away, and the “memory of light” question.

### Intervention 4 — Give winning and ruin a wager carrier

**Failed element:** Ka-Syphiron deals cards and Sa-Kailan wins, is punished, and is “ruined,” but no stake, counter, mark, or transferred object has been established. Later “tokens” are hypothetical and “money you did not have” is comic summary. The body cannot play ruin if nothing leaves it.

**Bounded repair:** when the existing box opens, name a minimal carrier for the wager and move it at the early win and final ruin. The carrier may be something already on the table, a small set kept with the cards, or an explicitly spoken tally; production should choose one and use it consistently. Keep the game summary brief. The discard pile and shoe must remain the carriers for past and future.

**Preserve exactly in function:** early winning, immediate punishment, Va-Raedin lasting longer, both losing, the Mountain's tie rule, the question of eventual ruin, and the shift from game to warning when Ka-Syphiron closes the box.
