## Voice and speakability

Paul's voice is recognisable because he does not merely “sound scholarly”; he behaves like a custodian. He labels the reel, notices three distinct cup handles, speaks dates while lifting burned leaves, states what document integrity does and does not establish, and stops Sarah from opening the hard case before its custody is recorded. His strongest spoken line is the plain professional correction, “The paper does not.” It gives Sarah an immediate choice and changes her hand. His first-person sentence after the frame—training in dead things, no training in living people under pursuit—also earns the account because it states his competence and its boundary.

Sarah's speech works through decisions that do not wait for comfort. “Let them ring,” the crossed call signs, “Burn the rest correctly,” and her measurements to Va-Sheva all make another person act. Her occasional long explanation at the station is playable because the listeners need her inference before they can close the network. On the plane, “Take every comfort you can hold” is more composed than her usual clipped orders, but the act of sleeping immediately after it keeps it from becoming a free-floating aphorism: she is granting Paul one last usable luxury and ending the conversation.

Amastan's language is inseparable from his hands. He rejects ringing caps, erases a proposed road, reveals and hides a well, shortens Sarah's passage, and withholds his name from the final bend. His lines about sand and ownership are stylised, but they answer Kailan's claim that the folds are the Mountain's wall and are paid off when he makes Kailan repeat the warning to Shield. They should not be polished further.

Jean-Charles remains correctly unresolved because the prose declines to explain him. His helpful foot on the recorder, excluded route access, knowledge of post language, ignorance of the alarm word, captured case, and failure to ask what the annex says all produce pressure in opposite directions. No interior justification should be added. His dry “Still taking notes on me, professor?” and Paul's literal answer give both men something to do: he probes what is recorded; Paul refuses selective omission.

Va-Sheva's public voice is command under triage. She assigns wardens before speaking, defines exactly how far admission extends, kills the seam, and asks Sarah questions whose answers can be acted upon. Her private breath changes only once: the statements about containment move from judgement, to refusal of forgiveness, to the practical boundary “remain of use.” That change is earned by the step, the lamp, and her resumption of climbing. Preserve the outcome and restraint.

Kailan's strongest tactic is refusal. He refuses Paul's first demand, refuses to defend the hearing through someone else's angle, and at the Mountain refuses to ask Justice for belief: read the papers, listen to stone. His Farid questions are short because he is not inviting narrative; he is finding who can order, stop, or communicate. This makes him acoustically distinct from Paul's evidentiary clauses and Sarah's institutional imperatives.

## Bounded performance revisions for production consideration

These are coherent beat-level adjustments, not line polish and not changes to architecture.

### 1. Keep Paul's source visible in three transit/document assertions

Most of the narration obeys the source lock, but three statements need a more explicit carrier.

- At the start of the lawn account, “The enemy knew the Mountain existed” is retrospective knowledge presented as if Paul possessed it before the evidence scene. Attach it to what Paul had heard, what Sarah's orders made him infer, or what the later case established. The point can remain; the source must be audible.
- The strip being “swept and lit between one of Sarah's calls and the next” states an unobserved preparation interval. Paul can report the newly swept tarmac and jar lamps, then identify the timing as his inference from the calls.
- The concession number, permits, and customs entries being “genuine” needs an attributed test: Sarah identifies them, the documents contain independently checkable marks, or Paul says what feature made him judge them genuine. The following custody paragraph already models the correct language by separating what the papers establish from what they do not.

One further sentence is a direct safety-veto failure: “Va-Sheva memorised them and wiped them out with her sleeve.” Paul cannot see memorisation. Give it an observable test—she repeats the bearings or answers Sarah without looking—before she wipes the wall. This is the only clear moment in which the account enters another person's mind.

### 2. Reperform the camp dossier as a hand-to-hand action

The custody interruption is excellent, but the paragraphs after the case opens briefly flatten into consecutive institutional summary. Keep every necessary fact and the same documentary chain, while making the receiving bodies continuous: Paul aligns issue marks and passes the sheet; Sarah finds the French mark; Kailan holds the live-capture page; the radio interrupts; the annex changes hands; Jean-Charles remains with Farid rather than joining the page circle. The key pressure change is not “more exposition”: each page reduces a different person's room to deny what is happening.

Do not resolve Jean-Charles through this adjustment. His spatial separation and silence are the useful action. The folded corridor page remains evidence marked “serious, not proof,” never a verdict.

### 3. Break Va-Sheva's first charge around the search already in progress

Her long sentence beginning with Kailan's writ contains accusation, inventory, law, and threatened execution in one balanced delivery. It is speakable, but it risks sounding composed for the reader while Sarah's knee is bleeding, weapons are being surrendered, and devices are being wrapped. Reperform the whole reception as one unit: let search results or the binding of Sarah's knee interrupt the charge at its existing logical turns. Va-Sheva can name each danger when the corresponding object or body is placed before her—Kailan, two Outsiders, Sarah, recorder, unknown devices, seam—then reach the death threat only after she has physically contained them.

The facts, pregnancy, Shield authority, Justice access, recorder condition, admission limit, and killed seam must remain. The purpose is to make the accusation ride the room rather than hover above it.

### 4. Preserve the stair stillness and resist explanatory closure

The common-ward passage and blast need no added warning dialogue. Paul's archaeological comparison is his addressed testimony, not omniscient commentary, and the child's trapped loaf gives the ordinary life a body. Va-Sheva's hand on his sleeve is sufficient to stop disclosure. At the blast, her delayed answer to “What lies there?” is a productive silence: the shock has finally interrupted a commander whose speech has never failed. Do not explain the pause or Kailan's palm. The recorder lifted toward grinding and the lamp carried into the run are the correct final carriers.
