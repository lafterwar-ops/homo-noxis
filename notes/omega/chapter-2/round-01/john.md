### Governing diagnosis

Chapter 2 works. Its soul is not the curriculum alone but a contest over who gets to define the frame of evidence. Va-Raedin teaches Kailan to distinguish an offered choice from the room that produced it, a trace from the story laid over it, and illumination from the hand that placed the lamp. In the interview frame Paul is performing the same operation on Kailan's account, while Blackwood's house is performing it on both men. The title therefore pays three times: the practice-hall object, the institutional surveillance wall, and the interview's reflected methods.

The chapter's main defect is not missing invention. It is that the apprenticeship's middle uses the same dramatic cadence several times: a problem is posed, Kailan works through a physical apparatus, Va-Raedin corrects the first interpretation, and an aphoristic rule closes the unit. The water, obscured mirror, six-mirror chain, and later speaking-mirror lesson are intellectually distinct, but their delivery briefly makes them feel additive rather than escalating. The reader grasps the governing epistemic lesson before Va-Raedin states it at greatest length. Some image/interpretation/lesson triads then pay the same point twice.

That repetition slightly suppresses the emotional story already present. Kailan begins determined not to look foolish; lies about pain; gains competence; starts challenging the moral use of Light in the Faith rehearsal; is refused an answer; and finally falls in love with an apparatus that is also a system of anonymous surveillance. That progression is potent because the danger is his attraction, not an imposed punishment. It should be the chapter's rising dramatic line. At present, the sequence's conceptual clarity is sometimes stronger than its changes in relationship, complicity, and danger.

The Estate frame is structurally necessary. Urs's teasing, Sarah's entrance, Martinez's watchfulness, Paul's token comparison, the rule of compartmentalisation, and the midnight chess exchange all demonstrate that the Outsiders also place lamps, restrict keys, monitor rooms, infer from traces, and withhold the story they have built. The frame is not detachable banter. Its risk is diffusion: the stranded vessel, Isfahan contact, whisky, chess, and picture book arrive late enough that their exact relationship to the chapter's central pressure can feel cryptic on a first read. The correct response is not to delete their outcomes, but to make every retained beat do its parallel-work cleanly.
