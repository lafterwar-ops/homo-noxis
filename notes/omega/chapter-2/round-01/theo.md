### Unaided reconstruction — fixed before reopening

The chapter first leaves Paul and Urs teasing one another over a care package,
whisky, an unfinished chess game, and Urs's work, before Paul resumes Kailan's
account. The morning after Kailan is named, he reaches Light determined not to
be made a fool again. A room interruption explains how the Pendulum and Faith's
moving marks give time without sky.

At Light, Kailan's new name is checked and the hall makes seeing feel governed.
Va-Raedin gives him a three-door problem. Kailan distrusts the obvious bright
lure, chooses cleverly, and still fails; only from a worn floor-circle does he
see the unoffered hidden door. Kailan says that light deceives. Va-Raedin's
larger lesson, as remembered, is to ask who arranged the available choices and
placed the lamp.

In a dark room Kailan uses a shard and then water to send a point of light to a
wall. The trembling water preserves evidence of disturbance without identifying
the disturbance or the disturber. Further exercises blur together in memory:
Va-Raedin uses his body near a black panel to hide the condition that makes a
false appearance persuasive; later, Kailan orders reflections around a lamp,
finds a low seam, and earns a finely made pale token with a central pin-hole and
many even concentric rings.

Mrs Blackwood enters briefly, accompanied by the older Martinez, whom Kailan
later notices as her shadow. Paul returns to the token, leaves, and brings back
a red printed image that Kailan says matches it exactly. Paul will not explain
how he knew. Kailan says he learned the token's meaning later.

Kailan then watches Faith singers below while mist, a hidden lamp, holes, and a
felt vibration make a prayer look and feel answered. He asks whether valves
disprove God or serve Him; Va-Raedin declines Faith's quarrel. Near exhaustion,
Kailan gives the literal maintenance answer to “where light falls.” Va-Raedin
calls that the answer of a mirror: faithful, empty, never made wiser by what it
returns. He opposes mirror to eye and asks what an ancient system has removed
at every equal-light festival to keep the next one the same. Paul interrupts to
reckon twenty-three thousand festivals as eleven and a half thousand years;
Kailan recalls a clock that beat only twice yearly.

Va-Raedin takes him below the practice halls to the Mirror: hundreds of threads
enter through one wall and end as a star-field on another, with scored lines
making the field a map of the Mountain. A point dims when a body crosses its
beam. The system need not watch constantly if nobody can know when it is awake.
The wall first followed the Mountain; now the Mountain follows the wall. There
are 256 points, all to be memorised. Kailan sees the wall that can find any body
and considers it the most beautiful thing he has ever seen.

The session closes with plans for a tutor and physician and a statement that
the House keeps accounts compartmentalised. After midnight, Kailan cannot shut
out an afterimage from the tutor's lesson and notices that the Estate burns
lamps in empty rooms. He asks about the sea. Paul momentarily answers Urs in
English about the chess game and Urs's missing boat in a strait, then finds an
aerial picture book for Kailan.

Remembered wants: Kailan wants to avoid humiliation, master the tests, and know
what the teachings actually mean; Va-Raedin wants him to stop returning given
appearances and attend to sources, conditions, and arrangers; Paul wants a clean
account while recognising correspondences he withholds; Urs wants his game and
his teasing intimacy; the older Kailan wants the sea made imaginable.
