### Oral and microprosodic performance

- Urs's opening paragraph should run as occupation of Paul's air: the parcel items
  are taps, “third week running” is the actual demand, and the rook lands the
  challenge. Paul's reply is not the same rhythm; it cuts, redirects, and ends on
  the tanker where Urs is vulnerable. Their syntax is therefore exchangeable
  neither in vocabulary nor tactic.
- Young Kailan's cheapest defeat is “No / It should / It does a little.” The tiny
  concession establishes the breath of the whole lesson: a quick defence,
  silence, then narrower truth. His later long answers should still begin from
  that competitive breath rather than from neutral exposition.
- Va-Raedin earns his long formulations by first withholding speech. In the door
  and black-panel work, the silence is his active tactic. In the water lesson he
  watches the still-visible tremor while distinguishing disturbance, trace, and
  story. If those sentences are spoken away from the object they become lecture;
  with the object they remain pressure.
- “A Keeper of Light does. I am a Veil.” needs a rank-bearing pause and stress on
  the two titles. The second sentence corrects what Kailan thinks was hidden; it
  must not be smoothed into one explanatory breath.
- Paul's token sequence is already tuned to interruption: exact restatement,
  provenance refusal, an announced short absence, the too-long gap, Kailan's
  direct challenge, silence, then the repeated “All right.” Adding explanation
  would remove the playable shock.
- Sarah never needs vocal force. “Slowly and well will do” and “leave them be”
  are decisions wearing social ease. Martinez's silence is not dead because
  Kailan identifies what his eyes have been doing.
- The midnight passage performs three simultaneous claims on Paul—record,
  chess, and Kailan's question—by language switches. “Knight d three” must be a
  genuine move made while Paul is still listening to Kailan; otherwise the
  divided attention disappears.

### Blocking and object custody

The chapter is continuously placeable through the gate, candidate view, floor
circle, hidden rear panel, narrow work chamber, passages, overlook, lower stair,
and Mirror wall. The body carries the pedagogy: Va-Raedin's stillness makes
Kailan search harder; his body occludes a low mirror; Kailan crouches to see a

Most objects have explicit custody or a stable resting place. The package is
ordered down; the bowl remains on its work table; Kailan pockets the token; Paul
fetches and returns with the printed sheet; the chessboard waits for Paul; the
book is found under the Kolbert volume and handed to Kailan. The narrow mirror
strip is the exception: Va-Raedin takes it from the niche, manipulates the two
reflections, says “Come,” and descends while the text never releases it from his
hand. In a continuous performance the actor either carries it implausibly into
the Mirror chamber or invents a return. A bounded beat repair should have him
replace or set down the strip before the descent.

The first door exercise also needs one bounded spatial repair. The establishing
view gives an open dark door, a metal-closed door, and a half-open bright door;
Kailan then reasons from a left-hand light at the base and a right-hand reflected
light. The audience has not been given both pieces of visible evidence in a
stable left/middle/right arrangement. Revise the whole action unit only far
enough to make the initial view, Kailan's chosen façade, his failed push/pull,
and the worn-circle reveal describe one stage picture. Keep all three offered
doors, the rear panel, the circle, and the outcome.

The hand-mirror sentence has the other eye-line fault. Kailan first sees his own
face when Va-Raedin angles the strip; the following turn says Kailan's face
displaces Va-Raedin's, reversing which face was previously visible. Choose a
single observer and reflection sequence, then let the physical turn carry the
“mirror or eye” question. No new event or meaning is required.
