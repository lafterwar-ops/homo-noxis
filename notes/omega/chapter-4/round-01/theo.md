## Frozen unaided reconstruction

Written after the single continuous cold read and before reopening the chapter. This section is not to be revised after comparison with the source.

### What happened, in remembered order

The frame opens with the third interview. Kailan reports trying the pond, microscope, and *Cosmos*. The pond lets him test a mediated claim with his own hands; the heavens and historical reconstructions do not. Eratosthenes' public measurement and Kepler's beautiful error press him toward a problem the Mountain previously solved by assigning authority: outside it, how can he choose what or whom to believe? Paul answers with provisional trust earned by repeatable tests, exposed limits, and correction. Kailan remembers the phrase "what survives the test."

The account returns to the Mountain just after the Oasis. As a new Light candidate, Kailan is assigned one beam and ordered to learn its whole route. Oren and Sela, servants doing the work Kailan himself did until two days ago, carry his tools. He tests and retraces the beam through the Mountain.

Shield closes a service way. Through a partly opened door Kailan sees attendants move the sealed dish associated with Mara and her grey shawl. Sela notices his notice; he sends the servants onward along the beam.

The beam crosses Balance's water queues, kitchens, underground crops, and the salt-house, where samples of spring water are boiled and their residue weighed without the child recording it being given the meaning. Paul interrupts to make Kailan unpack machinery hidden inside quick narration.

Kailan briefly loses the beam among brackets, recovers it by reconstructing the clean reflections, and notices Sela's embarrassed attention. This recalls Sheva giving him blue glass and letting him believe it a jewel.

Sa-Tavan of Justice waits for Kailan and asks him to state the order of the Oasis incident. Kailan gives the official sequence: Sheva named a mirage, Shield found a turned marker, then Mara was found apart from it. He protects Mara by placing her outside the danger and protects himself by putting his own seeing under Shield's word. Sa-Tavan recognizes that this is "an order," not necessarily the order, keeps the record open, treats Mara under the gentle precedent of "strayed," and announces that he will compare Kailan's Oasis visit against all such candidate visits. Kailan says this is when he began counting the counters.

Children learn the Mountain's compressed history: the forest was broken and the surrounding land became desert in a day after a wrath. Paul objects that forests do not die in a day; Kailan withholds the Book of the Beast until its proper place.

At a Balance room also marked by Justice, the beam enters at eight marks and exits at four. Ka-Cheryn lets Kailan prove there is no other light source but will not let him enter. The room has kept half the light. Later the frame tells us that Light knowingly watched such rooms, sometimes closing them and sometimes leaving them ignorant so their activity could be counted.

Sela asks what a person learns by following one beam all day. Kailan feels the answer ready, but the robe speaks first: he shuts the knowledge inside Light and enjoys possessing what she wants for a moment. She withdraws into the formal "Candidate," and he recognizes that he has lost the person asking.

Passing a ceremony, he hears the injunction to keep the Mountain, Oasis, and Temple and to keep secrets divided. Only then does he realize that he has recited "Temple" all his life without once wondering where it is. In his quarters, the Light token appears displaced and enlarged in water, teaching him how light bends and recalling the misleading water level. He draws the beam's route from memory and names the dark Balance room's unaccounted share "contraband light."

In the frame, Paul's wish to see the Mountain finally breaks through his restraint. He admits the scholarship that bound his career to these people and the enormity of Kailan giving him eleven thousand living years. Kailan promises to try to bring him to the door, warning that entering has a price. Kailan also reveals that Va-Kailan is the expelled name; in Horizon he is Sa-Kylian, placed under instruction by Elise with Sarah's authority. Paul recognizes Mrs Blackwood as this hidden organization's Keeper and feels compartmentalization from below.

Kailan takes Paul outdoors. Standing under the open sky is real progress, but it is also a way to reach ground without walls or ears. Kailan turns Paul's rule of evidence into a test: Paul must look behind the interview room's glass. Kailan suspects a Mirror in the house and asks Paul not to reveal discovery, because an unwarned watched room gives a truer map. If anyone is behind the glass, Paul's loyalty goes to Kailan. Paul promises because he believes the condition impossible. Kailan ends by volunteering to return to swimming, go deeper, and work without armbands while Paul performs the test.

### Who wanted what

- Kailan wanted a method for choosing belief without an office choosing for him; inside the remembered Mountain day he wanted to account for the beam and quietly learn what the divided offices concealed; in the frame he wanted Paul to test the house and, if the suspicion survived, shift allegiance to him.
- Paul wanted Kailan's account in usable physical detail, then openly wanted access to the Mountain itself. By the lawn scene he also wanted the suspected watcher to be impossible.
- Sa-Tavan wanted an order the tablet could preserve while keeping room to test that order against precedent.
- Sela wanted the meaning behind the beam, and perhaps Kailan's recognition rather than the robe's answer.
- Ka-Cheryn wanted the beam measured while the room's purpose remained hers.

### Emotional telemetry from memory

The high point is the lawn bargain: Kailan converts an abstract philosophy of evidence into a personal test carrying Paul's loyalty. Paul's disclosure of how deeply he wants the Mountain is the warmest point and also makes the bargain dangerous. The low, intimate point is Kailan withholding the beam from Sela and immediately feeling the human contact close. The Balance room produces the strongest investigative unease; the children reciting a one-day destruction produces the widest historical dread.

I expect the watcher to connect to Sarah/Horizon, but the chapter does not prove whether Kailan or Sarah is the target.

What felt certain without proof: the glass test will vindicate Kailan; the apparently technical beam lesson is also training in institutional surveillance; Sarah knows much more than Paul; and the Mountain's division of knowledge is being reproduced in Blackwood House.
