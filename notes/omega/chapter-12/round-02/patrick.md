## Oral and acoustic pass

### Kailan

Kailan's opening breath is deliberately measured. The Candidates, soot, maintenance column, pen, basins, and mirror are not scenery: he is using Light procedure to prove how long he kept functioning while the scrap touched his body. Play the first pages as a man laying evidence before Sarah, not as retrospective lyrical narration. The isolated names on line 7 should receive four separate weights; do not turn them into a smooth list.

His syntax distinguishes two modes. While he thinks through institutions, he builds qualified clauses: what Light can test, what Justice would expose, what Shield can receive. Under Va-Sheva's questions he collapses to “No,” “Yes,” and exact inventory. That contraction is his pressure change. Preserve it. His longer legal challenge at lines 205–223 should accelerate because he is trying to make a door out of reasoning; Va-Sheva's replies should not accelerate with him.

At seizure, the missing spoken name must remain physical: lips shape “Va-Sheva,” cloth prevents sound, and she answers the shape. Later, when the gag is removed, Kailan's pause is a tactic—he refuses to give Shield immediate proof that returned speech can be managed—not generic shock.

### Va-Sheva

Her voice is already differentiated by tactics, not decorative mannerism. Questions are short because each answer authorises the next containment step. Office statements become compact parallel forms: accusation/containment, body/speech/proof/seal. Her rare personal language therefore lands: first “I believe you,” finally “Kailan” and “I am sorry.” Keep the institutional diction dry enough that those breaches in it can be heard.

The hand against her chest at lines 41 and 103 is one repeated tell with escalation: first it rests for a count, later it closes. It is not an illustrative gesture to cut. Her fingers on the door at line 277, the muscle at her mouth at 491, and the caught breath at 501 occur at different choices and should not be merged into a single general display of guilt.

### Public voices and silence

The household count needs overlapping human tempi without adding words: the clerk's flat call, the mother's too-fast explanation, the child's angry waking, the Faith attendants' walking repetition. That contrast makes the Lesser Seal a bodily system rather than exposition. The wardens' refusal to speak is itself their tactic. Do not give them commands during seizure; “No shout. No command.” is one of the scene's decisive acoustic facts.

The sound sequence should remain legible in performance: wall-slit whisper; case clicks; departing feet; buried bars; chain in stone; then suppressed footfall and finally the soundless chamber. The final body sounds are not ornament. They are the only spatial feedback Kailan can source.

### Present-room return

Kailan's line 507 should begin after an earned silence. He is not summarising the chapter; he is using his body-memory as a trap for Sarah: if his room explains her, then his choice and hers can be placed on the same moral scale. “Tell me I have it right” is the playable demand.

Sarah's reply at line 509 is long but speakable if treated as successive captures, not a single declamation. Breath after “Nobody had to.” Reset on “I did not break into your Mountain.” Let “He made me an offer” fall without emphasis, then use the printed ellipsis as the hard turn into her soundless room. The chain “I walked / I drew / I turned / I have been / I called” must gather responsibility. Withhold the man's name until Kailan is reduced to the two-word demand.

## Two bounded rewrite units

### 1. Give Va-Sheva's widened custody question a carrier

The intended shift—from the ribbon chamber to anything beyond the first seal—needs to be playable in her body and explicit in the question. Replace the complete exchange from her look at his fingers through the scrap answer with:

> Her eyes moved to the fingers of my right hand.\par  
>  
> ``Did you remove any part of it?''\par  
>  
> ``No.''\par  
>  
> ``Anything else from that chamber?''\par  
>  
> ``No.''\par  
>  
> Her eyes left my fingers and returned to the seam of my robe.\par  
>

This keeps her knowledge observable: she saw the seam on entry and looks back to it. It does not state that she knows what is there or why.

### 2. Introduce the fourth carrying body when Kailan can perceive it

At lines 387–389, replace the lift and passage entry as one action unit:

> She stepped back. The three wardens lifted me. My feet brushed the floor while they carried my weight, my arms bound close beneath a dark outer cloth.\par

> They took me through the wall niche. A fourth warden waited on the first step and took my left side. The passage behind him was narrow and steep. Other Shield wardens stood in darkness at intervals and turned aside as we reached them. Small covered lights glowed at floor level. Their light reached each foot placement and no higher.\par

This makes the later four-person load on the floating inner floor continuously stageable. It does not add a decision, alter resistance, or change the wardens' silence.
