### What happened, in remembered order

Kailan carried the stolen scrap against his body for two days while continuing exacting Light work. He considered destroying it, then considered taking it to Ka-Raedin or Justice, but concluded that either route would expose the sealed route or let Shield recover the proof before it could be tested. Va-Sheva was both the office-safe recipient and a dangerous choice because Kailan had found what he believed was her ribbon caught in old blood in the hidden chamber.

He used a dry technical inquiry to arrange a meeting with Va-Sheva in a narrow observation room between Light and Shield. He confessed the route step by step. She controlled the pace, repeatedly narrowed his answers, and became especially alarmed by the soundless chamber, what he touched there, and the fact that he removed the scrap. The scrap named Sarah Blackwood, Amastan, Ka-Xhian, and a Threshold Exception. Va-Sheva accepted it into a dark case, then gave a covert hand signal to wardens visible through reflection. They ran in different directions.

Kailan challenged her about custody, lawful exceptions, Justice, and whether he himself was accused. She said Shield contained the breach and told him to remain in the room. He asked whether coming to her had been right. Her answer was that he had stopped carrying the proof.

After she left, Kailan tested the silent latch. The north route toward Light was open and apparently unguarded. He chose to close the door and stay. Almost immediately the Mountain began changing state: buried bars fell, air pressure changed, Shield appeared at both ends, and Balance, Justice, Faith, and Light moved through the residential corridor counting bodies, checking names, water, and tools, sealing household doors, and waking a child who failed to answer. Kailan recognized the Lesser Seal.

Back in the room, the lamp dimmed and a wall niche opened as wardens entered from the door, low and high, with another dropping behind him. They gagged and restrained him quietly, searched the empty robe seam, and Va-Sheva entered last. She told him that because he had waited, he was alive.

They carried him through concealed Shield routes, past a Justice witness, and down to the soundless chamber Kailan had previously entered. The blood-darkened fixed seat and Va-Sheva's ribbon were still there. He was placed in the seat but the rings were left open. Va-Sheva formally described Lesser Seal containment, witnessed recovery of proof and holding of speech, and the delayed notification of Light. Kailan learned that he would face a trial for breach, bearing forbidden proof, and revelation, but that speaking the facts in his defence would itself repeat the breach. Va-Sheva called the governing choice “containment before understanding,” apologized using his bare name, and left him alone with only the sounds of his own body.

In the terminal room, the teller uses the remembered soundless room to imagine Sarah was once dragged into an equivalent hidden interrogation and thereby learned to hate the Mountain. Sarah corrects him sharply: she entered willingly, was invited, was asked no questions, and accepted an offer that became a self-locked prison lasting most of her life. The man who opened it had a name. At the teller's demand, she says: Amastan.

### Who wanted what

- Kailan wanted the physical proof to survive private doubt and become testable by another hand. Once he surrendered it, he wanted lawful recognition, access to Light or Justice, and an answer that would distinguish custody from institutional self-protection. At the unguarded door he also wanted to preserve the meaning of his chosen trust.
- Va-Sheva wanted the proof, route, witness, and potential spread contained before anyone enlarged the breach. She also appeared to want Kailan alive and his family untouched, although memory cannot prove whether that was care, duty, or both.
- The wardens and counting offices wanted every body and movable danger located during the Lesser Seal.
- The terminal-room teller wanted Sarah's past to mirror and validate his own experience. Sarah wanted the exact difference preserved: coercion was not the origin of her long imprisonment; consent and an offer were.
- The reader wanted to know what Va-Sheva's signal started, whether the open north route was a test or mercy, what the Lesser Seal would do to Kailan, whether Va-Sheva had betrayed or protected him, and why Amastan recruited Sarah.

### Emotional high and low points

The first major rise came when Va-Sheva signalled the hidden wardens immediately after taking the scrap: custody and betrayal became indistinguishable. Pressure peaked across the apparently voluntary choice to stay, the deep bars dropping, and the clinically quiet seizure. The soundless chamber produced the most intimate dread because the punishment was not theatrical violence but the removal of all outside reference. The terminal-room correction then displaced the entire analogy and delivered the sharpest revelation with a single name.

The low-energy stretch was the early account of two days of ordinary Light work and procedural alternatives. Its drag felt purposeful—proof occupying the body while routine continues—but most particular tasks did not survive. A second relative lull came during some of the legal exchange after the case changed hands; the governing paradox survived more strongly than each individual challenge.

#### Vanished despite apparent importance

#### Falsely but plausibly inferred

- I inferred that Va-Sheva's repeated hand signal initiated the whole Lesser Seal. The text proves that wardens run after it and that she knew what would happen; it does not independently prove the entire Mountain response had no other trigger. This is a useful bounded inference and should remain unconfirmed.

- I felt certain that Va-Sheva's pain meant personal guilt or care. The actual sources are her pallor, mouth, tightened fingers, caught breath, bare name, apology, and conduct. The motive remains unproved and should remain so.

- From the moment the wardens run, it is likely that Kailan will be contained. - “The Mountain was changing state” and “The Lesser Seal” arrive after metal, air, count, and seals have demonstrated the change. They function as Kailan's recognition, not a substitute explanation, and should stay where they are.
- “That is why you are alive” confirms that waiting altered the manner and risk of capture after the reader has already seen his choice. It turns an abstract choice into a contested mercy without resolving Va-Sheva's ethics.
- Kailan's terminal-room explanation follows the stronger physical passage about bodily sound.
