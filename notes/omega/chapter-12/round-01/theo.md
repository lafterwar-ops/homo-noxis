## 1. Phase 2 — Unaided reconstruction (written before reopening; preserved with its errors)

**What happened, in remembered order.** Kailan carried Sarah Blackwood's record against his ribs for two days. He supervised Candidates through an aperture exercise (measurements an eyelash apart; a grain of soot under a frame edge), did Light maintenance, recopied a leaning column while Ka-Raedin watched. At night he laid the robe across his knees and considered feeding paper and cloth to the lamp — the names would remain in him with no mark for another hand to test — and wrapped the scrap and put it back. He had taken it so the three names and the exception could not be reduced to fever, pride or error. He weighed three doors: Light (Ka-Raedin could examine hand, ink, age — but could not close an outer threshold); Justice (the sealed route would enter the record before Shield recovered it); Va-Sheva (Shield, without crossing offices — and in the Tomb, under the three Shield seals, he had found a dark ribbon caught in old blood whose uneven edge he knew from beside her face; it might have accused her, might have been left by someone she failed to save; he had put it back without knowing). He slept in the robe, hand over the seam. On the third afternoon he sent her a route inquiry about a repeated exterior reflection — courtesy permitted between Veils, every word true. She named a narrow observation room between Light and Shield: a wall slit facing a sealed passage without showing it, a door with no visible bar, a bench under a lamp too high for reading and too low to leave a standing face in shadow. He arrived first.

She entered without an escort in sight, duty leather, hair bound high, eyes counting wall slit, lamp, floor, hands, door — stopping a moment at his robe seam. Her right hand settled flat against her chest, one count, then fell. Interrogation: outer marker no; Shield seal yes — one at the desert entrance, three more below, unopened. The mineral face, the plate answering the sun, the reflections moving the lock, the metal tongue that struck; its twin later, inside. "What lay beyond the first seal?" — "A chamber." — "Do not name it." — "I called it the Tomb." — "That name is yours. Keep it so." The chamber's contents: outside objects ordered by age and material, images of Outsider gods, machines, maps, a wheeled vehicle divided into parts and carried in, Mountain writing beside recent names. Her breathing shortened; "Stop." — "Do you order me not to complete the account?" — "I order you to answer only what I ask." Through the stone: to a door under three Shield seals, untouched; a side opening to a chamber made against sound. The hand at her chest closed. He entered it; touched a woven ribbon caught in blood at the foot of the fixed seat, lifted the loose end, the stain held it, put it down. Removed nothing of it. "What did you remove?" — "A scrap from a loose map bundle." The colour left her face; one look toward the door. Absence witnessed by stain, cord, weight or outline — no; attached — no; copied — no; seen by another — no. "What did you read?" — Sarah Blackwood. Amastan. Ka-Xhian. Threshold Exception.

Palm up: "Where is it?" — "On my body." — "Give it to Shield." The handover: folded cloth, scrap held by one corner, Mountain hand along the torn fold. "You came to the right door. You came holding the fire." — "Memory would not have made you believe me." — "I believe you before I touch it." Dark case, lifted by two bare edges, fingers never crossing the writing, two soft clicks. Then her hand turned beside her thigh — two fingers beneath the thumb, wrist inward, one cut toward the floor, palm opened down. A dim reflection gave him two wardens outside; one started for the door, the other caught his arm; she repeated the signal; they ran, footfalls dividing at the first junction and ending. The case hung within his reach; her hands stayed empty; she watched his shoulders; he kept them still. "What did you order?" — "A threshold drill." — "Those men did not expect it." — "They do now." Second strap. The proof-travels speech: hidden passage, outer route, sleeping chamber, Light's halls, this corridor; a child looking for thread could have opened the cloth. "I brought it to the office that could stop it." — "After carrying it away from the seal that stopped it." The lawfulness argument: if Ka-Xhian held the authority, the exception has a boundary; if he crossed it, Justice must know. "Justice will know what Justice is permitted to judge." — "That answer protects the office that wrote the permission." — "It protects the route while the office is judged." Custody: Shield's now; Justice's when the route is held. "And mine?" — "You carried sight away. Seeing was the first breach. Bearing it made the next." Requests refused: not Ka-Raedin; "Take me to Ka-Dhavar" — "I will report to my Keeper." Wait here, speak to no one, touch no public record. "Am I accused?" — "Justice accuses. Shield contains." — "Me?" — "The breach." — "You came alone to hear me." — "No. I came without bringing another witness into the room." — "Did I do wrong by coming to you?" — "No." — "Then what did I do right?" — "You stopped carrying it."

She left; the corridor was empty; "Until you are called." He weighed the northern turn six paces away, the Light stair without a Shield door, Ka-Raedin receiving a Veil under his own name, Justice having to witness a seizure in his chamber — but the case now carried the four entries, and going north would carry Sarah's name in every demand for protection. "I will wait." Her fingers tightened once on the door edge. "Good." — "Will I see you when Shield returns?" — "Yes." The lock made no sound. He tested the latch once: the door opened; the turn was still empty; the high lamp showed the white Light knot at his wrist; his own office would see it and answer; no Shield body between. He closed it and stayed. Then metal settling into place — a bar dropped where no public door stood; a second sound from another direction; pressure along the passage; the lamp flame bending toward the door and straightening. The Mountain was changing state. In the corridor: two wardens at the ends, not approaching; a Balance clerk with a slate and a Justice witness with a narrow black tablet at a family chamber three doors down. "Bodies." — "Six." — "Names." Voices answered; the clerk marked; a warden sealed the exterior latch — any opening would tear it; water counted, tools (a needle, a blade for cloth, two cooking hooks) shown, witnessed, sealed. At the next door the fifth name received no answer; the mother explained too quickly (the night water turn); the Justice witness cut the seal she had just marked; Shield opened the door; a Faith attendant went in; a pause, a child's angry cry, the attendant naming breath, heat and waking; the boy answered his own name; only then the fifth mark; resealed. Cold spread from his spine. The Lesser Seal. Faith attendants walking the corridor: "Remain in place until release. Let no fear cross the threshold before the body does." A Light attendant dimmed three lamps; each froze into a low stable flame. "What is this?" — the warden gave him nothing. A second deep sound, closer; a chain-rattle swallowed in stone, rising from below his feet.

He stepped back inside and closed the door before anyone ordered it. The room as before: bench, water jar, lamp, sealed niche. The flame too steady; the slit no longer whispering; if he shouted, the sound might fail. The first buried bar had fallen before the clerk entered the corridor — the Mountain locks before it counts. Time became difficult; one silence or three. He had told no one where Va-Sheva had received him. The lamp dimmed first; he rose; the door stayed shut; the wall niche opened; at the same moment the door came inward — two wardens low and high, a third dropping from a ceiling seam behind his right shoulder. He moved once, to put a body between himself and the lamp; the line closed; his raised elbow was caught; cloth, pressure, a thumb beneath the jaw; head fixed before sound became speech; knee touched from behind; the floor came up quietly; a hand caught his shoulder before stone. No shout. No command. He could breathe through the gag. They covered the upper lamp; low amber; one opened the robe seam and searched where the scrap had been — empty; wrists, belt, collar, sandal folds. Va-Sheva entered last, pale, mouth held still, a black line of seal-dye across the back of one hand. He shaped her name against the cloth; no word left it. "You asked Shield to handle it," she said. He drove once against the hands; a point beneath his shoulder; white pain down the arm; his fingers opened; the pressure left at once. "I told you to wait." — "You did. That is why you are alive."

They carried him through the niche: narrow, steep, wardens in darkness turning aside, covered lights at floor level reaching each foot placement and no higher. Within twenty paces he knew he had never been in this part of the Mountain. Through stone, a family answering the count; a child asking whether the drill would end before morning bread; a woman answering yes, quickly. His desert training read hidden angles; here each turn doubled under the last. At a junction, a Justice witness — someone other than Sa-Tavan — an older woman, white hair braided tight against the skull, black tablet. "Body held." — "Speech held." — "Proof recovered?" — "Recovered before seal." — "Seal witnessed." Downward. He knew the last turn before they reached it. The wedges came into the covered light first: ranks of blunt points faced with pressed wool and hair from walls and ceiling; an open lattice over points rising from below; the stone island with the fixed seat, wrist and ankle rings, the gutter, the black stain washing had left in its seams. Va-Sheva's ribbon remained at the foot of the seat; the pale fracture he had made in the dried blood showed when the floor light crossed it. Carried over the lattice, footfalls ended beneath them; nothing returned from the wedges to say where wall or ceiling stood; the inner frame within its second frame, felt and an empty hand's breadth; bound among four wardens he felt the inner floor move a fraction under their joined weight. Va-Sheva crossed the island without looking down.

The seat: rings left open, arm bindings removed, gag kept. "Listen. You are under Shield containment by Lesser Seal. Justice has witnessed recovery of proof and holding of speech. Light will be informed through proper sequence. You will not be questioned until the Seal completes." — "Your family is not informed." He closed his eyes once despite himself. "No public scandal. No corridor rumour. No devotional panic. No one wakes to your name." The gag removed only on her nod; speech a dangerous tool set back in his hand while they watched. "You knew what would happen when you told me to wait." — "Yes." — "You used my agreement to keep me still." — "Yes." — "And if I had not agreed?" — "The same Seal. More force. More risk. Fewer mercies." — "Mercies." — "Your arms are free. Your family sleeps. You are breathing." — "Shield counts those?" — "Shield counts every body it holds." What happens now — a trial; before those permitted; the charges, given at last: Breach of Seal; Bearing Forbidden Proof; Unauthorised Revelation in potential and act. "If I defend myself by speaking what I found?" — "Then you repeat the breach." — "And if I do not?" — "Then you stand silent before a danger you cannot name." At the invisible seam: "Did you choose death?" The muscle beside her mouth tightened. "No." — "Then what did you choose?" — "Containment before understanding." Her breath caught once. "Kailan. I am sorry." The door sealed without visible line. Then the silence the room was built for: no corridor, no water, no far prayer, no vent breath; his own body rising into the empty place — the knock of his heart, too loud; a thin sea-sound beneath it that was his own blood; the dry creak of a joint; the floor letting go of its certainty; a hand to the felt to keep his feet. However long the Seal took — the knock, and the sea, and the creak, and no other company at all.

Room block: Kailan, to Sarah — a room built to give a man back nothing but the sound of himself; Faith made the quiet and Shield made the seal; you went into it too, Sarah — not that room, but the deep of it, past the wall — your face in its drawer and your name in its ledger; a room the sound could not leave, one of Shield's own across the table, the long questions; that is where you learned to hate us cleanly enough to spend a life on it. Tell me I have it right. — You have it wrong, and mark exactly how wrong: nobody dragged me anywhere; I did not break into your Mountain, boy; I was invited through the front of it, with a lamp held low for my feet, and the man on the other side asked no question at all. He made me an offer. I walked into my soundless room on my own two feet, drew the door shut, turned the key from the inside, have been its one prisoner the better part of sixty years, and called it my life's work. He had a face, the man who held it open for me. He had a name. — Say it. — Amastan.
