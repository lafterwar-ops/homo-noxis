## Phase 1 — Room, cast, address, and perception

### Prose mode A: the first interview-room exchange

The speaker who begins is Paul. He is the host, demonstrator, and operator of the optical apparatus. He addresses Kailan, whom he places facing the pale screen. Paul can see the apparatus while the lamp is open, knows it by touch when the room is dark, and narrates each manipulation so Kailan can follow it. His playable objects are the doubled shutters, the house lamp, the card and narrow slot, the prism, the clean flame, the dishes of salts, and the screen.

Kailan is the observer. He sits or stands where the screen faces him; the text requires only that he remain close enough to receive the matches later. In darkness he reports absence, compares it with the Mountain's retained thread of passage light, and then reports only what arrives on the screen. His address is always Paul: the direct name in `What am I looking at, Paul?` recovers the relation if the opening alternation ever becomes uncertain.

The blocking chain is executable until the last reveal. Paul opens the lamp, narrows its light through the card, sends it through the prism, extinguishes it, lights the clean flame, adds the first salt, cleans or replaces the flame condition, and adds the second salt. Kailan first watches, then asks, then refuses Paul's offer to restore ordinary room light. Kailan takes the matches from the sill, creates the single flame, asks Paul to construct the cathedral in imagination, and begins testimony. The matches change custody from sill/Paul's room to Kailan. The small flame becomes the physical anchor that persists beneath the remembered Hall.

Perception is clean: Paul knows the apparatus through preparation and manipulation; Kailan knows only the darkness, projected colours, flame lines, and Paul's explanation. No claim in this mode needs an external observer.

### Prose mode B: Kailan's continuous testimony in the Hall

The teller remains Kailan and the listener remains Paul even when the typography leaves the `room` environment. Every assertion is therefore either Kailan's remembered sight, sound, touch, bodily impulse, count, or a plainly marked conclusion from those perceptions. This is spoken testimony, not an impersonal narrator taking over.

Kailan's fixed station is the northern tier of Light. At first he stands one step below Raedin's empty place, with the folded white cloth bound at his wrist. After investiture he takes that one step and occupies Raedin's former position. A stone rail is directly before him; it later stops his attempted movement toward Syphiron. From the tier he can see the forbidden circle, the god side beneath the gravure, the beast side facing the deep, the Pendulum and its wire, the descending dome, Sela below among the attendants, the northern descent, the five office arches, and the attendants at the circle once full illumination returns. He can hear the scripture leader somewhere below the gravure, the tiers, the dome locks and chains, Syphiron and Raedin, the caught breath above, the crowd, the child, the murmured scripture, and the five strokes through the stone. He can feel the cold floor at his knees, the rail, the cloth, the Pendulum through floor/teeth/chest, his foot moving toward the stair, his own blood in his ears, and the later answer through his feet.

Ka-Syphiron enters carrying the last strong lamp. He calls Kailan down, invests him, returns him to the tier, then takes the far edge of the forbidden circle on the god's side. His hands, lamp, outer robe, underrobe, blood, voice, collapse, and body remain continuously traceable.

Va-Raedin begins absent from the tier because he is already the candidate for the inner rite. He kneels at the near edge on the beast's side, facing the deep. He goes under the dome with Syphiron, then emerges on the god's side carrying the small flame in one hand and the ceremonial knife in the other, wearing Syphiron's larger robe. His position, objects, and changed office are all revealed to Kailan at the same time.

Sela begins below Kailan with the lamp attendants, holding an oil pitcher. She looks up during his investiture, then lowers her eyes. After the rite she joins the four attendants at Syphiron's shroud, catches his slipping hand, places it on his chest, and folds the cloth over it. Her action has consequence: it keeps the body composed and makes the carrying possible.

The gathered people occupy every visible tier and upper passage before Shield closes the last arch. Their collective actions are breathing, reciting, holding silence, answering the proclamation, shielding their eyes, crying, laughing, weeping, murmuring scripture, and soothing a child. The anonymous scripture voice is heard from below the gravure; the tiers answer it. Kailan does not pretend to see the speaker.

## Phase 3 — Full spoken run

### First room: every exchange

1. Paul's dated opening is long but playable because it is a host's deliberate preparation. The semicolon sequence lets him point to each object. `Bear with an old man playing at priest` is his bid for Kailan's indulgence, not commentary for the reader. He should act the inventory rather than declaim it.
2. Kailan's `Nothing` lands as an answer to the requested look. The Mountain comparison lengthens only after the blunt result and belongs to his habit of measuring Paul's world against confinement. `I see nothing at all` returns him to the present apparatus.
3. Paul's `Let me open the lamp again` restores action. The short clauses follow hand, slot, glass, screen. `There` is a showman's cue and gives Kailan a precise moment to answer.
4. Kailan's colour list should be spoken as discovery across the wall, not from memorized taxonomy. The repeated completeness—no seam, none left out, the lot—shows him checking the phenomenon before he trusts it; it is earned observation, not an explanatory duplicate.
5. Paul makes Kailan retain the band, then removes it. The commands, extinguishing, new flame, salt, and final `Watch` give the speech a changing physical task throughout.
6. Kailan's report narrows with the image: whole stripe absent, one bar present, both sides empty. The hard stresses belong on `one`, `yellow`, and `nothing`; the line's precision is his resistance to wonder.
7. Paul's clean-flame reset and second salt are terse because proof now matters more than setup. `Watch where the yellow stood` gives Kailan something answerable.
8. Kailan first reports displacement, then generalizes only as far as repeated kitchen experience permits, then asks Paul. `What am I looking at, Paul?` prevents the speech becoming a lecture to the reader.
9. Paul's atomic answer is the densest breath in the room, but its tactic remains concrete: allowed amounts become steps, heat throws upward, falling releases a fleck, fall distance fixes colour, different atoms possess different steps. A performer should take the full stop after `allowed amounts` and use the flame/screen at `this heat, through this glass`; no extra explanatory sentence is needed.
10. Kailan's `memory of light` is a naming act offered back to Paul. The italicized phrase should not be inflated; its force comes from the pause after Paul's density.
11. Paul confirms Syphiron's answer and tries to return ordinary light. `Small true thing` versus `great one` gives Kailan a handle to seize.
12. Kailan's `No` interrupts the restoration. The commands become increasingly exact—dark, matches, flame, cathedral, crowd, breath, stillness—until `Now I will tell you` locks Paul into listening. This is recognizably Kailan's breath: imperative, spatial, intolerant of premature comfort.

### Hall testimony: continuous speech and ritual utterance

The opening crowd paragraph is performed as Kailan locating pressure before locating himself: packed tiers, bodies brought or pressed in, then his wrist, the empty place, the forbidden step, Sela and her pitcher, and finally the Pendulum. The sentence about the disk carries a long crossing and return; its breath should follow the described sweep. Nothing is seen outside Kailan's available tier view.

The limited-light paragraph is an active search. Kailan traces lit edges back to hidden plates, loses and regains faces as bodies lean, and reads the gravure only in portions. The short final naming of the eye is a visual stop, not omniscient declaration.

Syphiron's entrance sentence puts the strong lamp before the man, then makes the lesser flames subordinate in Kailan's perception. The investiture questions are fully answerable:

- `What is the eye for?` seeks a declaration of active inquiry; Kailan answers `To find what hides.`
- `What is the hand for?` reverses the impulse to seize; Kailan answers `To let it lie.`
- `What is the tongue for?` makes concealment a performed duty; Kailan answers `To keep the dark.`

Syphiron's hands lift before he changes Kailan's name, and his hand on the shoulder physically turns the new Veil back toward the tier. Sela's half-smile is small enough to be private; her lowered eyes refuse to prolong it. The disk then resumes the space between them. Preserve all of this exactly.

The opposed-station paragraph should slow. `God's side` and `beast's side` are Kailan's learned spatial names; the bodies make them legible. The repeated `passed close ... and passed on` keeps the disk active rather than decorative.

The first scripture block begins after the Hall itself starts losing light. The anonymous lead voice remains somewhere below the gravure; the gathered tiers enter beneath it. Each block has one breath-task:

- The first recalls spoiled places, collective loss, and God's hearing. It ends by making the hidden crowd audible to itself.
- Between first and second blocks, Kailan attempts a lamp-by-lamp count. Multiple shadows defeat him. His wrist cloth becomes the remaining personal gauge of light.
- The second gives the existing darkness, stone, and deep water as protections, then ends by removing the eye's access to the people.
- Between second and third, the gravure disappears body by body. The five carved children remain one breath longer, then are lost.
- The third assigns five distinct keeping duties and prohibits wandering proof. Its repeated `Keep` is a communal work rhythm; the performers should not rush the longer sentence that distributes offices.
- The eye remains alone after that block, giving the crowd one hard point to hold.
- The fourth makes a conditional promise of restored water and trees. Its cadence must stay collective rather than become Kailan's private hope.
- The slit closes and removes the eye before the fifth block.
- The fifth announces opening, chaining, emergence, and safety beneath Heaven. `Very good` closes the learned formula. The last words are allowed to die without Kailan adding an interpretation.

The single-flame paragraph is one sustained held breath. The clauses accumulate Hall, crowd, blackness, and stillness against the small flame. Repeated `held` is physical, not ornamental: the crowd holds itself and the flame holds darkness. Shadow movement gives Kailan visible evidence of the two men; Pendulum vibration supplies what the eye loses.

The dome's arrival begins in bone before ear, then becomes locks, chain, weight, stone, eye, and narrowing swing. The repeated distances—out, back, less far—should be timed to an actual slowing arc. Kailan is not calculating machinery; he is reporting what he sees and feels.

Inside the dark, Kailan's performance is tactile: eyes useless, wrist to rail, cloth under fingers, sound through stone, another shorter sound, somebody else's caught breath, absence of the Pendulum pulse, blood in his ears. Raedin lifts the flame before speaking. His two clauses are official and closed. The Hall's exact repetition is the only possible answer. The ignition then acts on Kailan before he can describe it: hands rise to his face; only afterward do mirrors, gold, gravure, crowd, Raedin, disk, and blood become visible.
