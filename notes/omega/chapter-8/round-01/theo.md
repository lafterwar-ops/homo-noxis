## Unaided reconstruction (fixed before reopening)

### What happened, in remembered order

The room is deliberately made completely dark. Paul first sends ordinary lamplight through a slit and prism, producing the whole continuous spectrum, then feeds salts to a nearly colourless flame and shows Kailan isolated bright lines instead of the band. He explains those lines as the allowed steps and falls inside matter. Kailan calls this the “memory of light”; Paul identifies the step and fall as Syphiron’s answer. Kailan refuses the room’s light back, asks Paul to imagine a black cathedral packed with five thousand people around one flame, and begins the account of how Light crowns its Keeper.

Before First hour the Hall is packed. Kailan waits in Light’s tier with an unlawful white cloth at his wrist; Raedin’s former place waits above him, Sela stands below among the lamp attendants, and the Pendulum crosses the forbidden circle. Carefully hidden and reflected light reveals parts of the Genesis gravure and its eye of Heaven. Syphiron enters with the strongest lamp, calls Kailan down, and makes him Va-Kailan, Veil of Light, through the questions about eye, hand, and tongue: find what hides, let it lie, keep the dark. The cloth becomes lawful; Kailan steps into Raedin’s old place.

Syphiron and Raedin take opposite sides of the forbidden circle, the old Keeper facing light on the god’s side and the kneeling successor facing the deep on the beast’s side. The Hall’s lights are extinguished by degrees while scripture recalls the hidden people, the promise of a restored world, the division and confinement of secrets, and the five Keepers. The eye of Heaven disappears last. One flame remains in Syphiron’s hand amid five thousand silent people. The Pendulum can be felt through floor, teeth, and chest.

A black stone dome descends from the vault along the Pendulum wire. As it lowers, the Pendulum’s crossing contracts. Syphiron asks Raedin what the Keeper carries, holds, and keeps; Raedin answers the light, the chain, and what must remain dark. Syphiron opens his robe. The dome reaches the floor, encloses both men and the flame, and stops the Pendulum. In total darkness Kailan hears movement and two sounds but can see nothing.

The dome rises and the Pendulum resumes. Syphiron emerges bleeding and collapses after naming Raedin the new Keeper. Raedin stands on Syphiron’s former side wearing the larger man’s robe, carrying the flame and a bloody ceremonial knife. Syphiron is dead. Raedin declares Light reborn and the chain held; the Hall repeats him. Then every light ignites at once. The new Keeper stands in gold and blood beneath the bright Pendulum, with Syphiron’s blood black at his feet.

The interview room briefly returns: the full blaze has left the listener dazzled, and Kailan says he wanted to give Paul the great light whole and in the eye as Paul gave him the small one. Back in Kailan’s testimony, attendants wrap Syphiron. Sela catches his fallen hand, places it on his chest, and folds the cloth over it. The Keepers withdraw through five doors, leaving the Hall without a Keeper; attendants wash the forbidden circle. Five hard strokes and a deep answer pass through the stone, the doors reopen, and the Keepers return. Kailan stands there as a Veil only hours old, with the Pendulum moving and water black in the blood-stained seams.

### Who wanted what, as memory retained it

- Kailan wanted Paul to receive the ceremony sensorially rather than as an explanation: first the single flame and black mass, then the catastrophic return of light. Within the remembered ceremony, young Kailan wanted—or at least awaited—the legitimacy of Raedin’s former place, while also being pulled physically toward the fallen Syphiron and restraining himself.
- Paul wanted Kailan to see the small, true physical phenomenon before telling the great human one; after the great light, his immediate need was simply recovery from its force.
- Syphiron appeared to want the chain of office to continue through an enacted, costly transmission whose terms Raedin could answer.
- Raedin’s personal want did not remain distinct from the office’s demand. Memory retains his obedience, his whitened face, and his assumption of the lamp, robe, knife, and words—not an independently stated desire.
- Sela’s retained action wanted dignity for Syphiron’s body, but the text had not yet reopened to establish more than the care made visible by her hands.
- The gathered people wanted the rite completed and the assurance answered collectively: Light reborn; the chain holding.

### What remains in the senses

The isolated yellow line hanging in black where a whole rainbow had been; the imagined cathedral and one flame; the unlawful then lawful white cloth; faces appearing and vanishing in crossed beams; the hard eye of Heaven; the Pendulum felt in feet, teeth, and chest; a dome lowering along its wire and gathering the swing into stillness; two sounds in sightless dark; blood between the same dry hands that had rested on Kailan’s head; the oversized gold robe, knife, flame, and black blood; the Hall taking fire at once; Syphiron’s loose hand caught by Sela; dark water held in the floor seams.

Sentence fragments that remain rather than exact prose: the memory of light; the step and the fall; find what hides / let it lie / keep the dark; the light / the chain / what must remain dark; Light is reborn, the chain holds; whole, and in the eye.

### Emotional high and low

The high point is the compressed interval from the dome’s descent through the blind sounds to its reopening: dread becomes physical revelation, then the entire Hall’s violent light fuses awe, horror, and legitimacy. The later, quieter low is the Keeperless wait while the blood is washed and a child cries; the ceremony’s radiance has passed, but its cost remains in work, water, and delay.

### What seemed likely to happen next

The next pressure seemed likely to be an account of what the new Keeper and the other offices did behind their five doors, or of how Kailan learned what “the chain” actually required after becoming Veil. A second expectation was that the chapter or following testimony would test whether the rite’s shared declaration can contain the fact of a predecessor killed by his successor.

### What remains unresolved

Why succession requires the predecessor’s death; how voluntary Syphiron’s submission and Raedin’s act were beyond their ritual obedience; what, if anything, passed between them inside the dome besides the knife stroke; what the five strokes, deep answer, and closed-door interval accomplish; how the Hall’s total relighting is triggered; and how much Kailan understood at the time rather than only later.

### What felt certain without being proved

Syphiron knew and accepted what would happen. Raedin killed him inside the dome. The ceremony made secrecy and continuity materially inseparable, and the crowd’s response treated the death not as a rupture but as the mechanism by which the office survives. Kailan’s new rank binds him to that continuity before he has had time to absorb its cost.

### First and last things remembered

First: the manufactured total darkness and the move from a continuous spectrum to one hard line. Last: Kailan three hours into lawful office beneath the resumed Pendulum, with blackened water in the seams where Syphiron’s blood had been.

### Honest omissions and uncertainty in recall

The exact scripture wording and the precise order of most of its promises largely vanished, apart from hiddenness, separated secrets, the five offices, and eventual return to the outer world. The identity of the second salt and the exact colours of all its lines did not hold. Several title prefixes and the exact order of some room replies were uncertain. The source and mechanism of the simultaneous relighting did not remain. The timing of the body’s removal relative to the Keepers’ withdrawal had to be reconstructed only approximately. Those gaps are recorded without repair from canon.

## Residue discrepancies

### Alive and correctly retained

The chapter’s main carriers all survived unaided: full spectrum against isolated line; the white cloth becoming lawful; god’s side and beast’s side divided by the Pendulum; the dome collecting motion into stillness; unseen sounds; Syphiron’s blood on the hands that blessed Kailan; Raedin’s larger robe, knife, flame, and white face; the total blaze; Sela’s handling of the loose hand; and black water in the seams. The emotional sequence also held: curiosity, awe, dread, blind confirmation, terrible radiance, subdued aftermath.

The small experiment does not merely introduce a topic. In reader time it supplies a sensory grammar—whole light, permitted line, step, fall, dark between—before Kailan asks the reader to bear the larger experience. The relation remained without needing retrospective explanation.

### Vanished despite possible importance

Most exact Book of Promise wording vanished. What remained was its operative residue: the Mountain hides the people, separated secrets preserve them, five offices divide care, the outer world may one day be restored, and the Beast must remain chained. That compression appears proportionate to a long communal recitation heard while the lights are going out. No evidence in the chapter or locks requires every clause to dominate later memory.

The five children lingering a breath after Adam and Eve did not survive the initial list of strongest images, although the partially lit gravure did. This is a real omission from residue, but its intended later importance cannot be inferred from admissible evidence. It should not be promoted merely because it was forgotten.

Sela’s oil pitcher and her small smile receded behind her later handling of Syphiron’s hand. That is a productive reassignment of emphasis: the later physical care earns priority.

### Falsely but plausibly inferred

The first reconstruction made Syphiron’s intention sound more fully knowable than the testimony permits. What the reader actually possesses is his prepared conduct: he leads the questions, sets down the lamp, opens his robe, speaks the succession while bleeding, and dies. Willing acceptance is a powerful inference, not access to an unspoken interior state. The text itself maintains that distinction.

The reader may infer that the five strokes and deep answer enact a particular transfer or verification behind the five doors. The chapter gives only Kailan’s count, bodily perception, delay, and the Keepers’ return. Any more specific account would be invention.

### Understood early and explained after inference

The danger to Syphiron becomes available before the dome closes. Paul’s “step and fall,” Syphiron’s status as an answer “left,” the succession positions, the contracting Pendulum, and especially Syphiron opening his robe allow the reader to anticipate sacrifice. This is productive dread: the dark withholds the exact act and outcome long enough for expectation to become bodily.

The later evidence—bleeding predecessor, bloody knife, body that does not move—confirms the earned inference in successive visible proofs. It does not append a causal explanation or state that Raedin killed Syphiron from outside Kailan’s knowledge. “Did not move again” usefully closes the possibility that the injury is merely ceremonial while leaving motive and private exchange unclaimed.

The doctrine of separated secrets is inferred from Kailan’s new catechism before the scripture states it at length. The scripture is not redundant explanation because it changes the scale: an office rule becomes the people’s salvation story while the Hall itself disappears. Its exact clauses may fade; its communal pressure should remain.

### Productively unresolved

The chapter gives the reader the right questions without an illicit answer: how voluntary the act is for either man; whether anything is said or passed inside the dome; why death is necessary rather than merely customary; what happens behind the five office doors; what the strokes and answer verify; and whether the instantaneous blaze is mechanical, humanly coordinated, or ritually presented so that its mechanism does not matter. Each uncertainty has a visible or audible boundary.

Young Kailan’s understanding is also properly bounded. His mature testimony can name offices and reproduce the rite, but within the remembered event his body moves before he chooses and then obeys the rail. The text does not convert later knowledge into omniscient access to his younger self or to Raedin and Syphiron.

### Confusing without artistic return

None survived the comparison as a required repair. The untagged room speakers are resolved by alternation, the direct address to Paul, and the distinct ownership of Mountain experience and physical explanation. The room interlude after the blaze is a deliberate sensory transfer; the following testimony remains Kailan’s continuous account rather than opening an external camera. The dome, wire, and Pendulum need not become an engineering diagram for the reader to understand concealment, arrest, and release.
