## Unaided reconstruction — fixed before reopening

The room first compares Genesis with the Mountain's stories: Cain resembles a Mountain murderer despite changes of descent and setting, while Eve's forbidden crossing looks more like ascension than fall. The woman in one story chooses and is punished; in the other she is carried and therefore kept innocent. The question turns toward whether either tradition avoids asking what she chose.

The remembered account then gives the narrator a Pendulum problem. Ka-Raedin makes him watch and measure it for forty days. He finally combines the swing, a fixed northern star, and the Mountain's position to show that the Mountain turns beneath the Pendulum. Ka-Raedin confirms the calculation and tells him very few people could follow it. The result has survived divided among offices: Faith has observations, Justice a number, Light the instrument without its meaning. The narrator carries his proof to Va-Maera, expecting conflict. She tests it against old observations, including one deliberately inconvenient mark whose instrument bearing changed. His prediction survives. She will preserve the inherited heading and its error as well as the correction, because erasing error would make later readers inherit only her certainty. Their sealed prediction agrees with the later observation, and he becomes a carrier between Light and Faith.

On that route he notices a point of returned lamp-light in a vent. After twelve nights of permitted observation, he uses the empty road created by the mourning of an unnamed dead child to enter a hidden passage. He marks the oil in his lamp, takes a spare wick and no blade, and finds alterations and repairs made for more than one body or generation. The offices' marks fall away. He reaches the exterior and steps into a night that destroys his sense of scale. He sees a light move evenly among the fixed stars but has no category for it. Wind and darkness make the entrance almost impossible to recover. He finds a polished plate that returns the vent's point of light and a nearly erased Shield mark. Although the oil reaches his first return mark, he opens another old, tended mechanism and hears a metal tongue strike.

Inside is a Shield archive of things classed as harmless unless taught. I remember a water-lord figure whose tag preserves condemnation, correction, and the seal over both; a rigid, abundantly bodied woman whose meaning the narrator cannot settle; and an image of a crucified man that recalls Syphiron's wound. An altered Light lens contains an outside corrective element superior to Mountain craft. Through it, files become legible: peoples who refuse contact are represented to outsiders by the Blackwood Foundation and its black tree. A road request is refused across generations without any hidden person appearing in the record.

The Mountain's own file contains Sarah Blackwood and Amastan photographed by the Mountain in 1974; a white, ordered flower over Reggane in 1960 and sand turned to glass; a map that makes the Oasis a blank around which all useful lines bend; and a route marked with breach, containment, exchange, loss, sealing, and a Threshold Exception. A torn strip names Sarah, Amastan, and Ka-Xhian beside the incomplete exception. The narrator takes the strip. He also finds a dismantled vehicle marked 195 and with the black tree, recorded around 1980 as second contact and a field cover concluded.

A wall lamp is still warm and an alarm tongue protrudes. A Shield warden arrives already dressed and packed for outside work, resets the tongue, relights the lamp, and nearly sees the narrator hiding among the machine's panels. The narrator realises opening the door summoned him. Rather than risk the exterior, he flees inward through the passage the warden used.

There he finds a side chamber built to swallow sound: wedges on every surface, a separated frame, a fixed chair with wrist and ankle rings, a drain, and old blood. Va-Sheva's ribbon is stuck in that blood. He cannot decide whether she was held there, stood over someone, left a sign, or lost it in some other way. Pulling it loose would reveal entry, so he leaves it and keeps only the cleanly removable paper. This is the emotional low point I retain most strongly: the lamp burning down while he leaves her ribbon in blood.

At the second oil mark he turns back. Outside, the warden searches with a hooded lamp. The narrator uses the fixed star, his outward count, and the reflector's bearing to recover the hidden entrance. He cleans the traces. He tells neither Ka-Raedin nor Va-Sheva because any single interpretation would surrender the evidence or collapse the useful uncertainty. The strip bearing Sarah's name remains against his skin.

Back in the room, Paul says he did not interrupt because the account needed to remain clean. Sarah will join them on Good Friday to explain the photograph and its beginning. After the room's interval, the interviewee reports following Martinez after he removed a memory card from a second camera. Martinez leaves it under a newspaper; an unknown older man with a soldier's economy retrieves it. The interviewee photographed him. They will show the face to Sarah only when she is present. If she knows neither him nor the camera, Roger has been carrying their account out of her house.

- The narrator wants first to solve the Pendulum, then to make Faith yield to proof, then to learn what the point of light and Threshold conceal. Once inside, he wants proof that can survive his breach and wants to know what the ribbon says about Va-Sheva without prematurely choosing its meaning.

The archive produces alternating appetite and dread. The ribbon is the lowest and most intimate turn; the escape converts that grief into controlled fear.

- Unresolved: what the Threshold Exception allowed; why Sarah, Amastan, and Ka-Xhian share it; what Blackwood's representation conceals or protects; whether Va-Sheva was prisoner, operator, witness, or sign-maker; who receives the camera packet; whether Sarah knows the collector or the second camera; and what the moving light was.

Va-Sheva is connected to the silent room, but her role did not feel certain.

## Text-to-residue discrepancies

### Alive and correctly retained

- The chapter's movement from chosen problem to forbidden crossing survives without needing a retrospective explanation. The opening's question about choice remains available when the narrator crosses both his first oil limit and Shield's Threshold.
- Va-Maera does not remain as a simple opponent. Her hostile datum, her refusal to erase an inherited heading, and the sealed prediction all survive as one compound memory: proof is strongest when it preserves the record of what it displaced.
- The outside night, the altered lens, the black tree, the white flower, the blank map, the alarm tongue, the ribbon, and the fixed star all remain after the details around them recede. Each carries a distinct change of pressure.
- The ribbon's uncertainty remains bounded. The text gives every necessary fact for the question—recognised weave, blood, restraints, possible traces of removal—without turning any possible role for Va-Sheva into fact.
- The return converts the Pendulum's still star and the narrator's measuring habit into remembered survival resources. This recurrence is felt without a paragraph explaining the correspondence.

### Vanished despite possible importance

- The exact handoff among Faith's observations, Justice's sealed number, and Light's instrument softened after the first read, though the larger fact of divided inheritance remained. On reopening, the paragraph is compact and memorable enough; increasing it would add explanation to a residue that already holds what the later action needs.
- The dismantled machine remains vivid as a body, while the inventory's exact “second contact” and “field cover concluded” language is weaker. This appears to be acceptable disappearance: active Threshold use is proved moments later by the warm lamp, tongue, packet, and warden, which are stronger carriers than the tag.
- The lamp-edge objects—a gold mouth, child's clay foot, black feathers, half a red sentence—vanished almost entirely. Their disappearance feels intentional: they enlarge the unseen archive without competing for later consequence.

### Falsely but plausibly inferred

- On the cold read, the alarm's strike invited the inference that the warden's journey was created entirely by the narrator's opening. The later details complicate that: exterior clothing, packet, and lamp were ready before the breach, while the displaced tongue summoned or redirected an already prepared movement. The narrator's own “I had summoned him” keeps this inference emotionally true without claiming that no journey had been planned. The correction rewards attention rather than making the earlier inference an error to remove.
- The Blackwood Foundation initially reads as an outside captor speaking over hidden peoples. The road file also shows a refusal successfully maintained across generations. The evidence therefore supports control and protection at once and does not authorise either moral verdict. That doubleness should remain.

### Understood early or confirmed after inference

- The metal strike is available as an alarm before the warm lamp and protruding tongue confirm it. “I had summoned him” arrives after the reader can infer the mechanism, but it functions as the narrator accepting personal consequence, not as a redundant mechanism lesson.
- Once Martinez removes and deposits a camera card, the reader can infer that the packet contains what the room's eye and ear recorded. The following exchange briefly states that inference aloud, but it also establishes what these two tellers now share and the boundary of their next test. Its interpersonal function earns the confirmation.
- The first photograph already makes Sarah's connection to the Mountain certain; the torn Threshold strip does not repeat that fact but changes the available question from whether she had contact to what exception joined her to Amastan and Ka-Xhian.

### Productively unresolved

- The moving light has observable motion but no available category for this teller at that age.
- The water-lord tag preserves precedence as unsettled; the abundant woman's body remains promise, rank, warning, or a word the teller lacks.
- The Foundation may shield, represent, monopolise, or do several at once. The files provide conduct, not a final motive.
- The Threshold Exception lacks action, requester, permission, and price. The torn edge creates the right question rather than a generic blank.
- Va-Sheva's ribbon can accuse, ask for rescue, record presence, or mislead without intention. Blood is certain; role is not.
- The warden's identity, the purpose of his sealed packet, the collector's identity, Sarah's knowledge, and Roger's participation remain appropriately open because the tellers explicitly possess no more.

### Confusing without artistic return

- The only meaningful first-read blur was not where anything happened but which closure was being crossed late in the escape. The route contains the shaved vent slab between the Mountain and desert, then the door seam behind the polished plate between desert and archive, then a Mountain-end passage used by the warden. On the return, “outer stone” and later “slab” are technically distinct, but the earlier identifying phrases have been out of working memory for a long archive sequence. The uncertainty adds no emotional or interpretive value; the intended fear is whether the narrator can evade the hooded lamp and recover the entrance, not whether the reader can distinguish two pieces of stone.
