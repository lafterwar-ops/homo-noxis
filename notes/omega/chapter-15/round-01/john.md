## Phase 1 — Governing diagnosis

Chapter 15 works. Its soul is not the seismic explanation but the collision between three forms of testimony: Kailan's bodily and religious memory, Paul's disciplined test of what can honestly be claimed from the traces, and Sarah's decades-long operational archive. The chapter turns a private childhood sensation into external proof, then turns that proof against the present. Its decisive emotional event is Kailan recognising that two deaths he witnessed have been kept as paper in a cold northern house. Its decisive plot event is his deduction that two current readiness figures inside one hour cannot both be lawful rites.

The chapter also honours the no-omniscience rule at the architectural level: after the title it stays wholly inside `room`; information arrives through speech, silence, demonstratives, and neutral documentary dividers. Nobody's unspoken motive or unseen action is asserted. The final answer refuses false certainty: Kailan can prove the present pattern is impossible as an ordinary readiness, but cannot recover the hand, location, or intention from this room.

The governing weakness is local clarity, not missing story. In an unlabelled three-person transcript, Paul and Sarah sometimes occupy adjacent evidentiary registers. A first-time reader can reconstruct the speaker from role and syntax, but may have to do so retrospectively at the handoffs into “My own trade…women like me,” “I kept every one…,” “That is how I did it,” and the cautious objection about emergency readiness. A second, smaller friction is object deixis: the folder contains two historical traces while Chapter 14 has already placed a fresh live strip in the room. “These two,” “the paper,” and “that paper” remain logically recoverable, but the custody chain asks the reader to distinguish archive sheets from the live strip without one fully explicit spoken separation.

Those frictions do not warrant structural invention, a new scene, an explanatory narrator, or a changed reveal. A bounded production pass could make the speaker/source and sheet identity immediately recoverable while preserving the existing experience.

## Phase 2 — Logic and architecture map

### Fundamental dramatic question

Can a phenomenon Kailan knows only as forbidden bodily memory be independently proved, and if it can, what does the present double occurrence mean?

### Causal spine

1. Sarah stops Kailan's account; Paul refuses to continue merely as a passive recorder and opens the folder Sarah gave him.
2. Sarah asks how Kailan recognised the current drum figure before Horizon named it.
3. Kailan supplies two independent memories: two Keeper successions, four years apart, each followed by five equally spaced strokes and a slower answer.
4. The folder supplies two historical traces keyed to those mornings. Paul's qualification protects the proof from false exactness: different instruments and noise prevent edge-for-edge identity, but timing, source region, and figure converge.
5. Kailan translates the outside physics into Mountain understanding: the Bowl of the Last Prayer makes a recognisable ground signature.
6. Sarah reveals the archive's operational use. Repeated knocks plus Horizon names yield a calendar of Keeper deaths and accessions.
7. Kailan applies the historical rule to the live drum. One current figure could be emergency readiness; two inside one hour cannot be two lawful rites.
8. Sarah asks for the culprit. Kailan keeps the inference inside its evidence: the actor might be internal, external, or counterfeit; the answer is not recoverable in the Swiss room.
9. Sarah sends him back into the road account, forcing Chapter 16.

No link in that chain depends on an omniscient bridge. Each conclusion is either witnessed, read from a named physical trace, supplied from Sarah's archive, or explicitly marked as inference and limited accordingly.

### Emotional movement

- Irritation and custodial mistrust: Kailan resists Sarah's interruptions and watches Paul hold the machine.
- Confession under pressure: he gives Paul and Sarah a rite no Veil should know.
- Recognition: the forbidden memory becomes paper evidence.
- Personal violation and grief: the traces are not abstractions but “my mornings,” attached to the wounded Faith woman and the dying old Keeper.
- Strategic horror: Kailan sees what an enemy could count without seeing a face.
- Present alarm: the historical calendar becomes an impossible live double signal.
- Chosen action: uncertainty is preserved, but Kailan accepts that the next answer lies on the road.

### Proof architecture

The proof is properly cumulative rather than singular:

- Kailan independently supplies two dates by life event and one ritual figure by direct bodily perception.
- Two historical instruments recorded the corresponding disturbances in their own years.
- Paul refuses false precision and distinguishes a matched figure from identical traces.
- Sarah supplies the long archive and the independent Horizon name sequence.
- Reggane establishes that a readiness may answer danger rather than death, preventing an overclaim.
- The second current figure removes that single-event explanation without identifying an actor.

Removing any one of these checks would either weaken proof or make it too convenient. The qualification that the traces do not lie “edge for edge” is load-bearing because it makes the later certainty earned rather than magical.

### Room and documentary function

The room is an active pressure chamber, not a frame around retrospective narration. Sarah stops and redirects testimony; Paul holds the recorder and opens the evidence; silences register Kailan's recognition; “Here” and demonstratives conduct the paper examination; the three centred dividers mark documentary elapsed beats without creating an external camera. The road remains untold until the evidence has changed what everyone knows.

## Phases 7–9 — Causal, voice, and adversarial audits

### Plain causal test

The chapter's difficult inference survives plain restatement: a lawful readiness produces one culturally known ground figure; historic instruments independently captured that figure on two known Keeper-change mornings; Sarah used repeated correlations with Horizon names to infer the succession calendar; the current drum produced the figure twice inside the time in which one rite could occur; therefore the present pair is not two lawful readiness rites. The traces cannot determine the actor, location, knowledge, or intention. The chapter is at least as clear as that explanation, except for the small paper-deixis issue already identified.

### Source and voice test

- **Kailan:** Direct bodily memory supplies the childhood/young-adult rites. His categories are floor, arches, mapped absence, prayer, birth, office, and violated sacred custody. He is the only person entitled to infer what two rites in one hour mean inside Mountain law.
- **Paul:** His authority is procedural and evaluative. He owns the immediate recorder/folder handling, tests Kailan before opening the file, qualifies what the paper can honestly prove, and refuses to call near traces identical. His dry caution is essential.
- **Sarah:** Her authority is operational and historical. She stops the account for cause, supplies the archive, identifies the monitoring practice, correlates drums with Horizon names, invokes Reggane as a competing explanation, asks “Who,” and orders the road account. Her line about a trade hiding bombs from women like her also identifies the speaker from lived professional history.
- **Documentary record:** The centred dividers assert no unseen action or mind. They function as recorded gaps/handling intervals. Ellipses remain audible silence, not omniscient description.

No sentence asserts another person's interiority. No voice reports an unseen act as fact. The few handoff ambiguities concern which present speaker owns an assertion, not whether a recoverable source exists.

### Adversarial attack

The chapter's strongest device—the exact cultural recognition of an external seismic signature—could have felt contrived if the papers matched perfectly, if Sarah already knew the ritual semantics, or if Kailan identified the culprit. The text protects itself: different instruments do not overlay exactly; Sarah has dates and names but needs Kailan's embodied knowledge; Reggane supplies a lawful alternative; and Kailan refuses to claim the hand. The evidence is convergent, not magical.

The repeated five-plus-slow language could look overdetermined, but most repetitions change its function: memory, trace comparison, archive method, present diagnosis. Payment should target only a genuinely duplicate gloss, never the recurrence as a system.

### Adversarial defence of the weakest point

The unlabeled turns are intentionally participatory: readers identify people from what they know and how they reason. That purpose survives for Kailan, and largely for Paul/Sarah. It weakens only where both outside interlocutors speak in an evidentiary register across a turn boundary. Two endogenous anchors would preserve participation while removing avoidable rereading.
