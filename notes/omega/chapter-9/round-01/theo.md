## Unaided reconstruction — fixed before reopening

### What happened, in remembered order

The room asks when Pythagoras lived. The answer is roughly two and a half thousand years ago; the response, "Your kind is late," arrives with an unexplained smile.

After Syphiron's martyrdom, Kailan waits for the Mountain to feel changed, but its routines continue. His lawful elevation to Veil loads him with the northern halls and administrative work while rank makes Sela, his former peers, and Sheva recede from him. He still visits the Mirror, can find its illicit thread of light, and no longer wants an answer from it.

Ka-Raedin teaches diffraction and interference. Kailan refuses the next lesson because he has understood the succession chain: completing it means one day placing the knife under Ka-Raedin's ribs. He asks for another Veil and chooses stasis so Ka-Raedin can live. Instead he empties out while continuing every duty correctly. Six months later he stalls over a basin, unable to remember why its circle must be clean or whether he cares.

Ka-Raedin names this "ascension amnesia," says it has afflicted Light's line for generations, counts Kailan's eight years of service and training, removes the northern halls from him for a year, and orders him to waste that year until he can say why he still loves light. He then leads Kailan through a hidden upper route to an observatory in a stone bowl at the Mountain's crown, with an open sky and a long lens. There is no compulsory lesson there.

Ka-Raedin brings out two small white cups. For the first time they say Syphiron's name and grieve him rather than use his death as ceremony. Kailan says he knelt while others made a light of Syphiron's death; Ka-Raedin says Syphiron ordered the kneeling and admits that he held the knife and loved Syphiron. They weep openly. Kailan repeats Syphiron's "Speaking of archangels."

Kailan spends the year sleeping by day and climbing at dusk. The uninterrupted sky overwhelms him, then the habits trained by the Mirror turn wonder into measurement without destroying it. He finds that the apparent northern centre is empty rather than occupied by the nearby star; distinguishes fixed stars, wandering lights, the Moon, colours, and changing lesser lights beside a bright disc; sees the pale sky-river resolve into innumerable points; and records more empty fields than lit ones. A lunar eclipse, a lamp, and clay shapes persuade him that the ground is spherical. He cannot measure stellar displacement or solve the wanderers' reversals, so he leaves causes blank. His equipment and slate boxes multiply; his questions return.

Ka-Raedin visits monthly, corrects measures, and laughs—once at the eyepiece ring around Kailan's eye, later after drawing one around his own. Kailan fears that this new merriness belongs to the same succession chain as Syphiron's: perhaps consenting to death makes a Keeper light enough to guide his successor toward the knife.

At the year's end, Kailan shows Ka-Raedin that the stars and the sun do not return on the same count. He also says darkness is not merely failed lamplight: most places he aimed returned no light, so light may be the interruption rather than darkness. Asked if he still loves the light, he says, "More. It has farther to cross than I knew."

They drink to Syphiron again. Ka-Raedin says grief accumulates through every predecessor. He explains that the fragile white cups were made centuries ago by Ka-Noroth, a Keeper who painted the heavens and women, so grief must be handled gently or its vessel will break. Naming him makes Kailan carry him too.

Next Ka-Raedin arranges a tightly compartmented lesson from Justice. Sa-Tavan uses four wedges in a tray to demonstrate that the two squares on the perpendicular sides equal the square on the slope, then a unit circle and two "shadows" to show the squared components sum to one. Kailan may take the shape but reveal nothing of Light's question. Ka-Raedin seats him before the Pendulum with the northern star, a unit stick, its angle, and its two shadows, and tells him not to return with an answer until he can state the question. He also points toward a later lesson in what stellar colour says about substance. The chapter ends with Kailan watching the Pendulum cross its mark, back and forth.

### Who wanted what

- Kailan wanted Ka-Raedin to remain alive badly enough to refuse knowledge and succession; during the sickness he wanted nothing; in the observatory he wanted again to look, measure, and ask without being compelled.
- Ka-Raedin wanted Kailan to stop drowning in competent service, grieve Syphiron honestly, recover his unpriced love of light, and finally generate the question himself rather than receive another chained answer.
- I wanted the observatory to restore inquiry without cancelling grief, wanted to know whether Ka-Raedin's care could exist outside succession, and increasingly wanted to know what the Justice shape would let Kailan ask of the Pendulum.
- Sa-Tavan appeared to want only to discharge the exact lesson entrusted to him without seeking Light's reason.

### What remained

Images and objects: the white cloth suspended above the black basin; the concealed stone bowl and long metal throat; the small circle made by the northern star around an empty centre; black fields outnumbering lights; strokes filling slate margins; the lunar shadow and clay ball; charcoal rings around both men's eyes; two thin white cups with blue rims nested together; four wedges changing arrangement in one tray; the Pendulum crossing its mark.

Sounds and sentences: "Your kind is late"; the instruction to waste a whole year; "Speaking of archangels"; Ka-Raedin's laughter; "More. It has farther to cross than I knew"; the demand to state the question before returning with an answer; "Back and forth, back and forth."

Emotional low: Kailan holding the cloth over the basin, unable to recover purpose or care. Emotional high: the return of desire through unforced looking, concentrated in his answer that he loves light more because it has farther to cross. The shared, uncovered weeping was the chapter's deepest release, though it did not remove the knife.

### Expectation, uncertainty, and inference

I expected Kailan's next work to combine the star, angle, unit-circle components, and Pendulum into evidence about the world's motion, possibly the rotation of a spherical ground, and then to make the colour of stars measurable as substance. I expected the succession crisis to remain rather than be cured by the year.

Unresolved: the exact question the Pendulum answers; what the star colours will reveal; whether Kailan will ever consent to the knife; whether Ka-Raedin's laughter is evidence of such consent or only Kailan's fearful association; what the room speaker's Pythagoras smile knows about the difference between the two kinds' histories.

Certain without proof: the observatory is not merely a reward but a deliberately protected way for Light to produce discoveries unavailable to the other Kahirs; Kailan's recovery is real but bounded; grief and intellectual renewal will be carried together rather than one defeating the other.

Plausible but less certain: the Pendulum will expose rotation rather than only geometry; Ka-Raedin is preparing Kailan for succession even while sincerely trying to keep him alive inside it.

### First and last remembered

First: the room's Pythagoras question and "Your kind is late"; in the testimony, the Mountain failing to feel different after Syphiron's death. Last: Kailan alone with the Pendulum, equipped with pieces but required to discover the problem, while it moves "Back and forth, back and forth."

### Honest omissions and blurred areas

I did not retain every administrative item transferred with the northern halls, every observed celestial object, the exact month attached to each stage of Kailan's return, the exact generational distance among Ka-Noroth, Ka-Sarem, and Ka-Raedin, or the full wording of the lesson of the fourth shadow. Sela's struck lamp and Sheva's formal distance remained as a combined residue of rank-driven isolation rather than as equally distinct incidents. The observatory year survived much more strongly as a sequence of wonder, measure, darkness, laughter, and revived questions than as a complete catalogue.

## Text-to-residue discrepancies

### Alive and correctly retained

- Kailan's refusal is about saving Ka-Raedin, not merely resisting promotion.
- The sickness is rendered first as correct work without desire and then made physical by the cloth over the basin.
- The observatory joins grief and inquiry without presenting inquiry as a cure for grief.
- The cups retain both intimacy and succession across their two appearances.
- The year's scientific abundance condenses into a few durable proofs and honest limits rather than a general claim that Kailan has become knowledgeable.
- Ka-Raedin's laughter returns the knife to a sequence that might otherwise feel healed.
- The end leaves a bounded problem: the necessary pieces are named, while the exact question and answer remain Kailan's work.

### Vanished despite importance

No governing fact met this category. Several details vanished, but the chapter itself gives them collective rather than individual work: administrative inventories, the Sun–Moon width comparison, monthly equipment checks, and the exact distance between names in the cup lineage. The consequences they support remained. Making each louder would turn credible abundance into a checklist.

### Falsely but plausibly inferred

- That the Pendulum will prove the rotation of a spherical Earth. The pieces strongly invite this, especially for a reader with outside scientific knowledge, but the testimony has not yet stated Kailan's question or result.
- That Ka-Raedin's care is also a deliberate method for bringing Kailan back toward accepting succession. The text supports suspicion, especially through the later certainty of "one day," but Kailan does not know Ka-Raedin's whole intention.
- That the observatory's secrecy exists primarily to deny other Kahirs discovery. Compartmentation is explicit; its full institutional purpose is not.

These inferences should remain hypotheses, not be corrected in advance.

### Understood early

- The reader can identify exhaustion or depression before Ka-Raedin supplies the inherited name. This is useful: the new information is not that Kailan is unwell, but that Light recognizes the pattern, transmits permission to stop, and has failed to prevent recurrence.
- The emotional purpose of the observatory is apparent through voluntary looking, the cups, and uncovered grief before Ka-Raedin explicitly states what the place is for.
- A contemporary reader is likely to identify the wedge proof as Pythagorean as soon as the first arrangement is described, paying off the room question before Sa-Tavan states the relation.

### Explained after already being inferred

The clearest instance is the short hinge immediately after Kailan and Ka-Raedin weep: the narration announces that Ka-Raedin will say what the place is for, and Ka-Raedin then explains that it exists for grief and remembered beauty. The scene has already demonstrated both. The genuinely new fact in the speech is that every risen Light has come there; the interpretation of the experience is repeated before the year demonstrates it again.

Ka-Raedin's naming of ascension amnesia does not belong wholly in this category. Its diagnosis arrives after symptoms are legible, but its lineage, his personal admission, and the permission to be tired are revelations the reader could not earn unaided.

### Productively unresolved

- What the room speaker's smile contains and what "late" measures beyond the historical date.
- Whether the Pendulum inference available to a modern reader is the whole question or only its first layer.
- Whether Ka-Raedin's laughter indicates consent to death, recovered life, or both.
- Whether Kailan will accept, evade, or reinterpret the succession that Ka-Raedin treats as certain.
- What the empty fields mean beyond the careful claim that the instrument returned no light.
- How the measure of light will become the measure of stellar substance.

Each unknown has an available question and a boundary. None needs an added answer in this chapter.

### Confusing without artistic return

None established by the cold-read residue. The technical middle exceeds exact memory, but it returns wonder, method, epistemic restraint, and the ingredients of the final problem. The ending is inferable without being closed; that is suspense, not missing information.

This correction acts only on explanatory pressure. It does not add an anchor, move a revelation, alter an event, or change a character decision. The voluntary invitation, the cups, the uncovered weeping, Kailan's return the next evening, and the year of looking already let the reader feel what the observatory is for. Allowing those acts to carry the meaning keeps the emotional proof from being followed immediately by its gloss.
