## Governing diagnosis

This chapter works. It is a severe, intelligible climax built from procedure rather than spectacle: admission becomes ordeal, testimony becomes proof, divided knowledge becomes a legal exception, Paul's method turns five secret deposits into a contest of alternatives, and Justice converts unresolved uncertainty into an irreversible command. The last movement then makes that command physically harder to perform without making it less binding. The result has moral force because the Mountain does not prove itself right. It records the one-in-five denial road and chooses against it.

The chapter does **not** need a new plot, a larger catastrophe, a sixth alternative, a cleaner moral answer, or more Cataclysm explanation. Its main architecture should be frozen. The material defects are local but consequential:

Everything else is primarily a payment-pass question: keep the five-room pressure and its later echo, but resist adding interpretation where the physical procedure already means enough.

## Reading experience

### Fundamental dramatic question

Can Paul force a society designed to prevent any one hand from holding the whole to face a non-Cataclysm alternative before its own law makes Cataclysm irreversible?

The chapter's more painful answer is: he can force the alternative into the record, but he cannot force the offices to accept its uncertainty. The reader is denied the comfort of either a proven necessity or a simple atrocity committed by fools.

### Plain causal spine

The enemy has found the Mountain and intends to seize its operators and central mechanism intact. The existing Cataclysm law divides the necessary knowledge among five offices, so Justice cannot lawfully weigh the whole. The Threshold Exception admits a powerless outsider to receive all five parts without acting on them. Paul uses that whole to test alternatives. Most fail because the Cataclysm engine is also the city's life-support system, the aim is recoverable from the sky, succession survives the present Keepers, evacuation is militarily impossible, publicity starts a larger race, and negotiation has no authority capable of stopping the operation. Total destructive refusal remains: it offers a one-in-five bound of permanent denial and a four-in-five danger of eventual possession without Kahir law. Because no office can improve that bound or secure the ruin, Justice finds the possession condition met. The law makes Cataclysm a command. The attack then kills Faith's Keeper and incapacitates or bypasses the trained succession, but it does not reopen the ruling. Shield raises the Greater Seal while the surviving offices face a command whose Faith component has been damaged.

This chain is legible and should not be simplified into “the five prove Cataclysm necessary.” They do not. They prove why they choose it without proof of necessity.
