## Voice and acoustic performance

- Kailan's narration is recoverably first person throughout. His diction is office-literate and retrospective, but he repeatedly admits tactical motive and compromised feeling: he delays Sarah's identity, wants Light to close the refusal road, and blames Paul for finding it. Those admissions prevent the account from masquerading as omniscient judgment.
- Paul speaks in tests, imperatives, and distinctions that give the listener something answerable: “Which number is certain?”, “Break it,” “Negotiate,” and the paired one-in-five/four-in-five questions. His ethics of source and custody are performed with the machine, notebook, headings, and direct address to the record.
- Sarah is most individual when she compresses: “More,” “He will hear me,” “A chance.” Her silences are tactical evidence, especially with Roger and Jean-Charles. The longer offer to expose the operation retains her characteristic willingness to spend herself, her name, and her institutions as instruments.
- Ka-Leth's syntax is declarative and jurisdictional. He controls who may speak, what a finding does, and when a door in the law exists. Hands on stone and clay keep his legal language from floating free of action.
- The four other Keepers are distinguishable by the kind of correction they make. Balance qualifies counts; Light corrects proof claims; Shield sorts observation from inference and states defensive limits; Faith protects exact breath and listens through her palm. Their voices do not depend on decorative tics.
- Va-Sheva has the least speech and the most consequential motion. She crosses, turns Paul's hand, releases it, sends a warden, throws herself into stone, calls gates, spends four fingers, and counts Jean-Charles alive with one touch.
- Roger's courtesy is his aggression. His longest answer is intentionally controlled rather than breathless, but a live reading needs the sounding Mountain and Sarah's held handset to remain active counterpressure during it.
- The final passage correctly lets acoustics replace explanation: slabs, bells, stone meeting stone, Pendulum, reel, and breathing. Its stillness is earned by the preceding irreversible acts.

## Bounded performance failures and revision units

These are performance-level observations only. None requires changing the locked plot or inventing architecture.

1. **Jean-Charles lacks an initial stage position.** The entrance names Va-Sheva, Sarah, Paul, and Kailan. Later, Va-Sheva vouches for Jean-Charles and sends him toward the torn seam, but the hearing has not shown when or where he became physically available to be sent. His subsequent disappearance must remain ambiguous; his presence before departure need not be. A later performance pass should add one minimal placement—along the Shield wall, at the recess, or brought in by a named warden—before Va-Sheva gives the order.

2. **Kailan's bound-body action at the collapse is under-specified.** “I was moving too, my bound hands useless” is followed by “We got stone off her” and then by his hands against Ka-Elun's face. The emotional action is strong, but an actor cannot tell whether Kailan shoulders debris, is freed, is pulled in, or only joins the collective “we.” One concrete bodily carrier would preserve the bindings while making the rescue playable.

3. **Paul's leg injury drops out of the blocking.** Stone catches his leg; he goes down and protects the reel. The later movement through the Greater Seal and up to the Pendulum gives no limp, support, delay, or release from the injury. A single continuing action would keep the cost present without enlarging the event.

4. **The first, second, and fifth admission rooms are primarily solution report.** The false-witness room gains speech and the reconciled-record room gains slate work, withheld hurry, blows, and dust; the other three have less interpersonal resistance. Because all five rooms and problems are frozen and return as deposition rooms, they should remain. If revised, the unit to strengthen is Ka-Leth's physical testing under attack—hand, hesitation, breath, glance, or the next charge—not additional explanation of the mathematics.

5. **Shield appears to begin twice during Kailan's finding.** Ka-Dhavar first supplies the war-board comparison, then the next paragraph says he “answered before I finished” and launches a second speech. The content and Shield voice are sound, but the repeated onset makes the interruption feel reset. A performance pass should make the second intervention a continuation or make the interruption point physically explicit.

6. **Sarah's major self-account is compressed exactly where Paul demands live ownership.** Paul says she must tell it herself; “So they asked her, and she told them” then skips the account before he asks what she wanted. The prior account is deliberately not repeated, and that economy can remain, but in performance the immediate pressure briefly vanishes and “you have had it from her already” has an acoustically loose addressee inside the Paul-and-Kailan framing room. A minimal handoff—an explicit question, listener, or reel cue—could make the ellipsis feel chosen rather than missing.

7. **Several long speeches need their existing counter-actions protected in reading.** Ka-Cheryn's stores account, Roger's geopolitical refusal, and Paul's statement to the recorder are long because each speaker is trying to make evasion impossible. They should not be decoratively broken up. The playable breath points already available—counting fingers, handset silence, sounding sweeps, uncovered clay, arrested stylus, and turning reels—need to remain visible so the speeches continue changing pressure.
