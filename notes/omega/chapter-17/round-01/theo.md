### Remembered order

The chapter begins already inside an emergency argument: the drum keeps producing the shape of a Keeper rising, but no Keeper has died and the event returns on an inhumanly exact eleven-minute interval. Someone asks for the old cabinet and seals. The papers let the room distinguish a true deep event from a counterfeit surface stimulus: the new source walks east over the Mountain's skin, and multiple stations agree. The recurring signal is not a real rising but a sounding operation. Vibrator trucks drive known sweeps into the ground, geophones read the return, and one sweep has crossed the buried chamber's true note. The danger is mapping rather than immediate release; a rig and soldiers are waiting for the picture to become usable.

Jean-Charles arrives with a radar image of the desert camp: four trucks, a rig, a grid of shot points, and an inward-facing perimeter. The seismic specialist recognizes the grid as his own old field method, down to a private hook that should exist only in Horizon's technical archive or in the memory of a very small number of pupils. It proves a road out of Horizon, not yet which person walked it.

Paul then brings evidence from inside the house. A hair-fine wire leaves the fixed recorder for a locked service chase. Interview reels have been substituted despite matching boxes, seals, and lengths; a hidden scratch proves the exchange. A black car with dark glass and no country has twice come to the low gate. Sarah's right hand carried a flat case to it once and spoke to someone inside it another time. Asked to name the right hand, Paul says Jean-Charles while Jean-Charles is only paces behind them. Paul adds Jean-Charles's questions about the dead well and his request not to trouble Elise. Sarah refuses to turn this accumulation into certainty: Jean-Charles remains suspected, not proved, and she will aim his competence through compartmented instructions.

The camera bait has yielded no name. The wire, reels, car, stolen grid, protected aircraft, soldiers, archive access, and border reach make a state operation—or something designed at state expense to resemble one—the probable shape. Sarah identifies a part of the French state as most probable without naming it as fact. The recurring paper and telephone bells force decision. Kailan will return to warn the Mountain even though Sarah also wants what lies beneath it. His lawful ground is the mission his people used to shape his exile; the reason beneath it is Va-Sheva, who chose a containment that kept him alive and then walked back inside. He will carry warning to that choice even if the Mountain kills him.

Entry requires marked hands. Kailan will not cross with only his own erased right and Sarah's; he needs a third hand belonging to neither. He asks Paul to accept the last available mark. Paul initially separates the recorder's work from participation, then rejects the purity of staying behind while the subject of his record is destroyed. The travelling machine is started before the fixed recorder stops, and custody passes explicitly to Paul.

Elise brings passports and route papers. The escape chain moves through helicopter, private aircraft, vehicles, and animals, with identities and route knowledge split between people. Paul carries the recorder as a medical instrument and later on his body; the fixed reels go into a fire case. The house has separate burn and sealing contingencies. Jean-Charles touches Kailan's shoulder, echoing the dead well and the possibility that care and collection occupy the same gesture.

On the wet lawn beneath the wide tree, Kailan completes what he could not complete earlier. “Shadow” becomes a covenant. In the first scripture block Paul is charged to guard the Mountain from the enemy within, never seek the whole, keep the secret past pain and death, and kill Kailan if Kailan becomes the hand that reaches for the whole. Paul answers the threat of being opened with his face and nothing behind it. Kailan uses needle, stone, and blood to mark Paul's hand. The second scripture block puts blood into service, face into dark, and name into silence. Paul rises with the Shadow name, replies “Unlit?” when invited to say something deep, and the tape ends beneath rising rotor noise.

### Remembered wants

- Kailan wants first to name the false event correctly, then to return with a warning that can reach Va-Sheva and his people.
- Paul wants a record whose claims remain bounded; suspicion of the house changes his role from watcher to accountable participant.
- Sarah wants the survey stopped before any single hand can reach the buried chamber. She also wants command of the outcome, a motive the chapter keeps dangerous.
- Elise wants no route, identity chain, or record custody to be recoverable from one capture.
- Jean-Charles remains doubled in memory: the most competent operator present and the person around whom the strongest suspicion accumulates.

### Residue

The first thing remembered is the exact eleven-minute recurrence: a machine's appointment wearing the shape of a sacred death. The last is Paul's dry “Unlit?” under the rotor and the end of the tape.

The persistent images and sounds are the feeding drum; the cabinet wheels and old papers; an answer coming from around rather than under; four vibrator trucks and the private hooked shot-point; a wire finer than hair; the invisible scratch inside a reel flange; the black car's lowered rear window; Jean-Charles ten paces behind the accusation; simultaneous telephone bells; the fixed machine stopping while the travelling one continues; wet grass, needle, last stone, blood, and rotor wash.

The intellectual high point is the counterfeit-rising diagnosis becoming a physical survey with a moving surface source. The emotional high point is Kailan speaking Va-Sheva's name as the motive beneath duty, followed by Paul's acceptance of a covenant that may require him to kill Kailan. The coldest trough is Jean-Charles being named at whisper range and then immediately retained as the party's best field operator.

What seems likely next is a compromised journey toward the Mountain, with Jean-Charles's ambiguity travelling inside the group rather than being solved before departure. Paul is now liable not merely to record Kailan but to stop him. The Mountain may treat Kailan's warning as a trespass.

Unresolved: whether Jean-Charles is a traitor, a coerced courier, a monitored loyalist, or several of these at once; whether the hidden camera and the wire/reel/car exchange serve one owner; how directly the French-state probability maps to the camp; whether Sarah's private desire will align with rescue once access becomes possible; whether Va-Sheva can or will receive the warning; and whether “Unlit?” is levity, assent, or the first sound of Paul's new identity.

What felt certain without proof: Jean-Charles is compromised as a channel even if his intention remains unproved; the survey and the house leakage belong to the same patient pressure; Sarah will use any loyalty and any suspicion simultaneously; and Paul has crossed the line he hoped the recorder would let him avoid.
