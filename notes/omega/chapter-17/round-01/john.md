## Governing diagnosis

Chapter 17 works because it is not merely a briefing before the return. It is the instant three kinds of partial witness become one usable decision: Kailan recognises the sacred shape, Sarah proves that the asking point is a moving surface machine, and Paul proves that the house carrying their record has another route out. The dramatic question is therefore not simply *what is making the marks?* It is: **can the three establish enough truth to move before the survey finishes, without converting suspicion into false certainty or giving the whole route to the very hand they suspect?**

The chapter answers that question in action. The counterfeit diagnosis becomes cabinet proof; the cabinet proof becomes a field threat; the field threat makes Paul's withheld evidence actionable; the evidence produces Sarah's bounded probable-state conclusion; that conclusion forces a compartmented return; the return forces Kailan to speak the personal motive beneath his lawful one; the need for a disinterested third hand forces Paul's Shadow initiation; and the initiation transfers the living record from a room to a body before the rotor takes them. Knowledge, trust, office, identity, custody and location all change inside one uninterrupted recording.

The present text is structurally complete. It does not need perpendicular invention, a new action beat, a second scene, or a stronger culprit reveal. Its remaining recoverable points are local: some proof is interpreted twice after it has already landed; a few turns share the same polished aphoristic cadence; and the multi-person room occasionally makes attribution depend on voice recognition at the very moment the evidence becomes most intricate. Those are payment and source-legibility questions, not reasons to rebuild.

## Reading experience

The opening inherits Chapter 16's live emergency without reset: the drum has supplied a true sacred form attached to an impossible event history. The first exchange creates productive contradiction rather than confusion—Kailan owns the bodily recognition, Paul/Sarah demand a causal account, and the cabinet lets Sarah move the argument from belief to location, direction and repeatability. The strongest early choice is “Keep it in that order”: it makes the proof's sequence part of the story's ethics.

The seismic explanation is unusually successful exposition because every new detail removes an alternative. One station may lie; three may share a clock; nine stations across two arrays do not independently invent the same walk. The radar image then changes scale without claiming impossible resolution. Jean-Charles explicitly limits what the image can establish, after which Sarah's private grid supplies the personal wound. The threat is simultaneously industrial, military and intimate.

Paul's evidence turns the chapter. Until the window exchange, the danger could still be outside the house. The live wire, substituted reels, black car and route questions make the room itself unsafe, yet each claim remains separated from ownership. Sarah's refusal to collapse pupil, archive theft, bought office and trusted man into one answer preserves both epistemic discipline and the later story. Her choice to aim an unproved operational risk rather than pretend she can remove him is morally dangerous and operationally credible.

The final movement pays the information with bodies. Kailan's return has a lawful reason, then Paul asks for the reason beneath it, and Va-Sheva becomes a spoken choice rather than retrospective interior explanation. Paul's objection to becoming part of the subject is answered by the failure of an undelivered record. The fixed machine stops only after the travelling one starts; the record acquires legs before the room changes. The lawn rite then converts Paul's earlier conditional loyalty into scar, name, covenant and custody. “Unlit?” releases tension without dissolving the oath, and the tape ends on propulsion rather than summary.

## Voice and source audit

The chapter satisfies the no-omniscient-narrator lock. It remains inside one `room`; assertions arrive through live speech, named paper/image evidence, recorded silence, audible activity or sparse documentary cues. No retrospective interiority enters. Sarah states her own history, intent and probability bounds; Kailan states his own bodily recognition, law and motive; Paul states his own observations and methodological limits; Jean-Charles states the image's limits; Elise states the route and custody plan.

Voice separation is strong:

- Kailan notices prayer, throat, Mountain, walls, marks and lawful shape. His reasoning turns technical facts into sacred-spatial consequences.
- Paul notices dates, seals, scratches, alternative explanations, custody and what the paper can prove. His humour breaks pressure without denying it.
- Sarah notices intervals, archives, trade, resources, probability and control. Her metaphors arise from ownership and provisioning.
- Elise speaks in compartments, manifests, permissions, failure conditions and exact custody.
- Jean-Charles speaks through field competence, bounded image claims, touch and deflection.

The residual voice risk is cumulative polish. “A death does not keep an appointment,” “a forgery makes a true thing lie,” “kindness and collection,” “bad reasons,” “a record delivered to no one is paper,” and “prophecy with filing instructions” are each effective; in concentration they make several speakers sound equally ready with finished maxims. A payment pass should protect the lines that do causal or relational work and flatten only a non-load-bearing echo. It should not sand away Paul's final joke, Sarah's probability language or Kailan's sacred-spatial imagery.

Attribution remains recoverable through question/answer adjacency, direct address and voice, including after Jean-Charles and Elise enter. Production should nevertheless re-read the five-person stretches aloud after any compression. Adding narrator-like speaker identifications or reactions would solve the wrong problem and violate the form.
