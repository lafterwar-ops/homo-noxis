## Unaided reconstruction — sealed before reopening

### What happened, in remembered order

The interview opens with Kailan fitting the five figures around the judgement of Paris to the five solids and to the five Mountain offices. Eris matters especially: the excluded hand puts an object inside and lets the others reveal themselves. Paul permits Kailan's interpretation without certifying it and describes interpretive plurality as a late, imperfect arrangement rather than a natural Western virtue. This lets Kailan name what was carved on the Hall's southern wall and return to his account.

In the remembered past, the Keeper of Light tests Kailan on what a Veil is and tells him that a locked door may protect either the candidate or what is behind the door. Kailan tries to follow Va-Raedin twice and is caught; the surviving comic image is the written warning that a hidden watcher should not cast such a visible shadow. Unable to solve the five Light lessons, Kailan approaches Va-Maera of Faith and implies that his glimpse of mechanism could give him leverage. She strips that presumption from him at close range. Her force is not that mechanism disproves Faith but that Kailan has mistaken one small mechanism for superiority and weaponised the little he knows. He withdraws his request and leaves shaking.

Weeks or months of ordinary work follow. Kailan searches the public Hall while appearing to serve, listens to fragments of scripture, and tries to make the five lessons cohere. Ka-Elun eventually reads the Mountain's Genesis: creation in five days, with the Mountain, Oasis, surrounding forest and desert threshold, then Adam, Eve, and five children. The interview interrupts so Paul can fetch an English Bible. Its opening nearly matches, but the enormous, freely handled book astonishes Kailan. He says Paul has multiplied rather than abolished keepers.

The Festival of Equal Light arrives. Preparations make the Mountain briefly festive and inhabited: preserved food, bread carried in competitive relays, voices travelling through vents, cords tied as games, apertures and inserts cleaned under record. In a Hall crowded by thousands, Ka-Elun recites Genesis while the offices occupy their places. At the phrase “Let there be light,” sunlight enters and strikes the Pillar of Light. Kailan recognises the first of the five lessons and slips away, lying to a Faith attendant that he is on Light service.

He follows a timed optical path. A shadow hides a removable floor stone and stair. Focus requires lenses to place the small sun through an opening. A liquid-filled triangular vessel turns the beam; Kailan breaks a witnessed seal and adjusts its mineral content. A later vessel creates threshold return. Mirrors ignite one after another and draw a room from darkness before a hidden door opens.

The Veil's Chamber contains a red beam, a ringed black-and-white carving, and an empty cradle sized for the pinhole token Kailan has carried for years. Va-Raedin arrives breathless and gives him only a handful of breaths to place it. The resulting rings match the carving. Kailan falls to his knees: light has bent around the pinhole edge and recombined like water, creating bright and dark bands. The ray is not the whole account. The chamber is older than the office that teaches the question.

Va-Raedin immediately limits what Kailan may test or tell. The lesson has implications beyond Light, and Kailan has earned the fact but not the right to scatter it. Va-Raedin restores the broken seal and leads him out. When Kailan asks to tell Ka-Syphiron himself, Va-Raedin identifies the request as hunger to be seen and calls it the fault through which everything later done to Kailan will enter. Kailan must wait and discover whether he wanted the Chamber or the telling of it.

The next day contains social aftershocks without recognition. Sela knows he lied and infers either failure or a discovery too dangerous to name. Attendants fall silent near him. Finally a young girl brings a black token and summons him behind an old, smoke-blackened gallery full of cracked mirrors and odd drawings, to a room where Ka-Syphiron waits. Before that meeting is shown, the interview returns. Kailan connects Paul's word “unlit” to the fifth lesson: he believes a change of condition will create the occasion he needs, and he is waiting for it.

### Who wanted what

- Kailan wanted the Veil's Chamber, the knowing inside it, and—more painfully than he first admits—the witnessed distinction of having found it. In the interview he also wants authority to frame his people's story through the new comparisons Paul makes available.
- Va-Raedin wanted Kailan to discover rather than be handed the chamber, then to survive the discovery without mistaking it for permission or personal splendour.
- Va-Maera wanted to stop Kailan from turning partial knowledge of Faith's arrangements into leverage and to defend the boundary he had entered contemptuously.
- Paul wanted Kailan's testimony and interpretation without imposing agreement; he also wanted to expose him to a differently distributed custody of texts.
- Sela wanted to know what had happened to him and whether the cost had meaning.

### Emotional trace remembered without checking

The early intellectual pleasure of the five-god arrangement gives way to comic exposure, then to the chapter's lowest social/emotional point in Va-Maera's rebuke. The ordinary weeks create restless deprivation. Festival preparation releases warmth, play, abundance, crowd energy and communal sound. From the first sun-line onward, the pressure becomes urgent, clandestine and bodily. The chamber revelation is the high point: wonder inseparable from the collapse of Kailan's governing model. The deepest aftershock is quieter—the teacher naming vanity as the open door to Kailan's later ruin. The ending converts triumph into suspended judgement and then into menace: present-day Kailan believes he is waiting for the condition that will let his own hidden line escape.
