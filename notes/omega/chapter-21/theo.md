### What happened, in remembered order

Paul, recording behind the Greater Seal, explains that Kailan's account will be supplemented by reels made during the Vigil and by Shield's audit. The enemy takes four days to finish dying: the charges stop first, then movements propagate through the ground until Faith judges that the camp above has been swallowed.

The Mountain turns immediately from the firing to keeping bodies alive. Kailan carries a wounded woman with one of Jean-Charles's wardens and sees the old instruction hall become rows of living and dead. Oren washes and names the injured. The formerly sacred mercies reveal themselves as joined machinery: light, air, water and heat.

Balance and Shield impose a ninety-day lock before testing outside air. Ka-Cheryn makes the stores and their possible failure dates public. Festival drills and games become survival practice: children descend singing an evacuation order; bread, water and cloth move by relays. Sela puts Kailan to work rather than ceremonially welcoming him. Water- and air-listeners keep the Mountain's systems balanced. Under Sa-Orin, Kailan forces a kicking wheel while sound is used to reopen air to a sealed ward. Far hammering settles into song. Sa-Orin makes him release the wheel after the job is done. A Light boy with one usable arm ties rescue knots by touch. Sela later cleans and binds Kailan's bloodied palms, rebukes the legal explanation for his reported death, and begins sharing sleep and warmth with him.

The Mountain's ordinary competence persists in small figures: an old woman keeps an exact salt-fish account; Va-Maera returns unevenly from her injury, deaf and sometimes unmoored. Around day forty, an outside tasting plate comes in grey with ash, forcing old ash ritual into practical filter manufacture and exposing shortages. Around day fifty, a child is born. Va-Maera feels the infant's cry at its throat and alters or completes the blessing with the warning that no one hand should hold the whole.

The outside arrives only as failing instruments and voices. Faith's stations go silent one by one. Public transmissions from Marseille, a harbour, a tug and a hospital preserve local acts of rescue as their systems drown or fail. A woman reading rescued children's names loses the line; someone else takes over; then the carrier ends. Sarah crosses out the last French liaison signal, distinguishes it from the still-unowned camera, and admits that the network she thought she controlled had grown beyond her. Kailan can grieve seventeen named children but cannot make the millions into faces, and the disproportion frightens him. He tells Sela. She says that she loved him before Light elevated him and learned to hide it; she kisses him, and thereafter they seek each other deliberately. Ka-Cheryn's private list measures irreplaceable materials in years or decades, but gives no number to the gradual degradation of seed and blood.

Paul exacts separate, sourced accounts of the firing. Sarah refuses to let her own contributions remain unrecorded or privately held. The office-holders meet consequence differently: labour among the wounded, avoidance of machinery, repeated examination of the legal decision. Ka-Raedin asks Kailan to verify the fatal survey and refuses the invented mercy of an error. Paul insists that survivors belong under consequence, not cause. Va-Sheva takes one glass of solitude at a closed vent.

On day sixty-nine, Shield lays out the evidence concerning Jean-Charles. Details Kailan had told him privately during the flight appear in an enemy logistics annex before Kailan later repeated them for Paul's record. A restricted Horizon corridor and northern route narrow the possible hands. The camera, copied reels and transferred cases establish troubling acts without establishing one owner or order, so Va-Sheva removes those stones from the proof. Jean-Charles's preserved answers admit that he supplied details to the French liaison and knew it wanted a route, while telling himself each piece was too small. Shield confirms the operational consequence but leaves his purposes, orders, timing of knowledge and the camera's owner outside proof. When Jean-Charles asks for the other question, Va-Sheva refuses to extract its answer in a room that could coerce either truth or surrender. He remains watched, and dies holding the gate against the assault his disclosures helped enable.

On day seventy-nine, after the long ground movements have ebbed, a hidden mechanism opens an unmapped door. The fifth firing stroke armed it; only the later quiet lets its counterweight fall. Justice insists the wall is rock because his supposedly complete record says so. Shield secures the threshold. Paul, impatient with the competence that omitted the door, takes the recorder down the dark stair; Kailan follows; after hesitation, Justice follows too.

### People and wants retained

- Kailan wants to become useful inside the survival work, then to find a human scale on which guilt and grief can be borne without falsifying either.
- Sela wants the returned Kailan as a person rather than as Light's elevated figure, and eventually stops hiding that desire.
- Paul wants an exact, sourced record in which acts and consequences cannot be reassigned by remorse.
- Sarah wants failed contacts and her own enabling acts named accurately, while preventing one intelligence or one person from owning the whole.
- Va-Sheva wants proof bounded to what Shield can establish; she also wants one interval in which nobody asks anything of her.
- Ka-Cheryn and the working people want the Mountain's survival governed by visible counts and competent practice.
- Jean-Charles, in the preserved scene, seems to want the unasked personal question voiced, though what answer he wants to give remains uncertain.

### Residue before verification

The first cluster that returns is not the firing but the ninety-day sealed life: hands passing bread and cloth, listeners at stone, blood printed by a wheel, and festival skills stripped of play without losing their communal form. The last and sharpest image is the black stair behind a wall Justice's complete record calls rock, with Paul's recorder lamp descending first.

Other persistent carriers are the grey ash plate; a child restored to the singing line with a carved animal hidden in his robe; hammering in a suffocating ward turning into an answering song; Va-Maera's fingers on an infant's throat; seventeen copied names refusing to expand into millions of faces; the line about seed and blood coming up wronger; evidence stones removed rather than overclaimed; and Va-Sheva's palm against a vent where she is explicitly not listening.

The emotional high is shared between the newborn's welcome and Sela's decision to love Kailan without concealment. The low is the relay of outside voices becoming silence, made intimate by the reader of children's names and frighteningly abstract by the later population figure. The secret door supplies the strongest forward pull, not the deepest emotion.

I expect the next movement to descend the stair and reveal a deliberately excluded Keeper inheritance tied to the fifth strike and the aftermath rather than to an unfinished firing. I also expect the discovery to challenge Justice's trust in the completeness of the record.

Unresolved: what lies below; who owned the camera; what Jean-Charles's other question was and why he disclosed what he did; whether any outside communities survived; whether the ninety-day air test succeeds; and whether the Mountain can answer the long deterioration of seed, blood and irreplaceable stores. What feels certain without being proved is that the hidden chamber embodies intentional withholding by earlier Keepers, and that Jean-Charles's culpability and divided loyalty are both real even though the text refuses a total motive.

What I cannot retrieve cleanly: the exact store-duration figures, several office-holder names and duties, the exact order of the outside transmissions, most of the individual audit dates, and the wording or full old meaning of the newborn's chosen name. I may be falsely joining the single unseen camera, Roger's network, Jean-Charles's disclosures and the copied reels into one larger chain even though the audit deliberately keeps them separate. I am also unsure whether the chapter establishes Jean-Charles as dead before the ward work begins or withholds that temporal placement until the audit.
