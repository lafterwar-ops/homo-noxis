## Unaided reconstruction — sealed before reopening

This is the residue of one continuous cold read. It will not be revised after the chapter is reopened.

The chapter first left me with its ending: Sa-Kailan and Paul deciding not to turn suspicion into a completed story, and naming a new function, Shadow, for an enemy already inside the wall. Immediately behind that remained Va-Raedin draining the water after Sa-Kailan made trapped light escape, then insisting that discovery does not entitle continuation. Those two moments seemed to be the same lesson reaching across time.

In remembered order, Paul begins the fourth interview with two gifts: hollow geometric solids and a child's train of gears. Sa-Kailan playfully assigns some solids to the Mountain's orders. Paul uses the gears to suggest how the ancient Pendulum might tick or strike at immense intervals; Sa-Kailan says it stops for the instant when a Keeper of Light dies and is renewed. A question about a slow danger in Paul's world is opened but deferred. Sa-Kailan then answers the evolutionary films with an unsettling theory: refusal, especially sexual refusal, drives men to build civilisation; his own cleverness was built for a girl of Shield who did not look at him. Paul evades the dangerous subject and asks how long it took to learn the Mirror.

At nineteen, after four years, Sa-Kailan is taken through five training rooms by Va-Raedin. The preface is the old Balance-door trace: measured light entered and less left, proving retention but not use. First, a mirror makes an absent figure appear in the passage, then shows the back of Sa-Kailan's own head; he learns to question the path of the sight. In an interview interruption, Paul notices Sa-Kailan engraving a blank stone for some later use.

Second, altered lamps and shadows conceal a cord. Sa-Kailan pulls it and opens a panel before asking what it does. Va-Raedin calls the act pride; Sa-Kailan says it was his hand. Their argument turns tricks into doctrine: a sentence is itself a training room, and sight depends on where a person has been placed. Third, lenses make one seed-mark become a large wound while erasing others. The hand that places the lens, not the lens, grants importance. Water and a misused token return as examples of discovery and danger.

The contemporary interruption brings Urs's report and the blood result. A joke in English collapses into anger, then Paul explains in Noxius that Sa-Kailan's inherited code falls outside living humans and the known ancient human branches, nearest the old branches but distinct. Sarah's label is *homo absconditus*, not a scientific verdict. The result was repeated from another tube and checked against handlers. Sa-Kailan wants the whole Mountain on the black branch; Paul refuses to let one sample tell a whole history. They settle again on trace rather than story.

Fourth, Sa-Kailan must bend a beam onto a mark using liquids. Diluted salted water works; when that vessel is removed, he steals or reclaims salt grains from Balance's drainage channels, adding them until the beam turns enough. Light can measure a substance even when the changed water looks unchanged, and deeper knowledge belongs to a Veil who finds the Veil's Chamber. Fifth, a beam is trapped beneath water by a shallow angle. Salt worsens the trap; thinning the water eventually releases a hairline of light, then raises it to a gold plate. Sa-Kailan is exhilarated. Va-Raedin drains the trough, ending the proof, and makes him name the five shadows: reflection, shadow, focus, turning, and threshold return. Sa-Kailan withholds the expected answer about what a Veil learns and says instead that a Candidate learns where he has been made to stand. Va-Raedin laughs. Afterward Sa-Kailan understands that a path was hidden inside the lesson and is sent to Ka-Syphiron at First Light.

In the final interview, Sa-Kailan asks for a longer walk and insists that Paul's recording machine stay on. Paul explains that Blackwood resources and compartmentalised favours moved Sa-Kailan around the world without any one carrier knowing the route. Paul then answers what was behind the glass: CCTV guards, plus a second unwired recorder whose removable memory Roger Martinez secretly replaced. The genetic proof also makes Paul's promise fall due: he will be Sa-Kailan's man. Sa-Kailan feels the cord in his hand and wants to pull it; Paul asks for time to watch without warning the recorder's owner and has not told Sarah.

The blank stone was meant for an invented initiation: blood, covenant, and a lifelong name. Sa-Kailan postpones the rite because he had enlarged one mark into a wound before Paul supplied the rest of the room. The proposed word remains Shadow: Shield watches beyond the wall; Shadow watches within it. Its membership is only one, its suspicion only a question. Paul is placed to suspect; Sa-Kailan is placed to test and to decide which doors remain closed. Paul hints that entering a forbidden room as a young man cost him the rest of his life. Sa-Kailan accepts the lens and the burden of keeping watch.

### Wants remembered

- Sa-Kailan wants mechanisms to yield their doctrine, wants to be judged capable of deeper Light, and later wants Paul's allegiance and a disciplined way to pursue the hidden recorder.
- Va-Raedin wants curiosity to become restraint and positional awareness rather than clever manipulation alone.
- Paul wants to honour the bargain without letting proof overclaim, and to investigate the household trace without manufacturing a traitor.

### What remained

The strongest images were the cold hollow solids; a little flame standing upright; gears clicking toward an almost impossibly delayed gong; a hard line of light entering a dark room; the absent figure in a mirror; the cord inside shadow; a seed enlarged into a wound; salt scraped grain by grain; submerged light finally escaping as a thread onto gold; the mentor draining the discovery away; the black genetic branch; the needle on a blank stone; and Roger's removed memory.

The emotional high was the escaped thread of light followed by Va-Raedin's rare laugh; the conceptual high was the final refusal to manufacture an enemy. The low was the abrupt draining of the trough, a small grief that made the later caution feel earned. I expected the next movement to join Ka-Syphiron's hidden instruction to Paul and Sa-Kailan quietly testing Roger. I also expected the postponed Shadow rite to return only after evidence justifies it.

Unresolved: whom the second recording serves; whether Sarah knows; what happened in the forbidden room that cost Paul his life as it is now; what Ka-Syphiron will ask; how one man's genetic result relates to the Mountain; and whether Shadow will remain an ethic of restraint or harden into an order. It felt certain without proof that Va-Raedin recognised Veil potential in Sa-Kailan, that Paul would keep the bargain, and that Roger's act is a real trace but not yet evidence of betrayal.

The first thing I consciously recalled on closing the chapter was the final pairing of Shadow with the underwater-light lesson. The last opening detail I recovered was the set of hollow solids. I could not initially recover every liquid or the exact phrasing around the five named phenomena, and the Balance-door reference remained a carried trace whose earlier scene I did not reconstruct. The interview interruptions remained distinct, but their exact locations inside the five lessons were less stable than the lesson sequence.
