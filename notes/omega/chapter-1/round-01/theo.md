### Phase 2 — unaided reconstruction (fixed before reopening)

#### What happened, in the order I retained it

Paul Morgan begins a recorded interview with Kailan at Blackwood Estate. He
explains the recording machine through Kailan's idea of a tablet, identifies
himself as a trained but formerly doubtful outsider, and asks Kailan to begin
at the beginning. Kailan remembers being fifteen on the morning the Mountain
gave him his new name.

The Ascension takes place in an immense Hall organized around five Kahirs and
a long pendulum whose disk alternately shows God and the Beast. Thousands of
people are arranged outside its forbidden central path. A succession in Faith
comes first: the failing Ka-Mereth must surrender office while still alive,
and Va-Elun returns from a labyrinth with the Bowl of the Last Prayer. A tiny
metallic input makes the bright, waterlike substance move wrongly and produces
a thin answering note. Mereth accepts the answer; Va-Elun takes the cowl and
becomes Ka-Elun, while Mereth loses the `Ka` and is carried away alive.

Kailan then crosses the Hall for his own smaller naming. He notices Va-Sheva,
the older Shield initiate with whom he grew up, and wants her attention. The
Keeper of Light asks an unrehearsed question. Kailan answers that what is hidden
still has shape and may therefore be found; later, instead of the safe answer,
he says a Keeper learns what must remain dark. His blood chooses the Light
channel on a five-marked plate, and he becomes Sa-Kailan. Va-Raedin tells him he
answered poorly, then adds that he did not answer uninterestingly.

The expected dismissal does not happen. Shield bars the Mountain gates and all
five Keepers disappear into their office arches, leaving the Veils and the
congregation waiting. Prayer continues for hours. Five hard, evenly spaced
strokes rise through the floor. Kailan counts them and waits for a sixth. After
one more interval, a different broad pressure lifts the whole Hall; unattended
bowls sound and water rises. Ka-Elun emerges first and declares that God
answers. The other Keepers return, the gates open, and the people finally
leave.

Paul stops Kailan to recover the count, exact spacing, changed character of the
last movement, and the fact that such a rite follows a Keeper's raising. In a
separate English conversation, Paul and Mrs Blackwood compare Kailan's memory
with a trace she has kept since Reggane in 1960. They interpret the five marks
and following global swell as a readiness test for whatever lies beneath the
Mountain. Mrs Blackwood orders Paul to resume the interview without revealing
what they have understood and to win Kailan's trust while obtaining the trial
and exile.

#### Wants retained

Kailan wants Light, a name, approval from Va-Raedin, and—more privately—notice
from Va-Sheva. During the wait he wants the closed arches to answer and the
gates to open. Paul wants a precise testimony and, by the end, the technical
pattern hidden inside it. Mrs Blackwood wants confirmation of a decades-old
trace and then continued access to Kailan's unguarded account. The gathered
people want the ritual proof that permits them to regard the Mountain as safe.
Va-Maera seems to want Ka-Elun to return from the Faith arch, although I cannot
know more than her repeated attention and aborted step show.

#### Residue: images, objects, sounds, and sentences

The strongest carriers are the two-faced disk passing from Beast to God; the
Bowl's mirror-bright liquid recoiling from its edges; the almost disappointing
tick followed by the note inside the teeth; the enormous cowl moving from a
living old woman to a dust-marked younger one; Kailan's blood taking only the
northern channel; Va-Raedin's two correcting fingers; five identical blows and
the unbearable empty interval after them; the soundless swell felt in teeth,
eyes, and bone; bowls ringing without touch; and the gates opening only after
the answer. The lines that remain most distinctly are the law that no one hand
holds the whole, Kailan's claim that hidden things still have shape, the answer
that a Keeper learns what must remain dark, the name falling like a door, and
Mrs Blackwood's final demand that Paul gain Kailan's trust.

#### Emotional contour retained

The early unease gathers around Mereth being stripped of office and carried
away alive under the name of holiness. Kailan's naming briefly offers pride and
intimacy, but the closing-door simile and Va-Raedin's rebuke keep it from
becoming triumph. The sustained high-pressure passage is the congregation's
confinement, the counted blows, and especially the learned expectation of a
sixth. The broad swell releases the bodily suspense while increasing the
larger dread. The final room supplies the coldest turn: Kailan's sacred memory
has become usable evidence, and the people listening to him deliberately keep
that conversion secret.

#### What I expected next

I expected the next interview to move toward Kailan's trial and exile while he
remains unaware of the technical discovery his testimony enabled. I expected
the nature of the buried system to stay delayed, with Paul increasingly split
between humane listening and Blackwood's extraction. I also expected Va-Sheva,
Va-Raedin, the forbidden answer beneath the Temple, and Kailan's dangerous
habit of counting or asking shaped questions to return.

#### Unresolved or certain without proof

Unresolved: what is kept beneath the Temple; what physically makes the Bowl
answer; what generates the five sources and global return; why a change of
Keeper requires this test; what happened to Mereth; what Va-Sheva's wardens
noticed; what the Genesis gravure and hidden Oasis show; and how trial and exile
follow this initiation. I felt, without proof available to Kailan, that the
religious division of knowledge conceals an engineered system; that the five
blows correspond to the five offices or hands; that at least some Keepers know
more than the public rite says; that Va-Raedin's interest in Kailan is real but
not simple approval; and that Mrs Blackwood's apparent hospitality is part of
an asymmetrical interrogation.

#### First, last, and missing

The first thing remembered is Paul translating the recorder into Kailan's
world before Kailan names the day the Mountain named him. The last is Mrs
Blackwood telling Paul not to let Kailan know, then ordering him to gain the
man's trust. I did not retain the names of the Balance and Justice Veils, most
of the exact liturgical wording, the exact technical dimensions of the Hall,
or the precise order of every minor movement during the long prayer. I retain
the number five, the even intervals, and the different sixth-time answer much
more strongly than those details. These omissions are recorded rather than
repaired from the text.

### Text-to-residue comparison

**Alive and correctly retained.** The interview frame and its unequal trust;
the Pendulum’s God/Beast alternation; the Bowl’s sensory response; Mereth’s
living displacement; Kailan’s two unsafe answers, blood, name, and relation to
Raedin; Kailan’s attention to Sheva; the barred gates and absent Keepers; the
five equal blows, anticipated sixth, and different return; Reggane; the
readiness-test inference; and Blackwood’s concealment order all survived. The
chapter’s decisive question therefore arrives before its supplied answer: the
body learns the pattern in the Hall before Paul converts it into evidence.

**Vanished despite possible importance.** Sheva’s lowering of the collapsing
old man is the clearest demonstration of her competence and care, but it did
not enter the fixed reconstruction; what survived instead was Kailan looking
for her, her weapons, and the wardens’ alert. If her capability is intended to
be first-chapter residue rather than only her importance to Kailan, the current
carrier loses the contest to his romantic self-consciousness. Elun’s torn
pilgrim mantle and throat wounds also vanished, leaving the Bowl and cowl to
carry the succession; this loss seems permissible unless the cost of her road
must remain active immediately. The finger-struck fivefold response after the
young namings did not survive as an echo of the later five strokes.

**Falsely but plausibly inferred.** I expected a developing conflict in Paul
between humane listening and Blackwood’s extraction, but this chapter only
shows his precision, shock, compliance, and participation; the conflict is an
expectation, not a fact. I also linked each of the five seismic sources to one
of the five offices. The text makes that association available through
proximity and number, but neither Kailan nor the English speakers proves the
one-to-one mapping here.

**Understood early but productively.** The Bowl, pendulum, exact period, and
Paul’s mechanism questions make technological survival under sacred language
available well before the final room. That early suspicion is useful: it lets
the reader possess the right question while Kailan supplies observations
without authorial explanation. “Let no one hand hold the whole” reads as
controlled distribution of knowledge before any speaker confirms that
reading. The chapter should preserve that bounded inference.

**Explained after inference.** Paul’s post-Hall questions repeat the count,
interval, changed return, and recurrence that the narrative has made unusually
memorable. Blackwood then repeats the pattern again before naming the source
schedule and whole-Earth mode. The duplication has an artistic return—first it
legalizes the data as Kailan’s testimony, then matches it to Blackwood’s
recoverable archive—but the second recap is the one place where explanatory
pressure approaches the point of overtaking discovery. The unique new facts
(the 1960 trace, Reggane, schedule, planetary coupling, and concealed agenda)
remain necessary residue.

**Productively unresolved.** The thing beneath the Temple; the mechanisms of
the Bowl, sources, return, and Chains; Elun’s road; Mereth’s fate; the Genesis
gravure and Oasis; the wardens’ message to Sheva; and the reasons for Kailan’s
trial and exile are all bounded unknowns. The reader knows what to ask and
which teller currently lacks or withholds the answer. Kailan’s “Eventually”
keeps the gravure’s delay explicit rather than accidental.

**Confusing without artistic return.** None rose above the threshold on this
pass. The title rite, the youths’ side rite, and the later test occupy a long
ceremonial day, but the changing gates, arches, light, hunger, and prayer keep
the sequence recoverable. The identities of minor Balance and Justice figures
can disappear without damaging the live question.
