\typesetchapter{Chapter 24}{Build}

We took up the next of the sheets in the beam and read on without stopping to speak, and the measure went on as it had begun, numbered and cadenced and cut to be chanted, which is the thing I have never got past: that he set the worst of it down in the versicles my people use for morning prayer.\par

\ornament

\begin{scripture}
We found it in the third year out, and I knew it well before my engineers; \\
for I had left off hunting all the ground that men desire, and turned my search \\
to the empty ground that no man ever goes near, and there, at last, the earth gave in. \\
It offered nothing, and its nothing was the first and best of all of its defences: \\
\medskip

no green in it, no game, no road, no reason, just a shoulder of old naked stone, \\
and a great and killing width of distance laid on every side of it, and no more. \\
It lay far out inside a waste that murders any man by breadth alone, \\
and gives up nothing to the warm folk wanting south, who never turn their heads to it. \\
\medskip

Then, underneath the nothing, season upon season, my patient water-men \\
laid their slender rods against the ancient stone, and one by one the stone gave up \\
its buried secrets, and the first was water: so much water that my weeping chief \\
sat himself down upon the rock, and put his hard face down into his hands, and made no sound. \\
\medskip

More water than a siege could ever drink, enough to hold a whole sealed people; \\
and it was years before I let myself once say aloud for what the water waited. \\
And heat beneath it, the old heat of the deep, and shallow enough to reach and hold, \\
a patient fire that never wants for feeding, so the sealed folk should never once be cold. \\
\medskip

And ways: there were faults, and voids, and always the sure promise of yet more to come, \\
of more that could be cut and made, until a whole new world hung threaded down the rock. \\
Now understand the joy of it, I ask you, before you ever hear the rest of it; \\
for that one year was, past any argument, the very best year of my whole life. \\
\medskip

Three years upon a road that killed my people, and that told me hourly I was mad, \\
and at the far end of it I had the thing, and I was glad past all I dared to want. \\
We laughed aloud like boys down in the deep cool shade, and every rod we drove said yes; \\
and a man to whom the world lays out its tools all ready to his hand feels \emph{chosen}. \\
\medskip

And nothing in the whole of my long life, from that far day to this, has equalled it. \\
I saw the other thing as well, that year, deep at the bottom of a shaft, by lamplight. \\
The deep fire never dies; and water flashed upon it turns at once to driving steam; \\
and steam sent down a throat that runs clear through the deep roots of a world becomes a hand, \\
\medskip

a flat hand laid upon that turning world's own beating heart, to press it as I choose. \\
My engineer, and knowing nothing of my hidden mind, told me what force he could raise; \\
and that, though neither of us knew it then, was the beginning of the whole of it. \\
The ground had offered up my people's living, and it offered, in the same grey stone, \\
\medskip

the means to do to all the warm wide earth the very thing the earth had done to mine, \\
and worse than it, and all at once, and hidden well from every eye that might have seen. \\
And I told myself, in that same bright year, and told myself that I believed it too, \\
that the hand I now could lay upon the world would only ever be a thing of defence. \\
\end{scripture}

\begin{scripture}
Do not take the building for a wonder; the ones who came after me have made it \\
out to be the very masonry of God, and that is the first and largest lie. \\
It was labour, and it was mine alone: the most enormous labour ever raised, \\
and gathered into one place, done by living people, tens of thousands, one by one, \\
\medskip

and brought down that long and killing road across the better portion of my life. \\
It cost; and I paid out the cost of it in the one coin such things are ever bought with, \\
which is the lives of other living people; and I kept the account; the account is long. \\
We learned the work of it by failing at the work; the first great press we ever built, \\
\medskip

built only to prove the deep hand answered, in the proving of it killed its builders. \\
They died for what no man yet living knew, which is what water does, flashed into steam \\
inside a shut and airless place; and I stood still there at the gallery's mouth, \\
and watched them carry out the broken rest, and kept my own face still, and said no word. \\
\medskip

I did not halt the work; instead of halting it I merely went and fixed the drawings: \\
a stair we cut too near a hidden fault took off a hundred men in one short hour, \\
a wall let go one night, and a whole shift was drowned in it, and set us back ten years. \\
We never learned the rate to meter water down until enough men died of it; \\
\medskip

and I did not wait upon their dying; every increment was bought, and I stood firm. \\
You will want a single number from me now, and I have one, for I kept the count, \\
and I kept the whole of it true, the one plain virtue I can claim with no lie in it. \\
More than forty thousand souls came down that road alive while I still drew my breath, \\
\medskip

and fewer than a third of them would ever climb it up into the day again; \\
the difference between those numbers, if you do it, is itself a kind of death. \\
Some twenty-seven thousand of them stayed forever in the rock that they had cut, \\
in flood, and fall, and steam, and in the slow grind of a land that wanted nothing living. \\
\medskip

I built the greatest place of refuge ever built, a thing to keep my people whole, \\
and I built the whole of it upon a floor made out of their own bones, and did not slow. \\
When a chief came to me white in the face with word of yet another hundred lost, \\
I asked him first, before all else, whether the work that they had died upon had held. \\
\medskip

I watched the very hour he ceased to love me, and I let him cease, and said no word, \\
and turned my eyes back down to the cold drawings, and the man went from me, and I stayed. \\
Then I did the thing that let them do such work, to such an end, and never rise: \\
I saw to it, with care, that not a one of them could ever wholly understand. \\
\medskip

I divided up the knowledge of the work, and gave no team of them more than one piece: \\
the water-men knew water, and believed they sank a common well against a siege. \\
The light-men knew their bending glassy surfaces, and thought they brought the sun downstairs; \\
and those who cut the deep hot throat believed the thing was drainage, and was theirs alone. \\
\medskip

The men who raised the falling gates believed they raised them up against far armies; \\
and not one team among a hundred teams was ever let to hold two pieces at once. \\
Only my own few knew the well, the sun-ways, the deep drainage, and the gates were one, \\
one single engine, and the engine was a weapon aimed clean through the core of Earth. \\
\medskip

I did not once divide it up against an enemy outside my sealing walls; \\
I divided it against the very hands within, so that no man of them, alone, \\
should ever hold enough of it at once to look upon the thing that he had made, \\
and, understanding it entire, set it down, and turn from it, and quietly refuse. \\
\end{scripture}

Paul stopped there, and set the sheet down, and for a moment he could not speak for the shape of the thing. ``He is chanting it,'' he said at last. ``He put this in the office. Do you understand what I am holding? The founding law of the whole Mountain --- that no one hand may hold the whole --- was a labour-management technique, made into a religion so the division would outlive its reason, and he has set the confession of it to the versicles they chant on feast days. There is no philosophy in it anywhere.'' And I said, because it was mine to say and no one else's, ``It is worse than that, Paul. The same wall that stops the workers seeing the whole stops any one Keeper ever firing it alone. He built the division to hide the weapon from the builders, and it turned out to be the only thing that ever kept the weapon safe.''\par

\begin{scripture}
Five great works there were, and so five teams, and over each of them I set a head; \\
and the whole lay parcelled out among the five, and never gathered, and never spoken. \\
I could see, already, even then, that I would die, and that the thing must run \\
long after me, and longer than a name is ever kept; and so, before my end, \\
\medskip

I did the thing that turns a mere day's work into a people that outlasts its founder: \\
I ceased to call the teams by teams at all, and I began to make of them the lines. \\
The team of water and of stone, of heat and measure, keeping the world's own accounts, \\
I taught to hand itself on down the years from master unto child in guarded portions, \\
\medskip

with secret signs, and pride, and jealous keeping, till the work that this one team performed \\
had hardened into \emph{Balance}: a proud name, a blood, a calling, none knowing whence it came. \\
And those who listened for the deep return, and knew the timing of the buried strokes, \\
I made the keepers of the holy hour, and taught them God Himself spoke in the stone: \emph{Faith}. \\
\medskip

The light-men, they who set the shot and hold the aim, I made the keepers of a mystery, \\
a hidden mystery of alignment, and I gave that mystery the name of \emph{Light}. \\
And those who held my books and my permissions, who alone might say a thing may pass, \\
and kept the order of each single act that cannot be recalled, I named them \emph{Justice}. \\
\medskip

And the men of the gates and the containment, of secrecy and, at need, of violence, \\
I named the men of \emph{Shield}; and it was done; they never felt the doing; kept their silence. \\
I gave to them no new thing in the world to do; I took the doing each had always had, \\
and wrapped it round about in inheritance, and pride, and mystery, and a little dread. \\
\medskip

For a man will do one thing unchanged until the very ending of the ages, \\
if only you can bring him to believe the narrow task he does is his own self. \\
And to each line I gave a discipline, to hide its one true labour down inside it; \\
and the discipline grew a real knowledge, freely taught, deep, and every word of it true. \\
\medskip

I laid the true knowledge down close beside the secret one, so that a clever child, \\
a gifted child of that same line, might spend a brilliant life upon the cover of it, \\
and yet not once fall through the bright false floor of it to the true dark underneath. \\
To Balance I gave honest measure, so the finest builder of his age might live \\
\medskip

and die inside his craft, and never see the weapon lying plainly on the page. \\
To Faith I gave the true deep art of stone, of rhythm and resonance and return, \\
and I called the whole of it a prayer, so the best ear born in a hundred years \\
would spend itself upon the hearing of God, and never learn it heard a weapon breathe. \\
\medskip

To Light I gave the bending of a wave down to a single burning point of fire, \\
and holy, holy was the glinting sheath of it; and to my Justice I gave the record, \\
the sequence, and the weighing of whether a thing may lawfully proceed at all; \\
and folded deep in Justice, out of sight, I hid the very leave a firing needs. \\
\medskip

Truths, the whole of it, and every single separate piece of it a naked truth; \\
and I set the truths in a ring around a hole, and taught them that the hole was God. \\
And at the very lip of it I taught the kneeling soul a special holy dread, \\
which I had built into the teaching for that one sole purpose, and no other purpose: \\
\medskip

so that a curious mind would flinch back hard from the one question, at the very hour \\
it ripened and at last grew fit to ask it; and I taught them well to flinch, and taught them true. \\
Not one of them, in all the long line of them, has ever known that he was doing it; \\
and of all the things I ever made and hid inside this Mountain, that is my proudest. \\
\end{scripture}

\begin{scripture}
Then I did the thing that beats beneath the whole of all the rest that you have read; \\
and I did it for a reason that was sound, and the soundness of it is the worst of it. \\
The machine I made had one deep flaw, and it was the one flaw of all my kind: \\
that it must be kept alive by living persons; and a person changes, turns to dust. \\
\medskip

Each generation the five hands would fail, and new and untried hands would rise to it; \\
and a Keeper of them might know only the mere face of the rite that hid the engine, \\
and hold his holy office forty years, and never know if, at the hour of need, \\
he had it in him still to do the one hard thing that he was truly made to do. \\
\medskip

So call five such uncertain hands a throne, and call the throne of them a waiting weapon; \\
give them their forty quiet years of it; and they are five afraid and praying priests, \\
who have long forgotten they were engineers, above an engine none of them has run. \\
And so I built a test, and hid the test itself inside a deep devotion, made it law. \\
\medskip

At each succession, when a Keeper died and went to ground, and a new Keeper rose, \\
the people saw the five of them draw close, in secret and apart, to do a thing \\
that I had dressed for all of them as prayer, and that they came to call the covenant. \\
But underneath the words of the whole prayer there was a firing: one small firing, \\
\medskip

large enough to prove the engine answered still whenever it was firmly told, \\
and small enough to leave the whole wide world above unhurt, and never know its passing. \\
A few drops of clear water metered down upon the deep fire, and done five times, \\
and then the long slow wait that is its due; the engine knows one measure, and no other, \\
\medskip

and a single stroke struck off its proper measure proves to any listener nothing. \\
And the whole struck world, struck five even times inside its own slow interval, \\
would answer back to us one long wait later, one wait behind the falling of the fifth; \\
and that deep returning note would climb the track of the deep stone to the waiting ear, \\
\medskip

and say, to the one ear that knew to hear, that the old covenant of us still held. \\
I made it law, and made it secret too, and made it holy, all three things at once, \\
so that frightened, faithful folk would keep the whole of it forever, perfectly, \\
and never let it slip beyond the last of them who still could say what the strokes were for. \\
\medskip

I called the whole of it the readiness, and told them: God confirms His chosen Keeper, \\
and told them it did nothing more than that; and it was the one hour, in secret truth, \\
in which my patient waiting engine reached down deep and quietly confirmed itself. \\
The first time, in my own lifetime, to prove the thing, I stood inside the Hall \\
\medskip

that I had built to hold and shape that toll, and heard the whole Earth give the shape back: \\
five even, heavy blows, and a long soft roll of sound behind them, trailing after, \\
like a struck world turning over once, and heavily, and slowly, in its sleep. \\
It was the most beautiful and worst thing I have ever made, and it was wholly mine; \\
\medskip

and I knew, in that one hour, that I had given my cold machine a beating heart, \\
one single beat to every generation, sounding on beneath the covering sand \\
for as long as ever the Mountain kept its place; and I knew also, standing there, \\
that some far ear, one day, fine enough to hear, would come to find the body that it beat in. \\
\end{scripture}

Here Sarah took the sheet from Paul's hands, read that passage again to herself, and put both her hands flat on the cold stone of the machine, and was quiet for a long time. And when she spoke she did not speak to us. ``A few drops of water,'' she said. ``Metered onto the deep fire. Five even blows and a long soft roll. He is describing the thing I heard at Reggane when I was twenty-seven. The exact signature I chased across sixty-six years and two continents and everything I ever destroyed. It was a succession. Somewhere under that desert, in the year of the first French bomb, one of his offices buried a Keeper and raised a new one and proved the covenant held, and I was sitting forty kilometres away with a drum-paper on my knees, and I thought I had discovered something. I had heard his heartbeat.'' She took her hands off the stone. ``He knew one day someone with an ear would hear it and come. He built that risk in on purpose, and I am the ear he knew would come.''\par

\begin{scripture}
The machine and all the offices of it were, in the end, the easy part of it; \\
the hard part was the thing that no late people ever after could themselves invent, \\
being, as they are, the very offspring of it. A machine in trained and living hands \\
will last a few lives only, and no more; an age on from you, the trained hands have \\
\medskip

forgotten what the training was once for, and the whole thing is gone out of the world. \\
A people that has lost the why of an exact and needful task will, in the end, \\
begin at last to do it inexactly; and then the machine is dead, and all \\
that I had spent my whole life long upon is finished, and is past, and comes to nothing. \\
\medskip

I had not paid my father's ended world, and forty thousand dead men in the rock, \\
to raise a thing that ran six lives and failed; and so I set myself to stop the clock. \\
There is one method for it, only one: I built a people to be all the keeping, \\
I built the people's very selves to be the keeping of it; built them so they could not stop. \\
\medskip

I took children to me. I have said it once; I say it here again, and let it stand: \\
some were our own, the carefully kept seed, and some I bought, and some I simply gathered \\
up off the failing outer land. And then I raised them, every one, inside a lie: \\
that the Mountain always was, just where I built it; that the offices themselves were holy, \\
\medskip

there where I had merely written down a duty and a roster of plain men; \\
that the engine was a living god, just there where I, with my own hands, had made a weapon. \\
I wrote for them a past, and aged it well, so that the newest child, where he might turn, \\
would find yet more sure evidence that all of it was old past any counting, \\
\medskip

and would never doubt his own few days were a late leaf on a tree past reckoning. \\
I set among the first of them some actors who knew the entire truth of it, \\
and who agreed, for love, or fear, or promise, to spend their whole lives in pretending faith; \\
so that the second of the generations, watching the first, believed without pretending, \\
\medskip

and came to a true faith; and by the third, no least pretence was left alive at all, \\
and they knew it, wholly knew it, the same easy way that any child knows the sky. \\
I carved the Genesis myself, alone, in the very worst nights of the long build; \\
the aim of it was plain, that the whole outside must be made a horror to them all, \\
\medskip

and it came to me more easily by far than I could wish; so that no child of mine \\
should ever, ever want the world outside, I took the one true thing, and turned it once, \\
turned it by a single quarter only, which is the one turn a good lie ever needs. \\
The one true thing had been a murder; and I kept the murder, for a real thing anchors \\
\medskip

every lie; and I set the murder back in the very first and oldest of the ages, \\
and I made the murdered man a brother, standing loved and envied in a father's place; \\
and I gave the killer's punished line the open land itself for its inheritance, \\
cursed, and very many, hating evermore the cool green shade they never come to find. \\
\medskip

And I gave to them a woman also in it: taken as a spoil when the man fled, \\
and blameless as a stolen lamp is blameless, a thing done to us and not by her. \\
So I said in it, and so I cut the stone. But what I filed away, and kept within, \\
and never once let come at all near the marble, was the true thing that lay beneath her: \\
\medskip

that the knife had gone in by a living hand that I had loved, and that I chose, \\
from that same hour and ever afterward, to never ask that hand what it had known. \\
A people can be taught with ease to dread a murderer that is born among them; \\
and no people yet was ever taught to dread a wound a man brought half upon himself; \\
\medskip

and so, in the carving, I let that part go. I let her stand there in the stone \\
as a pure and simple loss, and drowned the question of her choosing in the grave, \\
the very grave my father lay in, in the very same cold ground; and I have watched \\
the Keepers read it out to trembling boys as the whole world's word; done in one evening. \\
\end{scripture}

Paul had to stop reading here, and it was Va-Sheva who took the sheet from him, Va-Sheva of Shield, and she read the passage about the children in the flat Shield voice, and when she had finished she said only, ``I was one of those children. So were you,'' to me, ``and so was every soul in this Mountain, back and back, a hundred thousand years of children taught that a cage was a sky. And the worst of it is the part he will not let himself lie about even now: that the first generation may have loved them. My mother loved me. She taught me the sky was above me and the cage was the world, and she was wrong, and she had been taught it too, and she loved me while she did it. You cannot even hate it cleanly. He built a prison so loving that the guards and the prisoners are the same people, and every one of them was somebody's beloved child.''\par

\ornament

We were quiet a long time after that, in the cold, and I could not look at Va-Sheva, because she had said \emph{so were you}, and it was true. The child he had built, the one raised to believe the cage was a sky, was me, exactly me, Sa-Kailan of Light, who had knelt around the forbidden centre and felt the holy dread at its edge and called it God. And the strangest thing is that it did not make me feel like a victim. It made me feel that the thing I had been raised inside had a shape, and an outside, and a maker, and could therefore be stood apart from and looked at. The Mountain had felt like a sky. It was a design, and a design can be refused. He had left, at the very bottom of it, the one door through which the design could be seen; and I was standing in it; and I understood that the reading would carry me past forgiving him and past hating him, into deciding what a man does when he has been handed the truth about a machine that only he, of all the people alive, has both the eyes to see and the standing to stop.\par

Some of them went away from there: sworn hard, and scattered wide across the land, and watched; \\
and some remained, the very first to labour in the sealed new world, and they were told \\

The account of that is long; I kept it too, as I kept all the rest; and it remains \\

Believe of me this much, and only this: that I could not require the doing of it \\

\medskip

And the children born from that day down below did not so much as know a world \\

\medskip

and they, in their own turn, would teach their children so. And I stood there among them, \\
an old and finished man who had done the thing, and I watched the great lie draw its first breath; \\
and it was good. God, God forgive me, it was the most beautiful thing I ever saw: \\
a whole new world, on the first morning of its life, believing it had always been. \\
\medskip

And I knew that it would hold; and knew that not one soul of them would ever thank me, \\
and that not one of them would ever know that I had once been real, and walked among them. \\
I am closing up the record now; I have at last said the whole of all of it; \\
and I set it here beneath the very god that I invented, in the one hand \\
\medskip

that I trusted, for the one reader I could not build my whole system to prevent. \\
So judge me now: you are the first in a whole age with the clear eyes at last to do it, \\

to be judged, at the end of all of it, by any living soul that still believed. \\
\medskip

Do now the one hard thing that I, myself, could never do: look on the whole of it, \\
and say aloud, at last, and to the world, the one plain thing that it was ever for. \\
I was Noxius Zero: I was the first of all of us, the maker of you all, \\
and I was a man whose father died by the outside, betrayed through the woman that he loved. \\
\medskip

I chose certainty; and given my century, and all my evidence, and all my dead, \\

then no one ever set that same choice in front of you, as it was set in mine, \\
and you should go a great deal slower than you mean to go, at the end, with your cold verdict. \\

Paul turned the last sheet, and there were no more, and the equinox beam had gone off the wall entirely while we read, and the Archive was lit only by our own lamps now, the borrowed sun withdrawn, the machine turning on in the dark as it had turned for an eternity and would turn whether we lived or died.\par

I heard myself ask them, into all of that, whether they remembered the painting in the corridor.\par

Sarah said its name as though the name settled it. ``The Basquiat. \textit{Pez Dispenser}, nineteen eighty-four. Out on loan most years; it was home the season you were with us.'' Then she named a sum of money, and stopped.\par

``I have thought of nothing else this last hour,'' I said. ``The crowned animal by the turn of the stair --- the red eye, the jaws too large for it, a dragon drawn by someone who had forgotten what a dragon was, and the crown afloat a little above its head, joined to nothing. A king and a monster and a joke in the one body. Your painter never heard the Mountain named, and he has made the truest likeness of it that anyone ever set down: Zero has just told us that our God and our Beast were one man's two hands, and that we split him and hung the halves at the two ends of a wire and forgot they had ever shared a body, and the painting puts them back. That is Zero. That is the Mountain. And one thing more, that I kept to myself.''\par

And I found that I was weeping, which I had not done since a cell in a country I could not find, and that I could not have said whether I wept for the drowned workers, or for the lied-to children, or for Ka-Elun under her roof, or for the whole ended world above us, or for a woman named Ithe, an eternity dead, who was used by an enemy and accused by the man who loved her and asked, by neither of them, what she had truly known, or for myself, who had read my own possible life to the end of it in another man's hand and found that the only difference between us was a fact-checker and a machine and one night's kindness in the desert. I think I wept for all of it. I think, by then, it was all one thing.\par
