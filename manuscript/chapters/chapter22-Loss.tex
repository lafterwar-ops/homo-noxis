\typesetchapter{Chapter 22}{Loss}

We read him together, in the Archive, in the equinox light, taking the sheets in turn: Paul, who could hear a voice in a text the way I could see a hand in a stone; Sarah, who had hunted this man across sixty-six years without knowing his name; and I, who was about to learn that I had been reading my own possible life all along and calling it another man's confession.\par

I will set his account down as he made it, because the making is half of what it says. He did not write a life. He built one. It is cut into measures and numbered, and it moves in the long cadence of the scripture my people have chanted every morning for eleven thousand years --- the unrhymed, rolling verse of the dawn office --- because the man who wrote that scripture could not, at the last, say even this in any other voice. He had a god's habit, and he kept it almost to the end. Where I break in, I will say so. The rest is his.\par

What follows is Zero's.\par

\ornament

\begin{scripture}
\textbf{i}\\
Let it be written by the one of whom no book that comes after will carry the name.\\
This was not given to me. This I did.\\
A mind stood in this place, and it stood well.\\
\medskip
\textbf{ii}\\
We were the summit of the world, and we held the warm lands and the cold.\\
We knew the lights of heaven, and we had named them every one, and set the names in the songs we carried.\\
Our dead went down into the ground with the things that they had loved.\\
Our tools were perfect, and older than any man's memory of their making.\\
We did not think ourselves a wonder. No mountain wonders at its own stone.\\
\medskip
\textbf{iii}\\
Afterward I drew us stooped and clubbed, brutes in the scripture I would make; for the victors paint in that manner the people whose place they have taken.\\
Know what we were. A hall in winter; lamps we had taught to burn without smoke; old men reciting the eleven fathers, and never a name let fall between them.\\
Checked, and checked again, memory against memory, that no one failing mind could lie to us.\\
We wrote down little. We kept the world in persons. And there, in that, the flaw stood waiting.\\
A library of loyal skulls is loyal only while the skulls remain. We had built our whole forever out of what can rise, and walk, and be carried away.\\
\medskip
\textbf{iv}\\
We were losing, and I was the first to say it, being cursed to see the sum of things.\\
No war took us. Hear that plainly: no blade, no siege, no fall of kings.\\
It came as \emph{preference}. It came as daughters going down at dusk to trade, and staying to listen, and at the last with nothing left in them that they knew how to ask for.\\
They said the fires below were warmer. They did not mean the fires. They meant a place where a woman was not required to be one finished thing. And they went.\\
Nobody drove them. Nobody stole them. No blade was drawn, and no wall was ever manned. They walked downhill, and they were glad, and they carried a quarter of our land away in their going.\\
\end{scripture}

\ornament

Sarah laid the sheet down here. ``He is describing demographic collapse by assimilation,'' she said, ``and he is describing it correctly, and it is the one ending no wall and no weapon has ever stopped. My whole century argued it and never said it as cleanly as a man a hundred thousand years dead.'' Paul said, ``Mark where he is standing, though. He is not raging yet. He is a man watching his people fade and refusing, so far, to reach for the blade. Keep that. We are going to need it at the end.'' And I said nothing, because I had begun, without permission, to be afraid of this man, and I did not yet know why.\par

\ornament

\begin{scripture}
\textbf{v}\\
My father saw it before I did, and his answer was the older answer: that a people does not live by being loved. Break them, he said, and have done.\\
He held the metal and the men, the labour and the hoarded gold. He had been afraid longer than I, and a fear that is kept long enough goes cold.\\
Set this above everything else I tell you, for the later scripture cut it away: \emph{I was the one who said no.} I said that a slaughter would be our stain.\\
I said: win in that manner, and what survives will not be worth the keeping whole. We argued the years away across one table. I won the argument. It did no good at all.\\
\medskip
\textbf{vi}\\
Her name was Ithe. I set it here against my own long rule, and plainly: a record that leaves out the one true thing is only the same lie, built again.\\
She was of my rank, my people, my kind, finished and raised as I was raised, the most complete soul I have known; mine for a while, and never once praised by me.\\
The fault was mine, and I will hold it. She told me what my love had been for: a beautiful and perfect room that had been built without a door.\\
I made her be one finished thing, because her wholeness was my own, and I could endure no crack running through it. So she went out. She went out alone.\\
She went where she was let be more than one thing; and I let her go with the cold grace of a wronged man who means his grief to be seen.\\
\medskip
\textbf{vii}\\
That was the door. My father had seen it sooner, and more coarsely, being the more afraid: a proud house can be neither bought nor frightened; it is entered where its love was laid down.\\
They reached her in her unhappiness. Bought, or frightened, or told some tale kind enough to be believed --- I do not know. \emph{The not-knowing is the whole of the betrayal.}\\
They sent her back to me wearing peace, and I opened every door I had. A man handed back the thing he has mourned does not weigh the gift; he is glad.\\
I told her small things: where he rode, the passes he trusted, the hour he would be lightly guarded and abroad. I did not think them secrets, then.\\
That is the whole genius of the method: you never do. You feel yourself approved, watching the beloved face take it in. You believe that you are being loved.\\
\medskip
\textbf{viii}\\
They killed my father with what she carried, in a pass he had ridden until it was home to him, lightly guarded because I had said he might be, by men who could have reached him no other way.\\
The one whose war I had refused was killed through the very door he had watched all his life and shown to me. I stood over his body, and I said to him: \emph{You were right.}\\
And here is the thing no scripture holds, that no other man has ever been told: under the grief, within the grief, there was \emph{relief}, and it was mine.\\
I had not lost the argument. It had been taken from me in a single night. The enemy had proved my father's case and set me free; I could cease, at last, to be the one who was right.\\
Do you know what a rest it is to be permitted, finally, to hate? I raised a mountain on the strength of that one hour's rest. It holds the weight.\\
\medskip
\textbf{ix}\\
I could have learned the truth of her. Mark this place: it is the door where I stopped being a man with a grief and became the founder of a law.\\
His people were very good at the asking. They came to me that morning with all the rest of it, and I had in my hands the means to know what I have done nothing since but guess:\\
whether she knew what she was carrying; whether she was used, and never told; whether she wept in the choosing of it, or wept only when the thing was done and cold.\\
\emph{I had the means. I did not ask.}\\
And not from mercy; hear the reason. If she had carried it and never known --- if love had been the road they walked her down, and she a lamp they bore through the dark --- then the fault was an accident, my own loose mouth, a thing that carried no name beyond my own; and both might go on living, the warm ones and my own people together, the same.\\
And then I should have had to go back to the table and argue it all again, the long grind of it, early and late, with no father left to argue against and no permission left to hate.\\
So I did not ask. I let her be guilty. Everything I looked at told me she was. And there was work in front of me that would not wait, and I let her go.\\
You will make more of that than I did. You have the leisure for it. I had a people to bury.\\
\end{scripture}

\ornament

Sarah put the sheet down at that and sat with it, and I saw her go somewhere far off, into a cellar, among a dead father's papers. ``There it is,'' she said. ``The unasked question. My father died with a question in his mouth he would not ask me, and let me have the money instead. And I have declined my own questions, more than once, in front of men whose answers would have cost me the thing I had decided to do.'' She did not pick the sheet back up at once. ``He is describing my family, boy, and not only his. He is describing everyone's.''\par

\ornament

\begin{scripture}
\textbf{x}\\
Then I built the law, before ever I built the Mountain. One betrayal that I would not read, whose depth I refused to take the measure of, I made into the nature of a whole breed.\\
I took a wound with a date upon it, done by named hands, in a named pass, and I grew it to the size of a species and the length of all of time. It will last.\\
My father had wanted only a war, and a war might have been forgiven me; a war ends. I gave him better than his war: an engine to wage it without end,\\
against every warm man ever to be born, the most of whom will do me no harm, and not one of whom will ever know my name --- and the whole of it laid upon a thing that one frightened woman may not have known she did.\\
I never learned. I built the ending of the world upon an untried charge, and I called the charge guilt.\\
She may have loved me to the last, and been spent like a coin she had no hand in the striking of. She may have hated me, and chosen, and walked every step of it wide awake.\\
She is to me this hour exactly as unknown as she was upon that dawn. I arranged that she should stay so. \emph{I did not ask.} And she is gone.\\
\end{scripture}

\ornament

Paul had been very still. ``This is the most dangerous document I have ever held,'' he said, ``and I once held a plan to end the world. It is dangerous because it is nearly true. He had a real enemy. His father was really murdered. A jilted boy who invented a grievance would be safe; we could dismiss it. He suffered a genuine, specific, evil act --- and took it, and made it permanent, and refused to check the one fact that might have kept it small.'' And Sarah, who knew more than any of us about what it is to convert a private certainty into an institution, said only, ``Yes. I wrote the shorter version. Keep reading. I want to know whether he ever asks it. I never did.''\par

\ornament

I did not, in the room, say what I was thinking, which was that I had begun to write this same document myself, not long before, in the desert, the night my own council cast me out. I had stood with my whole life taken from me and started, already, to compose the doctrine of my grievance. I had been stopped by nothing grander than a fact-checker with a machine, who made me say the small true things one at a time until the large false one had no room left to grow. I had been one refused question away from the man on the sheet in Paul's hands.\par

And I was afraid, in the equinox light, because I had my own Ithe, and I had her in the room. Va-Sheva had chosen a man of the outside who bought sweets for no reason --- and I knew now, we all knew now, that the sweets and the charm and the warmth had begun as an operation, that Jean-Charles had been sent into a proud house exactly as Ithe had been sent into mine. The mirror went past resemblance. It was the same event, a hundred thousand years apart. And I felt the low door open in me, the one Zero had walked through, the one that says: \emph{there, you see, it is their nature; they reach us through our loves; she is Ithe and he is the enemy's instrument and it is proven now, build the wall.}\par

\ornament

\begin{scripture}
\textbf{xi}\\
He was dead, and everything he had held came down to me: the metal and the men, the knowledge and the massed estate of the greatest of all our houses.\\
And I, who had refused his war, now possessed the whole world with which he would have waged it. I raised no army; armies lose. His answer had been a war. Mine would be a myth.\\
I gathered to me those who knew the stone, and those who knew the hidden springs, and those who knew the lights, and those who knew the making of hard things;\\
and soldiers also, for a work so dear must have a wall of men about its mouth. I emptied out my father's house, and I turned the whole of it south.\\
\medskip
\textbf{xii}\\
And I took children. Set that down. It is the seed of the worst thing.\\
Some were ours, and some I bought, and some I gathered up off the failing edge of everything.\\
A place that must outlast its own understanding has to be peopled young, with those who can be told that a thing was always so, and hold their tongues.\\
I said that they were being saved. They were being saved. They were also being made the first of a new kind of people, raised upon a past that I had laid down for them, to tend a thing they must never grasp and to kneel before a god not yet invented.\\
I watched them sleeping in the carts, among the metal. And no one prevented me.\\
\medskip
\textbf{xiii}\\
The road south took the better part of a year. I lost them to the heat, to the sand, to the slow leaking-away by which a great column dies in a dead country.\\
Each loss I entered in a reckoning. I did not mourn. We passed the green, and we passed the last water that any man of mine had ever seen,\\
and the last marks of any people, and we went on into a waste that lay beyond all wanting of anything alive, and said as much of itself every hour of the day.\\
My engineers began to tell me, in the careful manner of men speaking to a lord they have begun to fear, that there was no such place as the one I sought.\\
I hunted a thing that did not exist. I would not turn. I had gone blind to any road that bent, or ended; the want of it had eaten my mind.\\
\medskip
\textbf{xiv}\\
I went into the desert a young man, believing that I would save my own; and I was not lying. Hear it: my people truly were undone.\\
Someone had truly killed my father. Both of these were true. And there, exactly there, the ruin grew: the wound had learned to speak in sentences that were, every one of them, true.\\
And a grief that tells you only true things, you will follow to the end of the world.\\
\end{scripture}

Paul set down the last sheet of that day very gently, the way you set down a sleeping child, and no one spoke, and the equinox beam had moved off the machine and onto the wall while we read, the way it moves, a year at a time, patient, indifferent. And I found that they were all looking at me, Paul and Sarah and Va-Sheva, not unkindly, and waiting, and I understood that they had all three arrived, by their different roads, at the thing I had been refusing since the first sheet, and were waiting to see whether I would say it before they had to.\par

So I said it. ``He sounds like me,'' I said. ``He stood where I am standing, with the same wound and the same low door, and he walked through it, and built this. I am one refused question away from him.'' It cost me more than anything else in this whole account to say it, and Va-Sheva, who was the woman in my own version of his story, watched me say it and did not spare me and did not comfort me, which was the truest thing she could have done, and only said: ``Yes. He does. Now read the rest, and find out whether that is a warning to you or a permission. Because you have not decided which, and I can see that you have not, and it is the only thing left in this Mountain that still frightens me.''\par

I did not answer her, because she was right, and because the honest answer would have been to say that I did not know; that I had read a hundred thousand years down into the root of my own kind and found a man built to exactly my shape, holding exactly my wound, tempted by exactly my door; and that the thing I felt looking at him was horror, and under the horror a terrible companionship, the relief of the badly alone at learning that someone, once, had felt the precise thing and been unable to bear it either. I had come down that stair believing I would read the confession of a monster and be set free by the distance between us. There was no distance. And everything that came after --- the Mountain, the drill, the children, the god --- I read across that one closed gap, the way a man reads the history of a country while standing in the middle of it.\par
