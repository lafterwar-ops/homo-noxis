\typesetchapter{Chapter 22}{Loss}

We read him together, in the Archive, in the equinox light, taking the sheets in turn: Paul, who could hear a voice in a text the way I could see a hand in a stone; Sarah, who had hunted this man across sixty-six years without knowing his name; and I, who was about to learn that I had been reading my own possible life all along and calling it another man's confession.\par

I will set his account down as he made it, because the making is half of what it says. He did not write a life. He built one. It is cut into measures and numbered, and it moves in the long cadence of the scripture my people have chanted every morning for eleven thousand years --- the unrhymed, rolling verse of the dawn office --- because the man who wrote that scripture could not, at the last, say even this in any other voice. He had a god's habit, and he kept it almost to the end. Where I break in, I will say so. The rest is his.\par

What follows is Zero's.\par

\ornament

\begin{scripture}

Let this be written by the one whose name no book that comes after will carry.\\
This was not handed to me. This I did, with my own hands, in my own hour.\\
A mind stood in this place, and it stood well, and it is gone.\\
\medskip

We were the summit of the world, and we held the warm lands and the cold together.\\
We knew the lights of heaven, and we had named them one by one, and set the names into the songs we carried.\\
Our dead went down into the ground with the small things that they had loved in their lives.\\
Our tools were perfect, and older than any man's memory of the making of them.\\
We did not think ourselves a wonder; no mountain wonders at the stone that it is made of.\\
\medskip

Afterward, in the scripture I would build, I drew us stooped and clubbed, brutes in the cold of the world;\\
for the ones who take a people's place will always paint that people as beasts, to be easy in the taking of it.\\
Know what we were: a hall in winter, and lamps we had taught to burn without smoke, and old men reciting the eleven fathers.\\
We wrote down little; we kept the whole of the world in living persons, and let no name fall unremembered between them.\\
And there, in that keeping, the flaw of us lay waiting: a house of loyal skulls is loyal only while the skulls remain.\\
We had built our whole forever out of the one thing that can rise, and walk, and be carried away downhill.\\
\medskip

We were losing, and I was the first among us to say the word of it aloud.\\
No war took us; hear it plainly: no blade was drawn, and no siege was laid, and no king was ever thrown down.\\
It came upon us as \emph{preference}: as daughters going down at dusk to trade, and staying to listen, and at the last forgetting the road home.\\
They said the fires below were warmer; and by the warmth they meant a place where a woman was let be more than one finished thing; and they went.\\
Nobody drove them out, and nobody stole them away; they walked downhill of their own gladness, and carried a quarter of our land away in their going.\\
\end{scripture}

\ornament

Sarah laid the sheet down here. ``He is describing demographic collapse by assimilation,'' she said, ``and he is describing it correctly, and it is the one ending no wall and no weapon has ever stopped. My whole century argued it and never said it as cleanly as a man a hundred thousand years dead.'' Paul said, ``Mark where he is standing, though. He is not raging yet. He is a man watching his people fade and refusing, so far, to reach for the blade. Keep that. We are going to need it at the end.'' And I said nothing, because I had begun, without permission, to be afraid of this man, and I did not yet know why.\par

\begin{scripture}

My father saw it before I did, and his answer was the old answer: that a people does not live by being loved.\\
Break them, he said, and have done; and he held the metal and the men, the labour and the hoarded gold with which to do it.\\
He had been afraid far longer than I, and a fear that is kept long enough grows cold, and patient, and sure of itself.\\
I said no; I said that a slaughter would be our stain, and that a thing so won would never be worth the keeping whole.\\
We argued the years away across one table, and I won the argument, and it did no good in the world at all.\\
\medskip

Her name was Ithe, and I set it here against my own long rule, for a record that leaves out the one true thing is the same lie built a second time.\\
She was of my rank, and of my people, and of my kind, raised as I was raised, the most complete soul that I have known, and mine for a while, and never once praised by me.\\
She told me at the last what my love had been: a room, and a beautiful one, and a perfect one, and one that had been built without a door.\\
So she went out, and she went out alone, to where she was let be more than one finished thing; and I let her go from me.\\
My father had seen that road sooner, and more coarsely, being the more afraid: a proud house is neither bought nor stormed; it is entered where its love was laid down.\\
\medskip

They reached her in her unhappiness --- bought, or frightened, or told some tale kind enough to be believed; and which of these I do not know.\\
They sent her back to me wearing peace, and I opened every door I had; for a man handed back the thing he has mourned is glad, and does not stop to weigh the gift.\\
I told her small things: where he rode, and the passes that he trusted, and the hour at which he would be lightly guarded and abroad; and I did not think them secrets, then.\\
\medskip

They killed my father with what she carried, in a pass that he had ridden until it was home to him, lightly guarded because I had said that he might be.\\
The one whose war I had refused was killed through the very door that he had watched all his life, and had shown to me with his own hand.\\
I stood over his body in the pass, and I said to him only this: \emph{you were right}.\\
And under the grief, and within the grief, there was a \emph{relief} also, and it was mine, and I have told it to no one until now.\\
\medskip

His people were very good at the asking, and they came to me that same morning with all the rest of it laid open in their hands.\\
I had the means, that morning, to learn the whole truth of her: whether she had known what she carried, whether she was used and never told, whether she wept in the choosing or only when the thing was cold.\\
\emph{I had the means. I did not ask.}\\
For if she had carried it unknowing --- if love had been the road they walked her down, and she a lamp they bore through the dark --- then the fault was a loose word of my own, and no crime of her breed at all;\\
and then the warm ones and my people might go on living together as before, and I should have to come back to that table, and argue the whole of it again, with no father left to argue against.\\
So I did not ask. I let her be guilty; and everything I turned my eyes upon that morning told me that she was; and there was work before me that would not wait; and I let her go.\\
\end{scripture}

\ornament

Sarah put the sheet down at that and sat with it, and I saw her go somewhere far off, into a cellar, among a dead father's papers. ``There it is,'' she said. ``The unasked question. My father died with a question in his mouth he would not ask me, and let me have the money instead. And I have declined my own questions, more than once, in front of men whose answers would have cost me the thing I had decided to do.'' She did not pick the sheet back up at once. ``He is describing my family, boy, and not only his. He is describing everyone's.''\par

\begin{scripture}

Then I built the law, before ever I had built the Mountain, out of the one night that I would not look into.\\
I took a wound with a date upon it, done by named hands and in a named pass, and I grew it to the size of a whole breed, and the length of all the ages.\\
My father had wanted a war, and a war I might have been forgiven, for a war comes at the last to an end; and I gave him a thing far greater than his war.\\
I gave him an engine to wage it without ending, against every warm man ever to be born, the most of whom would never do me harm, and not one of whom would ever learn my name;\\
and the whole of it I laid upon a deed that one frightened woman may never have known that she did.\\
She may have loved me to the very last, and been spent like a coin she had no hand in the striking of; or she may have hated me, and chosen, and walked every step of it awake.\\
She is to me this hour as unknown as she was upon that dawn, for I arranged that she should stay so; \emph{I did not ask}; and she is gone.\\
\end{scripture}

\ornament

Paul had been very still. ``This is the most dangerous document I have ever held,'' he said, ``and I once held a plan to end the world. It is dangerous because it is nearly true. He had a real enemy. His father was really murdered. A jilted boy who invented a grievance would be safe; we could dismiss it. He suffered a genuine, specific, evil act --- and took it, and made it permanent, and refused to check the one fact that might have kept it small.'' And Sarah, who knew more than any of us about what it is to convert a private certainty into an institution, said only, ``Yes. I wrote the shorter version. Keep reading. I want to know whether he ever asks it. I never did.''\par

\ornament

I did not, in the room, say what I was thinking, which was that I had begun to write this same document myself, not long before, in the desert, the night my own council cast me out. I had stood with my whole life taken from me and started, already, to compose the doctrine of my grievance. I had been stopped by nothing grander than a fact-checker with a machine, who made me say the small true things one at a time until the large false one had no room left to grow. I had been one refused question away from the man on the sheet in Paul's hands.\par

And I was afraid, in the equinox light, because I had my own Ithe, and I had her in the room. Va-Sheva had chosen a man of the outside who bought sweets for no reason --- and I knew now, we all knew now, that the sweets and the charm and the warmth had begun as an operation, that Jean-Charles had been sent into a proud house exactly as Ithe had been sent into mine. The mirror went past resemblance. It was the same event, a hundred thousand years apart. And I felt the low door open in me, the one Zero had walked through, the one that says: \emph{there, you see, it is their nature; they reach us through our loves; she is Ithe and he is the enemy's instrument and it is proven now, build the wall.}\par

\ornament

\begin{scripture}

He was dead, and everything that he had held came down to me: the metal and the men, the knowledge, and the massed estate of the greatest of our houses.\\
And I, who had refused his war, now held the whole world with which he would have waged it; and I raised no army, for armies lose.\\
His answer had been a war; and mine would be a story; and a story, told well enough, outlasts every army that was ever raised.\\
I gathered to me those who knew the stone, and those who knew the hidden springs, and those who knew the lights, and those who knew the making of hard things;\\
and I gathered soldiers also, for a work so dear must keep a wall of armed men about its opening; and I emptied my father's house, and turned the whole of it toward the south.\\
\medskip

And I took children; set that down, for it is the seed of the worst thing I would do.\\
Some of them were our own, and some I bought, and some I gathered up off the failing edge of everything.\\
A place that must outlast the memory of its own making has to be peopled young, with those who can be told that a thing was always so.\\
I said that they were being saved, and they were being saved; and they were being made the first of a new kind of people, to tend a thing they must never understand, and to kneel before a god not yet invented.\\
I watched them sleeping in the carts, among the metal and the tools; and no one living had the standing to prevent me.\\
\medskip

The road south took the better part of a year, and I lost them to the heat, and to the sand, and to the slow leaking-away by which a great column dies in a dead country.\\
Each loss I entered in a reckoning, and I did not mourn; and we passed the green, and the last water that any man of mine had ever seen, and the last mark of any people at all.\\
We went on into a waste that lay beyond the wanting of anything alive, and that said as much of itself in every hour of the light.\\
My engineers began to tell me, in the careful voices of men who have begun to fear their lord, that there was no such place upon the earth as the one I sought.\\
I hunted a thing that did not exist, and I would not turn; I had gone blind to every road that bent or ended; the want of it had eaten my mind.\\
\medskip

I went into the desert a young man, believing that I would save my own, and in that I told no lie: my people truly were undone, and someone truly had killed my father.\\
Both of these were true; and there, precisely there, the ruin grew, for the wound had learned to speak in sentences that were, every one of them, true.\\
And a grief that tells you none but true things, you will follow to the end of the world.\\
\end{scripture}

Paul set down the last sheet of that day very gently, the way you set down a sleeping child, and no one spoke, and the equinox beam had moved off the machine and onto the wall while we read, the way it moves, a year at a time, patient, indifferent. And I found that they were all looking at me, Paul and Sarah and Va-Sheva, not unkindly, and waiting, and I understood that they had all three arrived, by their different roads, at the thing I had been refusing since the first sheet, and were waiting to see whether I would say it before they had to.\par

So I said it. ``He sounds like me,'' I said. ``He stood where I am standing, with the same wound and the same low door, and he walked through it, and built this. I am one refused question away from him.'' It cost me more than anything else in this whole account to say it, and Va-Sheva, who was the woman in my own version of his story, watched me say it and did not spare me and did not comfort me, which was the truest thing she could have done, and only said: ``Yes. He does. Now read the rest, and find out whether that is a warning to you or a permission. Because you have not decided which, and I can see that you have not, and it is the only thing left in this Mountain that still frightens me.''\par

I did not answer her, because she was right, and because the honest answer would have been to say that I did not know; that I had read a hundred thousand years down into the root of my own kind and found a man built to exactly my shape, holding exactly my wound, tempted by exactly my door; and that the thing I felt looking at him was horror, and under the horror a terrible companionship, the relief of the badly alone at learning that someone, once, had felt the precise thing and been unable to bear it either. I had come down that stair believing I would read the confession of a monster and be set free by the distance between us. There was no distance. And everything that came after --- the Mountain, the drill, the children, the god --- I read across that one closed gap, the way a man reads the history of a country while standing in the middle of it.\par
