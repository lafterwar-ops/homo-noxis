\typesetchapter{Chapter 24}{Whole}

When a thing is finished being read there is a silence that belongs to it, and we gave Ravar his, in the cold of the shrine, with the last wall read and the great clock turning far above us up the stair. Then the silence ended, the way all of them do, because someone has to say what happens now. It fell to me. I was the one he had built the door for.\par

\ornament

But we did not leave him at once. We answered him, each in turn, into the cold, knowing he could not hear and saying it anyway.\par

Paul spoke first, because he had read the whole of it with the scholar's ache in his face. ``He had a real wound,'' he said. ``That is the horror of it: every line is nearly true. But he put the question to the whole world and would not put it to the one woman who could have answered it. A man who would sooner build a hundred-thousand-year machine than ask a question he is afraid of the answer to is not a philosopher. He is a man running from a room.''\par

Sarah spoke to the wall, as though the man cut in it could be made to hear one thing. ``You made desire a doom that wears the face of fate, so that no one is ever made to answer for it. I have been that thing for sixty-six years, so take the flaw in it from an expert. I answered. I reached for the largest thing there was and closed my hand on it and burned down everything else, and not once did I tell myself a goddess made me, or fate, or my blood. You wanted certainty, and you dressed it as a doom laid on you by the nature of women. You called Helen's choice a whim because choice would have left it hers. There is no fate --- only a choice a hand could not bear to own, and hung on the sky, and knelt to. I am a woman. I chose. It was mine.''\par

Va-Sheva did not speak to the wall. She spoke to me, in the flat Shield voice, and it was the hardest thing said in that room. ``You want to believe him, because a man reached you through your own love the way one reached his house. I was a made thing, raised in his cage and told it was the sky, exactly as you were. And here is the thing he built the whole Mountain so that no child of his would ever say: you become real the first hour you answer for yourself, and not for the hand that made you. I chose Jean-Charles. And whatever comes of that choice will not be your evidence either. I answer for it, and you do not get to take it from me to mend yourself.''\par

And I said the last of it, because it was mine to say. ``He made us,'' I said. ``That is the truest thing on the wall. He cut the door for a reader who would understand him, and forgive him by doing the same. I understand him. I will not do the same.''\par

We were ready to leave him, at last. Before we did, I said a thing to Paul I could have said to none of the others, because only Paul would let it stay as small and strange as it was. ``Do you know what he makes me think of, this Ravar? Your Basquiat. The dinosaur king, in the corridor at the house. I cannot tell you why.'' And Paul, who had made me explain every word I ever gave him, looked a long moment at the wall, and let it stand.\par

The Mountain would do now what it had done the five times before: close over the truth and go on. The offices would go back into their walls; the six cycles, Ravar, the hand of the five, would sink again out of the daylight. Ka-Leth's tablet, the one he had cut beside the deep clock to tell the seventh judge he was not the first, would not be destroyed --- the Mountain does not destroy what a Keeper has set in the permanent record. It would be worse than destroyed. It would be kept. Within three generations a Justice never taught what it was would find it too holy to read, set a lamp before it, and fold it into his liturgy as a thing recited and never understood. The children would be taught that the world was young and the Beast a story and the Mountain eternal and God's, exactly as I had been.\par

The only thing still ours to decide was whether the truth would be let to die with the forgetting, as it had died five times before --- or whether, this once, a little of it would be let out the door before the door swung shut. And one thing harder, which came to us later: whether the machine behind the door would be left as we found it, or whether we would spend our one open hour taking from it something forgetting could not put back.\par

\ornament

He had left me more than a confession. He had left me a shape --- a way of turning a wound into a world --- and it fitted my hand because it had been cut for it, a hundred thousand years before, by a man of my kind carrying my hurt. And I felt it offer itself. I too had been left. I had watched a woman I had spent years revering step, for the length of one breath, into a man from the outside, and be, in that breath, more than the finished thing I had required her to be, and glad. Here was the doctrine that made sense of it, that raised it off the floor of my private humiliation and set it among the fates of peoples: that this is what the warm ones do; that they reach us through our loves; that where Va-Sheva seemed to choose a man, a species had claimed her; and that my hurt was a wound in the body of my people, and a reason.\par

\ornament

Ravar had a woman taken from him by the enemy's hand and called it the nature of a species. And I had loved her, and I could not love her and use her as evidence both.\par

\ornament

She came to me, near the end of the ninety days, because I had asked her to. I had meant to do a thing I am ashamed of: make her explain it, account for it, give me the reason as though I were owed one. She knew before I opened my mouth.\par

``You want me to explain him to you,'' she said, ``or to explain you to yourself by way of him. I will not do either.'' She sat, not close. ``I loved you. I want that said plainly, once, because I will not have you spend the rest of your life able to doubt it. I loved you, and I contained you to keep you breathing, and I grieved you while I did it. None of that is the same as choosing to stand the rest of my life beside you. You have always folded the loving and the choosing into one, and required that if a person loved you they must choose you. It is the thing I could not live inside.''\par

``And Jean-Charles,'' I said, because I could not help it, because Ravar was still in me, reaching. ``You know now what he did.''\par

``I know what he did,'' she said, ``and I have chosen not to know why. He is a man I chose. That is the whole of the sentence. I will not open him to find out in what order he did the terrible thing and the brave thing and the loving; I would rather carry the not-knowing than manufacture the verdict.'' She stood. ``Do not make my life into your reason. I am not a reason. The choosing is mine, and you do not get to have it.''\par

I let her go.\par

\ornament

There remained the account --- Ravar's confession, the mechanism, the year-clock, the six seams, all in one place --- and the question of what a Mountain does with the one thing it was built never to let anybody hold. Sarah was the one living Outsider able and willing to carry it out; Paul, the other, would stay. Everything she had spent sixty-six years reaching for was, at last, hers to take.\par

``You are all waiting to see what I will do,'' she said, with eleven days of the ninety left, the offices gathered and Paul's record between us, the door still open on the turning machine. ``You are right to be afraid. The habit of a life does not die because the life has learned better. I will not ask you to trust that I have changed. I have not. So I will do the one thing that works without my having to have become good.'' She laid her palm flat on the record. ``And do not imagine there is a world up there still to deter. I have heard it go quiet through these months on your own instruments; there is scarcely a state left standing to send a rival to this desert. But whatever climbs back out of the ash you have made --- and something always has, five times over --- will do again exactly what you did: forget, invent a kindly story, and be governed by a lie it cannot see. That is the only enemy left, and it is the one your machine cannot kill. So you will give this to me, and I will carry it out and lodge it where the next world will trip over it while it is still young enough to be changed --- before the survivors write their own Genesis over the top of yours. I am the only one alive who can carry it, and you know it, which is why you will say yes.''\par

The deciding was hard and slow, and it divided the offices as every true thing did, because it had to be the Mountain's own, made against its every instinct, or it would not hold. Shield could not bear it; Ka-Dhavar had kept the secret with his life. Balance counted, and could not make the old way come out safe against a world that had found us once and would find us again. And Justice, Ka-Leth, ruled last, and reached back to his own words, which I had never seen Justice do. ``I told a sealed chamber once that a man who walks far enough draws the last road shut and comes home holding the whole, and that a seamless world keeps no outside. I set it down as the reason we could let nothing out; I have lived to learn it cuts both ways --- there is no outside to hide this in, either. The stone we cut below opens to one hand only, the hand that has already fired the Beast to its end, and shuts with the reset. It reaches a man only after he has struck; it may tell him he is the seventh, and not the first, and it will stop nothing. If the truth is ever to stand before a Mountain while the striking is still a choice, it cannot rise out of our own floor. It must come down the stair from outside, before a firing has opened the door --- and there is one line in the living world that might carry it back: hers. We cannot bind her, nor her children's children; the gift is whole or it is nothing. But if it is ever brought home before the drop falls, then once, for the first time in six endings, a firing will be a thing chosen, and not merely inherited.''\par

\ornament

That was the ruling on the memory. There was a second, harder one on the machine, and it was Ka-Cheryn who would not let us walk past it. A record changes nothing a hand cannot change back, she said, in her flat way. We could carve the whole truth into the Archive, hand its twin to Sarah, and a Justice several generations on would read it as scripture and fire the seventh Beast with Ravar's confession folded into the liturgy as a hymn. If we wanted the next firing to be a thing a Mountain chose and could not merely inherit, a record was not enough: we had to look, while the door stood open and five who understood the machine still lived, at whether a hand could take anything from it a frightened future could not simply pick back up.\par

We could take out very little, and that is the part I have had to make my peace with. The Beast is the Mountain. No part of it, taken alone, could break the world: the deep water is the water the city drinks; the buried heat is the heat that keeps five thousand people from freezing; the throat that drives the blow down runs through the works that move our air and water. The ruin is in none of them and in all of them arranged --- the reserve spent as working mass, the old heat, the pressure pathways, the deep throat, the phase-true strokes repeated until the planet itself answers on its far side. And the works that could end a hemisphere are the works that keep the parish alive: pull the engine to spite the weapon and you kill the people it shelters. So the reset had to happen. The water had to run, the wards to close, the offices to divide the knowledge again --- which is also how the water is kept clean and the air kept moving. Life and fire were poured into one vessel a hundred thousand years ago, and no hand could unpour them in the days we had.\par

So the machine would go on entire. The one thing that had nearly been subtracted, the enemy had done for us. Faith had all but died in the firing. Ka-Elun was gone, and the timing she alone had carried --- the phase, the note beneath the note, the reading of the returning stroke that no reckoning off the stars can rebuild --- had been poured, the night we fired, into the only ear the law would suffer it into, which was Paul's. But the Veil of Faith had not died with her Keeper. Va-Maera had been dragged living from the same fall of stone, deaf for good and broken in the body, sunk too far in the hurt to speak; and she had come back. She would never hear the Mountain again --- Faith's own listening was burned out of her --- but her mind was her own, and she held the whole of the craft save the one fragment the roof had knocked loose into an Outsider's keeping. So Paul gave it back: not out to the world, which the law forbade, but in, to Va-Maera, the one place it could go and still be Faith. She rose from Veil to Keeper with an Outsider's returned gift in her. She would never hear the note herself; but a deaf woman can teach a hearing Veil what to listen for, the marks that tell a true call from a false one, and stand behind the next ear until it is trained --- so the procedure and its criteria pass whole to someone who can. Faith went down the inside of the Mountain entire, and the secret went neither into the stone nor out into the world.\par

So we built no lock into the machine, because there was none to build that would not also strangle the people it keeps breathing. We left instead the only safeguard a truthful hand can leave: the truth itself --- cut whole into the Archive for the next one down the stair, and carried whole out in Sarah's copy for the world above --- and the thin wager that a Mountain shown exactly what it is might, once, choose to be other than what it was built to be. We did not make the seventh firing impossible; we left the world above a chance to prevent it, and made certain that if it came anyway, the Mountain could never again bury what it had done beneath necessity or theology or lost history. None of us would live to learn whether it was enough.\par

\ornament

For the Mountain kept its own. That was the other half of the ruling, and it is the half the world will never hold, and it cost us the best of us to make.\par

The Archive opened once, and only once, and only to a Mountain that had fired the Beast to the end; with the reset it would close again and not open until the next hand fired the next Beast. Whatever was kept at the centre had to be finished and left inside before the door shut, in a form no age or made winter could eat. Tape perishes and paper burns, so it had to be cut into the stone of the shrine, beside Ravar's own account, where the next one down the stair would come. It could not be Paul's book word for word --- no hand could carve a novel into rock in the days the door would hold --- but it had to be whole in the only way that mattered: Ravar's confession, the true nature of the machine, the six cycles, this last firing, the names and the choices of those who had made it, and the fact and the destination of the complete account gone out into the world. Nothing causal left out; nothing a later mind could need and fail to find. The door could be held open only as long as the reset could be held off, which Balance had fixed to the hour: the air, the stores, and the closing of the wards fell due on the one day, and the shutting of the Archive was a single motion of that reset, never to be reopened. So the cutting would run to the last hour the door could be held, and the hand that did it would still be inside when the door came down.\par

There was only one hand it could be. Not a Keeper's: for a Keeper to carve the whole into the permanent record, in the standing of his office, would be Ravar's own crime committed again in stone --- an office holding the whole forever, the five gathered back into one; and Justice would sooner have let the truth die. Not mine: I was of the Mountain and bound by the same law, and needed alive besides, to carry Sarah's copy up and be the witness that any of it had happened. Only Paul held no office and could do nothing with the whole but remember it and cut it true --- and only Paul had spent a life learning to set a true thing down exactly as it was. It had to be Paul who held the chisel.\par

There was a shorter road, and I made myself set it in front of him before he could shut it in his own head. Cut the core only --- the diagram, the six cycles, Ravar's hand and his own laid against it --- and no more; leave the rest to Sarah's copy in the world above; and Ka-Dhavar would bring him out through the wards a day clear of the reset, alive. He heard me to the end, and did not throw the rope back unlooked-at, and I have loved him since for the length of time he held it in his hand. ``Her copy is in the world,'' he said, ``and the world loses things. It burns them, and buys them, and tidies them. The stone one is the copy still here when hers is gone, and it is read by one man, once, at the bottom of the worst day of his life, hunting a reason not to do again what was done to him. Leave it a diagram and a list of dates and I have handed the next Ravar the gaps to raise his wall in. It has to be whole. Whole is the eleven days, and the eleven days are the door shutting on me. That is not the machine choosing. It is me. Do not let anyone write it down, after, as the machine.'' And no one unsaid it, and that was the debate.\par

He was afraid, and said so, in the flat honest voice he had kept all week for true things: that he did not want to die in a cold room at the bottom of the world; that he had a life he had meant to go back to --- a narrow flat full of books in a northern city, the son of his brother John he had promised to teach to swim, a standing Thursday game of chess with Urs over a bottle they both pretended to like --- the ordinary unfinished business of a man who had always assumed there would be more Thursdays. He consented to finish it whole, with the shorter road lying open to the very last, and he never once reached for it. ``Someone stood in this room a hundred thousand years ago,'' he said, ``and sealed the truth away to keep a lie alive. I should like the last thing I do to be the exact opposite, in the same room. It is the cleanest thing I know how to do, and I have wanted, all my life, to do one clean thing.''\par

He worked eleven days at the end, with Sarah's copy already made and boxed for the world above and only the Mountain's own left to cut. I brought him food he mostly did not eat, and turned the pages of his account back to him when his hands shook too badly to hold them. On the last day, when the offices came down to close the Archive, it was I who set my hands to the counterweight beside Ka-Dhavar, because Paul asked it of me. He had asked me once, in a house by a lake, to take him into the Mountain; I had sworn only that I would try, and warned him I could not answer for what it would do with an outsider who had seen its whole. He set the last strokes as the door began to come down. He put his hand flat against mine on the cold stone through the closing gap, the way Va-Sheva counts a living man. He was still afraid, and did not pretend otherwise, and he said, ``Get it to Sarah. All of it. Do not let them tidy me out of it,'' and then the stone met the stone, and the last Outsider the Mountain took into itself that day was alone with it in the dark.\par

\ornament

Here the hand that set this account in order changes, and I owe you the telling of it. Everything before this page is Paul's account for the world --- the one you are holding. The testimony was mine, given in a room by a lake, a scholar asking a man he had been paid to disbelieve to describe a morning; the recording of it, and the ordering of it into a book, were his. He is sealed in the stone below now, his own copy cut whole beside Ravar's, and could not carry the outside one to its end; so the last of it falls to me, and from here the testimony and the ordering are both mine. I set these pages down not for the Mountain, which will forget my hand into the dark, but for the outside --- to go up, in time, and be laid at the end of what he made, so his account should reach at last the one hand he asked me to put it in. But before that ending, on the first leaf, in Paul's own hand, there is a dedication. It is not to me, and it is not to the Mountain. I did not understand the whole of it until I had watched Sarah carry the box up out of the folds and not once look back. It reads:\par

\begin{scripture}
To Sarah Blackwood.\\
You entered because we could not keep you out.\\
You leave because we have chosen to let you go.\\
Between those two acts lies the peace of our kinds.
\end{scripture}

She left as she had come: on animals, by a seam in the rock that is on no map, with a man of the sand walking ahead of her. She did not thank us. She did not promise us anything. She was the enemy to the last. What she would do with the account --- guard it, spend it, hold it over the powers that had discarded her --- she did not say, and I did not ask. It was hers now, and past our keeping, and we hung no condition on it, because we had all of us, at last, had enough of leashes. I knew only that she carried the whole of it up out of the Mountain and into the world; what became of it there is not mine to tell.\par
\ornament

Before the Mountain closed over its own memory for the last time, Ka-Raedin came to me. He was my Keeper still, in the one office that had never quite cast me out, and he asked the question a Keeper asks a Veil come back from the dead: whether I would take the road up again --- go looking, as every Veil of Light goes looking, for the Keeper's chamber no living man had found, and stand at last in the office, and train the young.\par

``I cannot,'' I told him. ``I hold the whole of it now, and the whole is the one thing the Mountain has never let a man hold and keep his place in it. The law that closed on Ravar's builders, and on Paul, closes on me. And the copy we cut for Sarah will not stay long in her hand. She is ninety-three; it is a miracle past any reckoning of Balance's that she lived to carry it out of the desert at all, and the truth cannot be left to grow old and die in one failing pair of hands above ground.''\par

Then I told him the rest, because he had earned it, and because I wanted one man of the Mountain to know I had solved the thing they made me for before I turned my back on it. ``I know where the Keeper's chamber is,'' I said. ``I have known since the shrine. There is a sphere in it, with water held against it on every side --- a small Earth, so that a man may learn to lay the blow anywhere upon a world he can hold in his two hands, before he is ever trusted with the true one. Va-Maera told me without knowing she told me, the day she said that not every beam belonged to her. That was not humility. It is a thing built into the Mountain against the day a Veil and a Keeper of one office die together and carry a craft into the ground with them: a hint kept in another Kahir's hand, so that no knowing can be wholly lost. Faith keeps the hint for the Veil of Light, and Balance for the Keepership; for to work the aim at scale, against the drag of a turning world, you must have a great body of water, and the great reserve is Balance's. Follow the water, and it brings you there. Sarah said I was bound to make at least one discovery, and she was never once serious when a listener most wanted her to be; she was handing me the truth in a jest, the way she always did. The chamber lies above the reserve, and you go down through the water to reach it. I am a strong swimmer now.'' And I asked him a thing I have wondered at since: ``Did you teach yourself to swim, Ka-Raedin?'' He did not answer it. He only looked at me the way Light looks at a beam whose thread it has lost, and asked whether I understood what I was choosing to become.\par

``I understand it,'' I said. ``I am the man who went outside and came back and brought the end in behind him. Let me be that, then: the outsider who carried the worst into the Mountain, the murderer they will curse at the root of the next lie, exactly as they cursed the first. Every people keeps one ready. I have earned the office better than the man they hung it on eleven thousand years ago, and I will carry the truth where Sarah cannot, for as long as I am given. I cannot stay here.''\par

And I would not go alone. Sela had found me again in the ninety days, out of the crowd of the sealed and the frightened, with the same corner of a smile she had never quite been able to put away when we were young and I was made too high to keep her; and in the long dark, when I was more alone than I have ever been, we had stopped pretending the office still stood between us. She chose to come. I did not ask it of her, and I will set her choosing down as nothing but her own --- for I had learned by then, at what price a soul is made a reason and not a person, and I would not make that error with the one the Mountain had never taught me to use. We would go out together into the world I had helped to end, and take our chance in it among the living.\par

\ornament

The forest came back, and Sarah set it coming before she left. I did not see it; I was gone from the Mountain by the time it rose. But the ashkeepers charted it, season by season, at the high slots, and word of the greening came out to me the way word always comes out of that country --- late, and by the old road. It came slowly and thin --- not the second year, nor the fifth; it was years before the charts showed more than a grey-green stain creeping from the poisoned ground, and longer still before anything a child would have called a tree. It had not come up out of nothing, and it had not come up out of her money, which was paper in a world where the states that had honoured it were ash. It came up out of the roads she had laid down long before: the buried water and the seed-caches she had spent a lifetime dropping across a country she once meant to own, and the guides of Amastan's line to open them and tend the ground season on season --- the one road still moving after every border and satellite had gone dark, kept off every ledger but her own. A dead desert brought quietly back to green and then left alone was the whole of what she asked, and she had set it beyond failing long before she was gone. Faith read the greening and did what Faith is for: it stood beneath the gravure and told the people the promise was kept --- that the wrath had passed, that God had remembered the forest and the rivers that were lost and made the desert withdraw, exactly as the Book had sworn. And the people wept, and believed. The forest was real, and the mercy was real, and the only thing hidden from them was the shabby human shape of the hand that carried the seed.\par

\ornament

And I let the whole leave my hand. I had been made the eye that saw it, and the offices, tired and grieving, would have let me keep it. I gave it away instead --- to Sarah, for the world, and to Paul, for the stone --- and kept no copy of it, and no office made from it --- only the memory I cannot give back.\par

Whatever I refused to become in the days since, I was the eye at the centre of the firing, the hand that read the phase and said the word that sent each load down into the far side of the world. I broke the continuation. I did not break the crime. Ravar's sixth, and mine.\par

And the Mountain reset, as it had reset five times before. Ending is the rule of this world, and the going-on only its exception: five times the slate had been scoured to the rock, and five times something crept back up out of the ash to write on it again, forget the scouring, and take its brief turn. The offices went back down into their walls. Faith was vindicated. The machinery hid itself again, granting the fire and keeping the why and the who and the how-many. I watched it happen, and I let it, because five thousand people who have just survived the end of the world cannot be asked to survive the truth of it in the same season.\par

For the truth left the Mountain. Whether it lived in the world above I cannot know; I know only that it went out of the Mountain, in her failing hands and in mine. If these pages have reached you, it endured. And at the centre of the Mountain, cut into the stone beside Ravar's confession in a dead man's careful hand, it exists again, whole and true, waiting for the next one who fires the Beast and comes down the stair. He will find, where I found a jealous god's lie dressed as scripture, the actual size of the thing he has inherited, and Paul's plain witness beside it. I do not know whether it will be enough. Ravar built with terror, judging truth the weaker mortar, and he may have been right. Perhaps the next heretic will read it and think \emph{enough}, as I did, and let a little more light in; or perhaps he will build the wall higher. I will not be there.\par

And the Pendulum swings again.\par

I stood beneath it once more, in the reconsecrated Hall, among the kneeling people who take it for the face of God weighing the world, and watched the great disk cross, and turn, and cross, taking up over them the slow patient count it had kept on the morning I was named; and then I left it to keep the count without me.\par
