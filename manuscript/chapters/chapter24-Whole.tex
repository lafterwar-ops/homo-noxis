\typesetchapter{Chapter 24}{Whole}

Here the ordering hand changes. Paul assembled the account through Ravar's last line: my testimony, Sarah's confession, the live reels, the records of the five offices and the writing on the shrine wall. What follows is mine, written for the outside from what I witnessed and from the reports that later reached Horizon.\par

We gave Ravar silence in the cold of the shrine, with the last wall read and the great clock turning above us.\par

\ornament

Before we left the wall, I said a thing to Paul I could have said to none of the others. ``Do you know what he makes me think of? Your Basquiat. The dinosaur king in the corridor at the house. I cannot tell you why.'' Paul, who had made me explain every word I gave him, looked a long moment at Ravar's final line and let this one stand.\par

Ka-Leth gathered his tablets and led us back to Justice's low room. Eleven days remained before the reset shut the five office rooms, the clock and the shrine. We had to test the Beast and place the account.\par

\ornament

I asked Va-Sheva to meet me before Justice convened. She came without Ka-Dhavar, and stood while I sat. The child did not yet show beneath Shield's dark cloth. Her hand went once to where it would. I meant to make her explain Jean-Charles, account for him, give me a cause I could place beside Ithe. Before I finished the first sentence, she stopped me.\par

\ornament

``You want an account of him. You want it to settle your own.'' She sat, not close. ``I loved you. I contained you to keep you breathing, and I grieved you while I did it. I did not choose a life beside you. You joined those acts and called them one. I could not live inside that.''\par

``And Jean-Charles,'' I said, because I could not help it. ``You know what he did.''\par

``His dying did not clean what he did. What he did did not erase the man at the gate. I chose him.'' She let the words stand, then rose. ``I will carry what I do not know. I will not cut the dead open to manufacture a verdict. Do not make this child your reason. My choice is not yours.''\par

I let her go. I returned to Justice without asking her again.\par

\ornament

The five offices gathered in Justice. Ka-Leth set Ravar's wall, the six cycle count and the present firing as three separate claims on wet clay. Paul placed his ordered pages beside them. Sarah laid the old Horizon register on the fifth ledge and kept her hand on it.\par

``Reverse Horizon,'' Sarah said. ``Preserve the outside account in more than one place and bring it back before another trigger.''\par

Ka-Dhavar put Jean-Charles's field radio between them. ``You ask us to give the whole to the hand that found us.''\par

``Give Horizon the cause, the six endings and the duty of return, not the firing craft. The account below opens only after the Beast. If the next warning begins in that stone, the next world is already ash.''\par

Paul objected. ``Too clean. Somebody will read that distinction as permission. Write down what was kept back and whose hand kept it, so the next reader can see the shape of the hole. Leave the absence visible.''\par

Va-Maera could not hear the room. Sa-Elian repeated each answer where she could read his mouth, and she wrote her question with one slow finger: \emph{Who keeps the keepers of the warning?}\par

Sarah read it. ``No one. Divide the copies, the money and the return roads. Horizon failed under me while I was alive and watching it. Build from that.''\par

Sela stood against the wall beside the box. Until then she had spoken only when asked to identify a seal. ``Who carries it? Not the ruling: the box. Somebody lifts it, walks it out and has to be alive at the far end. Nobody here has said whose back it goes on.''\par

Ka-Dhavar answered from Shield. ``Bring an army and Shield closes. Bring a Keeper and it becomes scripture. Bring an Outsider and Justice calls it theft.''\par

Sarah looked at me. ``Someone who can be accused of all three.''\par

Ka-Leth ruled. ``A warning after the act is a witness. We keep that witness below. A warning that can precede the act must leave under charge to return. Horizon will take the outside account under the seals of the five, preserve it, teach its danger and bring it back before the next trigger. The cause will travel with the warning. Justice will not close it again. Failure begins if the record is first read below.''\par

Va-Maera wrote once more with her slow finger before the clay had set: \emph{Was any law broken in the firing?}\par

Ka-Leth read it aloud and answered for Justice. ``None.''\par

Five separate impressions closed the ruling. Sarah's hand received no place among them.\par

Ka-Leth had carried the fourth cycle's closing tablet up with the six-count, and when the impressions were dry he read it, because Justice reads what stands nearest a new ruling. A hand from a cycle whose name we do not hold had ruled that the craft stay divided and the cause be written down whole. It had kept both below.\par

``They stopped there,'' Ka-Leth said. ``We go one step past them.''\par

Sarah asked how many of the six had stopped at that same step. Ka-Leth counted the tablets he had brought and gave her the number, which was three.\par

Before they lifted their hands, the five agreed that the outside account would open with a declaration addressed to Sarah and signed in their common name: \emph{The Mountain}.\par

\ornament

Ka-Cheryn would not let Justice close there. She put the question no office had put in eleven thousand years: whether the thing could simply be taken out. In eleven days the offices tested what could be removed. Flooding the throat returned water to the hot rock; draining the reserve killed the wards; breaking the sighting instruments left the sky; erasing Faith left an answer an Outsider had already recovered. When Shield closed the shared air branch for less than a minute, the lamps dulled and a child began coughing. Nobody proposed the test again.\par

Ka-Dhavar closed the question from Shield. ``Say a cut existed, and Balance found it tomorrow. Then I stand in the Hall and tell five thousand people that the thing their grandmothers were born to keep is gone, that the offices they entered at twelve have no work left in them, and that the Mountain is a hole in rock with a spring in it. They would not go out. They would find a Keeper who said it was still down there, and follow him, and in a hundred years they would be doing all of this again over an empty chamber.''\par

One fragment of the firing craft stood outside its office. Ka-Elun was dead. The roof had left Va-Maera deaf and broken in the body, but alive; Paul alone had heard the missing criterion during the firing, the note beneath the note by which Faith distinguished a true return from a counterfeit. In the room of Faith he faced her and spoke each interval while she held two fingers against his throat. She stopped him, corrected the third mark from memory, and made him begin again.\par

When they finished, Va-Maera pressed his fingers to the side of her neck and spoke the sequence back. She could never hear it again. She could teach a hearing Veil what to wait for and how to know a false answer. Paul returned the fragment inward, to the surviving mind of Faith. Sarah's copy excluded every operational instruction and carried the warning.\par

Va-Maera asked for a child of Faith with an undamaged ear. A girl came from the wards, still wearing the grey wrap issued after the roof-fall. Paul sounded two returns into a small bronze bowl: one true, one fractionally short. The girl chose the wrong one. Va-Maera made no sign of disappointment. She placed the girl's fingers at Paul's throat, then at her own, and began with breath, pulse and vibration. The lesson continued after we left.\par

\ornament

The outside account could leave on paper and Paul's surviving reels. The inside witness had to be cut beside Ravar before the reset made its stair a wall. Ka-Leth and Paul prepared two versions in Justice's stone hand: a bounded record that took three days, and the complete causal account that took all eleven and left its cutter inside.\par

Paul read the short account aloud. ``It is exact,'' he said. ``It is also bloodless. Ravar had proof, grievance and an engineered world; this has proof, dates and a machine. The next person who wants necessity without people can use either.''\par

Ka-Dhavar marked the escape line on the floor. Finish the bounded text, cross it by the ninth morning, and Shield would bring the cutter out. No office asked for more.\par

Paul stood over the two sets of pages for a long time. At last he put the bounded version into my hands. ``Keep that road open,'' he said. ``I have not chosen yet.''\par

He told me he was afraid. He told me of the narrow flat full of books he had meant to see again; of his brother's son John, whom he had promised to teach to swim; of a Thursday game of chess with Urs, left standing on the board since March, which both men had been losing on purpose for a year. He did not want to die in a cold room at the bottom of the world. He said this without shame, and made me promise that no account would call the fear a test he had passed.\par

I told him I wanted him to take the shorter road. I told him the future reader was hypothetical and Paul was not; that we had already killed the world for abstractions; that no record could earn another body. He thanked me for making it difficult.\par

Before the first day ended he began the complete wall. On the sixth, the skin split across his thumb and Sarah bound it. On the eighth, Ka-Dhavar came with the route open and his hand on the marked line. Paul stopped. He washed the stone dust from his face, ate the bread Sela had brought and walked as far as the line.\par

On the fourth night I found him asleep against Ravar's first wall, the chisel still closed in his hand. On the fifth he asked Sela to describe the ward at day forty because his reel had failed under the stone-fall. On the seventh he spent three hours recutting one line after discovering that \emph{we could not stop} had replaced \emph{we did not stop}. ``The first is a defence,'' he said. ``The second is true.''\par

He returned to the wall. By the last day his hands shook too badly to hold the pages, so I turned them for him. The offices came down at the hour Balance had fixed. Paul set the final strokes while the counterweight began to move.\par

The final passage was his own name. He first cut it smaller than the others, then recut it at the same depth as mine.\par

I took my place beside Ka-Dhavar because Paul asked it of me. At the house by the lake he had asked me to take him into the Mountain; I had warned him I could not answer for what it would do with an outsider who saw its whole. Now, through the narrowing gap, he put his hand flat against mine as Va-Sheva counts a living man.\par

``Get it to Sarah,'' he said. ``All of it. Do not let them tidy me out.''\par

Then the stone met the stone. Paul was alone with the account in the dark. The counterweight continued through the walls. Above us the reset opened water to the lower wards, drew air through the sealed city and returned the year faces to their first marks. The five office doors closed. The road to Ravar disappeared. The Mountain kept Paul.\par

The sound travelled upward after the door vanished: weights taking load, distant water, five shutters seating one after another. In the wards people cheered when the air strengthened. A woman brought her newborn into the corridor and held him toward the returning lamps. Sa-Elian began the first-day blessing. Va-Maera stood beside him, reading his mouth, and did not stop him.\par

Ka-Leth carried the tablets from Justice into the permanent record. By sunset a lamp had been placed before the sealed Archive road. Nobody ordered it.\par

I wanted to tear it down. Ka-Leth stopped my hand. ``The lamp violates no ruling. Leave it.''\par

We left it burning.\par

\ornament

Before Paul took up the chisel, he wrote a dedication on the first leaf of the outside account. After the reset Sarah opened the box in the presence of the five offices and showed it to me.\par

\begin{scripture}
To Sarah Blackwood.\\
You entered because we could not keep you out.\\
You leave because we have chosen to let you go.\\
Between those two acts lies the peace of our kinds.
\end{scripture}

Sarah waited until all five seals were back on the box. Then she called me to the record table and put the retained Horizon register beneath my hand. ``My father left me an empire that believed itself to be mines, laboratories, companies, houses and obligations,'' she said. ``I made its hidden part into Horizon. Most of the visible empire is paper in the ash. The hidden part was built to outlive states, and enough of it still answers. I am ninety-three. It requires an heir.''\par

She pushed the register across the stone. ``You inherit Horizon, Kailan, and whatever remains of Blackwood with it. Not to possess the Mountain. Preserve Paul's manuscript outside it. Teach its danger before the world writes over it, and bring it back before the next trigger. If the first warning comes from the stone below, Horizon has failed. That door opens only after the crime.''\par

``Before you answer: my roads brought the state to this Mountain.''\par

``My word answered it.''\par

``Then we enter both names.''\par

``Yes.''\par

I put my hand over hers on the register. ``I accept it,'' I said.\par

``Naturally,'' Sarah said. ``You have been trying to inherit my empire since the morning you entered my house and began telling me what I had misunderstood.'' But she left her hand beneath mine a moment longer.\par
\ornament

Before we left the lower wards, Ka-Raedin asked whether I would take Light's road again and find the Keeper's chamber no living Veil had found.\par

``I cannot. I hold the outside account.''\par

``Then Light's chain ends.''\par

``The chamber is inside the great reserve. A drowned passage leads through an underwater throat to a sphere touched by liquid metal. Did you teach yourself to swim?''\par

He looked once toward Balance's wall.\par

``Horizon carries the outside chain,'' I said. ``Light's remains yours.''\par

Ka-Raedin wrapped the old gold thread around his hand. ``I will find the water.''\par

Sela had already put her pack beside mine at the south doors. Sarah looked from the two packs to us. ``I assume she is part of the inheritance.''\par

``No,'' Sela said. ``He is part of mine.''\par

Sarah inclined her head.\par

Sela tightened the rope around both packs, tested the knot and made me do it again when mine slipped. She would keep the water tally because I forgot to drink when thinking. I would carry Paul's box on the steep ground because her left knee had never healed straight after the fall. If either of us wished to turn back, we would say it before making silence into consent.\par

Va-Sheva chose the Mountain. Jean-Charles had died holding its inner gate, and she would bear his child inside the wall he defended. She came to the south doors with Ka-Dhavar on one side and Ka-Maera --- Va-Maera newly risen as Keeper of Faith --- on the other.\par

Ka-Maera put two fingers against Va-Sheva's throat and her other hand over the life beneath the dark cloth. What she spoke was almost too low for me to hear. I caught the old stations only: the Eye Above named first, the Beast Below answering, the Stone Between; then an appointed boundary, that through which a thing would continue, and mercy not interrupting justice.\par

Ka-Dhavar lowered his head. Va-Sheva looked from Faith to Shield. ``I understand.''\par

Then she put her palm once against my chest, counted me among the living and went back into the Mountain between them. I did not follow.\par

At the first bend she looked back. Her eyes passed me and settled on the light from the open south door falling across the floor. Then the stone took her from sight. I have not written what came after, because I did not witness it. I record the prayer, Ka-Dhavar's lowered head and her answer.\par

\ornament

Years after we left, the forest began to return. I did not see it rise. The ashkeepers charted it at the high slots, season by season, and their copies came to Horizon late by the old road. The second and fifth years passed over bare ground. Then came a grey-green stain, scrub, and much later something a child could call a tree.\par

The first report reached us on a strip of cloth carried inside a water jar. The ashkeeper had drawn the old horizon line in charcoal and marked seven shoots where the western ground had held none. Sarah read it twice. She asked whether the scale was reliable, whether the keeper had mistaken salt brush and whether the pump records survived. Two more reports arrived before she let herself believe it.\par

She did not smile. She put the cloth beside Edward's Horizon register and said, ``One useful thing. At last.'' By then her hands could no longer close the clasps on Paul's box. Sela did it for her.\par

The reports named buried water, seed-caches and pumps without a state to own them. One damaged ledger reached us stiff with salt. Its last complete entry read: \emph{Opened before the appointed year. Thirty-two at the gate without water. Seed counted after the living.} Beneath it, another hand had entered nineteen names and two births.\par

A later packet carried three lines copied from a survivor's flour sack: \emph{The woman from the north showed us how to roof the seedlings. She died before the first leaf. Her name was Meriem. Keep it with the well.}\par

No order travelled intact. A cache opened early and was eaten. One pump drew salt. Two guides disappeared with the seed they carried. Elsewhere people who had never heard Sarah's name found the marked wells and did the work because water and shade were useful.\par

Faith stood beneath the gravure and said the promise had been kept: God had remembered the forest and made the desert withdraw. The people wept and repeated the promise. Beyond the gravure, human hands carried seed between marked wells.\par

\ornament

The outside account passed from Sarah's hand into mine and from mine into divided hands. One keeps the reason, another the sealed route; neither holds the complete return until it is due. Paul's engraving waits until the act is complete. If the next hand first learns the truth there, we have already failed. The box beside me carries his first leaf and the impressions of all five offices.\par

On the morning we left, I stood beneath the Pendulum in the reconsecrated Hall among people who still knelt before it as the face of God weighing the world. The reset had returned its slow count. A lamp burned below the road that no longer appeared in the wall.\par

I knelt one last time in the posture my people used to count grief, terror, gratitude and time.\par

The great disk crossed, turned and crossed again. I rose before it returned.\par

At the south doors Sarah waited with Paul's box upon an animal. Sela stood beside our packs. A man of Amastan's line had already turned toward the road. Sarah looked once at the Mountain and then at me.\par

``Well?'' she said.\par

I went out with them. Behind us the Pendulum continued without my witness.\par

Keep your distance. Live.\par

\emph{The Mountain.}\par
