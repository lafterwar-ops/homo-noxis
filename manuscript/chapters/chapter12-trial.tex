\typesetchapter{Chapter 12}{Trial}

\noindent \textit{Let the record show: the accused was condemned not for what he saw, but for proving the wall could be crossed.}\par
\noindent \textit{--- from the Trial record, Hall of Cataclysm}\par
 
\ornament

\noindent What I did in that chamber I invented while I was doing it, one sentence at a time, the way a man crossing black water finds the stones only by the fact that he has not yet gone under. You keep asking me when I decided to fight, Paul, as though there were a moment I chose and a plan I carried out from it. There was none, and I will not build you one now. Do not let me tell it as though I saw the far bank. I never saw the far bank. I saw the next stone, and then the next, and by the time I understood what I was building it had already half-killed me, and there was no unbuilding it.\par
 
\ornament
 
He has told me the shape of this night before, at its edges, and each time he stops at the same place --- the cell, the silence, the verdict he had to deduce from it --- as though the door of the room beyond will not quite open for him.\par
 
``You keep circling the decision,'' I say. ``When you chose to fight. I would rather have the room itself. What they made you see in it.''\par
 
``You want the trial,'' he says.\par
 
``I want the part where it turns.'' I keep my hands still and let the reels turn for both of us. Four days he has given me the boy, the Mountain, the long climb down into it, and all of it has been the walk toward this one chamber --- the hearing held beneath the reason for the rooms, where a thing was said aloud that was never meant to be spoken in any single room at once. It is the piece I have waited four days for, because if any of this is invention, a trial is where the invention shows its stitching --- a man's account of himself either holds under its own weight there or it splits. I want to see which. I do not tell him that is what I am watching for.\par
 
``It did not turn cleanly,'' he says. ``I have told you that. I found the next stone, and then the next.''\par
 
``Then find them again, slowly,'' I say, ``and let me watch your hands.'' It is a gentler way of asking, and he takes it for patience. It is patience --- but it is also the fact-checker in me, wanting the account laid down slowly enough that I could catch the place it stops being true. Four days in, I have not caught one yet, and I have stopped expecting to before he finishes.\par
 
\ornament
 
The Lesser Seal ended in the night, and I knew it had ended only because nothing in my cell changed. That is the first thing to understand about the Mountain: it does not announce its verdicts. It lets you deduce them from the silence. All through the dark the stone had spoken --- bars dropping, chains tightening, doors sealed and counted and witnessed --- and then the sounds reversed, one jurisdiction at a time, the Mountain returned to itself around me. A far gate. A water passage. A family district. Voices came faintly through stone: a child laughing from relief, a woman asking whether morning bread would be delayed. The ordinary world rising with the small shame of having been frightened without explanation. My cell remained the same. The Mountain had reopened around me and left me closed.\par
 
Proof recovered. Speech held. Body held. Justice had named those three facts over me while I was still bound, and they were accurate, which was worse than a trap. The scrap had left its chamber. I had carried it. I had brought it to Va-Sheva. Shield had contained me. The Mountain had not panicked, which meant the procedure had succeeded, and that I was alone inside its success.\par
 
Near first work the door opened, and it was not a warden who stood in it. It was Ka-Dhavar of Shield. That, before he said a word, told me the size of what I had done. Keepers do not fetch prisoners; wardens fetch prisoners. A Keeper comes for you only when a matter has grown too secret to pass through any hand but his own. He carried no weapon I could see, and did not need one; Shield is the office that has made a study of the body, and he stood in the doorway the way a shut gate stands, without effort, as if the corridor behind him had simply decided to end there. He looked at me a moment. ``You will not be bound,'' he said. It was not a mercy. It was a question wearing the clothes of a statement --- whether I could be trusted to walk to my own judgment on my own feet. ``There is nowhere in the Mountain a man runs to,'' I said, ``and no man runs from the five.'' He weighed the words, decided they would hold, and stepped aside to let me pass. I was not chained. I was trusted, and in the Mountain that is only the most expensive kind of chain, because a chain you can hate, and trust you must carry yourself.\par
 
He did not take me to any hearing room I knew. The route descended. The warmth of habitation thinned, the polish left the stone, and the lamps became strange --- one lighting a wall perfectly and leaving the turn ahead black, another lighting the floor behind us after we had passed. At every turn someone had already decided before I arrived. The Mountain did not hurry. It sequenced. Twice we passed close enough to ordinary life to hear it continuing above a man it had already erased.\par
 
Then white stone, and the Hall of Prayers --- larger for being empty, except it was not empty, and the sight of them assembled stopped me on the threshold. Ka-Elun of Faith at the centre in her plain white and the torn pilgrim's mantle I remembered from her Ascension. Ka-Leth of Balance, broad and grey. Ka-Orun of Justice, still as his own tablet. Ka-Raedin, Keeper of Light, my Keeper, a hooded hand-lamp in his grip. And apart from the five, only two: Va-Sheva of Shield, who did not look at me, and Sa-Tavan of Justice --- Sa-Tavan, whom I had knelt beside on the morning we were both named, a Candidate still, too junior by years to stand in such a room, and standing in it precisely because he was junior. When a matter must be recorded and must also never be spoken of, Justice does not bring its senior witnesses. It brings the one it can most completely hold. At their feet were packs: water, dried food, folded cloth. I understood, before anyone explained it, that wherever we were going was not near.\par
 
Ka-Elun looked at the lamp in Ka-Raedin's hand. ``Keeper of Light,'' she said softly, ``put that away.'' He did not move at once. ``It is hooded.'' ``That is not the same as away.'' She had not raised her voice, nor mocked Light. She had merely placed it outside competence. ``Better Keepers of Light than either of you,'' she said, ``have entered the Labyrinth believing brightness would make a path obedient. They were recovered by pilgrims who could not read a mirror but could keep breath with stone.'' Ka-Raedin held the lamp a moment longer than pride should have allowed. Then he set it against the wall, hooded and unlit, and left it there. ``Faith receives,'' Ka-Elun said, and did not touch it.\par
 
``You will walk,'' Ka-Elun told me.\par
 
``Guided by whom?''\par
 
``By what remains when sight stops governing the body.''\par
 
The wall behind her opened --- no seam first, no hinge; a section of carved prayer simply ceased to be wall and became depth, breathing out cold air that smelled of stone and distance. We went down into it in single file: Ka-Elun ahead, then Ka-Leth, then Ka-Raedin at my shoulder, then me, then Va-Sheva, then Sa-Tavan with the tablet and a pack, and Ka-Dhavar last, closing the dark behind us the way he had closed the cell door. The wall sealed, and the Hall of Prayers was gone.\par
 
I had thought, in my child's picture of it, that the holy places sat just behind the walls we prayed against. They do not. We walked for hours. The way did not fall by stairs --- there were almost none, and I understood why before anyone told me: a stair is a place an old Keeper's knee betrays him, and a straight flight is a thing the mind can measure and keep. This was neither. It was ramp and switchback and long shallow grade, doubling back on itself, turning through chambers that felt identical and were not, descending and descending until my ears changed and the air grew heavier and colder and older than any air I had breathed. The disorientation was not an accident of the digging. It was the digging's purpose. A man who could hold this route in his head could return to it, and the whole architecture existed to make certain no man could. We must have gone the height of the Mountain down into it, and then some.\par
 
Once, early, when the way was still merely strange and not yet frightening, I asked the question a child asks. ``Are we going to the Hall of the Last Prayer?'' Ka-Elun did not quite laugh. ``The Hall of the Last Prayer,'' she said, ``is a room a Veil of Faith may walk to and come back from. No. The Labyrinth opens onto many hidden places, and most of them have no names a Candidate has been given.'' She let that settle into the dark. ``You are not being taken to a room, Va-Kailan. You are being taken beneath the reason for the rooms.''\par
 
The Labyrinth received us without echo, which was the first wrongness --- I had expected Faith to own echo --- but the deeper cruelty was made of light, and it was aimed at me. The walls were striped. Floor to vanished ceiling, everywhere, ran bands of pale and dark, lamped and cut so that stone and shadow repeated in one endless rhythm, this indistinguishable from that and that from the next, a herd of sameness with no single beast in it to fix upon. It was Light turned against Light. A zebra is not hidden by its own stripes but by every other zebra's, so that the lion, faced with the whole moving mass, can cut no shape from it and lock upon none; paint one with a red mark, and that one dies first. The Labyrinth was that herd, in stone --- built so that a Light-trained eye, which lives by finding the one edge that differs, would find ten thousand edges, all identical, and starve among them. And still I hunted. After the ninth turn I passed a crack I was sure I had seen before, and my heart rose.\par
 
``No,'' Ka-Elun said, without turning.\par
 
``I did not speak.''\par
 
``You leaned toward certainty.''\par
 
I had not known a body could lean toward certainty. ``There is no crack you have seen before,'' she said, without turning. ``There are only cracks that repeat. The eye that lives by difference is blind in a place with none. You may observe, Va-Kailan. You may not follow yourself.'' And so it was not the dark that defeated me. It was the sameness --- and I would remember it, later, in the chamber, when I believed I had found the one edge no one else could see.\par
 
Somewhere below, water spoke behind a wall, and Ka-Elun stopped. ``Before the Beast, silence,'' she said --- and the answer came from behind me, low, from the Keepers themselves: ``Before silence, prayer. Before prayer, breath. Before breath, fear.'' The five most powerful people beneath the stone, reduced to pilgrims answering Faith in the dark. A seam opened ahead, not in the wall but in the floor of sound, and distance existed where it had not. ``Now,'' she said, ``Light may look.''\par
 
We came into the anti-chamber of the Hall of Cataclysm.\par
 
Set down your cathedrals, Paul. Your reactor halls, your launch gantries taller than churches --- set them down. I had believed the Hall of Ceremony large because every child believes the room that names him is the measure of the world. This made that belief a child's.\par

The Labyrinth did not open onto a floor. It opened onto a bridge --- a tongue of stone wide enough that thirty men might have crossed it shoulder to shoulder, running out level from the passage mouth and spanning a long gulf of open air to the far wall. And the air was open above and below. Above, the dark climbed until the lamps gave it up: so much of it that the lamps failed long before the top of the dark, higher than the Mountain stands above the sand and higher again, and all of it roofed by Mountain. Below, to the left of the bridge and to the right, the floor simply was not there. It fell away into two long pits, and out of them rose the pillars --- scarred, matte, past counting --- their feet a sheer drop beneath me on a floor of black polished stone, their heads lost past where the light could climb. Pale mineral veins caught the low light like a buried sky.\par

Down on that black floor, in the pit to my right, lay a city of gold in miniature: towers, walls, streets fine as scratches, perfect and empty, half-drowned in the dark stone so the eye could not tell whether it was sinking or being built. And near it, behind a curtain of beaten gold threads, a king whose face had been deliberately struck from the authority he still held --- his crown kept, his hands kept, the man himself erased.\par

The two long walls, to either side, were not walls at all. They were the Mountain, raw and irregular, the living rock left broken as it had been found, so that the whole enormous room felt less built than uncovered.\par

But the far wall was built, and the bridge ran straight at it, and it was the Gate of the Hall of Cataclysm. Here the Mountain did a strange thing with scale. The chamber was a size beyond any man; the Gate was made to be faced by one. Its foot stood level with the bridge, at my own floor, and it rose only the height of a great tree and spanned only the width of the bridge --- a door a hand could be imagined to have meant, set in a wall no hand could. And every span of it was mechanism. Chains entered it out of the rock, thick as the trunks, and climbed into slots above. Five rows of five signs had been cut across its face, five-and-twenty figures I could not read and have never since stopped trying to. And low to one side there was an empty slot --- a shaped absence, cut clean and waiting, for a thing that was not in it.\par

And at the centre of all that iron and grandeur, five-and-twenty paces to a side, was the gravure. I will not pretend to give it to you whole, Paul, because it was not made to be held whole. It did in stone what the two Books would soon do in words, so that a man who had heard the reading could look up and watch it happen. At the top, the Outsiders wandered under the open eye of Heaven, multiplying, city risen against city and king against king, their engines of war and their false prayers cut so fine you could count the banners. Below them the Mountain, not as refuge but as sealed engine, and the five who kept it standing apart in five arches, their hands not touching and the chains beneath them running from panel to panel all alike, linked without joining. And below everything, the Beast --- too enormous for form, straining upward out of Hell with its fists at the roots of the Earth --- and from its back ran the whole catastrophe the scripture would name: the deep waters falling into the fire, the poisoned seas climbing over walls on the right hand, the mountains breaking and the volcanoes opening on the left, the sky gone black with ash, the armies of the Outsiders burned and drowned and scattered. And at the foot of the ruin, smaller than all of it, the people going down at last into the stone, weeping for a world saved and lost in the same hour.\par

The gravure did not reveal a mechanism. It made the mechanism feel like scripture --- which was the point, and which was the lie, and I did not yet know it was a lie.\par
 
Five seats faced the accused mark, cut from the chamber's own stone, equal, spaced so no Keeper could easily whisper to another. At the centre lay a flat stone table where objects could be placed so all could see them and no single hand receive them. Nothing lay upon it. That absence frightened me more than the Beast.\par
 
Ka-Dhavar brought me to the mark --- a touch at the shoulder, no more; the trust still holding. The Five took their seats --- Ka-Leth of Balance, broad and still; Ka-Elun of Faith; Ka-Dhavar without ornament or visible weapon; Ka-Raedin beneath a dark aperture; Ka-Orun of Justice standing beside the central stone, his own hands empty, for Justice does not hold the record; it only reads into it. And at the wall, only two who were not Keepers: Va-Sheva of Shield, and Sa-Tavan of Justice with the tablet on which the record would be kept. No family. No wardens. No one else in all that dark. The way in had closed without echo.\par
 
``This anti-chamber opens under highest secrecy,'' Ka-Orun said, and the words did not enlarge in the vastness. ``No Kahir divulges what belongs solely to its own sealed charge. No accused repeats forbidden matter under claim of defence. No Keeper seeks total knowledge. The hearing binds the five without making one hand whole.'' I felt the structure close. ``Before judgment, the chamber remembers why judgment is divided.''\par
 
Faith read both Books entire. I could tell you I have forgotten most of it; it would be a lie. The Mountain reads scripture into a man the way water reads a stone, slowly and without asking, and I can give it back to you now, in your cold bright country, word for word. I set it down here in full, Paul, because the full reading was itself the first argument of the trial. Before one word of judgment, the chamber made you know what divided knowledge preserves, and what it cost to keep. Understand these two Books and you will understand why five careful men, an hour later, could not let me live among them.\par
 
Ka-Elun did not hold the scroll in her hands. It lay open on a low stand before her, and she did not need to touch it; she knew every word by the body's memory. Her voice changed when she began --- not louder, not theatrical, but placed so precisely that even the stone seemed required to listen.\par
 
``The Book of the Beast,'' Ka-Orun said, and Ka-elun started the reading.\par

And the people wept for the world that had been saved and lost in the same hour.
And God said unto them, Dwell no more in the open, but enter the Mountain, and keep the Temple, and remember the price of being seen.

Ka-Elun closed the second scroll, and no one spoke.\par
 
The chamber received the words and did not return them. I looked at the gravure and understood why the trial had been brought here: it was the argument before any judgment was spoken. This is what divided knowledge preserves. This is what cannot be risked by appetite, cleverness, or proof carried in a robe seam. And I felt, with a precision that surprised me, how the law would use scripture against me. I had not held the whole --- not close. But I had carried a piece from one sealed hand into the open space of another, and I had carried proof. The law would not need me to be monstrous. It would only need me to be structurally wrong.\par
 
Ka-Orun set out the charges: Breach of Seal, Bearing Forbidden Proof, Unauthorized Revelation in potential and act. ``The law judges not only the fire that has burned, but the place where fire was carried.'' And then the frame that closed the room like a lid: ``No cross-Kahir knowledge may be divulged. No sealed name may be spoken. No chamber under seal described beyond category. Any attempt to defend by renewing the breach will be stopped.''\par
 
``How do I answer what cannot be spoken?'' I asked.\par
 
``With what may be spoken.''\par
 
``And if what may be spoken is not enough?''\par
 
``Then you begin to understand the charge.''\par
 
There was no malice in him. A cruel judge can be resisted; this was not cruelty. He read seven facts into the record, and I contested no sequence, because the sequence was true. He turned to Va-Sheva, and she spoke to the centre stone and not to me: that I had disclosed unauthorised movement, entry into a sealed Shield archive, removal of copied proof; that she had recovered it from my hand and judged the danger to be a bearer travelling; that I had remained voluntarily until holding became necessary, and resisted once, under provocation, without force being exceeded. That last was her mercy --- not that I had not resisted, but that resistance was named precisely and not enlarged. ``What danger did you judge?'' ``Proof travelling by a credible bearer.'' ``Not treason?'' ``No standing to judge treason.'' The phrase became my category. She stepped back.\par
 
``There are only two outcomes lawful in this chamber,'' Ka-Orun said, and the Beast on the wall seemed to lean in to hear it. ``Death, or Exception.'' No acquittal. No correction. No confinement until memory softened. The chamber had not opened to decide whether I had done wrong --- that had been sequenced already, in a cell, in the dark. It had opened to decide how a living bearer of forbidden proof could remain, or cease to remain, without breaking the separation that made the Mountain possible. Then he turned the weighing over to the others, and for the first time I heard the five of them speak not as one law but as five minds that agreed on the end and on nothing else, each arriving at the one word by the private road of its own office. I had grown up believing the Keepers were a single will wearing five faces. That hour cured me of it. They were five knives, and the only thing they shared was the direction they meant to go in.\par

``You will each speak from your office,'' Ka-Orun said, ``and from nothing else. Not as the friend of the accused, nor as his enemy. As Faith. As Balance. As Shield. As Light.'' Then he did a thing I had not expected, and understood only after. In every ordinary matter Justice speaks last, so that nothing is ratified before it has heard the whole of a thing. But for a sentence of death Justice reverses itself and speaks first --- it votes blind, before any other voice can lean upon it, so that no man may ever say a life was argued out of the law. It binds itself in the cold, at the start, and returns only at the end to say aloud what the five together have made.\par

``Justice,'' he said, naming himself, and he did not come forward, because he was already at the centre, where Justice always is. ``I hold intent and outcome apart, because either alone makes a fool of the law. He did not act as an enemy; he acted as a Veil outside his standing, bearing proof through an unsealed sequence. So the law will not call him enemy by his act, nor innocent by his heart. It calls him what he is --- a ruin risk. A small chance of a total loss, carried by a credible man, set against a large comfort in his good intentions. And the law does not trade a total loss for a comfort. It never has. Where there is a true risk of ruin to the Mountain, there is one answer, and I will not dress it as a kinder one. Death.'' He said it without heat, the way a man reads the depth of a well. And having spent his voice first, he had nothing left to guard, and could hear the rest as only Justice can --- already committed, and so, by the strange arithmetic of that office, made free.\par

``Faith,'' Ka-Orun said.\par

Ka-Elun rose. She did not lift her voice; she made the chamber come to her, and the first thing she said she did not say as herself. ``Let no sign be carried from its chamber, nor proof borne beyond its seal; for a secret held in place may preserve the people, but a secret made to wander shall become fire among them.'' She let the words of the Promise stand a moment in the dark. ``That is not my reading of the boy. It is the oldest instruction we keep, older than any office, and he broke the letter of it --- carried the sign out of its chamber, bore the proof past its seal. The Book does not name that an error. It names it fire. And I do not weigh his youth, nor his usefulness. I weigh the pattern, for a pattern is the one thing my office is built to read, and his is not the pattern of a boy who stumbled once. He went to Va-Maera. He sought from Faith what Light is not given, and named it curiosity, and Faith remembers. A boy who reaches, is warned, and reaches again is not an accident; he is a shape, and a shape does not halt of itself.'' She let it settle. ``And Faith can carry his death where it could never carry his life. He has been seen, these past seasons, standing long hours in the Hall of Ceremony --- too long, some said, for a Light candidate; devout, we shall say now. A devout young Veil, grieved and seeking, who went into the Labyrinth on a pilgrimage beyond his right, and was lost. We will let the search begin and be spoken of, the pilgrims going down day after day in their devotion; and on the fourth day we will let them find him, and bring his body up, and grieve him honestly, because grief is easiest when it is true. We will spend his death to redraw the warning at every threshold, so that no child follows him down. Ka-Raedin loses a Veil, and is given reason to drive his next chain harder. Every part of it serves the Mountain.'' She lowered her eyes. ``Faith supports death.''\par

Va-Maera had taught me to fear Faith's power to name. Here was its other face: Faith could build a man a grave out of the very devotion he had faked, and make the whole Mountain weep honestly over it.\par

``Balance,'' Ka-Orun said.\par

Ka-Leth's voice was heavier and plainer, the voice of a woman who had spent her life being right about water in rooms full of people who wanted her to be wrong. ``I came ready to argue the cost of him. A Veil of Light, lost; a chain broken at the join; a household to be told something, and a Faith to make the something bearable --- water and labour and trust drawn from stores we drew down only last night for the Seal. I had the column ready.'' She turned one hand over, as if letting the figures spill out of it. ``But Faith has shown me a death that pays for itself: a grief that mends the very threshold he broke, a warning bought with a body. The lie is affordable. I can carry the lie. There is one quantity in this I cannot put a number to, and it is not his life. It is the chain of light --- the seeing that has already left him. Seeing runs. I cannot measure how far it has run, nor what it costs where it arrives, and an office that cannot measure a thing has no business pricing it. That reckoning is not mine.'' She looked, for the first time, at Ka-Raedin. ``I hand it to Light, where it belongs. On everything I can weigh, I weigh with Faith. Death.''\par

``Shield,'' Ka-Orun said.\par

Ka-Dhavar did not come forward; Shield does not close distance to be heard. He spoke the way a man reads a measurement aloud. ``The danger is a bearer, not a belief. A bearer carries. A dead bearer carries nothing --- not down a corridor, not in a weakness, not in a dream, not into a room he was never meant to enter. Death contains completely. It is the only outcome that leaves nothing to watch. Exception does not contain; it relocates the danger and asks all five of us to go on watching it for the rest of our lives, and Shield fears the hour the watching lapses, because it always, in the end, lapses. Light had already told the chamber that the chain of light was his to answer --- and here was an answer that took the seeing four weeks' walk into the desert and set it to the Mountain's use, and he knew it, and he let the silence do his voting. And Shield --- Shield's whole craft is men who go out and do not come back the same. I had offered to become one, on terms that cost Shield nothing and watched itself.\par

``Around the earth,'' Ka-Orun said once, softly, to no one --- the phrase he could not set down. Then, to the chamber: ``This is nowhere in the law.''\par

``No,'' said Ka-Raedin. ``It is in the Mountain's interest, and the Mountain's interest is the thing the law was raised to serve, not the reverse. I told the boy, not an hour past, the order of it --- the Mountain first, then the office, then the man. I will not unsay it now, merely because the same order that would have killed him has turned, and asks instead that he be used.''\par

I did not win it by speaking again. I had said what I had; the rest was theirs. I watched the thing move between them without a vote called --- the way the Mountain settles everything that truly matters, in the silence where the words nearly are --- and then Ka-Orun, who alone had standing to say it aloud, said it aloud, because I had contested my judgement and the old right demanded an answer spoken.\par

``The sentence of death is stayed,'' he said, ``and its cover kept whole. To the Mountain you are the devout boy the Labyrinth took; on the fourth day they will grieve you, and you will let them, and never once, while you live, will you send them word that the grief is misspent. What you are from this breath has no name in any Book. You go out under Shield, by the door we do not name above the deep, and you send back nothing but the truth, through Va-Sheva, until the five release you or the desert does.'' ``You asked to be made useful at a distance. The Mountain grants you the distance. Do not mistake it for a pardon. It is the longest leash the law has ever paid out, and it is a leash still.''\par

I had come down into that chamber a condemned Veil. I went up out of it --- no: not up; outward, and away --- the first man in the memory of the record to be sentenced to death and then, in the same hour, taken into the service of the office he had come to destroy. I had recruited myself, out of my own ruin, into the one operation whose name I was never meant to learn. I did not understand yet that I had not escaped the trial. I had only moved it onto open ground, and widened it to the size of the world.\par

Ka-Raedin came to me before the transfer, close enough that no one could mistake it for public instruction.\par

``You are alive,'' he said.\par

``Yes, Keeper.''\par

``You will be tempted to think that being useful is the same as being forgiven. It is the opposite. You have bought yourself time, and nothing else --- time to become capable of carrying what you forced yourself to see.'' The faintest edge moved at his mouth and did not become anything. ``Go and be the eye. It is Shield's answer to you now. It is also, and will remain, Light's.'' And he went back to his seat beneath the dark aperture, and did not look at me again, because Light does not decorate a thing it has already released.\par

Ka-Elun stopped me where the ways parted --- the others' road climbing back the way we had come, and mine going out by some other seam, toward the surface and the sky I had been raised to fear. ``Do not make a gospel of what has happened to you,'' she said.\par

``No, Keeper.''\par

``That will be harder than silence. You have a martyr's material now, and a preacher's tongue, and neither the office nor the innocence to be trusted with either. Carry the truth. Do not preach it.'' She almost, for one breath, looked as though she pitied me, and then Faith took it back into itself. ``The Beast knows neither faithful nor stranger. Neither, now, does your story. Keep it apart from other men.'' And she turned, and Faith took the dark with her.\par

Va-Sheva walked me the last stretch --- only her, for there had never been any wardens in this. When no witness stood near enough to make the words formal, I said the thing I had left.\par

``I made your crossing worthless.''\par

``Yes,'' she said. She did not soften it, and I loved her for not softening it, in whatever way a ruined boy is allowed to love the one person who will not lie to him.\par

``And now they have made you my keeper instead.''\par

``Now I am the thread they have let you keep,'' she said. ``Do not test what it will bear.''\par

``Were death and Exception always the only two?''\par

She looked at me then, fully, for the first time since the chamber. ``Yes,'' she said. ``There were only ever two. You made the third yourself, tonight, out of nothing they knew they had left lying about. I have kept Shield's secrets my whole life. I never once saw one of them turned into a door.''\par

We reached a passage I did not know. The air in it was colder, less of Mountain, more of sealed routes and long distance; and I understood that we stood as deep beneath the world as the Mountain stood above it, and that whatever way they meant to send me out could only run upward, toward the sky. She did not pull me forward. She waited.\par

``Will they truly believe I am dead?''\par

``They will grieve you on the fourth day,'' she said. ``The grief will be real. That is what makes it hold.'' Exception was not death. Exception was the living shape death wears when the Mountain still has a use for the body --- and I was walking into the longest Exception the record had never written down. I stepped forward, and no one dragged me; I want you to notice that, Paul, because it is the part I have never been able to forgive. I walked to it on my own feet, the way I had walked to all of it --- because trust, once it is given, you carry yourself.\par

He comes to the end of it --- how he built a door out of their own reasons and walked through it, and named the thing waiting on the far side. Horizon. He lays the word on the table between us, across the reels, and watches my face while he does it.\par
 
It is my own house's name in his mouth, spoken in a language I spent three years learning to hear it in, and the room goes very still. He is watching me the way he told me he watched the smooth stone that answered light too cleanly --- as though he has been waiting to see what I would do when the word finally reached the air between us.\par
 
``You know the word,'' he says. It is not quite a question. ``You have known it since I came into this house. And I have wondered these four days --- in your warm rooms, with your machine that keeps everything I say --- whether the thing I gave myself to that night is still the thing I believed it was. Whether I walked home. Or into something wearing home's face.''\par
 
It is the nearest he has come, and one sentence further is the whole of it. One more and he will have said aloud, in his own voice, onto the tape, the thing he has been circling --- that the house that sent me might not be the house I think it is. And I find I do not want to hear him say it. Not because it would endanger anything. Because some smaller voice of my own, that I have been overruling for four days, would answer it.\par
 
I do not let him reach the sentence.\par
 
``I am Horizon,'' I say.\par
 
I set it down the way you set the one true stone that locks an arch --- plainly, with nothing behind it for a man to work loose, because as far as I know there is nothing behind it. It is true. He came out of the Mountain and gave himself to Horizon, and here is Horizon across a turning reel, saying so to his face. The relief moves through him before he can govern it. He has thrown the whole weight of his suspicion against a true thing and broken on it --- and I let him break on it, and tell myself, as I have told myself for four days, that a house which pays a man well to hear the truth and carry it faithfully cannot be the house he fears. I almost believe it. That I have to almost is the part I do not look at.\par
 
``Yes,'' he says, almost to himself. ``You are.'' And he lets it go. The single time in all of it that either of us came to the edge of the thing --- and it was a true word, the plainest one I own, that stepped us both back from it.\par

You have that machine on the table, Paul, the little wheel of tape that circles and circles and only listens.\par

I still feel the cold when it turns.\par

They did not destroy me for what I knew. Half the Mountain knew the shape of the world and said nothing, and lived out their lives in peace. They tried to destroy me for the one unforgivable thing --- for making the knowing move, for taking what five hands were sworn to hold apart and forcing it, for the length of a single evening, into one room. And when they found they could not even do that cleanly, they sent the danger of me out into the very country it had come from: the land of the men whose eyes ride around the Earth, a world that keeps no secret past a season and teaches its children the exact shape of the things I was unmade for dragging into the light. I did not win. I bought a longer road and called it living, and I would do it again, and that is not courage. It is only the shape of the crack that runs through every man who would rather hold the whole than live quietly with his portion.\par

I have held it ever since.\par

It does not feel like winning either.\par
