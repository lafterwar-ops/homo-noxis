\typesetchapter{Chapter 13}{The Two Knocks}

\begin{room}

--- You have my account. All of it. You told me, when the last reel was cut, that I could rest.

--- You can. This is not the account. The account is finished, and it holds, and by the rule I signed to I should be gone from this house by morning. This is a question that came after it, in the night, while I was running two of your passages back over the heads until the felt went warm. It is mine. It is not the house's.

--- You came in with the machine under your arm and not the folder, and you have not sat down. You have learned to say a thing and its opposite in one breath, which is new in you this week, and in my experience a man only does it when he is about to reach for a thing he has not been given leave to hold. I watched Veils do it before they asked me for an office above their own. \ldots{} I am not going anywhere this morning, whatever the car you have ordered and the room you have waiting across the lake. Ask.

--- Twice in your life you watched a Keeper rise. The Faith woman, when you were a boy of fifteen. Your own teacher's Keeper, four years on, the old man in the lowered box. You gave me both mornings whole. The disk on its thread, the wound made holy at the throat, the taste of lamp oil, the grain of the alabaster on a bowl. You spared me nothing anywhere else in eleven thousand years.

--- I spared you nothing that was mine to give.

--- Both times, at the very end, you said the same six words, and then your account got up and walked out of the room. \emph{The five withdrew.} You did not say where. You did not say to what. A man inventing a mountain does not leave the same gap in it twice, in the same place, by accident. So I will ask you the thing your account stepped over, both times. What happens after a Keeper ascends?

--- \ldots{} You have been patient with me for the length of it, so I will be plain with you now, which is harder. When a Keeper rises, the five go apart. No Veil follows. No candidate, no attendant, no witness, and no record I ever saw or heard tell of. There is a door that stands on no route a Veil is given in all his training. They go behind it, the five of them, a short while. And they come out.

--- How short a while.

--- A silence. Two. Long enough to feel the wrongness of the waiting and no longer. It is Keeper work. A rite of readiness. That is the whole of what a Veil is told, and a Veil who asks for more is told he will learn it on the morning he holds the office, or die without it, and either way it is not his that day.

--- And in eleven thousand years no Veil ever forced the door.

--- Force it to where? You do not understand what you are asking. It is not a locked room a bold boy might break into on a dare. It is a thing that is simply not on the map you are handed. A Veil is given the whole Mountain, every gallery and stair and service run of it, pressed into him over years until he could walk the dark of it and never touch a wall. And this one door is left off that map so completely that a Veil does not even feel the shape of its absence. I felt it. It is my flaw and always was. I stood in a passage where the map promised only rock behind the wall, and I knew there was a room back there, the way you know a word has been left out of a sentence spoken to you in the dark. And I said nothing to anyone, because a Veil who tells the elders he can feel a room that is not on the map is a Veil the elders begin to watch.

--- And you never held the office.

--- No.

--- So you do not know what they do behind the door.

--- No. \ldots{} I know one thing about it, and I have never said it aloud to any living soul, because it is a child's thing to have noticed and I was ashamed of it for years. Both times, after the door shut on the five, the Mountain answered them.

--- Answered. Say what you mean by the word.

--- A sound. Under the floor, and late, after the Hall had half emptied and no one minded the quiet any longer. A sound you would miss unless you were a boy with nothing left to do but be frightened and count the pillars. A knock. One knock, and then a shape to it, small parts, one after another, evenly set, and then done. The second time, dust came down off a seam in the pillar above me, and I looked about for a servant to blame for the tremor and found none. I was small enough still to be standing among the feet of the elders, where the prayer stones are worn to a shine, and I felt it come up through the soles of me before I heard it at all in the air, the way you feel a cart in the road through the floor of a house before it has reached the window. Five times the stone pressed up against my feet, evenly, without hurry, and then it let go, and an old woman beside me said the blessing for the settling of the Mountain and laid her palm flat on the pillar. I told myself the Mountain was old, and heavy, and settling, the way a house ticks at night when the fires die. I am a poor liar, even to myself, and even I held that lie a long time.

--- A knock. With a shape to it. Small parts, in order.

--- Yes.

--- The same shape both times.

--- \ldots{} I was a boy the first time and a grieving man the second. I would not swear to it in your court. It felt the same. It felt like the same fist on the same door. \ldots{} Why does a man paid to count whether I am real care what the ground does under a rite no Veil is ever shown?

--- Because the ground kept a copy.

\centerline{[\,---\,]}

--- I have no fine language ready for this, and I keep a rule against reaching for grandeur when a plain thing will carry the weight, so I will give you the plainest thing I own. When something happens in the earth, it does not stay where it happened. It travels. It runs out through the rock in every direction at once, and if you set a patient enough instrument a long way off, the shaking arrives there, days or seconds later, and the instrument draws it. A line, on a slowly turning drum. You have watched your Pendulum draw one circle on a floor across the better part of three days. This is the same art turned the other way about: the ground drawing its own signature on a page across a few seconds.

--- And from the line you read the size of the blow.

--- That is what most men read from it, and it is why the work is so easy to do badly. The size is the least of it. \ldots{} Here. A footstep, through a wall. You are shut in one room; a man walks through the next; you never once see his face. You can hear the size of him, yes, whether he is a heavy man or a light one. But that is not how you know it is your brother in there and not a stranger. You know your brother by the rhythm. Where the weight comes down. How long falls between one step and the next. The little drag on the third step from a knee he broke as a boy. The size tells you a man is walking. The shape tells you which man.

--- \ldots{} A gong.

--- Go on.

--- You have a gong. Faith keeps one and strikes the hours on it in the home ward. Ten attendants strike that gong across a year and no two blows of it are the same. One rushes the beat. One leans on the rim and flattens the note. One is frightened of the elders standing near and strikes it soft, apologising to the bronze. The gong does not change. The gong is the gong. The blow is the hand. \ldots{} And the Bowl. You will have heard me name the Bowl of the Last Prayer, that stands in the ascension with its Ear and its Mouth. You speak into the Ear, and if you give it the one true note it gives you that note straight back off the Mouth, whole, exactly; and if you give it a false note it gives you nothing at all, only its silence. The Bowl does not care how loud you are. It cares whether the note is the note. \ldots{} You are telling me the ground is a bowl. That it gives the same shape back to the same blow. And that a man set far enough off, with an ear patient enough, could learn one blow from another the way I could tell you which of ten boys had struck the gong.

--- I am telling you that nothing in nature repeats itself exactly. A quake is never twice the same quake; the earth has no such discipline in it. So when a signature comes in twice, and I mean twice cut for cut, arrival for arrival, small part after small part in the very same order and the very same spacing, a man who has listened to the ground for a living stops believing he is hearing the earth. He begins to believe he is hearing a machine. Only a machine does a thing the same way twice.

--- How far. How far off can an ear sit and still catch a thing so small.

--- That is the wrong question, and it is the first one every general asks me. Not how far. How quiet, and how patient, and how certain you already are that there is a thing to be heard. A loud event any fool catches at a distance. A small one, precisely made, arriving in a place where nothing should be arriving at all, is caught by one person only: the one who decided it was there before she had a single proof of it, and then bent a whole life around the waiting until she was right.

--- Then your machine did not find it. A person found it.

--- A person always finds it. The machine only keeps her honest. \ldots{} And I begin to think you have already guessed whose life that was.

--- \ldots{} Then a man who had your drawings, and enough of them, could do a great deal more than know that the Mountain is there. He could count us. Every one of these knocks is a Keeper dead and a Keeper risen in his place. Give a patient man enough of them, laid out across enough years, and he holds the roll of every succession in all five offices. How often we change hands. In which of the five. How long a Keeper tends to stand before the chain takes him under. He would know the rhythm of our dying and never once have looked on a face.

--- He would.

--- And more than that. You said the knock does not change. The same small parts, the same spacing, always, across a boy's life and a grieving man's. So it is not the ceremony that makes the sound, because the ceremony changes; the hall is warmer or colder, the crowd is larger, the robes are older or newer, the hands on the gong are ten different hands. The knock does not change. Which means the knock is not the men at all. It is the thing the men serve. It is that thing saying the one word it has, under the floor, to prove it still answers when a new hand is laid on it. \ldots{} I stood in that room. Twice. I heard the thing speak its word beneath my own feet, and I told myself a house was settling.

--- You were a boy of fifteen the first time. And you were burying your teacher the second.

--- I was Veil of Light. I was trained past every other man in that Hall to know the one thing: when a thing set before me has been set there on purpose, by a hand, to be seen or to be missed. It was the whole of my discipline and the whole of my pride. And the single time in my life it would have mattered above all others, I heard a machine speak its own name in the dark under my feet, and I called it a house ticking in the cold.

\centerline{[\,---\,]}

--- Your people kept a rite so close that no Veil in eleven thousand years was let through the door of it. And every time they performed it, the ground carried the knock out from under the Hall, and out under the desert, and the desert did not stop it.

--- Someone drew it.

--- Someone drew it. \ldots{} I was given a folder when I was sent here. It is a thin thing at my level; I am trusted with little, and I had thought that an insult until this week. It holds what a fact-checker needs to catch a man in a lie about the very ground his people are supposed to stand on: a few traces, a few dates typed down the margin in a hand that is not mine, and one instruction, to see whether the drowned people's mountain had ever once announced itself in a way the outside record could be made to confirm. I took it for a test of you. I laid it aside for four days because you never gave me a lie to catch. Look. This is the morning the Faith woman rose, give or take a season, reckoned off your fifteenth year. This is the morning your teacher's Keeper rose, four years on. And these two are the lines the house holds nearest to those two mornings.

--- \ldots{}

--- Take your time. I am not the man who can read the last of it off the page. I know enough to be dangerous with it and no further. But even I can see a matched pair when it is set in front of me.

--- \ldots{} They are the same.

--- They are close. I have told you I am not the one who can tell you how close.

--- Then who is.

\centerline{[\,---\,]}

--- Paul. You were engaged to establish that this man is who he says he is. Nothing beyond it.

--- I established it. The first afternoon, near enough.

--- And then you kept walking.

--- I heard a knock. Twice, in his account, at the same place, and both times he stepped over it and would not say why. So I went in the night to see whether the ground had heard it too. That is the whole of the work you hired me for, done a little further than you asked.

--- A little further than I asked. \ldots{} Give me the two you built. Yes. And now these. They were drawn by a better instrument than ever made yours, and they have been kept better since, in a case, in the dark, for longer than you have stood upright. Lay them over his. One to one. \ldots{} There. You see the fit of it. That is the fit of a key in a lock that was cut for it.

--- That is not possible. No one is outside the desert. No one hears the Mountain. That is not doctrine, woman, that is the first thing, the thing that stands under every other thing, the ground the ground stands on.

--- It is the oldest thing your people believe, and it is the least true of all of them, and I am sorry to be the one holding the proof of it in her hand. I do not sound sorry. I have held it too long to sound like anything. Every time your five went behind their door and did their small exact thing in the dark, the whole earth carried it. For a very long age no one out here had an ear fine enough to catch it, or any reason to turn such an ear on an empty quarter of the Sahara where the maps promise nothing at all. And then, once, someone had both.

--- \ldots{} I have stood my whole life with my back to that wall. We all have. A child of the Mountain is taught it before he is taught to count: the desert keeps us, the outside cannot see us, no ear alive reaches the Mountain. Every hard thing my people ever did, they did leaning their whole weight on that one wall. And you are telling me the wall was never there. That while a Keeper opened his own body to hold the world shut, and a boy wept under a pillar, and a woman I loved taught herself to be glad inside a cage, there was a woman in a cold country with the whole of our secret hung out drying on a line.

--- I am telling you exactly that.

--- How long have you had this.

--- Longer than you have been alive.

--- \ldots{} These two are mine. Do you understand what you are holding. These are my mornings. This is the woman I watched wounded at the throat when I was a boy. This is the old man I watched climb into a stone box and die on purpose while I stood under the pillar of Light and wept. And you have them here, on a strip of paper, in a case, in a house in Switzerland.

--- I have every one your Mountain ever made. There are not a great many. Your Keepers do not die often, and that mercy is exactly what made the thing findable in the end: something very small, repeated exactly, at long and patient intervals, out of a place the maps all swear holds nothing but distance. A machine running slow. A machine so sure of its own silence between the strokes that it never thought to wonder whether anyone had learned to wait out the silence.

--- You have gathered these for longer than I have been alive. Not one of them. Every one. \ldots{} A person does not spend a lifetime collecting each stroke of a slow machine to satisfy herself the machine is there. You were sure of that by the second stroke. You gather them to learn the machine. Its habits. The one hour it is weakest.

--- And when is a thing weakest. Say it, since you are so quick this morning.

--- When it changes hands. That is what your knock is. Your knock is the sound of the Mountain changing hands, the one morning in a generation when the grip on an office is loosest, when the five are apart behind their door and the new hand has not yet closed on the thing. And you have kept the calendar of that exact morning, in the dark, in a case, for longer than I have drawn breath.

--- \ldots{}

--- And you keep me here in your house, and you set this man to open me on the table like a fish, and you lay my own two mornings out in front of me, and it is not to frighten me and it is not to do me honour. It is to hold your calendar up against a man who stood in the rooms while it was being written. I am not a wonder to you. I am the one correction you have waited an age to make to your instrument.

--- You are both of those things, and I have never in my life found the two hard to carry in the same hand. It is the only gift I was born with. I built everything else out of it.

--- I was engaged to hold a record against a man and see whether the man would break. I begin to understand that for four days I have been holding a man against a record, and that it was the record you cared for, and the man was only the last thing you needed before you could be sure of it.

--- Now you are quick as well. It is a poor week for me, Paul, when the help begins to think.

--- \ldots{} What did you do. When you found it.

--- There was a fire in the desert, once, when I was young. The largest fire men had yet learned to make, and the whole nervous world bent an ear toward that desert to hear it fall. And underneath the fire, very small, in the wrong place, arriving the wrong way and long after it should have finished, there was something else. Everyone in that desert was listening to the bomb. \ldots{} I was listening to the thing hiding behind it. That is how I found the Mountain.

\end{room}
