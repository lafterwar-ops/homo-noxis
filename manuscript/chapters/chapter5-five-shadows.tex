\begin{room}

--- The twenty-third of November; nine o'clock; the fourth of our interviews. I have brought you two gifts this morning. Here is the first. 

--- \ldots{} Kepler's five solids of \textit{God Himself}. Thank you. They are cold in the hand, and lighter than they look. Beautiful.

--- In truth they were another man's invention: \textit{Pythagoras} conceived them; Kepler only borrowed them to explain the universe. In any case, as you see, they are hollow: only the edges.

--- This one is mine. The fewest faces; the sharpest point; the one you can set down no way but upright, a little flame standing on the table. Light comes first, and complexity comes after. This one is surely Light's. The cube, perhaps, is Shield: a closed door on every side. The third, for its beauty, may be Faith. The other two I cannot yet place: Justice speaks last, which would leave Balance the fourth, and Justice the largest of them. It is not yet clear to me; I shall think on it.

--- The second gift I built myself, from a child's set, and I am not too proud to say I enjoyed it. Gears within gears within gears. You see: a full turn of this first gear makes the second click but once; and a full turn of the second makes the third click once as well. So it would take\ldots{} how many teeth upon these gears?

--- \ldots{}

--- Forty teeth. So you see, for that last gear to turn once and ring a gong, the second must turn forty times, or sixteen hundred teeth; and sixty-four thousand upon that first gear. Add two gears more, and we are past a hundred million; and one needs only fifteen million seconds to make half a year. Here, look for yourself.

--- \ldots{}

--- And there is something of my world I should like to share in turn. My own kind has lately built the very same design: a clock that ticks once a year. We take it for a wholly new idea, only a few years old. Here, I have printed you a picture of it. And your kind had the same thought eleven and a half millennia ago.

--- Is your world under some threat, then? Something you are trying to outlast through time, as we outlast the Outsiders?

--- Yes; and it moves slowly, across centuries, perhaps millennia. There is much debate as to its nature, whether it is real at all, and what ought to be done. Not unlike your own. Should you hear of it during instruction, do ask me again. But to return to the clock: your Pendulum, does it ever stop?

--- Yes: once in a lifetime, and only for an instant, upon the ascension of a new Keeper of Light. For Light came before Time; and when the chain of Light is renewed, when Light dies and is reborn, God holds His breath for an instant. Then the Pendulum begins again.

--- Then it is surely being wound, most likely by a weight. As the weight falls, it yields just enough to overcome the friction that would otherwise still the pendulum within a day; and the weight is raised again at intervals, according to its mass and the nature of the pendulum. And with a train of gears upon gears upon gears, the teeth carefully numbered, your clock might be made to strike but twice a year. I thought I ought to show you that. The toy you may keep, to study. Now, unless you wish to discuss instruction, let us begin.

--- You must forgive me the philosophy of doubt you would not hear of two days ago. I cannot help but be philosophical, Paul. I was taught to see mechanism as doctrine, and doctrine as mechanism; I look upon a thing, and my mind turns it over until I see how the doctrine both makes the object, and how the object makes the doctrine. Your clock is the perfect instance of an object whose birth comes from doctrine.

--- And what doctrine, what mechanism, did you find in the \textit{Species Odyssey}, in the journey from monkey to man?

--- It was one plain thing the film would not say aloud, that I could not stop hearing underneath it. Your man, climbing up out of the animals across all those ages: do you know what I have decided drove him up the ladder? Not hunger. The beasts have hunger, and stayed beasts. It was that he could be refused. A creature that can be told no, told not you, not now, not yet, is a creature that will build a wonder to be told yes. And a people that learns to refuse itself, by law, to dam the whole flood and open the gate only on the appointed day: that people turns every ungiven night into a cathedral. We did. We bred our Veils to their office, we scheduled every touch, we made an entire science of the thing we would not do; and all of it, every hunger we sent away hungry, came back out of us as light and water and law. Your kind is splendid and cruel, Paul, inventive and self-devouring, free and enslaving; the one animal that carries a god and a beast in the one skin, and cannot always tell, in the moment, which of the two is speaking. And I did not learn that from watching my people. I learned it watching myself. Everything I ever made of myself, I made to be looked at, once, by a girl of Shield who never quite managed to. The beam, the ascent, the whole of my cleverness: a boy raising a cathedral to be told yes, and told, in the end, nothing at all. I cannot look at your whole species climbing its ladder without knowing my own small scramble up it. And it frightens me, because I have begun to wonder what a man builds when the girl is not one girl but a world of them, and no night is ever ungiven: whether he raises a greater cathedral than we did, or lies down in the warm water of every easy yes and lets the light go out, and never once wonders why.

--- These are subjects our kind is loath to discuss. When such a conversation begins, the spirit is apt to be possessed by the Beast, to borrow your language, and it is generally the wiser course to change the subject. Thank you for your interpretation; I shall see that it appears in my account. Now: how long did it take you to learn every single beam on the Mirror?

Four years: Va-Raedin of Light had not lied; there was no shorter road. And I shall spare you the account of each beam, for it would give away the exact geometry of the Mountain. Let me take it up again at the morning Va-Raedin judged me worthy to be taught the five ways that light can lie. \par

\ornament

I came back to Light with the desert still in my sandals. I had cleaned them. I had even scraped the seams with a bone pin until one strap began to fray, and it had not been enough. Each step toward the eastern practice halls gave back a faint dry rasp, and the sound annoyed me because it seemed to report where I had been. The Oasis had followed me under stone. So had the sky, and the Book of Blood, and Va-Sheva's hand cutting the air at the marker line before any explanation could reach it. Two small wrongnesses at a threshold, and Shield had taught me at the cedar gate that they are not to be treated one at a time.\par

I had slept badly. The basin beside my mat was still, but my mind was not, and it kept returning to the token I had let fall into the water, to the sign of Light thickening and sliding when I moved my head, to the desert flash and the turned marker, to Va-Sheva refusing to say whether she had gone beyond the second ridge. The outside had multiplied the number of things that wanted an answer, and I carried all of them into a place built to give me only one.\par

Va-Raedin was waiting at the first eastern passage. He looked once at my sandals, once at the hem of my robe, once at the linen around my thumb.\par

``You went outside,'' he said.\par

``Yes, Veil.''\par

``And returned.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``That is the usual aim of controlled prayer access.'' He turned and began to walk before he asked the real question. ``At the Oasis, did the light behave?''\par

``No.''\par

``Good. Then you saw something.''\par

``Outside light is too full,'' I said. ``It comes from too many places at once. It strikes everything. Inside, light is disciplined. Outside, it hides by abundance.''\par

He glanced back, almost approving. ``And inside?''\par

``Inside it hides by scarcity.''\par

``Better.''\par

The word went into me too easily. I tried not to look pleased and failed by enough for him to see it.\par

``Come,'' he said. ``Today we begin again.''\par

We did not enter the first practice hall. He led me past the mirror rooms, past the chamber where the point had trembled on the wall the day I first embarrassed myself, and into a low service slit beside a lamp niche. The passage descended. The lamps were set near the floor, so their light kept my feet visible while the rest of me passed through dark. It made me feel divided --- obedient below, unwitnessed above. I have been in interrogation rooms since that were lit on exactly that principle, and I recognised them the moment I walked in.\par

At the end stood a plain door of pale wood. No sign of Light, no inscription, no aperture, no visible lock. Va-Raedin stopped.\par

``What do you see?''\par

``A wooden door in Light territory.''\par

``Better than yesterday. Continue.''\par

``It does not belong. Light uses stone, metal, glass, mirror. Wood swells and warps and burns and remembers damp.''\par

``Therefore?''\par

``Either it is unimportant, or its changing is the point.''\par

He opened it. Beyond was a room almost entirely dark. One lamp burned outside and behind us, high in the passage wall, and its light entered through a narrow slit in the door and cut a single hard line across the floor. He closed the door. The line stayed.\par

``Five shadows,'' he said.\par

I looked down. ``There is only one.''\par

``That is why you are Candidate.''\par

The answer irritated me exactly as much as it was meant to. He moved to the first covered object but did not uncover it.\par

``Yesterday you learned that light can embarrass you. Today you learn the principal forms by which it does. Name the first.''\par

``Mirrors.''\par

``That is the object. Name the danger.''\par

``Reversal.''\par

``A child's answer.''\par

``Vanity.''\par

``A moralist's answer.''\par

I looked at the covered mirror, the slit of light, the door at my back. A mirror does not merely reverse. It puts a sight somewhere other than where the body stands. It lets the eye travel without the man.\par

``Displacement,'' I said. ``Sight away from the body.''\par

He removed the cloth. The mirror beneath was tall and narrow, angled toward the far wall. I expected to see myself. Instead I saw the door behind me, the slit of light, the passage beyond it, and standing in that passage a figure where no figure stood. I turned before I could think. The passage was empty. When I looked back into the glass, the figure was still there.\par

``Who is it?'' Va-Raedin asked.\par

``No one.''\par

``Then why did you turn?''\par

``Because I believed the sight before I questioned the path.''\par

``Good.'' He adjusted the mirror by less than a finger's width. The figure vanished, and in its place was the back of my own head. ``A mirror shows what is behind the eye. That is its use, and its corruption; one fact, not two. Sight does not travel where the face points. Learn that, or stay Candidate.''\par

He set a small bronze token behind me, outside direct sight. ``Find it without turning.''\par

I used the tall mirror first. The token surfaced near the reflected door, but the distance was a guess. I shifted left and it slid behind my reflected shoulder; I shifted right and it seemed to withdraw. A smaller polished plate near the water trough caught the floor in a second reflection, distorted but useful, and I combined the two views, stepped back, and touched my heel to the ground until I heard the soft click of metal under my sandal. I bent and took the token without turning. He accepted it back.\par

``First shadow?''\par

``Reflection displaces sight. It lets the eye stand where the body is not.''\par

``And the danger?''\par

``The sight may be true and still not be where I think it is.''\par

``That will stand.''\par

The word wounded less than it had the day before, and that made it more dangerous, not less. He covered the mirror.\par

He lowered one lamp and raised another, and the room turned monstrous. The trough became a black wall across the floor. The covered mirror threw a wedge that reached the far stone, and the hard line from the door forked and multiplied wherever it crossed a standing thing.\par

``Second shadow.''\par

``Shadows.''\par

``Object.''\par

``Interruption.''\par

``Danger?''\par

``Assuming darkness is empty.''\par

``Find what is hidden.''\par

I looked at the floor first and disliked myself for the predictability. The largest darkness pulled at my eye, so I refused it and looked at the edges instead. A shadow is not absence; it is the record of something entering the path. A hidden object might cast one. It might also hide inside another object's shadow, or give itself away by keeping a shadow from falling where it should. The rods cast five dark lines, and the trough a heavy black band. I knelt by the band and found nothing. I stood, looked at the wall, then back to the floor, and there it was --- a faint brightness where darkness should have climbed. Something low was blocking the floor-light but not the wall-light. I reached into the trough's shadow and found a black cord hanging from a hidden hook.\par

``What is it?'' he asked.\par

``A cord.''\par

``What does it do?''\par

I pulled it before judgment arrived. A panel opened behind me with a hard click, and I froze. It revealed only stone. A false consequence --- but his silence made it clear the real one had already happened. He crossed the room, closed the panel, and came back.\par

``Shadow hid the cord. Pride pulled it.''\par

I lowered my eyes.\par

``If that had opened a gate?''\par

``I should have waited.''\par

``If that had released water?''\par

``I should have waited.''\par

``If that had alarmed Shield?''\par

``I should have waited.''\par

``You came back from the Oasis with Shield in your mind. Keep it there. Seeing is not permission. Finding is not permission. A Candidate learns where light falls. He does not become master of everything light touches.''\par

I thought of the old woman at the spring, the leaf in her hand, and Va-Sheva saying that meaning no harm was not the same as staying harmless.\par

``I understand.''\par

``No,'' he said. ``You agree. Understanding is not required.''\par

That struck me before I could stop it: Shield at the cedar gate, and Shield now in Va-Raedin's mouth. Either someone had told him what had been said to me out there, or the Mountain's offices had been saying the same sentences to boys for centuries. I had stopped trusting that kind of coincidence.\par

``Second shadow?''\par

``Darkness is evidence of interruption.''\par

``And the danger?''\par

``That discovery tempts the hand before judgment catches up.''\par

``Better.''\par

``Third shadow.''\par

``Lenses.''\par

``Object.''\par

``False importance.''\par

He did not correct me at once. ``Explain.''\par

``A lens makes one thing larger and another disappear. If a man does not know the lens is there, he mistakes focus for importance.''\par

``Better.''\par

The second station held a glass disk hung from three bronze cords. Behind it, on the wall, were five dark marks no larger than seeds. A side lamp threw light through the disk so that one seed-mark swelled into a patch the size of my palm while the others nearly vanished.\par

``Which mark matters?'' he asked.\par

``Closest to what?''\par

``To judgment.''\par

``That is not a place.''\par

``It is always a place. You only learn its position too late.''\par

I moved around the disk. The large mark blurred when I stepped aside; another, nearly invisible from the first position, sharpened; lowering my eye swelled the second while the first smeared. Each mark could be made to dominate if the glass, the lamp, and the observer agreed to make it so.\par

``None matters by itself,'' I said. ``The lens grants the importance.''\par

``The hand that places the lens grants it. The lens only obeys its shape.'' He tilted the disk, and the enlarged mark stretched into a long black wound. ``What is it now?''\par

``The same mark.''\par

``Would a crowd know that?''\par

``No.''\par

``Would Faith?''\par

``Faith might make it speak.''\par

``Balance?''\par

``Measure it.''\par

``Shield?''\par

``Keep people back until it knew whether the mark mattered.''\par

``Justice?''\par

I hesitated. ``Ask who placed the lens.''\par

``And Light?''\par

I looked at the wound-shaped mark. ``Light must know how easily the seed became a wound.''\par

``Good.''\par

We worked the lenses until my eyes ached. Glass disks, water-filled bulbs, oil jars, thin plates that widened light or narrowed it or sharpened one edge while ruining another. He made me stand close, then far, then above and below, then with one eye shut, then viewing by reflection first. Each success made the room less trustworthy, until size and clarity and nearness had all become suspect.\par

``Third shadow?''\par

``Focus creates false importance.''\par

``And the danger?''\par

``Seeing sharply can be another form of blindness.''\par

His face did not move. ``Careful. That one is almost useful.''\par

``You enjoy dangerous sentences.''\par

``I thought it was precise.''\par

``That is the usual excuse.''\par

He allowed water then --- a small cup, plain clay. I drank too fast and caught myself watching the rim instead of the room.\par

``You have been staring at water since yesterday,'' he said.\par

``It does strange things.''\par

``Everything does strange things when attention arrives late.''\par

``I dropped the token in my basin.''\par

``I assumed you would.''\par

``You knew?''\par

``I knew you would sleep, admire it, or misuse it. Sleep was unlikely. Admiration would bore you. Misuse teaches.''\par

I felt exposed and robbed at once. The discovery had felt private, and now it seemed Light had prepared even that humiliation in advance.\par

``I saw the sign shift through the water,'' I said.\par

``The sign did not shift.''\par

``I know. The light did.''\par

His gaze sharpened. Not praise, not alarm. Attention.\par

The fourth room was bright, white-walled, severe. A fixed lamp cast a beam down through a bronze slit onto a table cut with narrow channels. At the far end, under glass, lay a small black mark. The beam fell too far left to touch it.\par

``Strike the mark with light.''\par

I inspected the apparatus. The lamp did not move. The slit did not move. No mirror, no plate, no hidden aperture offered itself. On the table stood clear water, oil, crystal, a cloudy cup, a sealed clay pot, and several shallow recesses.\par

``May I move the vessels?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``May I open the pot?''\par

``No.''\par

I began with clear water. The beam crossed it and turned, but not enough. Oil thickened it and blurred its edge. Crystal turned it more sharply but broke the light where I needed a single clean point. The cloudy liquid scattered too much, waking the beam inside the cup and leaving it weak beyond.\par

``Name the event.''\par

``The beam changes path when it crosses a substance.''\par

``Which substance?''\par

``Water, oil, glass, whatever the cloudy liquid is.''\par

``And air?''\par

I almost dismissed air. Then I remembered the desert trembling in its own heat, the mist shining behind Faith's rehearsal in the passage above the work hall, the blue flame in the Hall bending toward Va-Elun. ``Air also,'' I said.\par

``Usually less. Not never.''\par

No single vessel struck the mark cleanly. Water and crystal broke the beam. Oil before water made it wander; water before oil came closer but ruined the edge. I wanted the clay pot, and he watched me want it.\par

``What is in the pot?'' I asked.\par

``That is what you are asking permission to learn.''\par

``Salt.''\par

He said nothing. The lid was sealed with Balance green wax --- a marked pot on a table of water and turning light. Salt changed water. I lifted the cloudy cup, smelled it, found the mineral bitterness.\par

``Salted water.''\par

Still no confirmation. I set the cloudy cup under the beam. The light turned more than in clear water, less cleanly than through crystal, and struck near the mark. I moved the cup a finger's width and the beam touched the black point.\par

``Acceptable.''\par

``That was the answer.''\par

``That was the prepared answer. Again, without the salted cup.''\par

I looked at the table, then at the sealed pot. ``May I open it now?''\par

``No.''\par

Clear water, oil, crystal, the salted cup removed, the sealed pot, the fixed lamp, the black mark, the channels in the table. I had taken the channels for drainage --- but drainage is a story one tells before knowing what has been drained. I poured clear water into a recess and scraped a little white grain from the table's edge with my fingernail.\par

``That is theft from Balance,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``It was already spilled.''\par

``It was already unmeasured.''\par

I stopped. Then he said, ``Continue.''\par

I stirred the grains into the water. Nothing changed to the eye. I put the recess under the beam and the light turned a little more --- not enough. Another scrape, more stirring, another test. Each grain became morally expensive. At last the beam struck the mark cleanly.\par

``How much salt?''\par

``Enough.''\par

``A cook's answer.''\par

``I do not know.''\par

``A dangerous answer, but honest.''\par

``It looks the same,'' I said, looking at the water.\par

``Many changed things do.''\par

``And the light knows.''\par

``If one knows where to look.''\par

Water could be altered without announcing it. Light could reveal the alteration, or use it. Salt could change the path without moving lamp, slit, vessel, or mark.\par

``Fourth shadow?''\par

``Turning. Light changes path when it crosses substance.''\par

``And the danger?''\par

``The condition may change invisibly.''\par

He took the bowl away before I could study it further.\par

The last chamber held a long trough of clear water set into black stone. A narrow tube beneath it carried light from a hidden source up into the water itself. At one end, below the waterline, a small white mark had been painted; at the other, a bronze plate stood dry outside the trough. The beam travelled underwater toward the surface at a shallow angle and vanished. Or seemed to.\par

``Make the beam strike the bronze plate.''\par

I leaned over the trough. The beam came up against the water's skin so shallow that if it escaped it should strike the air above and reach the plate. It did not. I passed my hand above the surface; no light touched me. I crouched lower, and at the surface the beam turned downward and stayed inside the water. The water had become a mirror.\par

``It cannot leave,'' I said.\par

``What cannot?''\par

``The light. No --- the beam. When it reaches the surface at that angle, it does not pass into air. It returns into the water.''\par

``Like what?''\par

``A mirror.''\par

``Water is not a mirror.''\par

``It is here.''\par

``Why?''\par

I looked at the angle, then at the surface. The water had not changed. The line had. ``If the beam rises steeply, it leaves. If it comes too shallow, the surface holds it.''\par

``Threshold return,'' he said. ``Every crossing has a condition. Some thresholds forgive. Some refuse. A door is not the only thing that can open or close.''\par

The phrase sounded old. Not invented for the room --- retrieved from somewhere deeper in Light.\par

``Change it,'' he said. ``Make it leave.''\par

I searched for controls. The source did not move. The tube, the trough, the beam's angle --- all fixed. A lever raised the water by the width of a fingernail; the beam returned. I lowered it; no use. I disturbed the surface with a rod; the beam scattered and resumed.\par

``Do not bruise water because you dislike its answer.''\par

I stepped back. Change the condition. ``Salt,'' I said.\par

This time a small open dish of coarse crystals waited beside the trough. I took a pinch and let it fall. The grains caught light as they sank. Nothing, then a tremble. I stirred, carefully, and the light shifted --- but downward, held more firmly inside the water than before.\par

``Worse,'' I said.\par

``Worse for what?''\par

``For reaching the plate.''\par

``For leaving the water, yes.''\par

The distinction opened the whole problem. Salt had not made the water better or worse. It had moved the threshold. More salt held the beam in; less might release it. I added clear water from a side vessel and the beam rose. At one mixture a faint trace escaped the surface and fell short of the plate. I lowered the level, diluted again, worked the trough's side gate, and the escaping trace strengthened and struck the lower edge of the bronze. A pale mark appeared on the metal.\par

``Acceptable.''\par

``No.'' The word came before obedience could stop it. He turned. I felt the danger at once, and the brightness in me was still too quick. ``I mean --- this is more than acceptable.''\par

``What is it?''\par

I tried to slow the words enough to make them lawful. ``It is a surface that becomes a mirror without being made into one. A door that opens or closes because of angle and water. A way to change a path when the source cannot be moved.''\par

His face did not change, but the room seemed to tighten. ``Continue.''\par

``If light can be kept inside water, water can carry it where air cannot. If salt decides whether it leaves or stays, then the same trough can show, or hide, or carry. A man looking down may see nothing, or see himself, or see through, or never know that light is moving beneath him.''\par

I stopped because the thoughts were arriving faster than I could make them safe. He covered the salt.\par

``That is enough.''\par

``No.'' Again, too quick. I lowered my voice. ``Please. One more thought. If the source is fixed, and the mark is fixed, and the angle is fixed, the answer is still not fixed. You change what the light crosses. You change what the light is allowed to do.''\par

He regarded me in silence. ``You have learned too much for one morning.''\par

It pleased me, and then it frightened me. ``Is that possible?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``For Light?''\par

``Especially for Light.''\par

He opened a hidden plug. The water drained, the beam broke and blurred and guttered into useless glow, and the trough became a trough, the bronze plate only bronze.\par

``You ended it,'' I said.\par

``I ended your belief that discovery entitles continuation.''\par

``I was not done.''\par

``That was visible.''\par

We came back to the first chamber of the Five Shadows. The mirror stood covered, the lenses hung still, the troughs and vessels waited, and the room felt rearranged though nothing in it had moved. A room changes when the mind finds a new way to be deceived by it.\par

``Name them,'' he said.\par

``Reflection. Shadow. Focus. Turning. Threshold return.''\par

``Not enough.''\par

``Sight away from the body. Darkness as interruption and evidence. False importance by focus. Light changing path through substance. Water becoming mirror at the surface.''\par

He waited, and I understood the silence only once it began to hurt. ``And each one is dangerous because each one can be arranged.''\par

``Now you are naming the danger.''\par

It did not satisfy me. It opened hunger. ``Are these the five lessons?''\par

``They are five lessons.''\par

``Not the five?''\par

``Do not turn grammar into revelation.''\par

``But they are ordered.''\par

``Everything in Light is ordered.''\par

``Mirrors first because I must distrust where sight stands. Shadows because I must distrust absence. Lenses because I must distrust importance. Turning because I must distrust the path. Threshold return because I must distrust the surface.''\par

His eyes narrowed by almost nothing. ``Who taught you that?''\par

``You did.''\par

``No. I showed you rooms.''\par

``The rooms taught the order.''\par

``Rooms do not teach.''\par

``Then whoever arranged them did.''\par

A silence entered that I had not met before. Not Faith's silence, full of prayer; not Shield's, full of readiness; not Justice's, waiting for a sequence. Light silence: a withheld angle. At last he said, ``A Candidate learns where light falls.''\par

I knew the answering sentence. A Veil learns who placed it. I did not say it. ``A Candidate also learns where he has been made to stand,'' I said instead.\par

He held my gaze. Then, unexpectedly, he laughed once --- a short, unwilling sound, gone before I could decide whether it had happened.\par

``You are dangerous when tired.''\par

``Is that praise?''\par

``No.''\par

``Is it warning?''\par

``Yes.''\par

That was almost better than praise.\par

I knew some of them --- aperture, lamp, eye, mirror, hand. Others I did not: a bowl beneath a star, a line entering water and returning, five small marks set not in a circle but in a descending sequence. I slowed. He stopped at once.\par

``What do you see?''\par

``Old marks.''\par

``That is not what I asked.''\par

``They are not decoration.''\par

``Very little in the Mountain is decoration.''\par

``Records?''\par

``Of a kind.''\par

``Of a kind.''\par

``That distinction is convenient.''\par

``They usually are.''\par

I wanted to ask about the panel, and about what a Veil learns after he stops being Candidate, and whether the rooms had been built for me or whether every Candidate reached this same edge and was turned back from it. I asked none of it. He seemed almost pleased, which annoyed me.\par

We came out near the eastern practice corridor. Beyond the walls the ordinary Mountain went on --- carts, children, water, the drag of gate chains, low voices. Outside Light, repetition sustains life. Inside Light, repetition concealed escalation: a test came back as a lesson, a lesson as a sign, a sign as a question.\par

At the candidate entrance he turned. ``You will write nothing of today.''\par

``I was not going to.''\par

``You were.''\par

``I do not keep a record.''\par

``No. You keep arrangements in your head and mistake that for obedience.''\par

I had no answer.\par

``You will not speak of salted water to another Candidate.''\par

``No, Veil.''\par

``You will not ask Balance for salt outside assignment.''\par

``No, Veil.''\par

``You will not attempt threshold return in basin, cup, channel, washing pool, jar, trough, or any other vessel whose proper use your curiosity believes it can improve.''\par

I looked down. ``No, Veil.''\par

``Good.'' This time the word was not praise. It was a seal.\par

He began to turn away, then stopped. ``Sa-Kailan.'' The new name still caught inside me. ``Yes?'' ``What did you learn?''\par

I could have named the five shadows again and made them clean and pleasing. I wanted the praise that comes when a structure settles correctly in another mind. But the answer was not the list. The answer was the panel, and the gallery, and the order, and the fact that he had ended the morning not when I failed but when I understood too quickly.\par

``I learned that a path can be hidden inside a lesson.''\par

His face changed by almost nothing. Almost.\par

``You learned,'' he said, ``that you enjoy dangerous sentences.''\par

``That too.''\par

``You will suffer for both.''\par

Then he left.\par

\ornament

I stayed in the passage, alone but not unwatched. I could still feel the desert in me though stone stood on every side; I could still hear the Book of Blood accusing the open land; I could still see Va-Sheva's hand cutting the air before explanation could reach it. Reflection, shadow, focus, turning, threshold return --- behind the eye, inside the interruption, through the lens, across the substance, and at the surface that would not let light leave. The five lessons had not only taught me how to see. They had taught me that a room could be asking a question while pretending to correct an answer.\par

Everything they would one day try me for began as a kindness in that room. The mirror had taught me to set a sight where my body was not. The shadow, to read what a darkness had failed to cover. The lens, to make a small thing carry more weight than it owned. The turning, to change what a substance did to a light without laying a hand on the light itself. And the last had taught me to keep a proof moving beneath a surface that showed a watching man nothing at all. Five instruments, put into my hand and forbidden me in the same morning. They are worth holding in mind. Each of them returns, later, in my own hands, doing the work Va-Raedin swore they were only meant to name.\par

No ordinary Candidate was meant to reach the end of a path by asking whether the lessons were a path. I knew that. And I knew, with a brightness that frightened me more than any pride, that somewhere in Light there was an end to be reached.\par

That is the morning I keep coming back to, more than the cutting or the ceremony. Not because anything forbidden happened in it --- nothing did. They taught a boy to see, carefully, and looked away at the exact wrong moment, and by the time I walked out I was already carrying the one thing they could never take back, which was the suspicion that the people teaching me to see had built the room I was seeing in.\par

\begin{room}

--- Turn it off, Paul.

--- It has never troubled you before.

--- It had never been heard by anyone but you before. I have changed my mind about your machine: it is not your safe channel; it is your leak. Turn it off.

--- There. It is done. You would not ask that of me on a wet lawn for a small thing.

--- You went behind the glass. Do not tell me what you would wish were true; tell me only what you saw.

--- There were two men. One I did not know. He was there some hours, and it was not idle: a trade, a man at his work.

--- Then the wager is settled, and you are the one soul in this house who cannot pretend otherwise: to pretend a test came back the way you wanted is the single thing your whole religion forbids you. You told me you trust what survives its test. This survived. So by your creed, Paul, yours and not mine, I am owed you.

--- That is a great deal to hang on a lawn.

--- Hear the rest before you decide I have bought you. A bargain has two ends. You are mine; and I am yours, which is the half no one warns a man of. I have no one else in the world. You have chosen, without meaning to, the losing side of a house you did not know was at war; and I have chosen the one Englishman in it who will not lie to me. We belong to each other now. That is the whole of what \textit{mine} has ever meant, in my tongue or in yours.

--- And what does a man buy with two strangers behind a glass.

--- Something worse than being owned. Think it through as I have had to. If those men are not the house's, and they are not, for you would know your own guards, then a hand has been put \textit{inside} Horizon. Which means the woman who keeps it does not know her own walls have ears. Which means she is not the hunter in this thing: she is the hunted. And she does not know it, and we are in the snare beside her, watching it close.

--- And what would you have two men do about it, on a wet morning, with nothing.

--- What my people do when the wall is breached and the enemy is already in the corridors. You do not fight him room to room: you go to the one hand that can still shut the gate. I must stand in front of Sarah, Paul. Not in a month. And you are the man who is going to put me there: because you are mine, and because it is also the right thing, and I have lived long enough to know that those two are rarely the same road, and this once they are.

--- Why do you take up that stone.

--- Because a man should carry one thing in his pocket that no hand gave him. Leave it be.

--- And the reels?

--- From this day you carry them as you would carry your own throat. They are the whole of me: whoever holds them holds me, and through me holds her. Do not let them out of your hand, nor your sight, nor into any drawer another man keeps a key to. Swear me that one small thing, or say so now, and we end it here, and I go back up the hill to be a clever animal doing sums in front of your films.

--- You have my word.

--- There is a word, in my world, for two set to watch the ones who watch. I will give it to you properly one day, and all that comes with it. Not today. Today you need only the first rule of it: the one your people and mine will both know on sight.

--- Then keep your face, and walk me back, before the cold finds your chest and the physician finds us both.

\end{room}
