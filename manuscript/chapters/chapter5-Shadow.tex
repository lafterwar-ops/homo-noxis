\begin{room}

--- The twenty-third of November; nine o'clock; the fourth of our interviews. I have brought you two gifts this morning. Here is the first. 

--- \ldots{} Kepler's five solids of \textit{God Himself}. Thank you. They are cold in the hand, and lighter than they look. Beautiful.

--- In truth they were another man's invention: \textit{Pythagoras} conceived them; \textit{Kepler} only borrowed them to explain the universe. In any case, as you see, they are hollow: only the edges.

--- This one is mine. The fewest faces; the sharpest point; the one you can set down no way but upright, a little flame standing on the table. Light comes first, and complexity comes after. This one is surely Light's. The cube, perhaps, is Shield: a closed door on every side. The third, for its beauty, may be Faith. The other two I cannot yet place: Justice speaks last, which would leave Balance the fourth, and Justice the largest of them. It is not yet clear to me; I shall think on it.

--- The second gift I built myself, from a child's set, and I am not too proud to say I enjoyed it. Gears within gears within gears. You see: a full turn of this first gear makes the second click but once; and a full turn of the second makes the third click once as well. So it would take\ldots{} how many teeth upon these gears?

\centerline{[\,---\,]}

--- Forty teeth. So you see, for that last gear to turn once and ring a gong, the second must turn forty times, or sixteen hundred teeth; and sixty-four thousand upon that first gear. Add two gears more, and we are past a hundred million; and one needs only fifteen million seconds to make half a year. Here, look for yourself.

\centerline{[\,---\,]}

--- And there is something of my world I should like to share in turn. My own kind has lately built the very same design: a clock that ticks once a year. We take it for a wholly new idea, only a few years old. Here, I have printed you a picture of it. And your kind had the same thought eleven and a half millennia ago.

--- Is your world under some threat, then? Something you are trying to outlast through time, as we try to outlast the Outsiders?

--- Yes; and it moves slowly, across centuries, perhaps millennia. There is much debate as to its nature, whether it is real at all, and what ought to be done. Not unlike your own. Should you hear of it during instruction, do ask me again. But to return to the clock: your Pendulum, does it ever stop?

--- Yes: once in a lifetime, and only for an instant, upon the ascension of a new Keeper of Light. For Light came before Time; and when the chain of Light is renewed, when Light dies and is reborn, God holds His breath for an instant. Then the Pendulum begins again.

--- Then it is surely being wound, most likely by a weight. As the weight falls, it yields just enough to overcome the friction that would otherwise still the pendulum within a day; and the weight is raised again at intervals, according to its mass and the nature of the pendulum. And with a train of gears upon gears upon gears, the teeth carefully numbered, your clock might be made to strike but twice a year. I thought I ought to show you that. The toy you may keep, to study. Now, unless you wish to discuss instruction, let us begin.

--- You must forgive me the \textit{philosophy of doubt} you would not hear of two days ago. I cannot help but be philosophical, Paul. I was taught to see mechanism as doctrine, and doctrine as mechanism; I look upon a thing, and my mind turns it over until I see how the doctrine both makes the object, and how the object makes the doctrine. Your clock is the perfect instance of an object whose birth comes from doctrine.

--- And what doctrine, what mechanism, did you find in the \textit{Species Odyssey}, in the journey from monkey to man?

--- It was one plain thing the three films would not say aloud, that I could not stop hearing underneath it. Your man, climbing up out of the animals across all those ages, and then from nomads to cities to nations: do you know what I have decided drove him up the ladder? Not hunger. The beasts have hunger, and stayed beasts. It was that he could be refused. When a group, or a system, can tell a creature no, and say not you, not now, not yet, that creature will build a wonder to be told yes. And a people that learns to refuse itself, by law, to dam the whole flood and open the gate only on the appointed day: that people turns every ungiven night into a cathedral. Your kind is splendid and cruel, Paul, inventive and self-devouring, free and enslaving; the one animal that carries a god and a beast in the one skin, and cannot always tell, in the moment, which of the two is speaking. And I did not learn that just from watching your people. I learned it watching myself. Everything I ever made of myself, I made to be looked at, once, by a girl of Shield who never quite managed to. The beam, the ascent, the whole of my cleverness: a boy raising a cathedral to be told yes, and told, in the end, nothing at all. I cannot look at your whole species climbing its ladder without knowing my own small scramble up it. And it frightens me, because I have begun to wonder what a man builds when the girl is not one girl but a world of them, and no night is ever ungiven: whether he raises a greater cathedral than we did, or lies down in the warm water of every easy yes and lets the light go out, and never once wonders why.

--- These are subjects our kind is loath to discuss. When such a conversation begins, spirits are apt to be possessed by the Beast, to borrow your language; and it is generally the wiser course to change the subject. Thank you for your interpretation; I shall see that it appears in my account. Now: how long did it take you to learn every single beam on the Mirror?

We entered the first practice hall. He led me past the mirror rooms, past the chamber where the point had trembled on the wall the day I embarrassed myself, and into a low service slit beside a lamp niche. The passage descended. A side passage breathed out cold and earth: the mushroom rooms, racked white to the roof, a woman going down them with a wetted cloth. The lamps were set near the floor, so their light kept my feet visible while the rest of me passed through dark. The lamp-cleaner's blackest leavings were kept: the soot he scraped from the flues and the lamp-throats was gathered, bound with gum and a little water, and became the ink the records were written in.\par

At the end stood a plain door of pale wood. No sign of Light, no inscription, no aperture, no visible lock. Va-Raedin stopped.\par

``What do you see?''\par

``A wooden door in Light territory. It does not belong. Light uses stone, metal, glass, mirror. Wood swells and warps and burns and remembers damp.''\par

``Therefore?''\par

``Either it is unimportant, or its changing is the point.''\par

He opened it. Beyond was a room almost entirely dark. One lamp burned outside and behind us, high in the passage wall, and its light entered through a narrow slit in the door and cut a single hard line across the floor. He closed the door. The line stayed.\par

``Five shadows. Today you learn the principal forms by which light lies. The first?''\par

``Mirrors.''\par

``That is the object. Name the danger.''\par

I looked at the covered mirror, the slit of light, the door at my back. A mirror could do more than reverse me. It could put what I saw somewhere away from where I stood, and let me look from a place my body had never reached.\par

``Displacement,'' I said. ``Sight away from the body.''\par

He removed the cloth. The mirror beneath was tall and narrow, angled toward the far wall. I saw the door behind me, the slit of light, the passage beyond it, and standing in that passage a figure where no figure stood. I turned before I could think. The passage was empty. When I looked back into the glass, the figure was still there.\par

``Who is it?'' Va-Raedin asked.\par

``No one.''\par

``Then why did you turn?''\par

``Because I believed the sight before I questioned the path.''\par

``Good.'' He adjusted the mirror by less than a finger's width. The figure vanished, and in its place was the back of my own head. ``A mirror shows what is behind the eye. That is its use, and its corruption. You were shown it — learn the difference, or stay Candidate.''\par

He covered the mirror.\par

``Second shadow.''\par

``Shadows.''\par

``Object.''\par

``Interruption.''\par

``Danger?''\par

``Assuming darkness is empty.''\par

``Find what is hidden.''\par

I looked at the floor first and disliked myself for the predictability. The largest darkness pulled at my eye, so I refused it and looked at the edges instead. A shadow meant something had crossed the light there. A hidden object might cast one. It might also hide inside another object's shadow, or give itself away by keeping a shadow from falling where it should. The rods cast five dark lines, and the trough a heavy black band. I knelt by the band and found nothing. I stood, looked at the wall, then back to the floor, and there it was --- a faint brightness where darkness should have climbed. Something low was blocking the floor-light alone. I reached into the trough's shadow and found a black cord hanging from a hidden hook.\par

``What is it?'' he asked.\par

``A cord.''\par

``What does it do?''\par

I pulled it before I had thought. A panel opened behind me with a hard click, and I froze. It revealed only stone. He crossed the room, closed the panel, and came back.\par

``Shadow hid the cord. Pride pulled it.''\par

I lowered my eyes.\par

``I should have waited.''\par

``I should have waited.''\par

``I know that Shield is often on your mind. Keep it there. \textit{Meaning no harm is not the same as remaining harmless}. Seeing is not permission. Finding is not permission. A Candidate learns where light falls. He does not become master of everything light touches.''\par

I had stopped trusting that kind of coincidence.\par

``Second shadow?''\par

``Darkness is evidence of interruption.''\par

``And the danger?''\par

``That discovery tempts the hand before judgment catches up.''\par

``Better.''\par

``Third shadow.''\par

``Lenses.''\par

``Object.''\par

The third station held a glass disk hung from three bronze cords. Behind it, on the wall, were five dark marks no larger than seeds. A side lamp threw light through the disk so that one seed-mark swelled into a patch the size of my palm while the others nearly vanished.\par

``Which mark matters?'' he asked.\par

``Closest to what?''\par

``To judgment.''\par

``That is not a place.''\par

``It is sometimes a place. You only learn its position too late.''\par

I moved around the disk. The large mark blurred when I stepped aside; another, nearly invisible from the first position, sharpened; lowering my eye swelled the second while the first smeared. Each mark could be made to dominate if the glass, the lamp, and the observer agreed to make it so.\par

``None matters by itself,'' I said. ``The lens grants the importance.''\par

``The hand that places the lens grants it. The lens only obeys its shape.'' He tilted the disk, and the enlarged mark stretched into a long black wound. ``What is it now?''\par

``The same mark.''\par

``Would a crowd know that?''\par

``No.''\par

I looked at the wound-shaped mark. ``Light must know how easily the seed became a wound.''\par

``Good.''\par

We worked the lenses until my eyes ached. Glass disks, water-filled bulbs, oil jars, thin plates that widened light or narrowed it or sharpened one edge while ruining another. He made me stand close, then far, then above and below, then with one eye shut, then viewing by reflection first.\par

``Third shadow?''\par

``Focus creates false importance.''\par

``And the danger?''\par

``Seeing sharply can be another form of blindness.''\par

Nothing moved in his face. ``Careful. That one is almost useful.''\par

He allowed water then --- a small cup, plain clay. I drank too fast and caught myself watching the rim instead of the room.\par

``Any thoughts on water?'' he said.\par

``It does strange things. I noticed it properly for the first time looking at the token, the day after I earned it.''\par

``Misuse teaches --- it is the surest teacher there is: the wrong instrument, in the wrong hand, in the wrong room, held with an erring intent. That is how a thing is found. Whether the finder outlives his finding is a second lesson, and not one Light sets out to teach.''\par

The fourth room was bright, white-walled, severe. A fixed lamp cast a beam down through a bronze slit onto a table cut with narrow channels. At the far end, under glass, lay a small black mark. The beam fell too far left to touch it.\par

``Strike the mark with light.''\par

I inspected the apparatus. The lamp was fixed; so was the slit. No mirror, no plate, no hidden aperture offered itself. On the table stood clear water, oil, crystal, a cloudy cup and a sealed clay pot.\par

``May I move the vessels?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``May I open the pot?''\par

``No.''\par

I began with clear water. The beam crossed it and turned, short of the mark. Oil thickened it and blurred its edge. Crystal turned it more sharply but broke the light where I needed a single clean point. The cloudy liquid scattered too much, waking the beam inside the cup and leaving it weak beyond.\par

``Name the event.''\par

``The beam changes path when it crosses a substance.''\par

``Which substance?''\par

``Water, oil, glass, whatever the cloudy liquid is.''\par

``And air?''\par

I almost dismissed air. Then I remembered the desert trembling in its own heat, the mist shining behind Faith's rehearsal in the passage above the work hall, the blue flame in the Hall bending toward Va-Elun. ``Air also,'' I said.\par

``Usually less. Not never.''\par

No single vessel struck the mark cleanly. Water and crystal broke the beam. Oil before water made it wander; water before oil came closer but ruined the edge. I wanted the clay pot, and he watched me want it.\par

``What is in the pot?'' I asked.\par

``That is what you are asking permission to learn.''\par

``Salt.''\par

He said nothing. The lid was sealed with Balance green wax --- a marked pot on a table of water and turning light. Salt would change the water. I lifted the cloudy cup, smelled it, found the mineral bitterness.\par

``Salted water.''\par

Still no confirmation. I set the cloudy cup under the beam. The light turned more than in clear water, less cleanly than through crystal, and struck near the mark. I moved the cup a finger's width and the beam touched the black point.\par

``Acceptable. Again, without the salted cup.''\par

I looked at the table, then at the sealed pot. ``May I open it now?''\par

``No.''\par

Clear water, oil, crystal, the salted cup removed, the sealed pot, the fixed lamp, the black mark, the channels in the table. I had taken the channels for drainage, though I had no idea what they were meant to drain. I scraped a little white grain from the table's edge with my fingernail.\par

``That is theft from Balance,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``It was already spilled. It was already unmeasured.''\par

I stopped. Then he said, ``Continue.''\par

I stirred the grains into the water. Nothing changed to the eye. I put the recess under the beam and the light turned a little more --- and fell short. Another scrape, more stirring, another test. Each scrape from the table felt more like theft than the last. At last the beam struck the mark cleanly.\par

``How much salt?''\par

``Enough.''\par

``A cook's answer.''\par

``I do not know.''\par

``A dangerous answer, but honest.''\par

``It looks the same,'' I said, looking at the water.\par

``Many changed things do.''\par

``And the light knows.''\par

``If one knows where to look. The turn is the measure, Sa-Kailan: whether the jar holds water or oil, and how much salt is dissolved in it, the light will tell you by the angle alone, near enough to the grain. A jar may lie to the tongue; it cannot lie to the angle. And there is more past Candidate knowledge, one day you may learn that substances block, or filter, or split light, or else, and the measure of the light is the measure of the substance. Part of a Veil's curriculum, if you ever become it. Fourth shadow?''\par

``Turning. Light changes path when it crosses substance.''\par

``And the danger?''\par

``The condition may change invisibly.''\par

He took the bowl away before I could study it further.\par

The last chamber held a long trough of clear water set into black stone. A narrow tube beneath it carried light from a hidden source up into the water itself. At one end, below the waterline, a small white mark had been painted; at the other, a bronze plate stood dry outside the trough. The beam travelled underwater toward the surface at a shallow angle and vanished.\par

``Make the beam strike the bronze plate.''\par

I leaned over the trough. The beam came up against the water's skin so shallow that if it escaped it should strike the air above and reach the plate. It stayed. I passed my hand above the surface; no light touched me. I crouched lower, and at the surface the beam turned downward and stayed inside the water.\par

``It cannot leave,'' I said.\par

``What cannot?''\par

``The light. No --- the beam. When it reaches the surface at that angle, it does not pass into air. It returns into the water.''\par

``Like what?''\par

``A mirror.''\par

``Water is not a mirror.''\par

``It is here.''\par

``Why?''\par

I looked at the angle, then at the surface. The line had changed. ``If the beam rises steeply, it leaves. If it comes too shallow, the surface holds it.''\par

``Threshold return,'' he said. ``Every crossing has a condition. Some thresholds forgive. Some refuse. A door is not the only thing that can open or close.''\par

The phrase sounded old --- retrieved from somewhere deeper in Light.\par

``Change it,'' he said. ``Make it leave.''\par

I searched for controls. The source stood fast. The tube, the trough, the beam's angle --- all fixed. A lever raised the water by the width of a fingernail; the beam returned. I lowered it; no use. I disturbed the surface with a rod; the beam scattered and resumed.\par

``Do not bruise water because you dislike its answer.''\par

I stepped back. Change the condition. ``Salt,'' I said.\par

This time a small open dish of coarse crystals waited beside the trough. I took a pinch and let it fall. The grains sank and dissolved, and the surface gave me back exactly the mirror it had shown before --- no thread, no glimmer, no change at all.\par

``Worse,'' I said.\par

``Worse for what?''\par

``For reaching the plate.''\par

``For leaving the water, yes.''\par

That told me what to do. Salt had moved the threshold. More salt held the beam in, so thinner water should let it out. So I thinned the water, clear from a side vessel, a little at a time --- and for a long while it gave me nothing. The surface stayed a whole mirror, the beam turning back down into the water exactly as before, with no hint that the mixture beneath was changing at all. Then, between one pour and the next, it broke: a single thread escaped flat along the surface, so nearly level with the water that it was barely a leaving. I thinned it further, and the thread lifted --- a little higher with each pour, climbing off the water in even steps as the water weakened --- until it reached the bronze plate and struck the lower edge. A pale mark appeared on the metal.\par

``Acceptable.''\par

``No.'' The word came out before I could stop it. He turned. I felt the danger at once, but my eagerness still ran ahead of me. ``I mean --- this is more than acceptable.''\par

``What is it?''\par

I tried to slow the words enough to make them lawful. ``It is a surface that becomes a mirror without being made into one. A door that opens or closes because of angle and substance. A way to change a path when the source cannot be moved.''\par

``Fifth shadow?''\par

``If light can be kept inside water, water can carry it where air cannot. If salt decides whether it leaves or stays, then the same trough can show, or hide, or carry. A man looking down may see nothing, or see himself, or see through, or never know that light is moving beneath him.''\par

He covered the salt.\par

``That is enough.''\par

``No.'' Again, too quick. I lowered my voice. ``Please. One more thought. If the source is fixed, and the mark is fixed, and the angle is fixed, the answer is still not fixed. One can change what the light crosses. One can change what the light is allowed to do.''\par

He regarded me in silence. ``You have learned too much for one morning.''\par

He opened a hidden plug. The water drained, the beam broke and blurred and guttered into useless glow, and everything went ordinary again --- a trough of wet stone, a plate of plain bronze.\par

``You ended it,'' I said.\par

``I ended your belief that discovery entitles continuation.''\par

--- \ldots{} A very old kind of human. Say the worse thing you have folded away.

--- The worse thing is not what you are; it is what it cost to fetch you. Three vehicles, seventeen men, an aircraft left in the desert. A thing that size does not stay hidden forever. A patient enough service, watching the borders and the fuel and the gold, sees the shape of it, and asks the very question Urs asks in jest: why would anyone spend a city's ransom to extract a man from the desert?

--- And a house that can lay out a city's ransom, and never be asked to account for it: what manner of house is that?

--- A house built by one man, and in his likeness: Edward Blackwood. When Kingston was on the edge of going dark, its energy minister Hugh Hart called Edward in the middle of the night and begged for assistance. When the whole world found its reasons not to touch the place, it was Edward who put oil into that harbour and kept the lights burning there, not for love of the island, but for the debts it left owing to him ever after. That is the house. And a man who can light a city no one else will light can move one man no one is meant to move, and never raise his voice doing it. It is how they carried you the last of the way: a diplomatic flight chartered by Kingston into Geneva, where no customs hand may open the bags; and then the family's own helicopter, which crosses these hills a dozen times in a week and is remarked by no one, for the final hour. Three trucks in the desert may be counted. A Blackwood aircraft cannot. But a house that can do all of that, so quietly, for a lunatic and a film, is exactly the house a patient service resolves, long before tonight, to keep within its eye. And so it sets a man it already owns to stand near you, and watch, and wait. A man of the desert, as you are: Roger Martinez.

--- Four nothings \textit{make a shape}. Chad. Niamey. The name of the man beside you. A France that follows the yellow rock wherever it is dug. Each alone is nothing. Set side by side, they are a shape; and a thing that has a shape can be found. You have the makings of a discipline you cannot yet name, Paul. Now hear me as a man who has stood where you are about to stand. This is dangerous ground. I can tell you, from a price I have paid, that to cross a threshold unready is a thing a man settles with his life. We go carefully; and more carefully still if this Martinez is trained to hold two purposes in the one skin, for that is a man who can keep his face, and a man who can keep his face is the hardest quarry there is. So we do not wait to be certain. \textit{Understanding is not required}: not yours, not mine, not this afternoon. We protect first. We think after.

--- I have heard that sentence before, in your own account: a warden gave it to you at a gate, at fifteen, and you told me you never forgave him for it.

--- And here I stand, giving it to you, and meaning every word. They caged me, Paul; and the thing I have carried all this way is that they were right to. Tonight I have become the warden at the gate, on purpose, and with my eyes open. You will forgive my arrogance. I did not know our bargain would end like this. But I thought, whatever it came to, that I could perhaps win it. So I brought this.

--- \ldots{} What in God's name is the purpose of this needle and stone.

--- They are for your ceremony: the rite of an order I invented yesterday. I have a certain talent, Paul, for inventing my own destiny.

--- An order. What order?

--- Shadow. That is its name, and the whole of its name. A new Keepership, made out of nothing, to fight the enemy that is already within; and it will hold the door where Shield let it fall. Kneel here, under the tree, in its shadow, where the open light you came out for cannot reach us, and neither can the eye that keeps this house. Shadow is fittest begun out of the light.

--- A knighthood, from a captive, on a wet lawn, at the point of a needle? You cannot mean this. And what does the captive give me for the knee?

--- I have never been further from jest. We are watched; we are entered; and the house does not know it. There is no other hand to do this, and no other hour. But I will not pretend I ask it of you for nothing. This mark I am about to set in your hand is a key. It opens the Threshold. One day you walk through it, into the Mountain, where no Outsider has ever stood and come out again. Not a photograph. Not a tape. You. That is what a Veil of Shadow is owed. And weigh what I am truly asking against it. You told me you would \textit{give your life} for the chance of one day seeing the Mountain; I ask you for a drop of blood, and an assurance of the good intention you have shown me all week: that you will help me guard the Mountain, and draw out the enemy within, should such an enemy ever prove to be there. Consider it honestly. If they exist, you will have sworn to help me save us, and the Mountain stands. And if they do not exist, then there was never any mission, and the oath has bound you to nothing at all. You win in both cases, Paul.

--- \ldots{} Then do it. Do it now, before I am sensible again.

--- There it is. You did not weigh it; you did not wait. This morning I told you how a boy pulled a cord he had been told to leave, and what it cost him for the rest of his life; and now I hold up the first bright thing, and you reach for it with not a breath between the wanting and the taking. We are the same animal, Paul. It is why you will be good at this. It is also why, one day, one of us will have to stop the other. Kneel.

--- Hear the charges of it, then, for there is no book to hold them but you.

    By the dark the first morning left unlit: \par
    I call upon you to defend the Mountain from the enemy within. \par
    By the proud who gathered the whole into one hand, and drowned for it: \par

    By God, who held His breath while the first Light was struck: \par
    I call upon you to carry this to your grave, and to give it up to no pain past it. \par
    By the Beast that sleeps beneath the Mountain, keeper of the first wrath and the next: \par
    I call upon you, for Shadow must guard also from Shadow, \par

And now the covenant, and answer me true: one day they will take you, and open you. What will they find?

--- \ldots{}

--- Stop smiling. This is not a joke.

--- Of course. Very serious indeed.

--- One day they will take you, and open you. What will they find?

--- My face. And nothing behind it.

--- Your blood, Paul. On the stone.

--- Rise now as Va-Paul, Veil of Shadow. The name you will hear only once, keep to the last hour of your life, and never once say aloud; for in Shadow we speak neither prefix nor suffix.

\centerline{[\,---\,]}

--- It is not a wound; nor will it scar; nor be visible to the untrained eye; but it is the mark of the ascended, and a day may come when it carries you through a door no Outsider has ever passed: through the Threshold itself, into the Mountain, on the strength of a thing pricked under a Swiss tree. It is done. How do you feel?

--- \ldots{} Hmm. This is the moment when I am supposed to say something deep and meaningful. \ldots{} Unlit?

--- Exactly. Now let us proceed as normal; nothing must change. Let us spend the time we have to think about the things that could betray us, the time we have before doors close on us, the things we could do that would make it worse for us. Let us think hard, and keep our faces.
