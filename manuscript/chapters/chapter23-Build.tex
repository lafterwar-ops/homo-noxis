\typesetchapter{Chapter 23}{Build}

We took up the next of the sheets in the beam and read on without stopping to speak, and the measure went on as it had begun, numbered and cadenced and cut to be chanted, which is the thing I have never got past: that he set the worst of it down in the versicles my people use for morning prayer.\par

\ornament

\begin{scripture}

We found it in the third year out, and I knew it before my engineers did, for I had left off hunting the ground that men desire, and turned to the ground that no one goes near.\\
It offered nothing, and its nothing was the first of its defences: no green, no game, no road, no reason --- a shoulder of old stone, and a great width of distance.\\
It lay in a waste that kills by breadth alone, and gives nothing to the warm folk wanting south, who would not turn their heads toward it in a hundred lifetimes.\\
\medskip

Then, under the nothing, season upon season, my water-men laid their rods against the stone, and one by one the stone gave up its secrets into their hands.\\
Water: so much of it that my chief sat down upon the rock, and put his face into his hands, and made no sound at all.\\
More than a siege could ever drink, and enough to hold a whole sealed people; and it was years before I let myself say aloud for what the water was truly kept.\\
And heat beneath it, the old heat of the deep, shallow enough to reach and to hold, a fire that never wants for feeding, so that the sealed folk should never once be cold.\\
And ways: faults, and voids, and the promise of more that could be cut and made, until a whole world might be threaded down into the rock and laid to rest in it.\\
\medskip

Understand the joy of it, before you hear the rest of it; for it was the best year of the whole of my life.\\
Three years of a road that had killed my people and told me hourly that I was mad, and at the end of it I had the thing, and I was glad past anything that I had dared to want.\\
We laughed like boys in the deep shade of the shafts, and every rod we sank said yes to us; and a man to whom the world lays out its tools ready to his hand feels himself \emph{chosen}.\\
Nothing in the whole of my life from that day to this has ever once equalled it.\\
\medskip

I saw the other thing also, in that same year, at the bottom of a shaft, by the light of one lamp.\\
The deep fire never dies; and water flashed upon it turns to steam; and steam driven down a throat that runs through the roots of a world becomes a hand laid flat upon that world's own heart.\\
My engineer told me, knowing nothing of my mind, what pressures he could raise down there; and that was the beginning of it.\\
The ground had offered me my people's living, and it offered, in the one same stone, the means to do to all the earth what the earth had done to my own --- and worse, and all at once, and hidden.\\
I told myself, in that year, that the hand would only ever be a defence.\\
\end{scripture}

\ornament

\begin{scripture}

Do not take the building for a wonder; the ones who came after me made it out to be God's own masonry, and that is the first and the largest of the lies.\\
It was labour, and it was mine: the most enormous labour ever gathered into one place, done by living people, tens of thousands of them, brought down that killing road one by one.\\
It cost; and I paid the cost in the one coin such things are ever bought with, which is the lives of other people; and I kept the account, and the account is long.\\
\medskip

We learned the work by failing at the work; and the first press, built to prove the hand, killed the men who had built it.\\
They died for what no man yet living knew, which is what water does when it flashes into steam in a shut place; and I stood at the gallery's mouth, and watched them carry out the rest, and kept my face still.\\
I did not halt the work; I corrected the drawings: a stair cut too near a fault took a hundred in a single hour, and a wall let go in the night and drowned a whole shift, and set us back ten years.\\
We did not learn the rate at which to meter the water down until men had died of the learning; and I did not wait upon it; every increment of it was purchased, and I did not flinch.\\
\medskip

You will want a number, and I have one, for I kept the count, and I kept it true, and that is the one virtue I can name with no lie folded through it.\\
More than forty thousand came down that road while I drew breath, and fewer than a third of them ever climbed up it again; and the difference of them is a kind of death.\\
Twenty-seven thousand stayed inside the rock that they had cut, in flood, and in fall, and in steam, and in the slow grinding waste of a country that wanted nothing alive.\\
I built the greatest refuge that was ever built, a thing to keep my people whole, and I built it upon a floor of their own bones; and I did not stop, and I did not slow.\\
When a chief came to me white in the face with word of a fresh hundred lost, I asked him first whether the work that they had died upon had held.\\
I watched the very hour that he stopped loving me, and I let him stop, and I said nothing, and I turned back to the drawings.\\
\medskip

Then I did the thing that let them do such work, to such an end, and never once rise against it: I saw to it that they could not understand.\\
I divided up the knowledge, and gave no team more than its own one piece: the water-men knew water, and believed they sank a well against a siege.\\
The light-men knew their bending surfaces, and believed they brought the sun downstairs to a hidden people; and those who cut the deep throat believed the work was drainage, and theirs alone.\\
Those who raised the gates believed they raised them against armies; and not one team in a hundred was ever let hold two pieces of it at one time.\\
Only my own few knew that the well, the sun-ways, the drainage, and the gates were one single machine, and that the machine was a weapon aimed through the core of the Earth; and it waits there yet.\\
I divided it against no enemy outside the walls; I divided it against the hands within them, so that no man should ever hold enough of it at once to look upon the thing that he had made, and set it down, and refuse.\\
I let that division harden into us, and it took, and it became the very soul of all that followed after.\\
\end{scripture}

\ornament

Paul stopped there, and set the sheet down, and for a moment he could not speak for the shape of the thing. ``He is chanting it,'' he said at last. ``He put this in the office. Do you understand what I am holding? The founding law of the whole Mountain --- that no one hand may hold the whole --- was a labour-management technique, made into a religion so the division would outlive its reason, and he has set the confession of it to the versicles they chant on feast days. There is no philosophy in it anywhere.'' And I said, because it was mine to say and no one else's, ``It is worse than that, Paul. The same wall that stops the workers seeing the whole stops any one Keeper ever firing it alone. He built the division to hide the weapon from the builders, and it turned out to be the only thing that ever kept the weapon safe.''\par

\ornament

\begin{scripture}

Five great works, and so five teams, and over each of them I set a head; and the whole lay divided among the five, never gathered into one place, never spoken whole.\\
I could see, even then, that I would die, and that the thing must run on without me, far longer than a man's name is ever kept; and so, before my own work was done,\\
I did the thing that turns a work into a people: I ceased to call the teams teams, and I began instead to make of them lines, and bloods, and inheritances.\\
\medskip

The team of water and of stone, of heat and measure, the material world and the keeping of its accounts, I taught to hand itself down from master unto child in small and guarded portions,\\
with signs, and with pride, and with jealous keeping, until the work that it did had become \emph{Balance}: a name, a blood, a calling, and no man within it knowing from where it had first come.\\
Those who listened for the deep return, and knew the timing of the strokes, I made the keepers of the holy hour, and taught them that it was God who spoke inside the stone: \emph{Faith}.\\
The light-men, who would set the shot and hold the aim, I made the keepers of a mystery of alignment, and I gave that mystery a name, and the name was \emph{Light}.\\
Those who held my books and my permissions, who alone might say that a thing may pass, and who kept the order of each act that cannot be recalled, I made \emph{Justice}, the last of the five.\\
And the men of the gates and the containment, of secrecy and, at the need, of violence, I named \emph{Shield}; and it was done, and they never felt the doing of it, and they kept their silence.\\
\medskip

I gave them nothing new at all to do; I took the doing that each of them already had, and I wrapped it about in inheritance, and in pride, and in mystery, and in a little dread.\\
For a man will repeat a thing unchanged to the end of the ages, if only you can bring him to believe that the task he does is his own self.\\
\medskip

To each line I gave a discipline, to hide its true work down inside of it; and the discipline grew a real knowledge, freely taught, and deep, and absorbing, and every word of it true.\\
I laid the true knowledge down beside the secret, so that a clever child of the line might spend a brilliant life upon the cover, and never once fall through to the floor of it.\\
To Balance I gave honest measure, so that the finest engineer of his age might live and die inside his craft, and never see the weapon standing on the page.\\
To Faith I gave the true art of the stone, of rhythm and resonance and return, and I called it prayer, so that the best ear born in an age would spend itself upon the hearing of God, and never learn that it had heard a weapon breathe.\\
To Light I gave the bending of a wave down to a single burning point, and holy was the sheath of it; and to Justice the record, and the sequence, and the weighing of whether a thing may lawfully proceed.\\
And folded into Justice, and kept out of its sight, I laid the very permissions that a firing of the engine would one day require.\\
Truths, the whole of it, and every single piece of it a truth; and I set the truths in a ring about a hole, and I taught them that the hole was God.\\
And at the very lip of it I taught the soul to feel a particular holy dread, which I built into the teaching for that one purpose and no other:\\
so that a curious mind would flinch back from the one question at the very hour that it grew fit to ask it; and I taught them that flinch, and they have never once let it slip.\\
Not one of them, in all the long line of them, has ever known that he was doing it; and of all the things I made in all this Mountain, that is the piece I am the most proud of.\\
\end{scripture}

\ornament

\begin{scripture}

Then I did the thing that beats beneath the whole of all the rest of it; and I did it for a reason that was sound, and the soundness of the reason is the worst of it.\\
The machine had one flaw, and it was the flaw of all my kind: that it must be kept by persons, and a person changes, and a person turns at the last to dust.\\
Each generation the five hands would fail, and new and untried hands would rise to take their place; and a Keeper might know only the face of the rite that hid the engine, and hold his office forty years, and never once know whether, at the hour of the need, he could do the thing that he was made for.\\
Call five such hands a throne, and call them a weapon; give them their forty years; and they are five frightened priests who have forgotten that they were ever engineers, standing above an engine that not one of them has ever run.\\
\medskip

So I built a test, and I hid the test inside a devotion, and I made the devotion law.\\
At each succession, when one Keeper went down into the ground and a new one rose, the people saw only the five gather, secret and apart, to do the thing I had dressed for them as prayer, which they called the renewing of the covenant.\\
And under the prayer there was a firing: a small one, large enough to prove that the engine still answered when it was commanded, and small enough to leave the wide world unhurt.\\
A few drops of water metered onto the deep fire, five times over, and then the long wait that is owed; for the machine knows one measure only, and a stroke struck off its measure proves nothing whole.\\
And the world, struck five times in its own slow interval, would answer back one wait behind the fifth; and that returning note would climb the track of the deep stone to the listening ear, and say that the covenant held.\\
\medskip

I made it law, and I made it secret, and I made it holy, all three of these together, so that frightened and faithful folk would keep it perfectly, and never let it slip past the last of them who could have said what the five strokes were ever for.\\
I called it the readiness, and I told them: God confirms His Keeper, and nothing more than that.\\
And it was the hour in which my machine confirmed itself.\\
\medskip

The first time, in my own lifetime, to prove the thing, I stood in the Hall that was built to hold that toll, and I heard the Earth give back the shape of it: five even blows, and a long soft roll behind them, like a struck world turning once over in its sleep.\\
It was the most beautiful and the worst thing that I ever made; and I knew, in that hour, that I had given my machine a heartbeat, one beat to a generation, to go on sounding under the sand as long as the Mountain kept its place.\\
And I knew that any ear fine enough might, on some far day, hear that heart beating down in the ground, and come looking for the body that it beat in; and I built that chance in also, with care, and with intent.\\
\end{scripture}

Here Sarah took the sheet from Paul's hands, read that passage again to herself, and put both her hands flat on the cold stone of the machine, and was quiet for a long time. And when she spoke she did not speak to us. ``A few drops of water,'' she said. ``Metered onto the deep fire. Five even blows and a long soft roll. He is describing the thing I heard at Reggane when I was twenty-seven. The exact signature I chased across sixty-six years and two continents and everything I ever destroyed. It was a succession. Somewhere under that desert, in the year of the first French bomb, one of his offices buried a Keeper and raised a new one and proved the covenant held, and I was sitting forty kilometres away with a drum-paper on my knees, and I thought I had discovered something. I had heard his heartbeat.'' She took her hands off the stone. ``He knew one day someone with an ear would hear it and come. He built that risk in on purpose, and I am the ear he knew would come.''\par

\ornament

\begin{scripture}

The machine and the offices were the easy part of it; the hard part was the thing that no later people could ever invent, being themselves the offspring of it.\\
A machine in trained hands lasts a few lives, and no more; an age on, the trained hands have forgotten what the training was ever for, and the whole of it is gone out of the world.\\
A people that has lost the why of an exact task will, in the end, begin to do it inexactly; and then the machine is dead, and all that I spent my life upon is past.\\
I had not paid my father's world, and the forty thousand in the rock, to raise a thing that would run six lives and fail; and so I set myself to stop the clock.\\
There is one method for the stopping of it, and only one: I built a people to be the keeping of the thing; I built them so that they could not stop.\\
\medskip

I took children --- I have said it once, and I say it again, and I let it stand: some were our own, the kept seed, and some I bought, and some I gathered off the failing land.\\
I raised them inside a thing that was not so: that the Mountain had always stood where I had lately built it, that the offices were holy where I had written down a duty and a roster, that the engine was a god where I had made a weapon.\\
I wrote them a past, and I aged it well, so that the newest child would find, wherever he turned his eyes, more evidence that all of it was old past any counting, and would never doubt his own days were a late leaf on a tree past reckoning.\\
I set among the first of them actors who knew the whole of it, and who yet agreed, for love or fear or promise, to spend their lives in the pretending of faith;\\
so that the second generation, watching the first, believed without any pretending, and came to a true faith; and by the third there was no pretence left alive at all, and they knew it as a child knows the sky.\\
\medskip

I carved the Genesis with my own hand, in the worst of the nights of the build; and the aim of it was plain, that the outside world must be made a horror; and it came to me more easily than I would have wished.\\
So that no child of the Mountain should ever want the outside, I took the true thing, and I turned it by a single quarter, which is the only turn a good lie ever needs.\\
The true thing was a murder; and I kept the murder, for a real thing is what anchors a lie; and I set it in the first of all the ages, and made the dead man a brother, beloved and envied in a father's place.\\
And I gave the killer's line the open land for its inheritance, cursed and many, hating forever the shade that they could never find.\\
And I gave them a woman also: taken as spoil when the murderer fled, and blameless as a stolen lamp, a thing done to us and never once done by her; so I said, and so I cut it.\\
What I filed away, and kept within me, and let come never near the marble, was the truth that lay beneath her: that the knife had gone in by a hand that I had loved, and that I chose, from that hour on, never to ask that hand what it had known.\\
A people may be taught to dread a murderer born among them; no people yet was taught to dread a wound that a man had brought half down upon himself; and so I let that part of it go.\\
I let her stand in the stone as pure loss, and I drowned the question of her choosing in my father's grave, in the very same ground; and I have heard Keepers read it out to trembling boys as the world's own word.\\
I did the whole of it in a single evening, and it has worked upon them better than anything else that I ever built.\\
\end{scripture}

Paul had to stop reading here, and it was Va-Sheva who took the sheet from him, Va-Sheva of Shield, and she read the passage about the children in the flat Shield voice, and when she had finished she said only, ``I was one of those children. So were you,'' to me, ``and so was every soul in this Mountain, back and back, a hundred thousand years of children taught that a cage was a sky. And the worst of it is the part he will not let himself lie about even now: that the first generation may have loved them. My mother loved me. She taught me the sky was above me and the cage was the world, and she was wrong, and she had been taught it too, and she loved me while she did it. You cannot even hate it cleanly. He built a prison so loving that the guards and the prisoners are the same people, and every one of them was somebody's beloved child.''\par

\ornament

We were quiet a long time after that, in the cold, and I could not look at Va-Sheva, because she had said \emph{so were you}, and it was true. The child he had built, the one raised to believe the cage was a sky, was me, exactly me, Sa-Kailan of Light, who had knelt around the forbidden centre and felt the holy dread at its edge and called it God. And the strangest thing is that it did not make me feel like a victim. It made me feel that the thing I had been raised inside had a shape, and an outside, and a maker, and could therefore be stood apart from and looked at. You cannot refuse a sky. You can refuse a design. He had left, at the very bottom of it, the one door through which the design could be seen; and I was standing in it; and I understood that the reading would carry me past forgiving him and past hating him, into deciding what a man does when he has been handed the truth about a machine that only he, of all the people alive, has both the eyes to see and the standing to stop.\par

\begin{scripture}

I will not explain to you every stone of it; the later people have bowed down to these stones, and made a fetish of them, and I will not feed the fetish; but a few of them I will give you, for you have knelt to them, and you are owed.\\
The great disk that swings in the Hall, which my late children take to be the very face of God at work, weighing the world, is a clock, and free of any god, and it belongs to the trigger.\\
Its slow round, three days in the coming back to where it stood, is the firing's own measure in a disguise, the counting of the strokes made slack and holy, a devotion paid to the hour;\\
so that the men who tend the engine beneath might take their timing in the open, before the kneeling people, and no suspicion ever fall upon the holiest thing in all that room, which is a fuse dressed as a prayer.\\
The Bowl, with its Ear and its Mouth, that gives the true note back and leaves a false note bare, is Faith's own instrument, dressed as the vessel of the last of the prayers.\\
The tokens that Light keeps, that no Keeper may fire without, and that must be turned together with the key of Shield, are the safeguard of the whole of it:\\
built so that I myself, who burned, even enraged, even at the very last, could never once fire the thing with a single hand; a proof laid in the open palm that the whole may not be held by one, and made so grand a relic that no man would ever think to melt it down.\\
The beam that comes down at the equinox and names the children is the winding of the year-clock, as you have already found.\\
\medskip

And the cleared centre of the Hall, the empty ground that no foot may cross, that they hold sacred above all else, is a place where to stand is to die.\\
It is kept empty because a man who stood upon that throat when the great stroke came would be destroyed by it, and I would take no worshipper of mine down into that flame.\\
So I made the danger itself holy; I called the killing-ground a sanctuary; and they have knelt about its edge and worshipped the one thing in all that Hall that they should flee, and the worshipping of it has kept them alive.\\
\medskip

Two black gravures face each other across the Hall, and the people kneel forever penned between their dawn and their undoing; and the first of them is the lie.\\
The second, the Cataclysm, is the one true thing that I let them keep: an honest picture of the work, hidden in the plain open, which a people reading it takes for prophecy, and leaves its plain diagram of ruin unread; for a warning that is worshipped is a warning that is never read.\\
The Seal, the falling gates, is the barest and the least dressed of all the works, for there are things that even I could not contrive to hallow, and a plain wall that drops to keep the sky out is one of them.\\
And this room --- the clock, the ledgers, the door that only a firing will open --- I built the last of all of them, and I told no one of it, not even the five;\\
for by slow degrees, across three lives, it would sink below the very memory of the offices, and grow into a thing that Justice, to this very hour, does not know that it does not know:\\
a hidden heart within a hidden body, waiting there in the dark, an age, for you.\\
\end{scripture}

\ornament

\begin{scripture}

Why keep a record at all, when the whole of what I was lay hidden from them? I have wondered at it myself, and there is more than one true answer, and the answers give each other the lie.\\
I kept it because I wished to be known; and that is the least of the reasons, and the one that shames me; and I made certain that none who lived within the work would ever know that a mind had been there at all.\\
And then I could not endure to vanish whole into the work, and leave no heir behind me; and I wanted one, at the least, to see the hand of it; for vanity outlasts even grief; and here you are.\\
\medskip

I kept it also to defend myself; for when I began it, I still supposed that a reader who had seen the whole of what I saw would find, at the account's end, that I had done the one thing a great man could, and would clear my name.\\
I believe that no longer; I have set myself down too honestly for it; and the clear-eyed reader who comes at the last to this door will be exactly of the kind that uses the door to cast me off; and I find that I mind it less than I had thought.\\
\medskip

And last, the reason that I could not own to until the other two were written down: I wanted there to be a door.\\
Under the lord, and under the plan, and under the wound, there was a man who knew that a thing which can never be stopped should never have been made, and who could not quite bring himself to build an engine with no way back out of it.\\
So I hid one way out; I laid the whole truth here, at the bottom, behind a door that only the firing can open, so that it should fall to a generation that has truly used the weapon;\\
and among them, only to an eye trained to see what has been set down on purpose, which is the very eye that my whole system was built to breed out of them.\\
I am an age dead as you read this; and yet I built the door; and whatever else you settle upon concerning me, hold fast to this: that at the bottom of the worst thing ever made, its maker left a door, and left it standing open for you.\\
\end{scripture}

\begin{scripture}

The end of the build was the cruellest part of all of it, and I did it cleanly; for I had grown old by then, and had lost the power to feel the parts I spent, and had gone cold.\\
The tens of thousands who had cut the thing could not be loosed to carry its pieces away into the world; for divided knowledge is safe only while it stays divided, and hands that scatter and grow old will meet, and drink, and slowly set the pieces back together.\\
What I did to prevent it made me tremble, and I will not write it here.\\
Some of them went away: sworn, and scattered wide, and watched; and some stayed on, the first to labour in the sealed world, and were told that they had always lived below.\\
And some did not go away at all.\\
The account of it is long; and I kept it, as I kept the rest; and it is the worst and the longest page I own; and I have set it apart from this one, and I do not ask you to read it; leave it alone.\\
Believe this much: that I could not require the doing of it of myself, and that, quailing, I did it through the hands of other men, and turned my face away.\\
\medskip

Then they were gone, the one way or the other, and the great gates were shut at the last against the world, though the whole sealing of it would wait for an age beyond my own.\\
Inside there was a people, a few thousand only: the kept seed, and the raised children, and the ageing actors; and on the first morning of the sealed world they woke to their land, and did their offices, and said their prayers, and knelt around the forbidden ground.\\
And the children born down there did not know that a world had ever stood above them, or that one might be found by the walking; and they would teach their own children the same.\\
I stood among them where I stood, an old man who had done the thing, and I watched the lie draw its first breath; and it was good.\\
God forgive me, it was the most beautiful thing that I have ever seen: a whole world, on the first morning of its life, believing that it had always been.\\
I knew that it would hold; and I knew that not one of them would ever thank me, and that not one of them would ever know that I had been a real man who had walked among them.\\
\medskip

I am closing the record now; I have said the whole of it; and I set it here beneath the god that I invented, in the one hand I trusted, for the one reader I could not build my system to prevent.\\
Judge me: you are the first in an age with the eyes to do it, and I made certain that you would be a heretic, because I could not bear to be judged by any soul that believed.\\
Do the thing that I could not do: look upon the whole of it, and say aloud what it is for.\\
I was Noxius Zero; I was the first of us, and the maker of you all, and I was a man whose father died by the outside, through the woman that he loved.\\
I chose certainty; and given my century, and my evidence, and my dead, a man chooses certainty; and if you would not have chosen it, then no one ever set the choice before you, and you should go slower than you mean to with your verdict.\\
\end{scripture}

Paul turned the last sheet, and there were no more, and the equinox beam had gone off the wall entirely while we read, and the Archive was lit only by our own lamps now, the borrowed sun withdrawn, the machine turning on in the dark as it had turned for an eternity and would turn whether we lived or died. And I found that I was weeping, which I had not done since a cell in a country I could not find, and that I could not have said whether I wept for the drowned workers, or for the lied-to children, or for Ka-Elun under her roof, or for the whole ended world above us, or for a woman named Ithe, an eternity dead, who was used by an enemy and accused by the man who loved her and asked, by neither of them, what she had truly known, or for myself, who had read my own possible life to the end of it in another man's hand and found that the only difference between us was a fact-checker and a machine and one night's kindness in the desert. I think I wept for all of it. I think, by then, it was all one thing.\par
