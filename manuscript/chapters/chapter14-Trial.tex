\typesetchapter{Chapter 14}{Trial}

The Lesser Seal ended in the night. I did not hear it end. Inside the soundless room, the Mountain could have lifted every bar, loosened every chain and restored every door without returning one sound to me. Proof secured. Speech held. Body held. Justice had named those three facts over me while I was still bound; they were the last words I could place in time.\par

Near First Light the door opened, and Ka-Dhavar of Shield stood in it. Keepers leave prisoners to wardens; a Keeper comes for a man only when a matter has grown too secret to pass through any hand but his own. ``You will not be bound,'' he said. His voice struck the inside of my head; for one breath I understood the sound before the words. ``There is nowhere in the Mountain a man runs to,'' I said, ``and no man runs from the five.'' He weighed the words, decided they would hold, and stepped aside to let me pass.\par

He took me past every hearing room I knew, and the route descended. The warmth of habitation thinned, the polish left the stone, and twice we passed beneath an inhabited gallery. A bowl struck a table above us. Someone laughed. A child ran six steps before a woman called him back.\par

Then white stone, and the Hall of Prayers. The sight of them assembled stopped me on the threshold. Ka-Elun of Faith at the centre in her plain white and the torn pilgrim's mantle. Ka-Cheryn of Balance, broad and grey. Ka-Leth of Justice, still as his own tablet. Ka-Raedin, my Keeper, a hooded hand-lamp in his grip. And apart from the five, only two: Va-Sheva of Shield, who looked past me, and Sa-Tavan of Justice, a Candidate still, too junior by years to stand in such a room. At their feet were packs: water, dried food, folded cloth.\par

Ka-Elun looked at the lamp in Ka-Raedin's hand. ``Keeper of Light,'' she said softly, ``put that away.'' He held still. ``It is hooded.'' ``That is not the same as away. Better Keepers of Light than either of you have entered the Labyrinth believing brightness would make a path obedient. They were recovered by pilgrims who could not read a mirror but could keep breath with stone.'' Ka-Raedin held the lamp through one full breath. Then he set it against the wall, hooded and unlit. ``Faith receives,'' Ka-Elun said, and let it lie.\par

``You will walk,'' Ka-Elun told me.\par

``Guided by whom?''\par

``By what remains when sight stops governing\ldots{}''\par

The carved prayer moved inward, opening a passage behind Ka-Elun. She took a narrow lamp from the bracket beside it. We went down in single file, Ka-Dhavar last, closing the stone behind us. At the first division Ka-Elun sang one low note and waited. The passage on our left returned it at once; the one on our right gave it back later and fainter. She took the right.\par

We walked for an hour --- ramp and switchback and long shallow grade, doubling back on itself, descending until my ears changed and the air cooled against my face. At the next branch she used a child's counting tune; at the next, four words from the prayer at First Light. She waited through every return before choosing.\par

``Are we going to the Hall of the Last Prayer?'' I asked. Ka-Elun came close to laughing. ``No. You are being taken beneath the rooms, Va-Kailan --- beneath the reason for them\ldots{}''\par

The walls were striped, floor to vanished ceiling, bands of pale and dark lit by Ka-Elun's lamp so that stone and shadow repeated in one endless rhythm. We turned left, right, then doubled back through a low chamber whose floor sloped towards a shallow bowl. Each time we reached the bowl, she changed the song. Once the answer came from ahead; once from behind Ka-Dhavar; on what I counted as the seventh passage, from an opening I had not seen. I could see three ways out, though I had counted two before. I left the file to examine a crack beside one opening. I was sure I had seen it before.\par

``No,'' Ka-Elun said, without turning.\par

``I did not speak.''\par

``You leaned toward certainty. There is no crack you have seen before. There are only cracks that repeat. You may observe, Va-Kailan. You may not follow yourself.''\par

I returned to the file.\par

In the next chamber three doorways faced us. Ka-Elun sang three rising notes towards them. The returns reached us one after another.\par

Ka-Elun passed behind Ka-Raedin and covered his eyes with both hands. ``Eyes closed. All of you.'' She lowered the hood.\par

When the light returned I waited, then looked. Her hands still covered Ka-Raedin's eyes, his head tilted back towards her. The others still had their eyes closed. The chamber stretched away to my right; five doorways stood where I remembered three. My heel still rested in the same hollow. Ka-Elun sang the three notes again. Five returns crossed the chamber.\par

Ka-Cheryn opened her eyes. ``Ka-Elun. Your hands.'' She watched until Faith released him. Ka-Raedin straightened his robe, and Ka-Elun took up the lamp.\par

At the next turn I heard water running on the left. Farther along, its note thinned beside a hollow in the wall. I turned towards it and put my hand on the stone. Cold moved through one narrow joint into my palm.\par

Ka-Elun let me lead for a few steps. When I stopped she came up beside me in the dark. ``A Light is given the eye,'' she said, barely above a breath. ``You were handed it as a boy, and you are good at it, and it is not the best of what is in you. Faith hears. You heard.''\par

``Before the Beast, silence,'' she said, at the wall where I had stopped --- and the answer came from behind me, from the Keepers themselves: ``Before silence, prayer. Before prayer, breath. Before breath, fear.'' The seam opened where my ear had set me, and there was distance now where there had been none. ``Now,'' she said, ``Light may look.''\par

We came into a circular room. Ten doors ringed it, five beneath carved arches and five flush with the wall. Nine were shut; the Labyrinth stood open behind us. Each office mark appeared twice.\par

At the centre stood a king cut from dark stone, his crown and hands left whole, his face chiselled away. A city in gold circled his feet. The closed arch of Faith stood at his back; his erased face was set towards the bridge and, beyond it, the Gate.\par

Ka-Elun sang once more, a measure she had not used on the descent. Her voice returned from the wall before him. Stone moved and opened the way into the antechamber.\par

The bridge was a tongue of stone wide enough for thirty men abreast, running level across a gulf of open air to the far wall. Above, the dark climbed higher than the Mountain stands above the sand, and higher again. Below, to either side, the floor fell away into two long pits. Out of them rose the pillars, scarred and past counting, their heads lost past where the light could climb. Around their bases, picked out in gold, cities crossed bands of carved sand. I could not tell whether they were emerging or being drowned.\par

The two long walls were the Mountain itself, broken rock on either side of the bridge. The far wall was built, and the bridge ran straight at the Gate of the Hall of Cataclysm. It rose the height of a great tree and spanned the width of the bridge, every part of it mechanism. Chains thick as tree-trunks entered it out of the rock and climbed into slots above. Five rows of five signs had been cut across its face, figures I failed to read; low to one side an empty slot waited in the iron. At its centre was the Beast's face from the Pendulum, a great cat with round ears flattened and five teeth bared beneath almost human eyes.\par

Across the gulf to my left, on the wall at right angles to the Gate, spread the Cataclysm gravure, five-and-twenty paces to a side. The Mountain stood above the Beast, which strained upward out of Hell with its fists at the roots of the Earth. Five figures stood apart in five arches, each holding one of its chains. Beyond the Mountain the Outsiders crowded under the open eye of Heaven; flame passed through their cities and great waves broke over them. At the foot of the ruin the people went down into the stone.\par

In the antechamber, five seats faced the accused mark, cut from the chamber's own stone, spaced so no Keeper could easily whisper to another. At the centre lay a flat stone table where objects could be placed so all could see them and no single hand receive them. Nothing lay upon it.\par

Ka-Dhavar brought me to the mark with one touch at the shoulder. He used no restraint and stayed within reach until the five had taken their seats. Ka-Leth of Justice stood beside the central stone, his own hands empty, for Justice keeps clear of the record and only speaks into it. At the wall stood Va-Sheva of Shield and Sa-Tavan of Justice with the tablet on which the record would be kept.\par

``This antechamber opens under seal,'' Ka-Leth said, and the words stayed small in the vastness. ``No Kahir divulges what belongs solely to its own sealed charge. No Keeper seeks total knowledge. The hearing binds the five without making one hand whole. Before judgement, the chamber remembers why judgement is divided.''\par

Ka-Leth set out the charges: Breach of Seal, Bearing Forbidden Proof, Unauthorised Revelation in potential and act. ``The law judges not only the fire that has burned, but the place where fire was carried.'' ``Then you begin to understand the charge.''\par

He read eight facts into the record, and I contested no sequence. The route had been reconstructed from Shield's seals and my own account: unauthorised movement through the exterior ways; defeat of the first archive lock; entry into a sealed Shield chamber; handling of restricted material; removal of two records and one woven object; carriage through inhabited galleries; concealment of one record beneath a Light seal; surrender of the woven object and the other record to Shield. Sa-Tavan set each fact into the tablet.\par

She stepped back. Ka-Leth asked whether I contested her evidence. I said no.\par

A beam from beyond the Mountain may be the first sign of an Outsider Shield has failed to detect; until its source is known, Light may be the only office capable of finding the threat and warning the Mountain.'' Ka-Leth's office had never once looked up. Justice had built a mathematics deep enough to hold the Mountain's fate in a square and a column of figures, yet never learned the shape of the world it judged, because that shape lay outside its charge.\par

His fingertips whitened against the centre stone. A line of sweat moved from his right temple into the collar of Justice. When he had pronounced my death, his hands had lain flat and dry in the same place.\par

Ka-Leth had spent forty years inside the record. Most wrongs came to him with a known shape and cost: a miscounted measure, a stolen ration, a struck child. Another kind of matter had entered the record perhaps a handful of times in eleven thousand years --- without precedent, too large and too little understood for the law to measure its reach. Almost every one had ended in catastrophe.\par

``You will not have heard this very old story. A single word was changed in a record, from \textit{unrelated} to \textit{kin}. The change almost brought about the self-destruction of the Mountain.''\par

``You know of \textit{the Three Seats}? This is a Balance story,'' Ka-Cheryn said.\par

Ka-Elun began, ``It is not a story. The three Veils did more than conspire ---''\par

``Ka-Elun, do not,'' Va-Sheva interrupted from Shield's seat.\par

``Enough. Silence.'' Ka-Leth stood.\par

``A thing has been done in this chamber tonight,'' he said, ``that has not been done in the memory of the record; and Justice has been handed, in open chamber, a thing its office was never given to hold: \textit{the shape of the world}. I know only this: a precedent has been made whose consequence could be that Justice is blind no more. That alone, whatever the truth of any accusation, is more than what the Mountain can carry.'' He looked at me without hatred, which I had stopped hoping for and now received as the cruelest thing in the room. ``I would rather bury a true accusation than let this move one step further.''\par

``I do not question a sequence I do not yet understand,'' Ka-Raedin said.\par

``The accusation against Shield is closed,'' Ka-Leth said. ``It is not named false. It is set beyond pursuit. Shield's motive is received: the threat is real, the watching is necessary, and necessity is not treason.'' He turned to me. ``The accuser may speak, if he has anything further.''\par

``Then hear this,'' I said, and heard my own voice reach the way a falling man reaches for a wall. ``The manner of his answer is the proof of it. He did not meet my accusation --- he drowned it, opening before all of you knowledge not all offices may hold, so that the horror of the crossing would swallow the smaller matter of his treason. I say the closing is itself the crime.''\par

``No.'' There was no anger in Ka-Leth's voice, and that was worse. ``We will not open a trial inside a trial inside another, each cracked to bury the last. That is the thrashing of a condemned man who has felt the water climb to his mouth. The matter is closed, unless you have anything further.''\par

``Ka-Dhavar, speak of the black tree. Speak of the Outsider who came within our walls and left alive, or I will pronounce her name.''\par

Ka-Dhavar stepped from the accused mark and walked its cut edge; he spoke before completing the circle.\par

``Sarah of House Blackwood, who commands the Black Tree, bears the mark of the ascended. She is Ka-Sarah, Keeper of Horizon under Shield. She is a cunning woman who found us by listening to the sound the Mountain makes when a newly raised Keeper is tested. For more than fifty years she has turned surveys, soldiers and instruments away from us. She saw the Pendulum and stood once in this very antechamber. She knew what she was protecting.''\par

He completed the circle. No one spoke.\par

``Shield holds the Threshold. I will say nothing further of my sealed charge. Va-Kailan of Light, if you begin the name of the man in that image, I will cut your throat before it is whole and give Justice my life afterward.''\par

I looked at Va-Sheva in Shield's seat. She did not move. I shook my head.\par

``Then the matter of treason is sealed,'' Ka-Leth said. ``The stay is lifted. I swear under the Promise that my Veil will not inherit the shape of the world. The original hearing resumes.''\par

Ka-Dhavar returned to Shield's seat. Va-Sheva stood again at the wall. I took the accused mark, and Ka-Leth pronounced the sentence again.\par

I had four days, no office and no accusation. The word useful had survived Ka-Raedin's vote for death.\par

I spoke softly enough that the five had to lean in.\par

``I do not contest that I am a danger,'' I said. ``I contest the waste of me. Hear it from each of your offices, since you judged me from them.'' To Faith: ``You named me devout and meant it for a shroud. Let it be a fact. Everything I did, I did believing the Mountain was betrayed and could be healed --- never once against it; and you who read patterns know the shape of me points inward.'' To Balance: ``You will spend nothing of your stores on me. I will cost the cistern nothing it would feel.'' To Justice: ``I regret the precedent; I would carry it out of the world if I could. But a thing that cannot be buried can still be spent --- outward, instead of festering here.'' To Ka-Raedin: ``You called me an eye that sees the seam others walk over. An eye is no use in a grave. It is no use, either, in the one sealed room where there is nothing left to look at.''\par

And then to Shield, and the whole night narrowed to the width of the next sentence.\par

``You called her cunning. How do you know whether she serves Shield or has waited fifty years to turn on the Mountain? You go out. You have always gone out. Men of Shield walk among the Outsiders and come back, and keep what they must keep, and the name they do it under is Horizon. Send me out upon that road. I have the eye.''\par

Ka-Dhavar's hand closed over the arm of his seat.\par

``Ka-Dhavar has spoken Shield's secret before the five,'' I continued. ``My death cannot make it secret again. Shield opened a road into the Outsiders and named it Horizon. You cannot know whether the same road opened back into the Mountain. Light found that road from one irregular beam. Use me to follow it in both directions.''\par

``You have stripped the name and standing of Light from me. I name what remains: shadow, to fight the enemy within. Give me no seat and no judgement. Let Va-Sheva, whom I trust, be the ear I speak into. What concerns the Mountain she will carry to the five; what belongs to one office, to that office alone. I will find, test and report. If her service is true, I will say so. If it conceals betrayal, I will follow it. You need not trust me here. Make me useful at the distance from which the danger cannot come back.''\par

``This is nowhere in the law,'' Ka-Leth said.\par

``No,'' said Ka-Raedin. ``Yet it is in the Mountain's interest, and the Mountain's interest is the thing the law was raised to serve, not the reverse. I told the boy the order of it --- the Mountain first, then the office, then the man. I will not unsay it now, merely because the same order that would have killed him has turned, and asks instead that he be used.''\par

Ka-Leth was quiet a long moment. ``Understand what is before us, and what is not. There are two outcomes in this chamber, and only two: death, or Exception. This is not a third. It is the Exception --- put to a use it has never been put to, sent outward where it has only ever sealed within. If it is granted, it is granted by the five offices explicitly, each in its own voice. Speak the word, or the sentence already spoken stands.''\par

He named them, and one by one they spoke.\par

Ka-Raedin first. ``You are sending light out by the narrowest door in the Mountain and asking it to stay a line. I am the one Keeper here who knows what light does with a narrow door, and I am sworn not to teach it in an open room. Hear this much: light that passes a narrow opening comes out changed, and changes what it falls on. His path, thrown so far, will mark the Mountain. Exception. My word does not restore his name.''\par

Ka-Elun kept one hand on the torn mantle. ``You weigh his distance; I weigh his rhythm. No tread keeps one beat. Give him a year and his foot will strike the Mountain's note ten thousand times. A small thing returning upon the stone's own note is what wakes the deep. Exception. And I do not trust his beat.''\par

Ka-Cheryn turned a hand over as when a column would not come even. ``You would put him outside to keep the inside clean. The sum never falls. Disorder swept from one room is paid for with more in the next; it gathers, warms and leans back on the door. There is no away. Exception, against my count.''\par

Ka-Dhavar remained standing. ``I have set every seal the Mountain owns. Not one is tight. What is known has already copied itself; the knowing has gone ahead of him into every mouth it touched. There is no shut door, only a slow one. Exception. The slow door is Shield's to hold.''\par

Four votes had been spoken. The sentence stood until Justice gave the fifth. Ka-Leth kept his hands above the centre stone and looked past all of us, to the black air.\par

``I never let myself know the world was a sphere. I know this much of closed spaces: some carry a throat, a loop that cannot be tightened to a point. Where every circuit can be drawn shut without tearing, the space is a sphere, however it is bent. We are sending this man towards what we call outside. A seamless world keeps no outside. If his road closes, it closes on us. If it stays open, whose law governs the road?'' He laid his empty hands flat on the centre stone. ``Exception --- and on my head be the throat I could not find.''\par

The five had spoken it. Ka-Leth returned, last, to say aloud what they had made.\par

``It is one voice,'' he said. ``The sentence of death is stayed, and its cover kept whole. To the Mountain you are the devout boy the Labyrinth took; on the fourth day they will grieve you, and you will let them, and never once, while you live, send them word that the grief is misspent. What you are from this breath has no name in any Book. This record will call it \textit{Keeper of Shadow}; the name grants no office and no seat. You go out under Shield, by the door we do not name above the deep, and you send back nothing but the truth, through Va-Sheva, until the five release you or the desert does. You asked to be made useful at a distance. The Mountain grants you the distance. Do not mistake it for a pardon. It is the longest leash the law has ever paid out, and it is a leash still.''\par

No one asked him to explain the sphere. I carried the words out without their sense.\par

Ka-Raedin came within an arm's length before the transfer and lowered his voice. ``Your seal held.'' He let that stand between us, then said, ``You are alive.''\par

``Yes, Keeper.''\par

``You will be tempted to think that being useful is the same as being forgiven. It is the opposite. You have bought yourself time, and nothing else --- time to become capable of carrying what you forced yourself to see. Go and be the eye. Shadow is Shield's answer to you now. The eye is also, and will remain\ldots{} Light's.'' He went back to his seat beneath the dark aperture and fixed his eyes upon it.\par

Ka-Elun stopped me where the ways parted --- the others' road climbing back the way we had come, and mine going out by some other seam, toward the surface and the sky I had been raised to fear. ``Do not make a gospel of what has happened to you,'' she said.\par

``No, Keeper.''\par

``That will be harder than silence. You have a martyr's material now, and a preacher's tongue, and neither the office nor the innocence to be trusted with either. Carry the truth. Do not preach it. The Beast knows neither faithful nor stranger. Neither, now, does your story. Give the account to the ear the five have named, and to no one else.'' She turned into the dark.\par

Va-Sheva walked me from the fork. When we were alone, I said, ``I made your crossing worthless.''\par

``Yes,'' she said. She kept it hard, and I loved her for keeping it hard.\par

``And now they have named me Keeper of Shadow, and made you my keeper instead.''\par

``Now I am the thread they have let you keep,'' she said. ``Do not test what it will bear.''\par

``Were death and Exception always the only two?''\par

She looked at me fully. ``There were only ever two. You made the Exception into a door.''\par

We reached a passage new to me. The air was colder there, carrying the mineral draught of sealed routes. Va-Sheva took a strip of dark cloth from her belt, covered the white knot at my wrist and bound the covering with a dark cord below it. She pulled the knot once, testing it, and waited.\par

``Shield receives a man at the threshold without office property, household message or private mark,'' she said. ``Anything carried out may become a route carried back.''\par

I opened my hands. She checked the inner seam where the Light token had rested, the belt folds, the stitching at both cuffs and the soles of my sandals. Her fingers found the place the disc had warmed and moved on. Ka-Raedin held it. The search was still made.\par

``Nothing from Light,'' she said.\par

``Nothing.''\par

``Nothing for your household.''\par

My mother's face came before me with the ordinary exactness of a meal-place: her thumb smoothing a cracked bowl, the small dark mark beside one nail. ``Nothing.''\par

Va-Sheva closed my robe and tied the belt herself.

``Will they truly believe I am dead?''\par

``They will grieve you on the fourth day,'' she said. ``The grief will be real. That is what makes it hold.''\par

I stepped into the rising passage. No one put a hand on me.\par
