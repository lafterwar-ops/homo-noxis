\typesetchapter{Chapter 9}{Glint}

\begin{room}


--- The thirty-first of March; the eighth of our interviews. Before we return to your account --- did you read the \textit{Genesis} I translated for you?

--- You promised me the first chapter and gave me four; thank you, Paul, though I do not yet know whether it was a kindness.

--- I could not sensibly stop before the first murder. But keep one thing near you as you set them side by side: a shared shape tells you nothing of which bore which, nor whether either was ever true.

--- Your murderer looks like ours. You call him \textit{Cain}, son of Adam; ours is his grandson. The envy, the secret killing, the blood in the ground, the exile, the gate shut behind him --- all of it kept, and the Mountain lost. Your God forbids the woman the fruit and swears it will kill her; the serpent tells her the boundary may guard God rather than her --- which is my own craft, Paul: a second meaning set beside the first. She looks. She crosses. Her eyes open --- and your own God says she has become like one of them, knowing good and evil. My Mountain would call that an ascension. You call it a fall, and the one who opened her eyes you call evil. So tell me: why is your serpent evil?

--- Hear what you asked. To weigh whether the serpent's gift was good or evil I must use good and evil to weigh it --- which is the fruit; that story cannot be judged from outside it. You have set the apple in front of me and asked how it tastes. Your reading is clean, and too clean. He tells them they will not die, and they die --- slowly, by what they have done; he opens one hidden thing and lays another down in the opening, as Light does. Her eyes come open on her nakedness, her fear, her separation. A crossing, yes --- and a crossing may go where there is no return.

--- You are playing a trick on me, Paul --- a Faith trick, and a poor escape for a man of science. I need no fruit to judge, only wisdom; I need not be Greek to judge \textit{Eris}, nor to have eaten to judge the serpent, and neither do you. If your \textit{Attenborough} and your \textit{Darwin} are to be believed --- and I believe them, for I built the test and it stood --- these are only stories, made for a purpose, and it matters nothing which was told first. 

--- Not a trick, just one poinf of view to consider. When a hundred peoples who never met invent the same one --- the crossing, the opened eye, the gate that shuts --- each is putting a face on a true thing that happened to them all. No serpent spoke; the shape it stands for is real. So, with no fruit in it: the tellers call the serpent evil because a story needs a name for the thing that opens a door which opens one way only, and hangs on that name everything that comes after. Judge the serpent as you like. It is the door I would have you look at.

--- \ldots{} That is the part I did not want. A forbidden act; widened knowledge; the self split into the one who lives and the one who watches himself live; exile; the home still standing, and shut. I began by reading \textit{Eve} as the first of my kind to practise Light. I ended by finding that her punishment is my life. Your \textit{Cain} was the resemblance I wanted; your \textit{Eve} the one I did not. I have not finished being expelled from \textit{Eden} --- and the next part of my account begins with the reward I was given for crossing a boundary blind.

\end{room}

It began to catch Light at the edges of Light. The watching had changed the Mountain.\par

Small leaks. Accidental glints. Exterior light entering in wrong seasons; internal lamp-lines escaping through vents and returning from stone beyond the official paths. At first I dismissed them as maintenance defects --- a tarnished plate, a wet patch catching more than it should. Yet certain wrongnesses repeated. A faint white flash beyond a high ventilation slot in the south-east service descent, visible only as the night lamps were trimmed. A reflected thread along the upper edge of a sealed Shield route, present three nights and absent the fourth. A pale return from far outside the Mountain when internal lamps burned late and the air beyond the Oasis was dry.\par

I let the first glint pass. I recorded nothing, spoke to no one. The old Sa-Kailan would have gone after the first brightness with a stolen lamp and a heroic justification; I watched for twelve nights instead. The flash failed when high dust crossed the moon. It fed on a deeper glow from a maintenance shaft worked after evening. It showed from one position only in the old vent gallery, and only if I lowered my head to the line where the outside air entered before it became Mountain air.\par

Then memory supplied the missing shape. As Sa-Kailan, Candidate of Light, with dust in my sandals and Va-Sheva beside me saying softly: \textit{Do not point.} Va-Sheva closing possibilities before danger had a name. A flash may be nothing. A turned marker may be nothing. An old woman out of place may be nothing. Three nothings close together become a shape. Shield had built the world so that small wrongnesses could be read before they became deaths: markers, watchers, angles, hidden bodies, silent signals. Light sees what can be shown; Shield sees what must be stopped before it shows itself. What had once been said to me as a rebuke now sounded like an invitation, and I distrusted it. For several nights I only made myself useful, supervising younger Candidates in the practice halls. I spared them the teaching Va-Raedin had given me; I was still too young to make cruelty look like skill. When one boy rushed a false door and struck his shoulder, I waited until the exercise ended and said, privately,\par

``The room did not defeat you. Your first answer did.''\par

The boy looked at me with a resentment so familiar that I almost smiled. Former peers avoided my gaze. Older Light attendants tested my authority with small omissions --- a report handed late, a lamp plate left unpolished, a route question phrased too casually. I did not answer with anger, and I did not soften it either. But in the empty hours of the night, I returned to the glint. \par

I chose the route only after I could close my eyes and walk it in memory. The eastern lower spine to the old vent gallery; down the maintenance stair past the disused oil shelves; the narrow inspection slit above the sealed north passage; a pause for the third Shield change; across the dark water service, where my robe would take damp if I brushed the left wall; then the old air chamber above the cedar descent, where outside breath moved through stone before it became Mountain air.\par

Deeper still lay the compost dark, where the Mountain took back what it had spent: kitchen scrap and night-soil and spoiled straw, turned and turned again by men working half-naked in the heat, the worms running thick under their forks. The cistern water here stood green and half-alive, and a keeper drew a measure of the weed from it each day.
So far I had only looked. On the night I chose, the Mountain was quiet after a minor mourning rite. Faith had held the lower Hall late for a child who had died of fever before naming. Such deaths were common enough to pass without shock, but they always altered its sound. The night lamps burned low. The air was dry. No dust cloud veiled the moon beyond the hidden openings. The glint appeared twice before second-night silence, faint and precise.\par

I waited until it appeared a third time. Then I went, and carried no blade; a blade would have made me a frightened Veil, pretending the desert was something metal could bargain with. The passage outward yielded to pressure where the lower stone had been shaved thin. Beyond it the Mountain became less human. Someone had cut handholds and worn them smooth, and carved a line that fingers could find and low light hid. It had none of Light's interpretive softness, none of Balance's measured signs. It existed because some men had to move through places the rest were forbidden even to picture.\par

Cold air came through cracks ahead. I paused before the final opening. The subtler fear was worse than any curse out of the Book of Blood: that what I was calling wisdom, here at the boundary, was hunger. I had an answer ready --- \textit{Light must follow what is revealed} --- and I distrusted it, because it sounded exactly like something I would say to excuse myself. Still, I opened the stone and stepped out.\par

The night desert was another country from the one I had seen by day --- larger, because it showed me almost nothing. The Mountain rose behind me as blackness with weight. I wrapped the mouth cloth against the instinct to breathe too quickly. The air tasted of stone, salt, and distance. 
I kept low in the Mountain's shadow until the ground fell away toward the outer folds. The Oasis lay out of sight below another ridge, hidden even from me. Once I found a line of small stones that might have been natural, except their flatter faces all turned the same way. I circled wider, losing time and confidence. I was Light, and the desert made that plain quickly. My sandals, made for stone, slipped on loose gravel; the cold stiffened my fingers; the water skin knocked against my side until I tied it higher. I misjudged distance again and again --- a rock that seemed close took a thousand breaths to reach. I carried the glint's direction from inside the Mountain, but without walls my eye had too much to hold, and it kept fooling me.\par

I stopped at the first low ridge and looked back.\par

From there the Mountain looked like a sleeping beast, vast and black, with a few hidden breaths of light where the vents opened under shadow. The land folded around it. The Mountain's shoulder cut the sightlines. A man could die out here within sight of the Oasis, only because the land never gave him the angle to see it.\par

The glint appeared again while I crossed the second wash --- once, far ahead and higher than I expected. Too clean, too brief; a white point, then gone. I froze and waited. Nothing. I shifted my head a finger's width. Nothing. I almost smiled, then remembered Va-Sheva: \textit{Do not start believing the outside speaks to you.}\par

The ground rose in broken shelves, each one stealing the next from sight. I marked three stones with dust rubs plain only to a trained eye, counted my steps between turns, and drank once. The water tasted of leather and guilt.\par

The outcrop, when I reached it, looked natural at first.\par

From below the glint stayed dark. I moved around the stone slowly, searching for wrongness --- a surface too smooth, a crack too straight, a place where the dust failed to gather. Shield would build so that no one would think to look for a door at all.\par

I found the polished mineral only after kneeling.\par

It sat in a shallow hollow no wider than my hand, behind a lip of stone: a mineral plate polished by hand and angled toward the Mountain, lost among pale scratches by day, but at the right lamp or moon angle it caught and returned a single point --- a signal only one placed to read it could read.\par

Beside it, almost worn away, was a mark cut into the stone.\par

A threshold line, broken twice, with a small inward notch.\par

Shield.\par

The outcrop was Shield. A point beyond the permitted prayer ground, aligned with Mountain light, invisible to ordinary eyes, reachable only by a dangerous route.\par

I felt no triumph. Light had led me sideways, into another Kahir's secret. The proper thing was to withdraw and report, and let Keeper answer Keeper --- which was also the surest way to have Shield seal the place before I knew what I had seen. I stood in the cold until the choice stopped arguing with itself.\par

I chose to go in and know, whatever it would cost.\par

The mechanism resisted silently, then gave with a thickness that spoke of long maintenance. Air breathed out from within. Dry. Old. Mineral.\par

Full.\par

I slipped inside and pulled the stone nearly closed behind me, leaving a finger's width for air and for return.\par

The passage sloped downward, narrow at first, forcing me sideways, then widened into a cut corridor whose walls bore neither Light markings nor Faith text. Dust lay thin over the floor, enough to show where feet had disturbed it, though few had passed lately. I held my lamp low and moved slowly.\par

After twenty paces the corridor divided. One branch descended deeper into darkness. The other angled inward toward the Mountain.\par

I chose neither immediately.\par

No entry mark, no return mark, no prayer, no measure, no instruction. Only stone and the smell of preserved air.\par

\textit{Quarantine,} I thought.\par

Then another word came, less precise and more terrible.\par

\textit{Tomb.}\par

I took the descending branch.\par

It ended in a chamber whose first impression was emptiness. Then my lamp found the nearest shelf.\par

Objects.\par

At first only a few: stones, broken blades, small bowls, fragments of painted surface, a corroded shape that might once have been a tool or an idol. Then the flame widened and more shelves appeared, rising along the walls, tier after tier, receding into darkness. A second chamber opened beyond the first, then a third, each cut lower and lined with recesses. No bodies lay there. No bones arranged for burial. Yet death was in it all the same.\par

Civilisations had entered here as fragments.\par

They had stayed.\par

I lifted the lamp to the nearest shelf. Every object was tagged, and every tag was Shield's --- the broken-threshold sign at the top, and beneath it an official hand closed to public reading. Shield alone had catalogued this place; Shield alone had ever been meant to stand in it. The other four Kahirs would have had to be told the room existed before they could so much as fear it. Each tag stated what the object was believed to be, how it had entered, who had handled it, and whether it could be touched, decay, or carry doctrinal danger.\par

Archive, then. Quarantine and memory together.\par

The first object I truly saw was a small bronze figure of a man holding a weapon shaped like three prongs. The body had gone green with age, the beard set in formal curls. Around the base, faint wave marks had been scratched or cast. The Shield tag gave no name I knew, only its own hand: \textit{water-lord; corruption of the deep-water memory; metal stable, salts present, do not wash.}\par

\begin{room}

--- I know him as Poseidon now. Thanks to your \textit{Iliad}.

\end{room}

A water god --- a man-shaped power standing proud with a weapon in his hand. Outsiders had taken the memory of deep water and made it a lord of appetite, the wish to command water without counting it.\par

Next came a dark figure with the head of an animal on the body of a man. Long snout, upright ears, narrow limbs. Black surface, gold traces, eyes shaped with an unsettling calm. Beneath it lay a cloth fragment marked with faded symbols and a small carving of a wrapped human shape, bound head to foot. The Shield tag bore a warning older than the hand that had written it. I looked away, then made myself look back.\par

The next chamber changed material. Painted stone. Carved panels. A fragment of image preserved behind cloudy glass. I held the lamp high and saw a man with a blade raised over a bound youth. The youth lay on something close to an altar. Above the man's hand, a sign of Heaven: lines descending from cloud or fire. The father's face had been damaged, but the blade remained clear.\par

For several breaths the horror escaped me.\par

Then the Book of Blood did it for me. Brother against brother. Father against son. Hunger made law. A broken piece of Gospel called whole.\par

Outsiders had carved a holy story: a father binding his own son to kill him while the animal stood spared beside them. They had made obedience a willingness to murder the child before the goat --- authority coming down on the helpless, a blade raised by nothing but the right of being the father.\par

The Shield tag below it carried more than the others: \textit{threshold danger; doctrinal contamination; retained for comparative doctrine; image damaged, blade line clear.}\par

Comparative doctrine. A phrase too calm for what it held.\par

The next image was worse, because the people in it looked tender, and what they were gathered around was cruel. A small metal cross, then a larger one of wood darkened by age. Then a painted fragment of people kneeling before a tortured man fixed upon crossed beams. His head drooped. His ribs showed. Wounds marked hands, feet, side. Around him the worshippers lifted their faces in reverence. Some wept. Some reached toward him.\par

I stared.\par

The Mountain had martyrdom --- I granted that now; I had seen Syphiron die. But this was different, or seemed so: Syphiron cut himself when the link was complete, while this man had been fixed, displayed, adored in his suffering. The Outsiders had turned pain itself into an altar and knelt to it.\par

I slowed myself. \textit{Veil, not boy. Interpret after seeing.}\par

I looked again. The worshippers' faces were grieving. Something lived in the tortured man's face that the artifact failed to carry across time, because I lacked the language for it. To the Outsiders, holiness seemed to go in through torture and come out wearing gold.\par

Beyond it, on a lower shelf, stood a figure of a woman unlike any gravure in the Mountain. Many arms spread from her body, each hand open or holding some small thing. Many breasts, or ornaments like breasts, covered her chest. Her face was serene and excessive at once. Around her feet were small animal shapes, fruits, weapons, and open hands. There was more of everything on her than the Mountain would grant a man in a year.\par

A false water lord. The animal's head above the wrapped body. A father's blade over a son. Worship before torture. The woman with too many of everything. Each might have meant something else to the people who made it; whatever reached me came damaged.\par

The shelves went on past these, older and stranger, more than one lamp and one night could hold, and I let most of them stay dark.\par

Each shelf carried its chain --- object, source, risk, keeping --- and one phrase recurred down the tiers like a refrain: \textit{harmless unless taught.}\par

Time became material. Stone became bronze, bronze became iron, iron became glass, glass became paper, paper became photograph.\par

The chamber widened at the modern section. Here the shelves became cabinets, some locked, some open. The air was drier, kept by hidden vents. The labels were newer and more dangerous, and all of them Shield's --- classification, preservation, restriction, the angle from which a thing might be safely looked at. The handwriting varied from tenure to tenure. The system held.\par

That was where I felt the danger.\par

Old corrupted gods could be explained --- Shield guarded the threshold, and a threshold needs a guard who has seen what waits beyond it. I could accept that, uneasily.\par

Then I saw the photographs.\par

The first showed a woman under hard light. Her blonde hair was tied back. Her clothes were unlike anything in the Mountain --- practical, pale, shaped for heat, marked by pockets and seams whose purpose escaped me. Beside her stood a man in desert clothing, his face lined, his posture loose in the way Shield posture loosened only after readiness had entered the bones. Behind them, partly visible, was a rock face I recognised with a sudden bodily certainty.\par

Another place, near the Mountain's outer folds.\par

The lower white border carried marks in an outsider hand. Beneath them, in careful Shield script, someone had copied two names.\par

Sarah Blackwood.\par

Amastan.\par

I stared at the woman. Recent enough that the colours still held, that the Mountain's current elders might have been breathing when she was. I had never heard the name, and finding it here, in a Shield drawer, was worse than if I had.\par
\begin{room}
--- Sarah Blackwook.

--- Sarah Blackwood indeed.
\end{room}

The second photograph showed no face. Only desert, distance, and a terrible flower of light rising from the horizon, too large to be flame and too ordered to be storm. The sky around it had been bleached almost empty. On the lower border, in the same unfamiliar hand, were marks I had never learned to read. One sequence had been copied beneath in Shield script:\par

Reggane, 1960.\par

The word and the numbers meant nothing to me --- a place, a date, a wound the Outsiders had set down because even they had been frightened by what their own hands had made. What that flower was lay beyond me. All I knew was that it was too large to leave.\par

I set the two photographs down as if they might accuse me of touching them.\par

The cabinet below held maps, some in outsider lines, some in Shield marks: Mountain folds, dry washes, glint points, hidden sightlines, the Oasis shown by the blank space the routes bent around. And a route leading from the outcrop inward, splitting toward the Labyrinth and, impossibly, toward service routes near the Hall of Ceremony.\par

I unfolded another map. It bore names in scripts beyond my reading, but three had been copied in Mountain hand: Amastan, Sarah, Ka-Xhian. \emph{Ka} --- a Keeper; Shield had recorded the contact with a title. Lines ran from the names to dates, routes, and small boxed terms: \textit{Breach. Containment. Exchange. Loss. Sealed. Limited disclosure. Threshold Exception.}\par

Exchange. I read the word three times.\par

At the back of the map cabinet lay a Shield record tablet.\par

I lifted it with both hands. Compressed, official, closed to public reading, it preserved a sequence --- and that was the dangerous thing.\par

\textit{Contact maintained under Threshold Exception.}\par

\textit{Outsider asset contained beyond full breach.}\par

\textit{Amastan intermediary confirmed.}\par

\textit{Sarah Blackwood retained under observation.}\par

\textit{Ka-Xhian authorised limited disclosure.}\par

\textit{Horizon note sealed.}\par

The last line was a word no lesson had ever given me, and the tablet let it stay sealed.\par


I set the tablet down carefully, because my hands had begun to shake.\par

I arranged the facts. Records of Sarah Blackwood and Amastan; a Keeper, Ka-Xhian, who had authorised disclosure under something called \textit{Threshold Exception}; maps of routes no hidden man was ever taught. Shield had kept it all in sequence, in its own closed hand, and none of it had ever been spoken in Genesis, Blood, Beast, Cataclysm, Covenant, ceremony, lesson, or public record.\par

It proved less than treason. Divided knowledge was law; an exception could be mercy. Yet corrected for every doctrine I was trained to correct for, the central fact stood: Shield had stood before the people as the threshold against Outsiders while keeping its own channels to them, and had watched children kneel before Blood and Beast while holding photographs of the murderer's children in a secret drawer.\par

And Va-Sheva belonged to Shield.\par

This lay beyond Va-Sheva. She was Veil, not Keeper, young for the office she held; she might know nothing of this chamber, or know everything, and have looked at me in the Oasis from inside a lie so large it had become her natural air.\par

I closed the drawer. The sound was soft, and even so it seemed too final.\par

Beyond the modern cabinets a narrow passage led back toward the Mountain, and I followed it with the lamp held low.\par

The smell reached me before the room did. The archive had been dry mineral; this was older air gone wrong --- piss, and sweat, and under both of them the flat iron of blood. The passage opened into a low cell cut from raw stone. A single fixed seat. Rings set into the rock at the height of a man's wrists and his ankles. A shallow gutter worked into the floor and sloped to a drain. The walls were thick, and the stone drank sound; I clapped once, softly, and the room gave me back nothing. A man could be held here and worked on for as long as it took, off every map and beyond every permitted route, and no living soul in the Mountain would hear him call.\par

Someone had called. The stains had gone down into the seams of the floor and dried there black.\par

Near the foot of the seat, pressed into the stone where the blood had set around it and held it fast, lay a narrow band of woven cloth. I knew it before I let myself know it. I had spent too many hours in the Oasis with nothing to do but look at her, and a man remembers what he has looked at too long. It was a ribbon from Va-Sheva's hair. There was no mistaking the weave of it, nor the colour, though half the colour had gone down into the dark stain that fixed it to the floor. It was hers. It was in the stone, and it was in the blood.\par

I knelt with the lamp, and I did not touch it.\par

Past the cell the passage ended at a sealed door, and the door bore one mark only, cut deep and cut old: Shield, and beneath it Shield again, and beneath that a Shield seal I had never been taught, older than Ka-Raedin's tenure, perhaps older than Syphiron's. Whatever lay behind it, Shield had sealed for Shield, and had never once meant a Veil of Light to be standing in this passage to read the sign at all.\par

I left the door sealed.\par

The old Sa-Kailan would have pressed, searched, scratched, risked the alarms, and told himself that holding back was cowardice and forcing the door was his duty. The proof I already held was more dangerous than anything I could force before dawn.\par

I had spent enough for one night.\par

To take anything tagged was breach, and would leave a gap in the count. I needed something slight enough that its absence would pass, and found it in a loose copy fragment at the back of the map bundle --- a scrap bearing four entries in Mountain hand:\par

Sarah Blackwood. Amastan. Ka-Xhian Of Shield. Threshold Exception.\par

I folded it once and placed it inside the inner seam of my robe. Then I stood in the Tomb --- or the Chamber of Thresholds, or whatever Shield named this place when it dared name it at all --- and let the full weight of it enter me.\par

It was the name that undid me. Chamber of Thresholds. I had crossed one to stand in it, and I understood at last what the whole Mountain was, above in the light and down here in the dark alike: an engine for the governing of crossings, and this room its hidden heart. My people had let all of it in --- the water-lord, the death-keeper, the father with the blade, the adored and tortured man --- and had kept each one by forbidding it to mean anything. The statue was admitted; its god was held at the door. Take in the body, and quarantine the meaning. Let a beam pass a wall, so long as no man passed with it. Let a boy walk down into the Oasis, if the water could wash the desert off him at the gate.\par

And kneeling there I saw why the art must fail, and had perhaps already failed in me. A threshold runs one way. A man may cross it; he cannot cross back into the one who had not. The dust washes off. The flash does not. They had been right to fear every object on these shelves, because the one thing no gate holds at the door is what has already gone in through an eye. I had come to learn what this room kept, and in the learning I had become the thing it was built to hold out --- a place inside the Mountain where the outside had entered and could never again be made to mean nothing. The scrap folded in my seam was a courtesy to my own memory. I was already the breach.\par

The Outsiders had remembered broken pieces; Blood had drawn its warnings from something real. Had the doctrine been merely false I could have thrown it out, but it was true, and being used to cover an exception, and there was no clean place to stand in that.\par

I lifted the lamp and looked once more at Sarah Blackwood's face. The Outsider woman stood under desert light, half squinting. She looked ordinary. I had been taught that the dangerous ones rarely looked dangerous at first.\par

I lowered the lamp.\par

I would hold my rage. I would keep away from Va-Sheva --- whose ribbon was set in blood in a room I had no name for, and who was either the hand that had drawn blood there or the girl who had left it, and I lacked the strength that night to learn which. I would keep the evidence in my hands. I would not spill it in front of Ka-Raedin like a child demanding the Mountain rearrange itself around his outrage. I would think. Sequence mattered. Witness mattered. A word in the wrong place could let them call it all a breach before the truth in it had a chance to hold.\par

I left as carefully as I had entered.\par

The return through the passage felt longer. The sky had paled at its lowest edge --- a warning before sunrise. I moved faster than I should have and slower than fear wanted. The marks I had made on the stones brought me back across the washes. Twice I stopped to restore disturbed dust with the side of my sandal. Nothing came. Or something came and stayed hidden.\par

When I reached the hidden vent above the old air chamber, my hands were numb, my mouth dry, and the water skin nearly empty. The Mountain received me without welcome. Stone closed behind me. Warmth returned gradually --- first as smell, then as pressure, then as accusation.\par

I replaced the grille, checked the seams, brushed grit from the lower ledge, and stood in the darkness until my breathing no longer sounded like a confession.\par

Inside my robe, the scrap touched my skin.\par

Sarah Blackwood. Amastan. Ka-Xhian. Threshold Exception.\par

I left it folded. Every word of it was already in me.\par

By the First hour I stood in the eastern chamber before Ka-Raedin arrived --- washed, robed, composed, no dust on my sandals and no sign that the world beneath the Mountain had opened sideways in the night. When the Keeper entered, his eyes moved once over my face, then down to my hands, then to the floor around my feet.\par

``You slept poorly,'' he said.\par

``Yes, Keeper.''\par

``Why?''\par

I looked at him. There had been a time when the question would have frightened me into cleverness. Now I let the silence hold.\par

``Because the title is heavier in the dark,'' I said.\par

He studied me.\par

``Good.''\par

He turned toward the basins.\par

``Today we learn a new interference pattern.''\par

I followed.\par

I had found something badly wrong, and I had not yet earned the standing to say so aloud.\par

--- You did not interrupt me a single time today.

--- No. I did not want to interrupt you story, or distract you from the account. I wanted it clean and true. We have arrived at an important moment of your account. One I was told to expect. From Thursday, Sarah shall be joining us, it's a long holiday week end; and she is finally available.

--- Why is this day holy? what is special about it ?

--- It has to do with the man you saw on the cross; and it is long and hard to explain. 

\end{room}
