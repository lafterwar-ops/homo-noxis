\typesetchapter{Chapter 24}{The Whole}

When a thing is finished being read there is a silence that belongs to it, and we gave Zero his, in the cold, with the last sheet down and the machine turning and the borrowed sun long gone off the wall. And then the silence ended, the way all of them do, because someone has to be the one to say what happens now, and it fell to me, because I was the one he had built the door for.\par

The question had changed while we read, and I want to set the change down plainly, because everything after it turns on it. We had come down that stair carrying the question every one of us had carried since the Beast fired: what have we done, and how do we live with it. That question was answered now, and the answer was Zero's, and it was no comfort: we had done what the machine was built to make us do, correctly, by law, for the sixth time, and we would live with it the way the five generations before us had lived with it, which was by forgetting. That was the offer on the table, though no one had yet said it aloud. The clock could be reset. The memory could be wound back to nothing. The children born into the new dark could be taught, as I had been taught, as Zero's first children had been taught, that the world was young and the Mountain eternal and the Beast a story, and the whole beautiful terrible machine would close over the truth again and run on toward a seventh firing that no one would know was the seventh. It works. Sarah had said it in the high dark by the seams. It works, and it wants to keep working, and the easiest thing in the world, the thing every generation before us had chosen, was to let it.\par

So the question was no longer how we lived with what we had done. It was whether the wheel would be allowed to turn again in the dark, or whether we would be the first spoke in a hundred thousand years to refuse to be wound back.\par

\ornament

I felt Zero working in me before I understood what he was doing, and I set this down as a warning to whoever reads it, because it is the most dangerous thing in this whole account and it does not look dangerous at all. It looks like grief.\par

He had left me more than a confession. He had left me a shape, a way of turning a wound into a world, and it fitted my hand because it had been made for my hand, a hundred thousand years before, by a man of exactly my kind carrying exactly my hurt. And I felt it offer itself. Because I too had been left. I had stood in a country I could not find and watched a woman I had spent years revering step, for the length of one breath, into a man from the outside, and be, in that breath, more than the one finished thing I had required her to be, and glad. And here, in Zero's hand, was the great and comforting doctrine that made sense of it, that dignified it, that raised it up off the floor of my private humiliation and set it among the fates of peoples: that this is what they do, the outside, the warm ones, the singers; that they take our women with their ease and their laughter and their permission; that Va-Sheva had not chosen a man, she had been claimed by a species, the way Ithe had been claimed, the way my whole kind had been slowly claimed across a hundred thousand years; and that my hurt was therefore not a small thing to be borne but a large thing to be answered, a wound in the body of my people, a reason.\par

It would have been so easy. That is what I need you to understand. It would have been the easiest thing I have ever done, to pick up Zero's shape and put my grief inside it and let the grief become a doctrine, because a doctrine does not ache the way a grief aches; a doctrine you can act on; a doctrine gives you somewhere to put the pain that is not your own chest. He built the Mountain to house a pain he could not otherwise carry. I understood, sitting there, that I was one decision away from doing the same thing on whatever scale was left to me, and that Zero had known I would be, and had left me the tools, not to warn me, but because he wanted company, because a man alone in a crime for a hundred thousand years will build a door in the hope that the one who opens it will understand, and forgive, by doing the same.\par

\ornament

I did not do the same, and the reason I did not is a small thing, and the whole of the book turns on its smallness, and I have thought since that this is the only wisdom I came out of that Mountain owning.\par

I made a distinction. Zero could not make it. Zero had a woman leave him and he called it a species. I made myself say the true and smaller thing instead, and I made myself keep saying it until it held: Va-Sheva did not choose Sapiens. Va-Sheva chose Jean-Charles. She did not leave me for the warm ease of a whole people; there is no whole people; there was a man, one man, particular past any generalising, who bought sweets for no reason and carried a heretic under a cloth and held a stair at the end of a world and stopped being able to smile, and she chose him, for reasons that were hers and his and no one else's in the history of the Earth, and it had nothing to do with what he was and everything to do with who. To make her choice into a fact about a species was to do to her exactly what Zero had done to Ithe, which was to refuse her the dignity of being a person who chose, and to turn her, instead, into evidence. And I had loved her. And you cannot both love a person and use them as evidence. Zero never learned that. It is the whole of what he never learned, and it is the reason there is a Mountain. He would not let Ithe be a woman who chose. He made her a symptom, and built a hundred thousand years of consequence on the diagnosis. I let Va-Sheva be a woman who chose. That is the off-switch. It is that small. It is only that.\par

\ornament

She came to me, near the end of the ninety days, because I had asked her to, and I had meant to do a thing I am ashamed of, which was to make her explain it to me, to account for it, to give me the reason in a form I could hold, as though a reason would close the wound, as though I were owed one. She knew before I opened my mouth. She always knew; it was her office and her nature both.\par

``You want me to explain him to you,'' she said. ``Or to explain you to yourself by way of him. I will not do either. I have watched you read a hundred thousand years of a man who could not stop demanding that a woman's choice make sense to him, and I will not be the woman who finally provides the sense, not to you, not to anyone, because the sense is not owed and it was never the point.'' She sat, not close. ``I loved you. I want that said plainly, once, because you have earned plainly, and because I will not have you spend the rest of your life able to doubt it. I loved you, and I contained you to keep you breathing, and I grieved you all the way to the end while I did it, and none of that is the same thing as choosing to stand the rest of my life beside you. Those are different questions. You have always folded them into one, the loving and the choosing, and required that if a person loved you they must choose you, and it is the most tiring thing about you, and it is the thing you share with the man in the ledgers, and it is the thing I could not live inside.''\par

``And Jean-Charles,'' I said, because I could not help it, because Zero was still in me, reaching.\par

``Is a man I chose,'' she said. ``That is the whole of the sentence. There is no \emph{because} that you are owed. He is not the outside. He is not the warm south. He is not a lesson about your people or a proof about mine. He is a man, and I chose him, and if you make him into anything larger than that you will have become the thing we just spent a season reading about, and I will grieve you a second time, and worse, because the first time I lost you to a cage and this time it would be to a doctrine, and I have buried enough of you.'' She stood. ``Do not make my life into your reason. I am not a reason. I am a person who chose, and the choosing is mine, and you do not get to have it, and that, and not the containment, and not the desert, and not any of it, is the last thing I have to teach you.''\par

And Jean-Charles was there, at the edge of it, because Shield had brought him and because he would not have let her come alone into a room with an old grief; and he did not smile, and he did not stand near her in the way a man stands who is claiming a thing; he only stood, present, and looked at me with the whole of what he had become in that Mountain in his face, and said one thing, quietly, when she had finished. ``I understand more than I say, man. I always did. That is why the jokes.'' And that was all. He did not defend her, because she needed no defending. He did not triumph, because there was nothing to triumph over. He had understood, before any of us, that the only decent thing a man can do in the presence of a choice that was not his to make is to be quiet, and be kind, and not require it to be about him. I have tried to learn it since. It is harder than the off-switch. It is the same lesson, worn all the way down to where you live.\par

\ornament

Sarah did the thing that saved us, and she did it as an enemy, which is the only way it could have been done, and she knew that, and she made me see it.\par

She had the whole now. That was the fact none of us could get around. She had come to that Mountain the enemy of it, the one hand it feared, the woman who had spent forty years reaching for exactly the thing that now sat in a chamber at the bottom of the world with the door standing open: the account, the mechanism, the drill, the year-clock, the whole of Zero's design, entire, in one place, readable, and she was the only person alive outside the five who could read all of it and understand what she held. Everything she had ever wanted was finally, actually, hers to take. And the Cataclysm above us was the proof of what taking it cost.\par

``You are all waiting to see what I will do,'' she said, on the last day, in the Archive, with the Keepers gathered and Paul's tapes and transcriptions stacked and the door still open on the machine. ``And you are right to wait, and you are right to be afraid, because I have spent my whole life becoming the person who takes this, and the habit of a life does not die because the life has learned better. So I will not ask you to trust that I have changed. I have not changed. I want it exactly as much as I ever did. I am going to do the one thing that works anyway, the thing that does not require me to have become good, because I have watched two civilisations now stake everything on a hand becoming good and I have seen how that ends.'' She put her hand on the stack of Paul's record. ``I am going to take it. All of it. The account, the confession, the proof, the whole. And then I am going to make it worthless to hold, by making certain that everyone holds it.''\par

That was her refusal, and it was active, and it was cold, and it was the cleverest and most honest thing I have ever watched a person do. She would take the complete record out of the Mountain, and she would copy it, and she would place the copies, deliberately, into the hands of powers that hated one another, archives and services and interests across the ruined world that would never cooperate, never combine, never let one another hold alone a thing this dangerous; so that no single hand, including her own, could ever again possess the whole in secret, because the whole would be everywhere, and a secret that is everywhere is not a weapon any more. She would turn Zero's own founding law, the division of knowledge that no one hand may hold the whole, inside out, and outward, and let it loose upon the world that had tried to seize the Mountain, and it would protect the Mountain better than any seal, because you do not mount a secret operation to steal a thing that is already in a dozen mutually hostile hands. The taking was the protecting. The only way to keep the whole from a single hand forever was to give it, on purpose, to everyone.\par

``And they will trust it came from you,'' I said, understanding, ``because you are the last person on Earth anyone would believe had given a thing away.''\par

``They will trust it because I had every reason to keep it and did not,'' she said. ``That is the only kind of trust that has ever meant anything. Trust that costs the trusted nothing is worth nothing. You are placing the most dangerous knowledge in the world in the hand of the one person who wanted it most, and who could most easily have taken it, and who is, instead, standing here giving it away, and the giving is the proof, and the proof is the deterrent, and the deterrent is the peace. It has to be me. It could only ever have been me. You do not hand the world's off-switch to a friend. You hand it to the enemy who has just, in front of you, chosen not to use it.''\par

It was not hers to decide alone, and she knew it, and she waited, the enemy of the Mountain, inside the Mountain, for the Mountain to say whether it would let itself be given away. I want it set down that the deciding was hard, and slow, and that it divided the offices the way every true thing divided them, because this was the last matter the machine still had a voice on, and it used the voice. Shield wanted the old way; Ka-Dhavar had kept the secret his whole life and every bone in him cried out against scattering it to the winds, and he said so, that a secret shared with the whole world is a secret lost, that we were proposing to do to ourselves the very thing the enemy had died trying to do to us. And Balance counted, and could no longer make the numbers of the old way come out safe, not with a world above that had found us once and would rebuild and find us again. And Justice, Ka-Orun, who had learned in the high dark exactly what it costs to inherit a lie, ruled last, and ruled for the scattering, and ruled for the unreset clock, and pressed it into the wet clay: that the sixth cycle chose, knowing, in full possession of the record its fathers had hidden, to end the erasure and let the truth go out. It was not my choice. I only opened the door. The Mountain walked through it, arguing, grieving, in disagreement to the very last, the way it was built to; and that is the only reason the walking counts. A thing one man decides is a thing one man can undo. This the five decided together, against their every instinct. It will hold the better for having been hated by the people who chose it.\par

\ornament

So we made the thing you are holding.\par

Paul assembled it, because that was his office to the last, the anti-erasure, the man who makes certain the true thing is written down: my whole account, the tapes of a heretic talking in a house by a lake; Zero's confession out of the bottom of the Mountain; Sarah's own, given plainly, sparing herself nothing; the proof of what came for us and what we did to answer it; the year-clock and the six seams and the drowned workers and the lied-to children and the drill that is the Mountain's heartbeat. All of it, in order, entire.\par

And it begins, this record, where my life as its narrator began: in a room by a lake, with a fact-checker asking a man he had been paid to disbelieve to describe a morning. You will have read that already; it is how you came to be here; it was the first thing Paul set down, and the first thing you were given. I did not know, sitting in that house, letting a stranger hand me the length of a pendulum and the breadth of a desert, that I was speaking the opening of my own people's confession to the world. Neither did he. We thought it was an interview. It was the first page of the Declaration. Zero closed his record at the bottom of his Mountain believing that no one would ever read it; I am closing this one at the top of mine, having made certain that everyone will; and that single turn, from a secret sealed to a secret scattered, is the whole distance my people travelled in a hundred and thirty-five thousand years, and it was travelled in one season, by a handful of tired people, in the dark.\par

But Paul did not decide what would be done with it. That is the one thing I most want understood, because a lesser telling would make the fact-checker the hero, the outside conscience who judged the Mountain and set it right, and it was not so. He gathered the whole. He did not rule on it. The ruling was ours, the Mountain's, mine, because it was our secret and our crime and our door; the outside does not get to absolve us, and does not get to condemn us; it only gets, now, to know. Paul made the record. The Mountain decided what the record was for. And what we decided it was for was this.\par

It is a declaration, and it is not a treaty, and it is not a friendship, and it is not an apology, and anyone who reads it as any of those has not read it. It says: we exist. It says: we have existed for a hundred and thirty-five thousand years, and we have ended your world five times before this, and we have now ended it a sixth, by law, because you came to take the whole, and the whole cannot be allowed into a single hand, not yours and not ours. It says: the account of us is now everywhere, in hands that hate each other, so that no one of you can take us in secret, and no one of you can hold us alone, and the trying will only teach the others where we are. It says: we do not want your friendship and we do not offer ours; we want the one thing we have always wanted, the one thing the whole terrible machine was built to buy, which is distance. Leave us in our desert. Do not come with your drills. There is nothing under this Mountain for you but the end of your world, and you have five graves' worth of proof of it now, distributed, permanent, unforgettable. Keep away, and live. That is the whole of the offer. It is signed, because no one hand may hold the whole, by no one hand.\par

It is signed \emph{The Mountain}.\par

\ornament

I did not become him. That is the victory, and it is a small and quiet one, and it does not look like a victory, and I have made my peace with that, because I have learned what the loud victories cost.\par

I lost Va-Sheva. She stood beside Jean-Charles at the last, without theatre, and I let her, and I did not turn the losing into a doctrine, and I did not build a Mountain to house it, and I did not require her choice to be a fact about anyone but her. I let it be the size it was. Ithe asked that of a man a hundred thousand years ago and he could not do it and you have all read what he made instead. I could do it. It is the only thing in this whole account that I did that Zero could not, and it is the smallest thing in it, and it is the reason there will not be a seventh cycle wound back into the dark, because a people that can let a thing be the size it is does not need to forget in order to go on.\par

And I let the whole leave my hand. I had been made the eye that sees the whole, the one mind that held every office, the exact thing the Mountain feared, and I had fired the Beast with it, and I could have kept it, could have made myself the new Zero, the one who understands, the necessary hand; the offices were tired and grieving and would have let me; Ka-Raedin had all but handed me Light already. And I gave it away instead, to Sarah, to the world, to everyone, and kept for myself no more of it than any other Veil, and that giving-away, that refusal to be the one who holds, is the only thing I have ever done that I am certain was right. You keep the whole safe by refusing to hold it. Zero could have written that sentence. He did write it, nearly, at the bottom, in the dark, in the one door he left. The difference between us is only that I meant it, and he wished he had.\par

I have been asked, by the few who know to ask, whether it was hard to give it away, the whole, the eye, the thing I had bled to earn and that the offices would have let me keep. It was not hard. That is the part I cannot make anyone believe who has never held a whole and felt what it does to the hand that holds it. It was the easiest breath I have ever drawn. A whole is not a possession; it is a weight; it is a thing that stands behind you in every mirror for the rest of your life; and to set it down, to divide it, to make it everyone's and no one's, was to be allowed, after a season of carrying the end of the world in my two hands, to have my hands back. Zero carried his for a hundred and thirty-five thousand years and called the carrying his immortality. I carried mine for ninety days and could not wait to be rid of it. That is the difference between a man who is left and cannot bear it, and a man who is left and, in the end, can. I bore it. I set it down. My hands are empty. It is the richest I have ever been.\par

\ornament

We did not reset the clock.\par

That was the last decision, and it was the whole of everything, and it was made without much speech, because by then there was not much left to say. On the ninetieth day Shield cracked the high vent and the world above was cold and grey and alive, barely, changed, beginning again, and the ninety days were done, and the offices could have gone down into the Archive and wound the great wheel back to nothing and begun the hundred-thousand-year lie again, clean, and taught the children born into the new dark that the world was young and the Mountain was God's and there had never been a Beast. It is what had always been done. It is what the machine wanted. It is the easiest thing in the world.\par

We left the clock alone. We let it keep its count. Six seams, and the long true climb of the years, and no new erasure, so that the children of this cycle and every cycle after it, if the desert lasts, will grow up knowing exactly how old their Mountain is, and how many times it has ended the world, and that it was built by a man and not given by a god, and that the man had a name, and a wound, and a door he left for them. They will carry the truth instead of the terror. I do not know if it will hold. A terror is a stronger mortar than a truth; Zero knew that; it is why he chose it. Perhaps a people cannot keep a machine like ours for a hundred thousand years on truth alone, and perhaps some Keeper after me will decide, in some hard year, that the children are safer frightened, and wind the wheel back, and begin the lie again, and I will not be there to stop him. But he will have to choose it. He will have to do it on purpose, knowing. We have made it, after a hundred and thirty-five thousand years, a thing that must be chosen and cannot merely be inherited. That is all we could do. It is more than any generation before us did. It is the door, held open one more day.\par

And the clock steps. I have stood beside it and watched it, the great notched wheel in the deep, the equinox beam gone from its face and the borrowed sun withdrawn, and I have felt the year turn under the desert, and I have watched the wheel take its single tooth, once, the way it has taken it a hundred thousand times and will take it a hundred thousand more, patient, indifferent, counting; and this time, over a people who know at last what it is counting, and have chosen to let it count on, unreset, into a future that will remember.\par

That is the end of it. What you are holding is one of the copies. You are one of the hands we could not trust and gave it to anyway, because there was no longer any such thing as a hand we could trust, and the giving is the only safety left. Now you know. Keep your distance. Live.\par
