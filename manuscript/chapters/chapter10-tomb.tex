\typesetchapter{Chapter 10}{Tomb}

\noindent \textit{Light sees what can be shown.}\par
\noindent \textit{Shield sees what must be stopped before it shows itself.}\par
\noindent \textit{--- Shield doctrine}\par

\ornament

\noindent Everything I did in the Tomb, I did with my hands. That is the thing to hold on to. The question put to me four nights running is when the crime began --- the crime, not the trial --- and the answer is not a date but a single night: the night I stopped being a man who only looked. What the Mountain drills into a child of Light I have already set down --- that the eye is innocent and the hand is not; that a man may gaze on anything so long as he leaves it where it lies, and that the crime is never in the seeing but in the carrying. I gazed a long time in that chamber. Then I carried something out.\par

\ornament

For several days after the martyrdom, I waited for the Mountain to feel different.\par

It did not.\par

Water still came by measure.
Bread still cooled on the same racks.
Children still recited badly in the teaching halls and were corrected by voices patient enough to become cruel.
Lamps still smoked when the vents were slow.
Shield still closed passages without explaining itself.
Balance still marked loss and use with the same green dye.
Faith still spoke over jars and wounds and dead bees and frightened mothers and old men who had begun to forget the names of their own children.
Justice still sat where people wished no one were sitting.\par

The Mountain had changed my name, recorded my sequence, bound white cloth to my wrist, and placed me beneath Ka-Raedin in the chain of Light.
Then it had gone on living.\par

That too was instruction.\par

A boy might have resented it. The old Sa-Kailan would have. I could feel that old hunger sometimes, not gone, only shamed into a quieter shape. It rose when a junior attendant forgot the prefix and corrected himself too quickly. It rose when younger Candidates stopped speaking as I entered and looked at me with the startled obedience given to office rather than friendship. It rose when Ka-Raedin dismissed me from a chamber before I had asked the question burning behind my teeth.\par

But Ka-Syphiron's blood had entered me deeper than pride could reach.\par

Who would sprint toward that? The next chamber. The next rule. The secret Ka-Raedin had crossed into with a clue I had given him without knowing it. A few months earlier I would have burned myself trying to follow. I would have watched corridors, misread reflections, risked humiliation for one stolen angle of my teacher's path, and called it courage.\par

Now, when the impulse stirred, I saw Ka-Syphiron opening his robe below the ribs. I saw the blade drawn from the lamp. I saw an old man's body become the clean price of a completed link.\par

No.\par

A Veil who rushed toward Keeper had not understood the last Keeper's blood.\par

So I did not rush. I learned the Veil's Chamber the way a man learns a room in which someone has died, though no body had ever lain there.\par

Permission had changed the room. A forbidden place has glamour; a workplace has only demands. I cleaned channels, copied markings under supervision, maintained the dry basins, checked the hidden apertures for dust, and stood in silence before the wave diagrams that had made Ka-Raedin Keeper. Lines spreading. Lines crossing. Lines broken around obstacles and returning where direct sight failed.\par

The hidden side was not empty.\par

On the sixth morning after the ceremony, while I was cleaning the rim of the small black basin, Ka-Raedin said,\par

``You have not asked me how I found it.''\par

I kept the cloth moving until the circle was clean.\par

``No, Keeper.''\par

``Why?''\par

``Because you would not answer.''\par

``That has not always stopped you.''\par

``No.''\par

He waited.\par

I set the cloth aside --- not hurriedly, not because I wished to perform maturity, but because objects deserve to be put down properly before dangerous sentences are lifted.\par

``Before the ceremony, I would have thought every answer withheld from me was a door shut against my worth. Now I think some doors are shut because the person outside them has not yet learned what opening them costs.''\par

Ka-Raedin looked at me long enough that the silence became part of the answer.\par

``Ka-Syphiron did not die to make you timid.''\par

``No, Keeper.''\par

``Then what did he die to make you?''\par

``Sequenced.''\par

Ka-Raedin crossed to the wave wall and touched one of the old carvings with the back of his finger. Not the secret. Never the secret. Only the edge of the mark that held it.\par

``You will be tempted,'' he said, ``to confuse patience with obedience.''\par

``I already am.''\par

``And?''\par

``They are not the same. Obedience waits because it is told to wait. Patience waits because the thing would be damaged by being taken too soon.''\par

He turned back to me.\par

``That sentence is too good for you.''\par

``Then I must have heard it somewhere.''\par

``No. That is what worries me.''\par

It was the closest thing to amusement he ever offered, and I received it without smiling. There had been a time when I would have stored such a line like stolen honey. Now I let it pass through me and leave its instruction.\par

``I will not chase your chamber,'' I said.\par

``My chamber?''\par

``The Keeper's Chamber. The path you found.''\par

``Why?''\par

``Because if I reach it before I understand why I should not desire it, I will arrive as the wrong man.''\par

His face stilled. Ka-Raedin had blood on that knowledge. I had seen it spill.\par

``Good,'' he said --- without sting this time, and without indulgence either. It was recognition, narrowly measured.\par

Then, because he was Ka-Raedin, he added, ``Now clean the basin again. You left oil near the lower groove.''\par

I looked down. I had.\par

I cleaned it again.\par

I did not become less curious. That would have been a simpler transformation and therefore less true. The title did not extinguish the hunger; it only refined its disguises. I no longer asked immediately. I no longer pressed at Faith's doors or followed Ka-Raedin through reflections like a child trying to catch an adult's shadow.\par

Instead, I watched.\par

And waiting changed the Mountain.\par

I began to see Light at the edges of Light. Small leaks. Accidental glints. Exterior light entering in wrong seasons through cracks too high to matter. Internal lamp-lines escaping through vents and returning from stone beyond the official paths.\par

At first I dismissed them as maintenance defects --- a tarnished plate, a poorly seated lamp cover, a wet patch catching more than it should. Yet certain wrongnesses repeated. A faint white flash beyond a high ventilation slot in the north-east service descent, visible only when the night lamps were being trimmed. A reflected thread along the upper edge of a sealed Shield route, present on three nights and absent on the fourth. A pale return from far outside the Mountain when certain internal lamps burned late and the air beyond the Oasis was dry.\par

I did not leap at the first glint. I recorded nothing. I spoke to no one. But I placed the observations inside myself in order. The old Sa-Kailan would have gone after the first brightness with a stolen lamp and a heroic justification. I watched for twelve nights.\par

I learned that the flash did not appear when high dust crossed the moon. I learned that it did not depend on the ordinary lamp rotations of the eastern halls, but on a deeper glow from a maintenance shaft used after evening work. I learned that it appeared only from one position in the old vent gallery, and only if I lowered my head enough to see along the line where air entered before it became Mountain air.\par

Then memory supplied the missing shape.\par

Not as Veil. As Sa-Kailan, Candidate of Light, with dust in my sandals and Va-Sheva beside me saying softly: \textit{Do not point.}\par

The flash. The turned marker. The old woman out of place. Va-Sheva closing possibilities before danger had a name.\par

A flash may be nothing. A turned marker may be nothing. An old woman out of place may be nothing. Three nothings close together become a shape.\par

I had believed then that Shield had taught me about danger. It had. But it had also taught me something else: Shield built the world so that small wrongnesses could be read before they became deaths. It placed markers, watchers, angles, hidden bodies, count lines, and silent signals. It made a landscape into a warning instrument.\par

Light sees what can be shown.\par

Shield sees what must be stopped before it shows itself.\par

The sentence, once a rebuke, became an invitation I did not trust.\par

For several more nights I did nothing but make myself useful. I supervised younger Candidates in the early practice halls. I did not teach them as Va-Raedin had taught me; I lacked the age for cruelty to become elegant. When one boy rushed a false door and struck his shoulder, I did not embarrass him before the others. I waited until the exercise ended and said, privately,\par

``The room did not defeat you. Your first answer did.''\par

The boy looked at me with a resentment so familiar that I almost smiled.\par

Former peers avoided my gaze. Older Light attendants tested my authority with small omissions --- a report handed late, a lamp plate left unpolished, a route question phrased too casually. I answered with neither anger nor softness. If correction was needed, I gave it exactly. If silence served better, I used it. I was beginning to understand how Va-Raedin had carried so much power without raising his voice. Raised voices spend authority quickly.\par

But in the unoccupied spaces of night, I returned to the glint.\par

I chose the route only after I could close my eyes and walk it in memory. From the eastern lower spine to the old vent gallery. Down the maintenance stair past the disused oil shelves. Through the narrow inspection slit that overlooked the sealed north passage. Pause for the third Shield change. Across the dark water service, where my robe would take damp if I brushed the left wall. Then the old air chamber above the cedar descent, where outside breath moved through stone before becoming part of the Mountain.\par

I did not sneak like a boy. I moved like a man learning the shape of permissions without asking them to announce themselves.\par

That did not make the act innocent.\par

On the night I chose, the Mountain was quiet after a minor mourning rite. Faith had held the lower Hall late for a child who had died of fever before naming. Such deaths were not rare enough to shock the Mountain, but they always altered its sound. The night lamps burned low. The air was dry. No dust cloud veiled the moon beyond the hidden openings. The glint appeared twice before second-night silence, faint and precise.\par

I waited until it appeared a third time.\par

Then I went.\par

I carried no blade. A blade would not make me Shield; it would only make me a frightened Veil pretending the desert could be negotiated with metal.\par

The passage outward was not one I had used before. I had watched two maintenance workers remove its inner grille once, months earlier, under Shield supervision, and they had replaced it badly or deliberately. Either way it yielded to pressure applied not at the visible latch but where the lower stone had been shaved thinner.\par

Beyond it, the Mountain became less human.\par

Not natural, exactly. Shield had touched it. Someone had cut handholds into the stone and worn them smooth with use. Someone had set dark pegs where the descent turned sharply. Someone had carved a line that could be felt by fingers but not seen in low light. Yet the passage lacked Light's interpretive softness and Balance's measured signs. It offered no explanation. It existed because some bodies had to move where other bodies were not allowed to imagine movement.\par

Cold air came through cracks ahead.\par

I paused before the final opening. The subtler fear was worse than any curse out of the Book of Blood: does wisdom become appetite when it justifies crossing a boundary? I had an answer ready --- \textit{Light must follow what is revealed} --- and I distrusted it, because it sounded noble enough to hide hunger.\par

Still, I opened the stone and stepped out.\par

The night desert did not resemble the desert I had seen by day. At night it was larger, because it gave less. The Mountain rose behind me as blackness with weight. The sky above was crowded with stars I had no training to name. I wrapped the mouth cloth around my face, not against sand but against the instinct to breathe too quickly. The air tasted of stone, salt, and distance.\par

I kept low in the Mountain's shadow until the ground fell away toward the outer folds. The Oasis lay out of sight below another ridge, hidden even from me. That seemed right. Sacred things did not become visible merely because a man had violated a route.\par

The first part of the journey was calculation. Once I found a line of small stones that might have been natural, except their flatter faces all turned the same way. I did not cross it. I circled wider, losing time and confidence.\par

I was not Shield, and the desert made that plain quickly. My sandals, made for stone and formal passages, slipped on loose gravel. The cold stiffened my fingers. The water skin knocked softly against my side until I tied it higher beneath my robe. I misjudged distance again and again. A rock that seemed close took a hundred breaths to reach. I had memorised the glint's direction from within the Mountain, but direction under open sky was a different thing. Nothing stayed framed. Without walls the eye has too much freedom, and freedom makes it stupid.\par

I stopped at the first low ridge and looked back.\par

The Mountain did not look like refuge from there. It looked like a sleeping beast, vast and black, with a few hidden breaths of light where the vents opened under shadow. I understood then, in the body, why the Oasis could remain unseen. The land folded around it. The Mountain's shoulder cut the sightlines. The desert did not need to conceal the hidden people actively; scale did most of the work. A man could die within sight of salvation if he did not know the angle from which to see it.\par

The thought gave me strength and fear together.\par

\ornament

``Stop there a moment,'' I say. ``You had never been outside the Mountain at night.''\par

``Once, as a child, before I was taken in. I do not trust the memory.''\par

``What did you make of the sky?''\par

``That it was crowded, and that I had no names for any of it.'' He looks at me across the reels. ``You have names for them.''\par

``Some.'' It is no secret of my century, the names of stars, and I hand it over without weighing it. I tell him the lights are other suns, unthinkably far; that men have drawn shapes between them and hung old stories on the shapes; that one steady light stands near the north and has brought travellers home for longer than his Mountain has kept records. He listens the way he listens to everything --- as though he is setting it on a shelf against a later need.\par

``A fixed light in the north,'' he says, and something in him goes quiet and careful.\par

That quiet is data, whatever else it is. I decline, for now, to make it mean more than that --- though it is a peculiar place for an inventor to fall still, the one fixed point every navigator in my world has ever steered home by, and men who fabricate a people do not, as a rule, salt the fabrication with a true north. I set the observation beside the others I have been setting aside all week and move us on, because I want the next of the account more than I want to sit with the size of the last of it. ``You were keeping to the Mountain's shadow,'' I say. ``Go on.''\par

\ornament


The glint appeared again while I was crossing the second wash --- once, from far ahead and slightly higher than I expected. Not moonlight on random stone. Too clean. Too brief. A white point, then gone. I froze, crouched, and waited. Nothing. I shifted my head by a finger's width. Nothing. Not a beacon. A refusal that could be made to confess.\par

I almost smiled. Then I remembered Va-Sheva saying: \textit{Do not start believing the outside speaks to you.}\par

The final approach took longer than I expected. The ground rose in broken shelves, each one stealing the next from sight. Twice I nearly turned back --- not from fear of Shield but from the dawning recognition that the return was also a route to be preserved. A man can be brave going outward and die because he spent all his intelligence in one direction. I marked three stones with dust rubs invisible from a distance but plain to my trained eye. I counted steps between turns. I drank once, sparingly. The water tasted of leather and guilt.\par

The outcrop, when I reached it, looked natural at first. That was its first art.\par

The glint did not show from below. I moved around the stone slowly, searching not for brightness but for wrongness. A surface too smooth. A crack too straight. A place where the dust failed to gather. Shield would not build a door that looked like a door. Shield would build a place that did not invite the concept of door.\par

I found the polished mineral only after kneeling.\par

It sat in a shallow hollow no wider than my hand, hidden behind a lip of stone. Not a mirror in the Light sense --- a natural crystal face or mineral plate, polished by human effort and angled toward the Mountain. In daylight it would vanish among other pale scratches. At night, under the right internal lamp or moon angle, it caught and returned a single point: a signal, a check, or a warning that could be read only by one placed to read it.\par

Beside it, almost worn away, was a mark cut into the stone. Not Light's aperture, not Balance's cup, not Faith's cord, not Justice's black line.\par

A threshold line, broken twice, with a small inward notch.\par

Shield.\par

My mouth went dry.\par

The outcrop was Shield. Not simply watched by Shield --- built into Shield's exterior architecture. A point beyond the permitted prayer ground, aligned with Mountain light, invisible to ordinary eyes, reachable only by a dangerous route.\par

I felt no triumph. Only the cold rearrangement of categories.\par

Light had led me not upward toward Keeper knowledge, but sideways, into another Kahir's secret.\par

I should leave. I stayed still and let the thought finish itself.\par

I should return, report to Ka-Raedin that a Shield mark and an exterior glint had been found by Light observation, and then allow Keeper-to-Keeper sequence. That was the proper structure. It was also a way to ensure Shield sealed the site before I knew what I had seen. The thought was not paranoia. It was procedure. Shield defined danger before others were allowed to understand it.\par

A second thought followed: if I entered, I might commit breach. A third: if I did not, Light would surrender what had been revealed.\par

I waited until the three thoughts stopped competing and became a single condition. Whatever I did next would have a cost.\par

I chose the cost of knowing.\par

The mechanism resisted silently, then gave with a thickness that spoke of long maintenance, not abandonment. Air breathed out from within. Dry. Old. Mineral.\par

Not empty.\par

I slipped inside and pulled the stone nearly closed behind me, leaving a finger's width for air and for return.\par

The passage sloped downward. It was narrow at first, forcing me sideways, then widened into a cut corridor whose walls bore neither Light markings nor Faith text. Shield did not decorate itself with explanation. The floor had been worn by many feet, but not recently by many. Dust lay thinly enough to show disturbance and thickly enough to preserve it. I held my lamp low and moved slowly.\par

After twenty paces the corridor divided. One branch descended deeper into darkness. The other angled inward toward the Mountain.\par

I chose neither immediately.\par

No entry mark. No return mark. No prayer, no measure, no instruction. Only stone and the smell of preserved air.\par

\textit{Quarantine,} I thought.\par

Then another word came, less precise and more terrible.\par

\textit{Tomb.}\par

I took the descending branch.\par

It ended in a chamber whose first impression was emptiness. Then my lamp found the nearest shelf.\par

Objects.\par

At first only a few: stones, broken blades, small bowls, fragments of painted surface, a corroded shape that might once have been a tool or an idol. Then the flame widened and more shelves appeared, rising along the walls, tier after tier, receding into darkness. A second chamber opened beyond the first, then a third, each cut lower into the rock and lined with recesses. It was not a tomb in the ordinary sense. No bodies lay there. No bones arranged for burial. Yet death filled it --- not as corpse-smell, but as conclusion.\par

Civilisations had entered here as fragments.\par

They had not left.\par

I lifted the lamp to the nearest shelf. Every object was tagged. Not with explanations any child could read, but with containment marks. Shield sign at the top. Justice witness line beneath. Balance preservation notch. Sometimes a Faith refusal mark. Sometimes a Light observation point. Each tag stated not only what the object was believed to be, but how it had entered, who had handled it, whether it could be touched, whether it could decay, whether it carried doctrinal danger, whether viewing required witness.\par

Archive, then. Quarantine and memory together.\par

The first object I truly saw was a small bronze figure of a man holding a weapon shaped like three prongs. The body had gone green with age. The beard spread in formal curls. Around the base, faint wave marks had been scratched or cast. The Shield tag gave no name I knew. Faith had marked it: \textit{water-lord corruption.} Balance had added: \textit{metal stable; salts present; do not wash.}\par

A water god. Not the hidden waters beneath the Mountain, not the spring God had placed in shadow --- a man-shaped water power standing proud with a weapon in his hand. Outsiders had taken the memory of deep water and made it into a lord of appetite. A false Keeper of waters, perhaps. A corrupted Balance. Or something worse: the desire to command water without counting it.\par

I did not know the name Poseidon. I did not need the name to feel the accusation the chamber had been built to preserve.\par

Next came a dark figure with the head of an animal on the body of a man. Long snout, upright ears, narrow limbs, hands arranged over something that might have been a staff or a scale. Black surface, gold traces, eyes shaped with an unsettling calm. Beneath it lay a cloth fragment marked with faded symbols and a small carving of a wrapped human shape. The tag bore a Faith warning and a Justice witness mark older than the handwriting around it.\par

Death.\par

I knew that without being taught. Outsiders had given death posture, office, beauty. Faith taught that the dead returned under God's keeping, their names held in prayer and record. This figure looked as if death itself had been made a Keeper --- a corruption of Justice, perhaps, or Faith rotted into terror.\par

I looked away first, then forced myself to look back, because Veils do not look away from objects merely because they clarify fear.\par

The next chamber changed material. Painted stone. Carved panels. A fragment of image preserved behind cloudy glass. I held the lamp high and saw a man with a blade raised over a bound youth. The youth lay on something close to an altar. Above the man's hand was a sign of Heaven rendered as lines descending from cloud or fire. The father's face had been damaged, but the blade remained clear.\par

For several breaths I could not place the horror correctly.\par

Then the Book of Blood did it for me. Brother against brother. Father against son. Hunger made law. A broken piece of Gospel called whole.\par

I understood what I saw, and because I understood it through the Mountain, I misunderstood it with great force. Outsiders had carved a holy story in which a father bound his own son and prepared to kill him while the animal stood spared beside them. They had made obedience into a willingness to murder the child before the goat. They had taken sacrifice and inverted it. Not the completed link releasing for the next, as Ka-Syphiron had done with terrible dignity. This was father over son. Power over helplessness. The blade descending from authority into blood not yet earned by office.\par

The tag below it was more complicated. Shield danger. Faith contamination. Justice: \textit{retained for comparative doctrine.} Light: \textit{image damaged; blade line clear.}\par

Comparative doctrine. The phrase chilled me because it was so calm.\par

The next image was worse, because it carried tenderness around cruelty. A small metal cross. Then a larger one, of wood darkened by age. Then a painted fragment of people kneeling before a tortured man fixed upon crossed beams. His head drooped. His ribs showed. Wounds marked hands, feet, side. Around him the worshippers lifted their faces not in horror but in reverence. Some wept. Some reached toward him.\par

I stared.\par

The Mountain had martyrdom --- I could not deny that now. I had seen Ka-Syphiron die and heard Faith contain the meaning before panic could profane it. But this was different, or seemed different to me. Ka-Syphiron had cut himself when the link was complete. This man had been fixed, displayed, adored in his suffering. The Outsiders, I thought, had turned pain itself into an altar and then knelt to it. They did not only accept a necessary death. They made cruelty visible and called the visibility sacred.\par

I slowed myself. \textit{Veil, not boy. Interpret after seeing.}\par

I looked again. The worshippers' faces were grieving. The tortured man's face was not rage. Something else lived there, something the artifact tried to carry across time and failed to deliver, because I did not possess the language for it. Still, the image confirmed too much of what I had been taught to set it aside. Outsider holiness had passed through torture and come out adorned.\par

Beyond it, on a lower shelf, stood a figure of a woman unlike any gravure in the Mountain. Many arms spread from her body. Many breasts, or ornaments like breasts, covered her chest. Her face was serene and excessive at once. Around her were small animal shapes, fruits, weapons, and open hands.\par

Fertility, perhaps. Abundance. Or greed made divine. Too many hands. Too much giving and too much taking at once.\par

I thought of the Mountain's half measure, its injury allowance, its waste counted, its honey given in amounts so small that sweetness became an event --- and I looked back at the many-breasted figure and saw Outsider appetite made maternal, excess disguised as blessing.\par

The chamber did not make me rage. It made me careful. I moved through the older sections slowly, forcing each object into observation before doctrine could seize it.\par

A false water lord. A death keeper. A father's blade over a son. Worship before torture. Abundance without count.\par

Each artifact might have meant something else to the people who made it. I knew that in the abstract. But meaning does not travel freely across ignorance. It arrives damaged, and the damage itself becomes evidence.\par

The later sections grew stranger. Coins with faces in profile, some crowned with leaves, some with hard noses and a godlike arrogance. Metal helmets eaten thin by rust. A broken eagle standard. Fragments of cloth with crescent shapes and angular letters. Curved blades wrapped in oilskin. Pottery marked with blue patterns. Small glass bottles. Beads. A bronze hand. A prayer charm. A piece of parchment with a map drawn in brown ink, the Mountain not named but hinted by lines and old riverbeds.\par

Each shelf had its chain: object, source, risk, witness, preservation. \textit{Object recovered beyond Threshold. Object acquired by exchange. Object removed from a dead caravan. Object seized after breach attempt. Object received under exception. Object copied, original destroyed. Object retained by Shield only. Object forbidden to Faith novices. Object harmless unless taught.}\par

Harmless unless taught.\par

I stood a long while before that phrase.\par

A brass-and-glass instrument lay in a fitted case, its purpose unclear, though its lenses told me it belonged to sight or measurement. Beneath it, a Shield translation had copied one outsider word from the original label: \textit{French.} I did not know whether it named a people, a place, a house, or a dead authority. Nearby were coins marked under another translated name: \textit{Algerian.} Later, sharper coins, stamped with faces and signs whose meanings had been carried into the archive only as sound and category. Cloth badges. Shell casings. Surveying rods. A cracked compass whose needle trembled uselessly when I moved the lamp close.\par

Time became material. Stone became bronze, bronze became iron, iron became glass, glass became paper, paper became photograph.\par

The chamber widened at the modern section. Here the shelves became cabinets, some locked, some open. The air was drier, kept by hidden vents. The labels were newer and more dangerous --- Shield classification, Justice witness number, Balance preservation instruction, Light observation angle, Faith doctrinal restriction. The handwriting varied. The system did not.\par

That was where I felt the first true danger.\par

Old corrupted gods could be explained. Shield might keep such things because Shield guarded the threshold. Justice might witness them because evidence has to be held. Light might study them because error has a shape. I could accept that, uneasily.\par

\ornament

He has been walking me down into that chamber for the better part of an hour, and I have not once asked him to hurry.\par

It is not patience, whatever he takes it for. It is method --- the one habit of my trade I have never once had cause to distrust: let the account arrive whole before you rule on any part of it. A reading you lean on is a reading you have spoilt; you do not press the instrument to get the number you wanted. I did not cross an ocean and take a great deal of their money to carry home a bent one. So I keep my voice level and my questions small. I ask what the air smelled like. I ask how the shelves were cut. I ask him nothing that would tell him what I am listening for.\par

``You are patient tonight, Paul,'' he says.\par

``The account first.'' I let the reels turn. ``All of it. The order is yours.''\par

He almost smiles; he takes it for his Mountain's manners rubbing off on me. What he is doing is reciting an inventory, and an inventory is the hardest thing on earth to counterfeit, because it must hold its count wherever you open it. A hoax has a shape and defends the shape; this has no shape, only contents --- and the contents keep adding up. \textit{Ex nihilo nihil fit}: nothing is made out of nothing, and a boy in a cave does not conjure a coherent archive out of the air. This one, item by item, declines to be nothing.\par

``Then I will tell you what was on the shelves,'' he says.\par

I fold my hands on the table. Somewhere in the last hour I have begun to want the count to come out true, and I have not yet decided what a scientist is supposed to do about wanting.\par

\ornament

Then I saw the photographs.\par

The first showed a woman under hard light. Her hair was tied back. Her clothes were unlike anything in the Mountain --- practical, pale, shaped for heat, marked by pockets and seams whose purpose I could not guess. Beside her stood a man in desert clothing, his face lined, his posture loose in the way Shield posture became loose only after readiness had entered the bones. Behind them, partly visible, was a rock face I recognised with a sudden bodily certainty.\par

Not the outcrop where I stood. Another place, near the Mountain's outer folds.\par

The lower white border carried marks in an outsider hand. Beneath them, in careful Shield script, someone had copied two names.\par

Sarah Blackwood.\par

Amastan.\par

I stared at the woman.\par

Not idol. Not ancient corruption filtered through myth. Not a god with a trident or an animal's face. A woman. Recent enough that the colours had not fully died. Recent enough that the clothing, though strange, did not belong to dead ages. Recent enough that the Mountain's current elders might have been breathing when she was.\par

Sarah Blackwood. The name meant nothing, and therefore everything.\par

\ornament

``Sarah Blackwood,'' he says, and looks at me while he says it.\par

I have kept my face smooth all week. I have sat across from this man and let him hand me the length of a pendulum and the breadth of a desert and given back nothing but level attention, because a number is cold going in and asks nothing of you.\par

But a name is not a number. This one has a face I have stood in a room with; it has a father, and the father has a house, and the house is the one I am sitting in, with his reels turning on its table and its money three years deep in me.\par

I do not decide to react, and I react anyway. Whatever I would have called my composure does not come through it clean. It is not that I am caught at something --- I have nothing here to hide from him, and no craft worth the name to hide it with --- it is that the desert story I was paid to disbelieve has just, with no warning and no notion of what it is doing, read out the name at the head of my own contract.\par

``You know the name,'' he says. Not a question.\par

``No.'' It arrives ahead of any decision of mine, because the true answer --- \textit{yes, and I cannot tell you how} --- is a door I have no leave to open and, just now, no steadiness to open it through. ``Should I? Is she one of yours?''\par

``She is a woman in a photograph, in a Shield drawer, under desert light.'' He watches me. ``I thought you might know her, being of the outside.''\par

``The outside is very large,'' I say, flat, and true as far as it goes. ``There are a great many women in it, and I have met almost none of them.''\par

He lets me keep the answer. That is the part I was not braced for. He sets it aside where he keeps the pendulum and the flash and goes on --- trusting, of every man at this table, the one he should not --- and I sit in the house that owns the drawer he is describing and understand it at last, all the way down: I no longer believe he is lying. I came here to prove the man did not exist. He has handed me my own employer's name out of a mountain not one soul in my world can find, and meant nothing by it, and I have never in my life been so wholly convinced of anything.\par

The second photograph showed no face. Only desert, distance, and a terrible flower of light rising from the horizon, too large to be flame and too ordered to be storm. The sky around it had been bleached almost empty. On the lower border, in the same unfamiliar hand, were marks I could not read. One sequence had been copied beneath in Shield script:\par

Reggane, 1960.\par

I did not understand the word or the numbers. A place, perhaps. A date, perhaps. A wound recorded by Outsiders because even they had been frightened by what their own hands had made.\par

What that flower was, I did not know. Others would --- boys in countries of clean mountains who saw it carried in their newspapers, who had a word for it, and a number, and later a treaty. I had none of those things. I knelt in the dark of another people's archive and looked at the most important photograph any human being had ever failed to explain to me, and I thought it might be a place-name. Let no one who hears this imagine I understood what I carried out. I did not. I only knew it was too large to leave.\par

A third photograph lay partly hidden behind the second. It showed equipment on metal legs, men standing at a distance, a line of wire crossing pale ground, and the same awful emptiness of desert after violence. The Shield label did not attempt doctrine. It read only:\par

\textit{Outer fire. Witnessed beyond range. Do not expose to untrained Faith.}\par

Do not expose to untrained Faith.\par

Not false. Not demon. Not corruption. Information dangerous by category.\par

I set the photographs down as if they might accuse me of touching them.\par

The cabinet below contained maps. Several layers, some drawn in outsider lines, some annotated in Shield marks. Mountain folds. Dry washes. Old approach paths. Observation points. The Oasis hollow indicated not by name but by absence --- a blank space around which the routes bent. Marks corresponding to outer stations. Glint points. Hidden sightlines. And a route leading from the outcrop inward, then splitting toward the Labyrinth and, impossibly, toward service routes near the Hall of Ceremony.\par

My breath slowed. The disciplined body does that when fear becomes large enough. It refuses waste.\par

I unfolded another map. This one bore names in scripts I could not read, but three had been copied in Mountain hand.\par

Amastan. Sarah. Ka-Xhian.\par

Ka. A Keeper.\par

The prefix was unmistakable. Not outsider notation. Not a child's guess. Shield or Justice had recorded the contact with a title.\par

Lines connected the names to dates, routes, and small boxed terms. \textit{Breach. Containment. Exchange. Loss. Sealed. Limited disclosure. Threshold Exception.}\par

Exchange. Not attack. Not capture. Exchange.\par

I read the word three times, because the first reading could not be trusted.\par

Inside the next drawer were objects wrapped in cloth and tagged. A small metal cylinder. A folded paper covered in dense outsider writing. A tool with glass at one end and knobs along the side. A strip of black material that reflected my lamp with a dead sheen. A dried flower pressed between two sheets. Several pages had been cut out, copied, translated, and marked by Justice as incomplete.\par

At the back of the drawer lay a Shield record tablet.\par

I lifted it with both hands. The script was compressed, official, not meant for public reading. It did not tell a story. It preserved a sequence. That was more dangerous.\par

\textit{Contact maintained under Threshold Exception.}\par

\textit{Outsider asset contained beyond full breach.}\par

\textit{Amastan intermediary confirmed.}\par

\textit{Sarah Blackwood retained under observation.}\par

\textit{Ka-Xhian authorised limited disclosure.}\par

\textit{Horizon note sealed.}\par

Horizon.\par

The word meant nothing in any lesson I had been given. Yet it had the feel of a word designed not to mean anything until one had already lost his innocence. Horizon: the place where Heaven touched Earth but could never be reached. The line the desert taught the eye to desire. The tremble beyond the Oasis where the world looked unfinished.\par

\ornament

``Horizon,'' he says. ``The Shield tablet ended on it. \textit{Horizon note sealed.} A word I had never been given.''\par

I keep my hands folded. I have had that word three years. It is the name of the people who moved me across an ocean, drilled his language into me, and pay for the reels turning between us; it never once occurred to me to ask where else it might be written down. To have it read back to me now, off a record his own Shield kept in the dark of a mountain I cannot put on any map, is to be told that his side and mine have stood in the same room a great deal longer than anyone saw fit to tell me --- and that his side wrote it down, and mine did not think the writing-down was my affair. That is not a comfortable thing to learn about one's own house.\par

``What did you take it to mean?'' I ask. My voice has the decency to stay level.\par

``The line where Heaven touches the Earth and cannot be reached,'' he says. ``The place the desert teaches the eye to want. I thought it was another of their false names for a thing they had spoiled.''\par

``And now?''\par

He is quiet a moment. ``Now I think it was a name for people. And that Shield sealed it because people are more dangerous than gods.''\par

I could tell him what Horizon is. I gave him a version of it his first night in this house --- \textit{I am Horizon} --- before either of us had the wound for it to land. I do not give it again, and for once it is not only the instruction that stops me. I have just watched my own employer's name come up out of his account, and before I explain one thing to him I would like to know what my own side was doing inside his mountain long enough to be filed there in a dead hand. \textit{The account first,} I say. It is the nearest thing to a lie I have told him --- the account is exactly what they sent me to fetch --- and it is also, tonight, the plain truth: I want the rest of it more than I have ever wanted anything, and no longer for them.\par

``Go on,'' I say. ``You were kneeling in the dark with a Keeper's name in your hands. Tell me what you did with it.''\par

I set the tablet down carefully, because my hands had begun to shake --- not with rage, but with order trying to hold against consequence.\par

I arranged the facts.\par

First: the chamber existed. Second: Shield kept it hidden beyond permitted routes. Third: it did not end in old proof --- it continued into recent contact. Fourth: Shield had preserved records of Sarah Blackwood and Amastan. Fifth: a Keeper, Ka-Xhian, had authorised limited disclosure or contact under something called Threshold Exception. Sixth: maps showed routes, stations, and knowledge beyond what the hidden people were ever taught. Seventh: Justice had witnessed enough of this that the records bore sequence rather than mere rumour. Eighth: none of it had been spoken in Genesis, Blood, Beast, Cataclysm, Covenant, ceremony, lesson, or public record.\par

I forced myself to slow further. What did this prove?\par

Not treason, necessarily. It proved divided knowledge --- but divided knowledge was law. It proved Shield knew more than others --- but Shield's charge required hidden danger. It proved contact with Outsiders --- but perhaps under containment. It proved a Keeper had authorised it --- but Keepers were not gods. It proved an exception --- but exceptions might be necessary where ordinary law would break the very thing law served.\par

Then I looked again at the older artifacts.\par

They had prepared my mind, and I knew it. They were a lens. I tried to account for the lens, because a Veil must know the surface through which he sees. Yet even correcting for doctrine did not remove the unbearable central fact.\par

Shield had stood before the people as the threshold against Outsiders while keeping channels to Outsiders. Shield had taught the Mountain that the outside was curse while keeping maps through the curse. Shield had watched children kneel before Blood and Beast while holding photographs of the children of the murderer in secret drawers.\par

And Va-Sheva belonged to Shield.\par

This was not about Va-Sheva. It could not be allowed to become about Va-Sheva. She was Veil, not Keeper --- younger than the office around her, older than I had ever known how to reach. She might not know this chamber. She might know only its outer habits, its markers, its containment language. Or she might know everything, and have looked at me in the Oasis while standing inside a lie so large it had become her natural air.\par

I closed the drawer. The sound was soft. It seemed too final.\par

Beyond the modern cabinets, a narrow inner passage led back toward the Mountain. I followed it with the lamp held low. After thirty paces it reached a sealed door marked not with Shield alone, but with three signs.\par

Shield. Justice. Light.\par

Light.\par

I stood before it for a long time. The sign was older than Ka-Raedin's tenure, perhaps older than Ka-Syphiron's. Some earlier Light office had known of this route --- or witnessed its sealing, or been bound to silence by it. The Justice mark meant sequence had entered. The Shield mark meant threshold danger. The Light mark meant the thing behind the door had been seen, or must not be seen without someone trained to know how sight itself could be corrupted.\par

I did not open the door. I did not try.\par

That, more than anything, told me I had changed. The old Sa-Kailan would have pressed, searched, scratched, risked alarms, called restraint cowardice and curiosity duty. I stood before the sealed door and understood that the proof I already held was more dangerous than anything I could force before dawn.\par

Curiosity was not free.\par

I had spent enough for one night.\par

I returned to the modern cabinet, took nothing at first, then reconsidered. Accusation without evidence would be boyishness dressed in office. Evidence stolen without sequence would be breach. I needed something small enough to conceal, strong enough to anchor memory, and not so essential that its absence would immediately close the chamber around me.\par

The photograph was too obvious. The Shield tablet too dangerous. A map would be missed. A tagged object would change the archive's count.\par

I chose a loose copy fragment from the back of the map bundle --- a scrap bearing four entries in Mountain hand:\par

Sarah Blackwood. Amastan. Ka-Xhian. Threshold Exception.\par

There was no safe theft. Only measured theft.\par

I folded it once and placed it inside the inner seam of my robe. Then I stood in the Tomb --- or the Chamber of Thresholds, or whatever Shield named this place when it dared name it at all --- and let the full weight of it enter me.\par

I had found a truth. Not the whole; I knew that now. The whole is always withheld by design, sometimes wisely, sometimes monstrously. But this truth was solid enough to bruise the hand. Artifacts from dead civilisations. Corrupted sacred echoes. Maps. Photographs. Outsider names. Shield records. Justice witness marks. A Keeper's authorisation. An Exception.\par

The Mountain's official story had not been false in the simple way children imagine lies. That would have been easier to condemn. The old artifacts did look like corruption when seen through the Gospel. The Outsiders had remembered broken pieces. They had made gods of water, death, a father's blade, suffering, excess. Blood had not invented all its warnings from air.\par

The danger was real. That made the rest intolerable.\par

A false doctrine can be rejected. A true doctrine used to hide an exception becomes a chamber in which a man can lose the order of his own mind.\par

I lifted the lamp and looked once more at Sarah Blackwood's face. The Outsider woman stood under desert light, half squinting, unaware or uncaring that one day a Veil of Light would stare at her image in a chamber built to contain the evidence of her existence. She did not look like a demon. That did not comfort me. Demons in Faith's warnings rarely look like demons at first sight. Appetite does not always show its teeth before it enters the gate.\par

I lowered the lamp.\par

I would not rage. I would not run to Va-Sheva with accusations shaped by heat. I would not stumble before Ka-Raedin and spill evidence like a child demanding the Mountain rearrange itself around his outrage. I would think. Sequence mattered. Witness mattered. Words placed wrongly could turn truth into breach before truth had time to stand.\par

But I also knew this: I could not unknow the chamber.\par

The glint outside had not been nothing. The turned marker at the Oasis had not been isolated. Va-Sheva's refusal to answer had not been merely rank. Shield's silence had structure, and the Tomb had given that structure walls.\par

I left as carefully as I had entered.\par

The return through the passage felt longer. The sky had paled at its lowest edge --- not sunrise yet, but warning. I moved faster than I should have and slower than fear wanted. The marks I had made on the stones brought me back across the washes. Twice I stopped to restore disturbed dust with the side of my sandal. Nothing came. Or something came and chose not to show itself.\par

When I reached the hidden vent above the old air chamber, my hands were numb, my mouth dry, and the water skin nearly empty. The Mountain received me without welcome. Stone closed behind me. Warmth returned gradually --- first as smell, then as pressure, then as accusation.\par

I replaced the grille, checked the seams, brushed grit from the lower ledge, and stood in the darkness until my breathing no longer sounded like a confession.\par

Inside my robe, the scrap touched my skin.\par

Sarah Blackwood. Amastan. Ka-Xhian. Threshold Exception.\par

I did not take it out. I did not need to.\par

By the First hour I stood in the eastern chamber before Ka-Raedin arrived --- washed, robed, composed, no visible dust on my sandals and no visible sign that the world beneath the Mountain had opened sideways in the night. When the Keeper entered, his eyes moved once over my face, then down to my hands, then to the floor around my feet.\par

``You slept poorly,'' he said.\par

``Yes, Keeper.''\par

``Why?''\par

I looked at him. There had been a time when the question would have frightened me into cleverness. Now I let the silence hold until it became answer enough to choose from.\par

``Because the title is heavier in the dark,'' I said.\par

He studied me.\par

``Good.''\par

He turned toward the basins.\par

``Then we will begin with weight.''\par

I followed.\par

I had proof I did not yet know how to carry. I had found a real wound and had not yet earned the right to name it.\par

That is the true beginning of the thing worth the hearing --- not the trial, not the exile, but a folded scrap against a young man's ribs and a word he could not yet read. \textit{Horizon.} It stayed where the chamber had placed it: far, unreachable, trembling at the edge of sight.\par
