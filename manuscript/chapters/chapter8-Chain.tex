\typesetchapter{Chapter 8}{Chain}

\noindent \textit{The chain is complete only when the lower hand opens what the upper hand has hidden.}\par
\noindent \textit{--- on the raising of a Veil}\par

\ornament

\noindent You asked me once, Paul, what a reward looks like inside a place that distrusts pleasure. I can tell you. It looks like a small blackened room that smells of oil and dust and old paper and mistakes, and it fits four men and a lie about how much any of them deserved to be happy.\par

The room was low-ceilinged and warm, its walls darkened by years of lamp smoke, its shelves so crowded they seemed to survive by mutual pressure.
Lenses wrapped in cloth. Brass tubes, old diagrams, trays of powdered glass, a cracked bowl, lengths of wire, mirror shards, sealed jars, folded papers, two blackened spoons.
A small hand-lamp with its flame turned downward into water.
And an object that resembled the skeleton of a bird built by someone who had never seen a bird but had heard disturbing rumours of one.\par

Va-Raedin stood near the table.
Ka-Syphiron sat behind it --- not in a formal chair but on a low stool with one uneven leg, wearing no ceremonial outer robe, his sleeves rolled to the wrist.
A dark mark crossed one finger: soot, or ink, or blood long dried.
He looked less like one of the five Keepers of the Mountain than like a man interrupted in the middle of ruining something interesting.\par

``Close the door,'' he said.
I obeyed. The latch fell into place with a small sound.\par

For several breaths nobody spoke.
The silence did not resemble the silences of training, where Va-Raedin let an error ripen until the boy who had made it could smell his own stupidity.
This silence had witnesses inside it.
It waited not for an answer but for sequence.
Ka-Syphiron placed both hands flat on the table.\par


``Sa-Kailan of Light. Yesterday, by witness of Va-Raedin, you found the Veil's Chamber.''\par

``Yes, Keeper.''\par

``You entered by lesson, error, discipline, insight, and disobedience.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``You did not break the path behind you.''\par

``No.''\par

``A Candidate learns where light falls. And a Veil?''\par

``A Veil learns who placed it.''\par

``That is the public version, intelligible to all. A Veil learns who placed him.
And tonight you should see that Va-Raedin placed you there as surely as any lamp --- in every correction, every prohibition, every experiment he refused you, and every error he let you survive.
Tell me. By what condition does a Candidate of Light become Veil of Light?''\par

``By finding the Veil's Chamber.''\par

``And by what condition does a Veil of Light become Keeper of Light?''\par

I looked at Va-Raedin. No rescue came.\par

``By finding the Keeper's Chamber.''\par

``Incomplete.
A Veil who finds the Keeper's Chamber has reached the upper path. He has not completed the chain.
Light does not pass upward because one man climbs. It passes when the lower chamber is opened again by one he trained.
The Veil must find the Keeper's Chamber, and a Candidate beneath him must find the Veil's Chamber. Only then is the chain complete.
Faith, not long ago, had two Veils --- two who had found the Veil's Chamber --- but none had found the Chamber of the Last Prayer until recently.
When Va-Elun found it, she rose at once to Keeper, because there was already a Veil of Faith beneath her to complete the chain. You know her now as Ka-Elun.''\par

I understood slowly, and turned toward Va-Raedin.\par

``And did you find it?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``When?''\par

``Six years ago.''\par

``Six years?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``And you waited.''\par

``I served.''\par

``Waiting for me?''\par

``Waiting for the chain to complete.''\par

I looked down.
I had spent one day waiting and found it almost intolerable.
He had spent six years ready --- not speaking readiness aloud, not claiming what the Mountain had not yet permitted him to hold. Six years correcting boys. Six years waiting for a candidate worthy of becoming Veil.\par

You have asked me, Paul, why I trust anything that came out of that Mountain. Here is one honest answer.
I trust it because a man once stood ready for six years and did not tell me, so that I would not turn his patience into my ambition.\par

``Va-Raedin of Light found what he was required to find,'' Ka-Syphiron said.
``He did not become Keeper because the lower proof had not been renewed. He trained, withheld, corrected. He endured the indignity of being ready before the Mountain was ready to receive him.''\par

Va-Raedin said nothing.
The stillness on his face was not modesty. It was discipline so old it had become shape.\par

Then the old Keeper stood, without ceremony.
He came around the table, the uneven stool complaining behind him, and placed one hand on my shoulder. The hand was warm, dry, and surprisingly heavy.\par

``Congratulations, Sa-Kailan. You have done a difficult thing without making it unbearable to praise you. That combination is rarer than genius.''\par

I tried to answer. Nothing came except the start of a smile I could not govern.\par

He set his other hand on Va-Raedin's shoulder, and the three of us stood linked beside the table: the Keeper about to be released, the Veil who had waited, the Candidate who had opened the lower door.
Nothing in the Mountain's visible order had yet changed. No robe exchanged, no office named aloud, no Hall warmed, no scripture read.
Yet the chain had already formed in that cramped blackened room, among broken lenses and forgotten diagrams.\par

``Congratulations, Va-Raedin.''\par

``Keeper.''\par

``No. Not Keeper. Not tonight. Tonight we celebrate.''\par

For one absurd breath I thought my teacher might refuse the praise as an imprecision.
Instead he bowed his head.\par

``Thank you.''\par

The words were quiet enough that the room had to earn them.
Ka-Syphiron released us both and stepped back.\par

``There. The chain has now been congratulated in all its necessary links. If Justice complains tomorrow that joy was not recorded, we shall say the failure was theirs.''\par

I laughed once. It came out broken.\par

Va-Raedin looked at me. ``The Keeper has praised you. I will not add to it --- praise is oil, and it has drowned better boys than you. You did not fail. Take that instead.''
He had not softened it --- he did not know how to soften anything without deforming it --- but the warmth was unmistakable, because the refusal was the most he knew how to give.\par

``Enough,'' Ka-Syphiron announced, to no one.
``Absolutely enough. If Light spends every important moment proving it can stand still, then Shield has already won civilisation.''\par

The solemnity fled like a startled bird.\par

He crossed to a wall panel so blackened by smoke it seemed merely dirty until his fingers found three hidden catches.
It opened with the reluctant sound of secrecy asked to perform after years of disuse. Behind it was not a shelf but a hoard: two dark bottles stopped with wax, a tray of small white cups, parcels tied with string, flat boxes, dried fruit, a cloth of nuts, a round cheese, bread, strips of salted meat, and small cakes pressed with seeds.\par

He pointed a soot-marked finger at me. ``Clear the table.'' Then, to Va-Raedin: ``Ray. Those, there.''\par

\emph{Ray}, I repeated in my head.\par

``Keeper,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``Do not Keeper me. I am arranging a feast.''\par

``You cannot be serious.''\par

``I have never been more serious.''\par

``You are impossible.''\par

``An excellent quality in a Keeper approaching replacement.''\par

The spread grew, and it was absurd, and it was magnificent --- more food than I had ever seen set before ten people without a festival, a funeral, or an inventory mistake.
Ka-Syphiron uncorked the first bottle and set a white cup before me. The smell of hydromel rose warm and sweet into the room.
Va-Raedin's expression tightened. ``He is fifteen.''\par

``An age at which a man may discover that honey, water, time, and disobedience can produce civilisation.''\par

``He is fifteen,'' Va-Raedin said again --- looking now not at the bottle but at the tray of cups.
Ka-Syphiron followed his gaze. ``Ah.''\par

``Absolutely not.''\par

``Absolutely yes. These are for your first Light lesson as Veil.'' He said it with an illuminated gaze.\par

I looked from one man to the other. ``What is wrong with the cups?''
I could not help it. ``May I see?''\par

Both men paused to look at me.
Va-Raedin's face said no with the force of a sealed corridor. Ka-Syphiron's brightened with exhilaration and impatience. He lifted one of the small cups and set it before me.\par

It was not glass after all, but fine bone or something like it --- white, thick-sided, faintly blue at the foot, worn by many hands. At the bottom, beneath the glaze, a cloudy disc waited like a blind eye.\par

I took it before my courage could find a more careful course. It was heavier than it looked.
I tilted it toward the lamp, and the dull pale circle beneath the glaze stirred into a smear of colour, too faint to name, then vanished in the shine.\par

``A flaw,'' I said. ``Or a mark beneath the eye, perhaps.''\par

Ka-Syphiron looked at Va-Raedin. ``You trained this?''\par

``Under difficult conditions.''\par

``I see.''\par

I turned the cup again, more carefully. ``Thick wall. Curved base. The glaze distorts whatever has been set beneath it.''\par

``Better.'' I had learned to distrust that word.\par

Ka-Syphiron lifted the bottle. ``Look again.''\par

``I am looking.''\par

``And now?''\par

The hydromel poured. It climbed the white inner wall, trembled, and settled over the rounded base.
A figure appeared. I understood the optics a breath before I understood the subject --- and then I understood both.
The smear beneath the glaze gathered itself, through liquid and curvature, into a naked woman reclining at the bottom of the cup, one arm behind her head, the other arranged with a confidence no woman of the Mountain would ever have permitted to be drawn, seen, owned, hidden in a wall, or used as instructional material before a newly recognised Veil.\par

``Oh,'' I said. And then, uselessly, ``Oh.''\par

My face burned so fast I felt betrayed by my own blood. Ka-Syphiron struck the table with his palm. ``There.''\par

Va-Raedin actually laughed --- a rare sight. I stared at the cup, horrified that the room had broken open around me, and for a while I let myself be the target of a good joke. Then I pulled myself together.\par

``Inverse turning,'' I said. ``Instead of scrambling a source, we are rebuilding one.''\par

``Spectacular, is it not?''\par

``And old,'' Va-Raedin added.\par

``Older than several current moral prohibitions and at least two failed reforms of candidate discipline.'' Ka-Syphiron raised his cup. ``To Light, and all its mysteries.''\par

We drank.
The hydromel was sweet first, then hot, then sharper than I expected. It found my empty places too quickly. I tried not to cough and failed, which produced another round of laughter --- and this time even Va-Raedin did not retreat from it quickly enough.\par

Only then did the feast truly begin.
Ka-Syphiron broke the bread with both hands and gave the first piece to Va-Raedin. ``To six years.''\par

Va-Raedin closed his eyes. ``No.''\par

``To six years,'' I said --- and by the time I realised I had joined the Keeper against my teacher, it was too late.\par

Va-Raedin looked at me. ``You are learning dangerous habits early.''\par

``Good,'' said Ka-Syphiron. ``A Veil of Light must learn to disappoint his teacher without becoming useless.''\par

We ate. That alone felt improper --- not the food, but the abundance of it.
I took bread, then cheese, then one dried fig, because restraint still seemed, to some foolish part of me, like something I ought to display. Ka-Syphiron saw the gesture and put three more figs on my plate.\par

``This is madness,'' I said.\par

His face lit. ``Yes.''\par

``That is not a recognised category of feast,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``It should be. A truly great feast is one at which Balance has not been invited, Justice has not been informed, Shield has not inspected the door, Faith has not decided whether the songs are appropriate, and Light has hidden the cups.''\par

``Then it is not a feast. It is institutional collapse.''\par

``Only if discovered.''\par

The evening loosened.
Bread broke; cheese softened near the lamp; nuts wandered across the table; the hidden woman appeared and vanished in the cups until scandal became part of the room's impossible warmth.
Ka-Syphiron told a story about an attendant who had once tried to classify an obscene cup as a devotional vessel. Va-Raedin corrected him twice and was accused of vandalising joy. I laughed until my stomach hurt.\par

``To Va-Raedin,'' the old Keeper said suddenly, softening despite himself. ``Tonight you receive praise. To readiness, properly humiliated by time.''\par

Va-Raedin held his gaze, then lifted his cup. ``Yes.''\par

And we drank again.\par

Ka-Syphiron cut three uneven pieces of cheese with a knife far too ceremonial for the task. ``You understand what begins now?''\par

``The office?'' I asked.\par

``The partnership.''\par

``He will remain junior,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``Obviously. Partnership, not equality. The eastern halls, candidate sequences, dangerous apparatus, cross-Kahir requests, old prohibitions and new permissions --- all of it will begin to touch him.''\par

``You will still be corrected,'' he added to me.\par

``Relentlessly,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``There. You see the burden.''\par

I laughed again, and this time Va-Raedin did not immediately punish it with decorum.\par

\ornament

\noindent He has come to the night they made him a Veil in fact if not yet in name, and he tells it the way a man tells the one evening he was let all the way in. I am listening the way I am paid to listen: for the place a story like this should fall open --- how the Mountain moves a man upward, who is trusted with what when he arrives, and whether it holds when you press on it.\par

``This raising of yours,'' I say, when he stops for the reels. ``One man finds a room, another finds the room below him, and only then does the office pass. Did any of them hold the whole of it? The teacher, the old keeper --- did one hand ever hold every part?''\par

He answers without suspicion, because it is the plainest thing he knows. ``No. That is the first law of the place. No one hand holds the whole. Each keeps his portion and is weak without the others. It is built so that no man, however high he climbs, can ever hold it all.''\par

``And that seemed wise to you.''\par

``It seemed like the wall between us and every ruin the outside ever made of itself.'' He says \emph{the outside} the way a boy says the name of a country he has never seen. ``One hand holding everything --- that is how you make a god, or a catastrophe. Usually both.''\par

``Usually both,'' I say, and let the reels turn. I do not tell him the thing I have just caught, because it is mine to check and not his to answer: a lock built this carefully cannot help but betray its maker. For men to cut the whole into portions no single hand may hold, someone, once, must have held the whole in order to cut it --- must have known what was being divided. I came here to find the seam where a story like this falls open. This is not that seam. It is the opposite --- a place where it holds together better than a hoax has any right to.\par

\ornament


Ka-Syphiron opened the second bottle --- which Va-Raedin had not approved but had also not prevented --- and I found the courage to ask what had troubled me since the previous day.\par

``Keeper, I have a question.''\par

``Worse.''\par

``It concerns Faith.''\par

``Catastrophic. Continue.''\par

I looked at the cup, where the woman blurred beneath the hydromel.\par

``Va-Maera said she knew about what she called our little training rooms. She had never entered them, but she knew enough to press on them.
And there are other things. Their prayers for despair. Their archangels. Their demons. Their chambers. The way they speak of voices, silences, thresholds.
Va-Maera frightened me because she seemed to know where to press. As if despair had architecture. As if a prayer could be a tool. As if a voice could enter rooms where hands cannot.''\par

Ka-Syphiron set down the bottle and leaned back.\par

``Cross-Kahir knowledge is a dangerous appetite. A little is necessary. Too much, too quickly, convinces a young man that the Mountain is merely a puzzle discourteously divided before his arrival.''\par

``It is not?''\par

``No. It is a wound, a machine, a refuge, a law, a prison, a school, a weapon, and occasionally a badly ventilated experiment room with immoral cups.''\par

He pointed a strip of salted meat at Va-Raedin. ``Ray. Do you remember what Ka-Xhian showed you when you became Veil?''\par

Va-Raedin's expression altered. For a moment he looked younger. ``Yes.''\par

``What?''\par

``A wheel turning inside glass.''\par

``By?''\par

``Steam.''\par

I leaned forward. ``Steam?''\par

``A sealed vessel,'' Va-Raedin said. ``Water heated beneath it. A narrow tube. A wheel so light it looked useless until vapour touched it. I thought the lesson concerned motion.''\par

``It concerned pressure,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``And restraint. And then --- if I remember Ka-Xhian correctly --- dough.''\par

``Dough?''\par

``Bread. Yeast. Fermentation. Warmth. Sugars. Transformation by a living process too small to see.''
He looked delighted. ``He was insufferable about dough.''\par

``The worst kind of insufferable,'' Va-Raedin agreed.\par

``A Balance device?'' I asked.\par

``Yes,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``Most people take them for bean-counters,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``Ledgers, jars, grain, water, birth allotments, death registers, goat inventories.''\par

``They are that,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``Oh, entirely. Let us not rescue them from justice.'' Then his voice changed.
``But at their heart? Chemistry. Transformation. Patience applied to matter.
Balance knows what heat does to milk, what sealed darkness does to grain, what pressure does to vapour, what rot becomes before poison, and what poison becomes before medicine, dye, solvent, warning, or regrettable experiment.''\par

``They can help Light?''\par

``They already do,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

Ka-Syphiron lifted a sealed jar from the shelf. Inside was a powder so dark it seemed to drink the flame.\par

``Rare dyes. Glass additives. Oils. Powders that bite light at one colour and spit it back at another. Crystals grown slowly enough to become useful. Coatings thin enough to be almost absent. Vapours that reveal a beam because the air itself has been persuaded to misbehave.''\par

``Balance makes those?''\par

``Balance prepares the matter. Light studies what matter does when a condition changes. We arrange sight. They arrange substance. Neither office should confuse assistance with possession.''\par

``Many compounds useful to Light,'' Va-Raedin added, ``would kill an inattentive boy before teaching him anything.''\par

Ka-Syphiron smiled. ``You see? This is why we keep attentive boys.''\par

He lifted my cup before I could look away from it, and turned it slowly, so the woman at the bottom blurred, vanished, returned, blurred again beneath the hydromel.\par

``This particular object, for instance, uses an almost pure Faith principle. Not decomposition, but recombination --- though Faith would probably call it resurrection.''\par

``Generous tonight, Keeper,'' Va-Raedin said, disapproving.\par

Ka-Syphiron tapped the cup on the table. ``Faith humiliates the ear as we humiliate the eye.''
Another tap. ``Echo.''
Another. ``Delay.''
Another. ``Stone.''
The room returned each sound differently.\par

``Faith listens to stone the way we look through glass. A corridor lengthens a note. A vault divides it. A chamber stores part of it and returns it late. A rhythm becomes an instruction, if the wall has been shaped to understand it.
A prayer may be words addressed to God --- I do not settle that tonight. But it may also be a shaped sound moving through stone. A woman sings here; the Mountain answers elsewhere. Faith hears the alteration and reverses it, as you reversed the image under liquid.''\par

``So their prayers are mechanisms?''\par

``No.'' The answer was immediate. ``That is the foolish version of the clever answer. Do better.''\par

I thought. ``Their mechanisms pray.''\par

His eyes brightened. ``Closer.
Have you never noticed that five voices may sound louder than five voices should? Stand five singers in one place and the sound is pleasant. Move them two steps, and the room seems to join them.
The air does not add like jars, Sa-Kailan. Sometimes a system answers. Sometimes a chamber is waiting for exactly the rhythm that makes it confess its shape.''\par

``Resonance,'' Va-Raedin said quietly.\par

Ka-Syphiron looked at him. ``Look who is generous with knowledge now, Ray.''\par


He sat back suddenly. ``Speaking of the decomposition of sound and light --- aren't you supposed to give him something?''\par

Va-Raedin became still. ``It was not for tonight.''\par

``What a miserable phrase. No wonder you are nearly Keeper.''\par

Va-Raedin ignored him, reached into the inner fold of his robe, and drew out a small object wrapped in grey cloth. He held it a moment before offering it.
I took it carefully.\par

Inside was a triangular piece of clear glass, no longer than my thumb, polished on three faces and chipped at one corner.
When I turned it toward the lamp, the flame broke faintly along one edge into a thin wound of colour.\par

``What do you call this?'' I said.\par

``A prism.''\par

``What does it do?''\par

``The \emph{what} you will have worked out by morning. The unpierced mystery is the how, and the why.''\par

``For the ceremony?''\par

``No.'' His eyes held mine. ``For after.''\par

I closed my fingers around it.\par

``When a beam seems whole,'' Va-Raedin said, ``do not believe it too quickly.''\par

Ka-Syphiron looked away, but not fast enough to hide that the line pleased him.\par

``For teaching,'' Va-Raedin went on. ``If you remain teachable after having been praised.''\par

``That is not guaranteed,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``Pride has ruined boys more definitively than hydromel.''\par

He turned my wrist until the lamp struck the prism more narrowly.\par

``You are right to wonder about Faith. Right to be unsettled by Balance. You will be tempted by Justice. But do not become drunk on other people's locked doors. Light has enough of its own.
There are office mysteries: apparatus, ceremonies, petitions, requests you must refuse though you admire them, permissions you must grant though they frighten you. There is training the young without making them arrogant or tame.
And there are mysteries still unpierced. Like the memory of light. A question I have spent years failing to understand.''\par

``The memory of light?''\par

``A poor name for an unopened door. Something none of us has opened.''\par

``It may not be a door,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``Everything interesting denies being a door at first.'' Ka-Syphiron turned the lamp.
``You know how a song may seem one thing until Faith separates the voices inside it. Light may do the same. Some lights are a sum --- they look single because the eye is merciful, but break them correctly and many colours are travelling together.
Others are almost a single thing. A gas may filter out one colour from the world, and, struck correctly, give back just that one colour.''\par

``As if it remembers it?''\par

``As if it is permitted nothing else.''\par

The words stayed in the air longer than the lamp-glow did. Then he smiled.
``There. A useless gift. The best kind for a young man with too many useful hungers.''\par

\noindent He is telling me what the old keeper told him: steam shut in a vessel, grain that changes in the dark, a wheel too light to turn until vapour touches it. He tells it as wonder --- a secret shown to a boy for a reward. I let him wonder. It would cost me the account, and a week besides, to tell him that the whole world he was warned about is built on that wheel; that the water heated in a keeper's toy pulls ten thousand wagons and lights the house he is sitting in.\par

``You have such things,'' he says. It is not quite a question. He has begun to guess that the outside is not only cruelty --- that it makes as well as breaks.\par

``We have such things,'' I say. That much is cheap; a man may have the whole of it from any window, if he ever stands at one. ``Steam that pulls. Fire caught in a wire until it gives light. The wheel your keeper turned by hand, we turn by the ton, and it turns for us while we sleep.''\par

He goes quiet, refitting the Mountain's small marvels into something vaster, and I feel a thing I had not thought this work would put in me: that it is a hard kind of gift to show a man the whole size of the world through the one window someone else has chosen for him. I do not yet know whose hand chose his. I am uneasily aware that I could not say, for certain, who chose mine.\par

``And the listening,'' he says. ``The stone that answers a voice. Do you have that too?''\par

There it is --- my own field, the thing I was schooled in long before this house bought my time, held out across the table by a boy who does not know what he is offering. I take one breath longer than courtesy needs. ``The account first,'' I say, and I say it gently, because this once the wall is as much for me as for him.\par

He stood suddenly. ``But not all mysteries deserve solemn faces. A civilisation that never plays repeats its oldest fears --- only with better posture.''\par

``That sentence will not be used tomorrow,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``No. Tomorrow belongs to people with cleaner sleeves.''\par

He pointed toward the side curtain. ``There are office mysteries, unpierced mysteries, and fun mysteries --- which irresponsible men preserve until they become respectable or are confiscated by Shield.''\par

Va-Raedin's eyes narrowed. ``No.''\par

``Ray, your objections have been received and placed beneath the Mountain.'' He was already moving. He disappeared through the curtain.\par

The room quieted behind him. For several breaths the old gallery entered: lamp hiss, cooling stone, distant water striking a channel below.\par

I looked at Va-Raedin. Without Ka-Syphiron's brilliance filling the room, the Veil seemed more tired than before, but less distant.\par

``Six years,'' I said.\par

``Yes.''\par

``Why did you never tell me?''\par

``Because you would have made it part of your hunger. If I had told you, you would have tried to become the boy who completed my chain. That boy would not have found the chamber.''\par

``I thought you were withholding because you did not think me worthy.''\par

``I was withholding because worthiness is often damaged by being named too early.''
He paused. ``You did well.''\par

``I did not understand everything.''\par

``No.''\par

``I still do not.''\par

``Good. A Veil who thinks the chamber has been solved because it has been opened is unfit to stand near it.''\par

He finally ate the bread in his hand. ``You are no longer only my student. Not yet my equal. Do not look pleased.''\par

I smiled despite myself, and silence invited itself back into the room.\par

``You will not believe this,'' I said. ``Earlier today, I said \emph{acceptable} to a servant of Light.''\par

Va-Raedin became very still. ``With satisfaction?''\par

``Sadly, yes.''\par

``There. The chain closes onto itself. You are already becoming me.''\par

I made a sound of horror --- which was when Ka-Syphiron returned through the curtain, carrying enough contraband to make the room feel guilty before he had set it down.\par

Va-Raedin looked at the objects in his arms. ``I object to all of them.''\par

``Your objection has standing and may be ignored with respect.''\par

He set two objects on the table and kept a wooden box beneath it. The first was a small book bound in worn brown hide. The second was a black cylinder mounted on a wooden base.\par

He opened the book.
On the first page, a drawn woman stood in a long robe. On the second, one hand had moved to her collar. On the third, the collar had loosened. On the fourth, the robe had slipped from one shoulder. On the fifth, I looked up too quickly.\par

``Observe.''\par

He held the pages beneath his thumb and released them fast. The woman undressed --- not crudely, not fully enough to be called more than suggestion, but unmistakably, gracefully, scandalously, with the technical care of a serious Light study and none of the moral seriousness by which the Mountain survived itself.\par

``This is historically important,'' Va-Raedin said.
I looked at him. He lasted almost one breath before he failed, and Ka-Syphiron laughed.\par

``The first version was almost certainly not this subject.''\par

``Why is it kept?''\par

``For study,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``For morale,'' Ka-Syphiron said.\par

``For evidence,'' Va-Raedin corrected.\par

``For shame,'' I said.\par

Both men looked at me. Then Ka-Syphiron pointed. ``There. He is learning.''\par

He set a third object on the table: a small brass shutter, circular, slotted, blackened near one edge.\par

``Before the cylinder --- a necessary scandal in smaller form.''\par

Va-Raedin's expression changed. ``The Upfall Lamp.''\par

``What is the Upfall Lamp?'' I asked.\par

``A falling thread of water,'' Va-Raedin said. ``A lamp broken into pulses by a turning shutter. If the drops fall at one rhythm and the light strikes them at another, the eye accuses them of slowing. Stopping. Sometimes climbing.''\par

``Drops climb?''\par

``No.''\par

``But the eye sees them climb.''\par

``Sometimes.''\par

``That is impossible.''\par

Ka-Syphiron tapped the shutter. ``Ka-Dareth once carried a leaking goatskin into a disciplinary hearing and made water climb for six witnesses. Three demanded an inquiry. One began praying. One asked whether the basin could be returned.
Ka-Dareth said the Mountain had spent too many generations teaching children to fear error, and not enough teaching them to make errors perform useful labour.''\par

``And then?''\par

``Then he was forbidden to bring water to hearings.''\par

I looked from the shutter to the little book. ``So motion can be broken.''\par

``Observed in fragments,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``Misassembled by the eye. Stilled when moving. Moved when still. Sight is not a window, but a negotiation with interruption.''\par

He pushed the shutter aside and turned the black cylinder on its base. ``And this one is from five links above us. Look through the slits.''\par

At first I saw only interruptions: darkness, image, darkness, image. He turned the handle. The drum spun quicker, and the woman inside shattered into repeated glimpses, each broken by blackness.\par

Then the drum found its rhythm.\par

The woman moved.\par

Cloth loosened. A shoulder appeared. The garment slipped. Then the sequence reversed and she dressed again --- condemned by Light to undress and redress forever inside a spinning cylinder.\par

I stepped back. ``This is obscene.''\par

``Yes,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``And mechanically excellent.''\par

``The second quality is the official reason for its preservation,'' Va-Raedin added.\par

``The first,'' said Ka-Syphiron, ``is why nobody misplaced it.''\par

Va-Raedin looked at me. ``Is he disappointed it is not Va-Sheva?''\par

I opened my mouth, found no safe word in any Kahir, and closed it again.\par

``He looks as though he is going to be sick,'' Ka-Syphiron said, delighted.\par

``Men are all sick in that regard,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

The silence that followed was inescapable.\par

Ka-Syphiron lifted one finger. ``Speaking of archangels.''\par

Va-Raedin laughed once. ``Ha. Speaking of cleanly done.''\par

``Archangels?'' I said at once, grateful for any path away from Va-Sheva.\par

``When embarrassed men have run out of things to say, they come into this world to suspend time in an awkward silence.''\par

``And the Beast?''\par

The room stopped breathing.\par

No lamp dimmed. No cup moved. Yet something drew inward, as if everything on the shelves had remembered at once that the Mountain was not a toy.\par

I want you to notice, Paul, that I asked it as a joke. That is the part I have never been able to forgive in the boy I was --- that the largest question I would ever carry, I first set down like a card, to be clever.\par

Va-Raedin did not look at Ka-Syphiron. ``I only meant---''\par

``No,'' Ka-Syphiron said softly. ``You meant exactly what a boy should mean after too much hydromel and not enough terror. That is not a crime. Only a condition.''\par

He leaned forward. His face had not hardened. That was the frightening part.\par

``You ask whether I believe in the Beast. Remember, Sa-Kailan, I am one of five who keep it prisoner beneath the Mountain. Not only a shape in scripture, nor a story we tell the young. A thing that is held --- tonight, while we drink --- and that would not stay held one season if the five of us agreed to forget it.
On your path to Keepership --- if the Mountain does not kill you first --- you are bound to make at least one chilling discovery. One that leaves the room colder than it was before you entered. One that teaches you the difference between a mystery that invites the mind and a secret that burdens the hand. Everything you have laughed at tonight --- the divided offices, the walls, the portions no one hand may hold --- was built to keep it, and for no smaller reason. When your turn comes to carry your portion of that weight, you will speak of it like that no more.''\par

His gaze drifted, for a fraction too small to accuse, toward the stone beneath our feet. Not down, exactly. Through. Then it returned.\par

``No office talk tonight.''\par

``But---''\par

``No. Especially not if the question begins with \emph{but}. But is the hinge on which young men swing themselves into locked rooms.''\par

The Beast remained in the room after the joke ended. Ka-Syphiron let it remain exactly long enough. Then he clapped both hands once.\par

\noindent He has said the word. \emph{The Beast.} He set it down as a boy's joke and watched the room go cold around it, and now, thirty years and a life later, he sets it down again between us --- and watches me instead of the fire.\par

I should let it pass the way I have let the rest pass. That is the discipline they drilled into me before they let me near him: weigh the marvel no heavier than the small thing, believe nothing on a first hearing, let the account arrive whole before deciding what in it is true. Four days I have kept it without effort.\par

I do not manage it.\par

``What did he say it was,'' I say. ``The old keeper. Not what he told the boy to feel --- what he said it \emph{was}. One of five who keep it prisoner: prisoner where? Kept how? A thing that is fed, or a thing only held shut? When he looked through the floor --- did he mean a machine, or did he mean a door?''\par

The questions come out of me too fast, one on the heel of the last, and I hear them the way he must hear them --- not \emph{the account first}, but this first, this before anything, this set above the house and the rich rooms and every wall I have laid in his road for four nights. For four days I have wanted the whole account and let no part of it matter more than another. I have just let one matter more than all the rest, out loud, where he could hear it.\par

He does not answer at once. He looks at me the way he looked at the cup before the wine went into it: at a thing whose flaw has just stirred beneath the glaze.\par

``You want that one,'' he says slowly. ``Of everything I have set before you, you want that one.''\par

``I want the account whole,'' I say, and reach the wall again --- but I have reached it a breath too late, and the breath is on the tape, and we both hear it turning there.\par

``No,'' he says. Not angry. Cold. It is the first cold I have felt come off him, and it is not fear of me; it is worse than that for my purpose. It is a man who has begun to sort his host into a shape. ``Every night I have handed you the house, and Horizon, and the keeper behind the keeper, and every night you have said \emph{the account first}. I say one word about a thing kept in the dark under a mountain, and you forget your own wall.''\par

I let the reels turn. It is the only thing in the room still doing its work exactly right.\par

``Go on,'' I say.\par

And he does. That is the part I will have to send up the line and cannot yet make sound like anything but failure --- though it is not the failure they will take it for. He goes on: he gives me the Beast, and the offices built to wake it and to hold it, and he watches me the whole while as a man watches a fire he has decided he cannot trust but is too cold to leave. The failure is not that I broke his wall. It is that I have stopped being certain he is lying.\par

The game was simple enough to learn and difficult enough to distrust. Each hand sought twenty-one without exceeding it. Aces shifted between one and eleven, which I found morally suspicious. Candidate, Veil, and Keeper cards were worth ten. Equal results did not rescue the player; the Mountain won what would elsewhere have been called a tie.\par

``The Mountain wins ties?''\par

``Justice dislikes ambiguity.''\par

``Justice invented ambiguity.''\par

``Which is why they know how to charge for it.''\par

We played. I won early and was punished almost immediately. Va-Raedin lasted longer --- not because fortune loved him, but because fortune did not appear to know he was present.
I was ruined before half the shoe had gone. Eventually Va-Raedin lost as well.\par

``The Mountain wins,'' I said.\par

``Does she?''\par

``She did.''\par

``This time.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``Then why play?''\par

``That is the correct question asked too early.''\par

``That is impossible.''\par

``Then how do they do it?''\par

``Extremely generous tonight, Keeper,'' Va-Raedin murmured.\par

``If the record of the past is kept perfectly,'' Ka-Syphiron said, ``it alters the decision required for the future. Not by prophecy. Not by hope. By condition. What has already appeared changes what remains possible. Justice knows this so deeply they have made a morality of it.''\par

``Sa-Kailan,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``Be very, very careful.''\par

``Knowledge divided is not only jealousy. It is structure. Sometimes cruelty. Sometimes protection. Always older than your impatience.

I did not fully understand. I understood enough to feel the shape of danger.\par

There it was. The door inside the door.\par

I had been brought near. Fed. Praised. Warned. Taught beyond my level. Allowed to laugh in the room where tomorrow had already begun. But proximity was not possession. Veil in fact was not yet Veil in office. Some words still passed above me.\par

I rose. For a moment I wanted to say something worthy of the evening. Nothing came that would not diminish it. So I bowed --- lower than I had bowed on entering.
When I straightened, Va-Raedin was watching me. The warmth was gone from his face, but not from the room. It had simply taken the form he could bear.\par

I turned toward the door. I carried the prism in my hand and too many mysteries in the rest of me: Faith as echo, prayer, and signal. Balance as matter taught to obey conditions Light could only study once they existed. Justice as a future read by refusing to forget the past. The Beast as the word men stopped joking about before the joke had finished forming. And the memory of light, which even Ka-Syphiron had not pierced.\par

I opened the door.
The old gallery waited outside, dim and smoke-blackened. Behind me, the two men did not speak until the door had nearly closed. I heard one fragment --- Ka-Syphiron's voice, lower than before.\par

``Here. For tomorrow, and for---''\par

Then the latch caught, and the rest belonged to those not yet released and those not yet raised.
I stood a moment with my hand still near the closed door. The corridor had not altered. Everything in it had.\par

That is the honest shape of it, Paul, and it is the shape of everything that came after: I was handed the past as a gift, told it would become the future, and sent out of the room one sentence before the sentence that would have explained me to myself.\par

I did not know --- how could I? --- that this was the last ordinary evening of Ka-Syphiron's life. \emph{The Keeper about to be released.} I had taken the word to mean his office, passing down the chain we had spent the night congratulating; the Mountain meant something colder, and would show me in the Hall what it meant. He had arranged a feast on the edge of that and spent it making a frightened boy laugh.\par

The public ceremony began before the Hall filled.
That is how it felt later, when I tried to remember the sequence. One moment I stood in the old gallery, carrying mysteries away. The next, attendants of Light were dressing me in pale robes I had never worn before that morning. The folded white cloth was bound around my wrist --- not like a wound, but like a visible condition.\par

Va-Raedin led me through corridors that had emptied ahead of us. Shield had already closed the side routes. Balance had already marked warmth and lamp oil for the Hall. Justice had already opened the record.\par

The Mountain had begun to change around the fact.\par
