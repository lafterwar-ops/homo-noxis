\typesetchapter{Chapter 12}{Confession}

I carried Sarah Blackwood's record against my ribs for two days.\par

On the first morning I supervised two Candidates through an aperture exercise. Their measurements differed by the width of an eyelash. I made them strip both frames, and they found a grain of soot beneath one lower edge. They cleaned the wood and began again. The scrap lay inside the inner seam of my robe while I watched their hands.\par

Sarah Blackwood. Amastan. Ka-Xhian. Threshold Exception.\par

I copied three damaged Light marks into the maintenance record, checked the dry basins in the Veil's Chamber and reset a loosened mirror. At the midday measure Ka-Raedin asked why my last column leaned. I cut a fresh pen and copied it again. He watched until I had finished.\par

That evening I laid the robe across my knees. The plain cloth around the scrap had taken the heat of my body. I could have fed paper and cloth to the lamp together. The names would have remained in me, with no mark for another hand to test. I wrapped the scrap and put it back.\par

The paper kept the torn edge, the order of the entries and the Mountain hand that formed them. I had taken it so the three names and the exception could not be reduced to fever, pride or error.\par

I walked once to the turn above Ka-Raedin's chamber. Light could examine the hand, the ink, the age of the paper. Light could not close an outer threshold. I went down again before his attendant saw me.\par

Justice could witness the scrap. To show it there I would have to name the sealed route before a clerk, a guard and whoever Justice summoned to establish standing. The proof would enter the record before Shield had recovered it.\par

Va-Sheva could receive it without crossing offices. Below the three Shield seals I had found the dark ribbon caught in old blood. I knew the uneven edge. I had watched it beside her face. The ribbon might have accused her. It might have been left by someone she had failed to save. I had put it back without knowing.\par

On the second night I slept in my robe. Each time I woke, my hand was over the inner seam.\par

On the third afternoon I sent Va-Sheva a route inquiry concerning a repeated exterior reflection near an outer ventilation seam. The courtesy was permitted between Veils. Its technical language was dull enough to pass a clerk under maintenance overlap, and every word in it was true.\par

She named a narrow observation room between Light and Shield. A wall slit faced a sealed passage without showing it. The door had no visible bar. A bench stood beneath a lamp set too high for reading and too low to leave a standing face in shadow.\par

I arrived first.\par

\ornament

Va-Sheva entered without an escort in sight. Dark duty leather covered her robe; her hair was bound high from her neck. Her eyes counted wall slit, lamp, floor, hands, door. They stopped for a moment at the seam of my robe.\par

``Va-Kailan.''\par

``Va-Sheva.''\par

``You requested Shield view on a Light irregularity.''\par

``Some exterior glints repeat from beyond permitted routes. I followed one.''\par

Her right hand settled flat against her chest. One count. Then it fell.\par

``Did you cross an outer marker?''\par

``No.''\par

``Did you pass a Shield seal?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``How many?''\par

``One, at the desert entrance. I found three more below, on an inner door. I did not open them.''\par

``Begin with the first.''\par

I gave her the mineral face in the rock, the plate that answered the sun and the order of reflections that moved the lock. I told her of the metal tongue that struck when the stone opened.\par

``Once?'' she asked.\par

``When I entered. Its twin moved later, inside.''\par

``Did you touch it?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``Did you disable it?''\par

``No.''\par

``What lay beyond the first seal?''\par

``A chamber.''\par

``Do not name it.''\par

``I do not know its name. I called it the Tomb.''\par

``That name is yours. Keep it so.''\par

``It held objects from the outside, ordered by age and material. Images of Outsider gods. Machines. Maps. A wheeled vehicle divided into parts and carried in. Mountain writing beside recent names.''\par

Her breathing had shortened. Nothing else moved.\par

``Stop,'' she said.\par

I stopped. Air passed through the wall slit with a dry whisper.\par

``Do you order me not to complete the account?'' I asked.\par

``I order you to answer only what I ask.''\par

``Then ask.''\par

``Did you enter by the Mountain passage?''\par

``The second tongue opened a stone. I went through.''\par

``How far?''\par

``To a door under three Shield seals. I did not touch them. A side opening led to a chamber made against sound.''\par

The hand at her chest closed.\par

``Did you enter it?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``What did you touch?''\par

``A woven ribbon caught in blood at the foot of the fixed seat. I lifted the loose end. The stain held it. I put it down.''\par

Her eyes moved to the fingers of my right hand.\par

``Did you remove any part of it?''\par

``No.''\par

``Did you remove anything else from that chamber?''\par

``No.''\par

``What did you remove from beyond the first seal?''\par

``A scrap from a loose map bundle.''\par

The colour went from her face. She looked once toward the door.\par

``Was its absence witnessed by stain, cord, weight or outline?''\par

``No.''\par

``Was anything attached to it?''\par

``No.''\par

``Did you copy it?''\par

``No.''\par

``Did any other person see it?''\par

``No.''\par

``How long have you carried it?''\par

``Two days.''\par

``What did you read?''\par

``Sarah Blackwood. Amastan. Ka-Xhian. Threshold Exception.''\par

Va-Sheva held her hand between us, palm up.\par

``Where is it?''\par

``On my body.''\par

``Give it to Shield.''\par

I reached into the robe slowly. Her eyes stayed on my hand. I brought out the folded cloth, opened it and held the scrap by one corner. The four entries showed in Mountain hand along the torn fold.\par

Va-Sheva did not take it at once.\par

``You came to the right door,'' she said. ``You came holding the fire.''\par

``Memory would not have made you believe me.''\par

``I believe you before I touch it.''\par

``Then take it.''\par

She drew a dark case from her belt, opened it beneath my hand and lifted the scrap by its two bare edges. Her fingers never crossed the writing. Cloth and paper went into the case. The lid closed with two soft clicks.\par

Her right hand turned beside her thigh: two fingers beneath the thumb, wrist inward, one cut toward the floor, palm opened down.\par

A dim reflection in the opposite wall gave me the two wardens outside. One started towards the door. The other caught his arm. Both looked at Va-Sheva through the slit.\par

She repeated the signal.\par

They ran. Their footfalls divided at the first junction and ended.\par

The dark case hung within my reach. Va-Sheva's hands remained empty. She watched my shoulders. I kept them still.\par

``What did you order?''\par

``A threshold drill.''\par

``Those men did not expect it.''\par

``They do now.''\par

``Va-Sheva.''\par

She fixed the case to her belt with a second strap.\par

``The scrap crossed your hidden passage, the outer route, your sleeping chamber, Light's working halls and the corridor outside this room. Any hand searching your robe could have found it. A child looking for thread could have opened the cloth. Proof travels.''\par

``I brought it to the office that could stop it.''\par

``After carrying it away from the seal that stopped it.''\par

I looked at the dark case.\par

``If the chamber is lawful, why must proof of it be treated as fire?''\par

``That question belongs after custody.''\par

``Ka-Xhian's name stands beneath an exception to Shield law. If he held the authority to make it, the exception has a boundary. If he crossed that boundary, Justice must know.''\par

``Justice will know what Justice is permitted to judge.''\par

``That answer protects the office that wrote the permission.''\par

``It protects the route while the office is judged.''\par

``Whose custody?''\par

``Shield's now. Justice's when the route is held.''\par

``And mine?''\par

``You carried sight away. Seeing was the first breach. Bearing it made the next.''\par

``I showed no one.''\par

``I believe you.''\par

``Then let me go to Ka-Raedin before rumour reaches him.''\par

``No.''\par

``Take me to Ka-Dhavar.''\par

``I will report to my Keeper. You will wait here. You will speak to no one, return nowhere and touch no public record until Shield releases you.''\par

``Am I accused?''\par

``Justice accuses. Shield contains.''\par

``Me?''\par

``The breach.''\par

``You came alone to hear me.''\par

``No,'' she said. ``I came without bringing another witness into the room.''\par

The wall slit whispered between us.\par

``Did I do wrong by coming to you?'' I asked.\par

``No.''\par

``Then what did I do right?''\par

``You stopped carrying it.''\par

\ornament

Va-Sheva opened the door. The corridor beyond was empty.\par

``Remain here. Let me handle it.''\par

``Until tomorrow?''\par

``Until you are called.''\par

The northern turn lay six paces beyond her shoulder. From there I could reach the Light stair without crossing a Shield door. Ka-Raedin would receive a Veil who arrived under his own name. Justice would have to witness any seizure made in his chamber.\par

Va-Sheva waited.\par

The dark case at her belt carried the four entries now. If I went north, Sarah's name would travel with me in every demand I made for protection.\par

``I will wait,'' I said.\par

Her fingers tightened once around the door edge.\par

``Good.''\par

She stepped into the corridor.\par

``Va-Sheva.''\par

She looked back.\par

``Will I see you when Shield returns?''\par

``Yes.''\par

She closed the door. The lock made no sound.\par

I sat on the bench. Before a full silence had passed, I stood and pressed the latch. The door opened. The northern turn remained empty. Six paces. The high lamp showed the white Light knot at my wrist. Beyond the turn, my own office would see it and answer. No Shield body stood between us.\par

I closed it again and stayed.\par

Then the first sound came through the stone: metal settling into place, a bar dropped where no public door stood.\par

I rose.\par

Another sound answered from another direction. Then a faint change in the air: pressure along the passage, the lamp flame bending toward the door and straightening.\par

The Mountain was changing state.\par

I opened the door.\par

The corridor beyond was no longer empty. Two Shield wardens stood at opposite ends. One faced the northern turn; the other watched the junction behind me. Neither approached. A Balance clerk moved with a slate, accompanied by a Justice witness carrying a narrow black tablet. They stopped at a family chamber three doors down.\par

``Bodies,'' the Balance clerk said.\par

A woman inside answered. ``Six.''\par

``Names.''\par

She gave them. One by one, voices answered from within. The Balance clerk marked. The Justice witness watched. A Shield warden placed a seal across the door's exterior latch. Any opening would tear it.\par

Water was counted, and tools named aloud --- a needle, a blade for cloth, two cooking hooks --- shown, witnessed, sealed. Then they moved to the next chamber.\par

At the next door the fifth name received no answer. The clerk repeated it. The mother inside said the boy was asleep and began to explain, too quickly, that he had worked the night water turn. The Justice witness cut the seal she had just marked. Shield opened the door while a Faith attendant went in. A pause followed, then a child's angry cry and the attendant's voice naming breath, heat and waking. The boy answered his own name. Only then did the clerk set the fifth mark. The latch was resealed. The count moved on.\par

My hand went to the empty seam of my robe.\par

The Lesser Seal.\par

Two more Faith attendants entered from the far end of the corridor, speaking softly as they walked.\par

``Remain in place until release. Let no fear cross the threshold before the body does. Remain in place until release.''\par

A Light attendant arrived behind them and dimmed three wall lamps in sequence. Each froze into a low, stable flame.\par

I looked at the nearer warden. ``What is this?''\par

The warden gave me nothing.\par

The second deep sound came then, closer, followed by a chain-rattle muffled by the stone. The rattle rose from below my feet.\par

\ornament

I stepped back into the side chamber and closed the door before anyone ordered me to.\par

Inside, the room looked exactly as before. Bench. Water jar. Lamp. Sealed niche.\par

I sat.\par

I studied the lamp. The flame stood too steady. The wall slit no longer whispered as it had while Va-Sheva sat with me. If I shouted, the sound might fail. If I spoke, who would hear? I heard no feet beyond the door.\par

The first buried bar had fallen before the Balance clerk entered the corridor. Now the count had reached the family doors.\par

I waited.\par

Time became difficult. Whether one silence had passed or three, I had no way to tell. The sounds continued, deeper, farther, then nearer again. Somewhere in them the count was walking toward a door where my name would receive no answer.\par

I had told no one where Va-Sheva had received me.\par

The lamp dimmed first. I rose at once. The door stayed shut.\par

The wall niche opened.\par

At the same moment, the door opened inward and two wardens entered, one low, one high. A third dropped from a ceiling seam behind my right shoulder.\par

I moved once. If I could place one body between myself and the lamp, I might see enough to avoid the first grip. The high warden closed that line while the one behind caught my raised elbow.\par

The first hand went to my mouth --- cloth, pressure, a thumb beneath the jaw. My head was drawn back and fixed before sound could become speech. My knee was touched from behind, the floor came up quietly, and someone caught my shoulder before it hit stone.\par

No shout. No command.\par

I tried to draw breath through the gag and found I could.\par

They covered the upper lamp. Low amber remained beneath it. One warden opened my robe seam and searched the place where the scrap had been. Empty. Another checked wrists, belt, collar, sandal folds.\par

Va-Sheva entered last.\par

She stood in the doorway, clear of the physical restraint. A black line of seal-dye crossed the back of one hand.\par

I shaped her name against the cloth. No word left it. She looked at me and answered.\par

``You asked Shield to handle it,'' she said.\par

I drove once against the hands holding me. The warden behind touched a point beneath my shoulder; pain went white down the arm and opened my fingers. I stilled. The pressure left at once.\par

Va-Sheva came closer. ``I told you to wait,'' she said.\par

I held her gaze above the gag.\par

``You did.'' Her voice lowered. ``That is why you are alive.''\par

She stepped back. The wardens lifted me. My feet brushed the floor while they carried my weight, my arms bound close beneath a dark outer cloth.\par

They took me through the wall niche. A fourth warden waited on the first step and took my left side. The passage behind it was narrow and steep. Shield wardens stood in darkness at intervals and turned aside as we reached them. Small covered lights glowed at floor level. Their light reached each foot placement and no higher.\par

Within twenty paces I knew I had never been in this part of the Mountain.\par

Through stone I heard a family answering Balance count, and a child asking whether the drill would end before morning bread. A woman answered yes, quickly.\par

I tried to mark turns. In the desert my Light training had let me read the hidden angle of a route. Here each turn doubled under the last; the covered lights showed feet and withheld the walls.\par

At one junction, a Justice witness waited. Someone other than Sa-Tavan. An older woman with a black tablet, white hair braided tight against the skull. She looked at me, the gag, the bindings, Va-Sheva, then marked the tablet.\par

``Body held,'' she said.\par

``Speech held,'' Va-Sheva answered.\par

``Proof recovered?''\par

``Recovered before seal.''\par

``Seal witnessed.''\par

They continued downward. I knew the last turn before we reached it.\par

The wedges came into the covered light first: ranks of blunt points faced with pressed wool and hair, thrust from the walls and ceiling. At the edges an open metal lattice crossed above another field of points rising from below. The central stone island held the fixed seat, the wrist and ankle rings, the gutter and the black stain that washing had left in its seams.\par

Va-Sheva's ribbon remained at the foot of the seat. The pale fracture I had made in the dried blood showed when the floor light crossed it.\par

The wardens carried me over the lattice. Their footfalls ended beneath them. Nothing returned from the wedges to tell my body where wall or ceiling stood. The inner frame sat within its second frame, divided from the Mountain by felt and an empty hand's breadth. It had been visible when I entered alone. Bound between four Shield wardens, I felt the inner floor move by a fraction beneath their joined weight.\par

Va-Sheva crossed the island without looking down.\par

They placed me in the fixed seat. The rings stayed open. One warden removed the bindings from my arms while two held position and a third untied the dark outer cloth. The gag remained. Low light reached the seat, though I could see no lamp. Behind the seat, water stood beyond a narrow grille.\par

``Listen,'' she said.\par

I did.\par

``You are under Shield containment by Lesser Seal. Justice has witnessed recovery of proof and holding of speech. Light will be informed through proper sequence. You will not be questioned until the Seal completes.''\par

I stared at her.\par

``Your family is not informed.''\par

I closed my eyes once despite myself.\par

``No public scandal,'' she said. ``No corridor rumour. No devotional panic. No one wakes to your name.''\par

She nodded to the warden. Only then was the gag removed.\par

I held my tongue. My jaw ached. My mouth was dry. Speech, once returned, felt like a dangerous tool set back in my hand while they watched.\par

When I did speak, my voice was rough but level.\par

``You knew what would happen when you told me to wait.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``You used my agreement to keep me still.''\par

``Yes.''\par

I looked at the open wrist rings. ``And if I had not agreed?''\par

``The same Seal. More force. More risk. Fewer mercies.''\par

``Mercies.''\par

``Your arms are free. Your family sleeps. You are breathing.''\par

``Shield counts those?''\par

``Shield counts every body it holds.''\par

``What happens now?'' I asked.\par

``A trial.''\par

``Before whom?''\par

``Those permitted.''\par

``On what charge?''\par

``Breach of Seal,'' she said at last. ``Bearing Forbidden Proof. Unauthorised Revelation in potential and act.''\par

My fingers closed around the edge of the fixed seat.\par

``If I defend myself by speaking what I found?''\par

``Then you repeat the breach.''\par

``And if I do not?''\par

``Then you stand silent before a danger you cannot name.''\par

She turned to leave.\par

``Va-Sheva.''\par

She stopped at the invisible seam where the door would open.\par

``Did you choose death?''\par

``No.''\par

``Then what did you choose?''\par

She looked at me once more.\par

``Containment before understanding.''\par

At the seam she said, ``Kailan. I am sorry.'' She turned before I could answer, and the door sealed behind her without visible line.\par

Then the silence the room was built for closed over me. The last of her went out of the stone, and after it came nothing: no corridor, no water, no far prayer, no breath of the vents. Into the empty place my own body rose. The knock of my heart came first, too loud. Then a thin sea-sound beneath it that was my own blood. Then the dry creak of a joint as I turned my head. With no sound to set me in the room, the floor let go of its certainty, and I put a hand to the felt to keep my feet. However long the Seal took, I would spend it here --- with the knock, and the sea, and the creak, and no other company at all.\par

\begin{room}

--- \ldots{} A room built to give a man back nothing but the sound of himself. Faith made the quiet and Shield made the seal, and between them they left one place in the whole Mountain where a man cannot get behind the size of what he has done. \ldots{} You went into it too, Sarah. Not that room: into the deep of it, past the wall, and you came out with your face in its drawer and your name in its ledger. So they had you, somewhere back there. In a room the sound could not leave, with one of Shield's own across the table and the long questions coming. That is where you learned to hate us cleanly enough to spend a life on it. Tell me I have it right.

--- You have it wrong, and I would have you mark exactly how wrong, because the whole of the difference between us is folded into it. Nobody dragged me anywhere. Nobody had to. I did not break into your Mountain, boy; I was invited through the front of it, with a lamp held low for my feet. And the man waiting on the other side asked me no question at all. He made me an offer. \ldots{} You want a soundless room? I will give you one past anything Shield ever sealed. I walked into mine on my own two feet, and I drew the door shut after me, and I turned the key from the inside, and I have been the one prisoner in it the better part of sixty years, and I called it my life's work. He had a face, the man who held it open for me. He had a name.

--- \ldots{} Say it.

--- Amastan.

\end{room}
