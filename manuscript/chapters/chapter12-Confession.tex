\typesetchapter{Chapter 12}{Confession}

Being dragged into a cell is easy to forgive. What I could never explain is that I chose each door myself, congratulated myself at every one, and arrived at the last still believing I was wise. I carried the scrap two days before I chose the door to receive it.\par

\ornament

A boy would have run. The old Sa-Kailan would have run --- rushed to Ka-Raedin, or forced Justice to receive what it lacked standing to hear, or gone back to the Tomb, drunk on first discovery, to open the next sealed door.\par

I did none of those things.\par

I performed my duties. I supervised two Candidates through an aperture exercise and copied three damaged Light marks into the maintenance record. I checked the dry basins in the Veil's Chamber, restored a loosened shutter, and said nothing when Ka-Raedin watched me longer than usual. I ate what was placed before me and drank what was measured. I slept less than I pretended, and moved through the Mountain with the composure expected of a Veil of Light.\par

Inside the inner seam of my robe, wrapped in plain cloth, lay the fragment.\par

Sarah Blackwood. Amastan. Ka-Xhian. Threshold Exception.\par

I left it where it lay. The words had already entered me. They stood behind my eyes when I cleaned a lamp. They touched the edge of my sleep and turned it before it settled.\par

On the first day, I arranged the evidence without touching the conclusion.\par

On the second day, the scrap itself became the fact that frightened me most.\par

At first it had felt like prudence. Memory alone could be corrected, diminished, or made to resemble fever. If I accused anyone without evidence, they would treat me as a child. The scrap would make them take it seriously.\par

But the scrap could not be managed the way those things could.\par

That should have made me destroy it. I kept it. It should have made me lay it before Justice; I refused, for Shield's sealed contradiction brought to Justice without its witness might widen the breach I meant to repair. It should have made me confess to Ka-Raedin; I stayed silent, for Light had found the wound, and closing it was not Light's to do.\par

There was one correct door.\par

Va-Sheva.\par

I examined that conclusion with severity, because Va-Sheva's name had power in me, and I no longer trusted what held power over me. She had been the older girl beyond me, then the woman shaped by office before I had finished being a boy, then the Veil of Shield who made the desert more dangerous because she could read it. As a boy I had wanted her.\par

But the reasons survived the distrust. She was Shield. She was Veil. She had been at the Oasis when the glint first entered my life. She knew outer markers, silent signals, containment, and thresholds. She could receive what belonged to Shield without dispersing it through the wrong chambers.\par

\ornament

I waited until the third afternoon after the Tomb, when Shield rotations changed between the eastern service spine and the lower routes toward the cedar gate. A public petition would have failed --- Shield delays or rejects anything that names too much in a line of record. Cornering her in a corridor would have been boyish and dangerous. Instead I used a courtesy permitted between Veils: a route inquiry concerning a repeated exterior reflection near an outer ventilation seam. I phrased it in technical language dull enough for a clerk to file under maintenance overlap. It was true as far as it went.\par

Va-Sheva came herself.\par

The meeting place was a narrow observation room between jurisdictions, claimed by neither Light nor Shield. A wall slit looked toward a sealed passage, never into it. The door had no visible bar. A bench stood along one wall, the lamp set above it at an awkward height. Shield liked rooms that could become other rooms quickly.\par

Va-Sheva entered without escort.\par

She wore dark duty leather, her hair bound high, her face unsoftened by anything that could be mistaken for private memory. She looked older than she had at the Oasis.\par

``Va-Kailan,'' she said.\par

``Va-Sheva.''\par

Her eyes moved over the room before resting on me: wall slit, lamp, floor, hands, robe seam. If she saw where the scrap lay, she gave no sign.\par

``You requested Shield view on a Light irregularity.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``Then speak of the irregularity.''\par

No warmth. No old remark about boys and eyes.\par

I chose the first sentence carefully.\par

``Some exterior glints repeat from beyond permitted routes. Not all are accidental.''\par

``No.''\par

The answer was too clean to be an opening.\par

``I followed one.''\par

Now she changed. Her breath shortened. Her attention, normally spread across threshold, corridor, body, and air, gathered fully onto me.\par

``You should speak carefully from here.''\par

``I know.''\par

``No,'' she said. ``You know you should be careful. That is not the same as knowing how.''\par

The old edge was there, though it no longer made me feel fifteen.\par

``I found an outer rock,'' I said, ``marked by Shield. It answered Light --- a polished mineral face, a concealed entrance, a passage cut by hands that did not wish to be studied.''\par

``That sentence already concerns me.''\par

I looked at her.\par

``Do you want me to stop?''\par

``Yes.''\par

Her honesty struck me.\par

``Do you order me to stop?''\par

Something like pain moved beneath her composure.\par

``No.''\par

I continued.\par

``The passage led to a chamber.''\par

``Do not name it.''\par

``I do not know its proper name. I thought \emph{Tomb}.''\par

Her eyes hardened. ``That is an invention.''\par

``Then correct me.''\par

``I cannot. Correction would be speech.''\par

I pressed, but without heat.\par

``The chamber held objects from the outside.''\par

``Many chambers hold many objects.''\par

``Arranged by age. By material. By danger. Some ancient enough to have lost their makers. Some recent enough to accuse the living.''\par

Va-Sheva turned her head slightly toward the door, toward the unseen wardens. I continued before the room closed around silence.\par

``I saw the false water lord with the trident. The father with the blade over the bound son. The tortured man worshipped on crossed wood.''\par

``Stop.''\par

The word was quiet.\par

I stopped.\par

For several breaths there was only the low movement of air through the wall slit.\par

``You walked through a sealed chamber,'' she said. ``You read what you were not named to read.''\par

``Yes.''\par

The answer was clean.\par

``You should have come with memory alone,'' she said.\par

Va-Sheva looked at me fully.\par

``What did you remove?''\par

The question was so direct that a lie would have exposed me on the spot.\par

``A scrap.''\par

Her face lost colour. Enough.\par

``What scrap?''\par

``A copied fragment from a map bundle. Names in Mountain hand.''\par

``Which names?''\par

To refuse now would make my confession theatrical and useless.\par

``Sarah Blackwood. Amastan. Ka-Xhian. Threshold Exception.''\par

Va-Sheva closed her eyes once. Only once.\par

``Where is it?''\par

``With me. On my body.''\par

``Show me.''\par

I reached slowly into the inner seam of my robe. I withdrew the folded cloth, then the scrap, and held it between two fingers.\par

Va-Sheva left it in my hand. She looked at the scrap as if it were flame.\par

``You came to the right door, Va-Kailan,'' she said. ``But you came already holding the fire.''\par

``I came to Shield.''\par

``With proof removed from seal.''\par

``I showed it to no one else.''\par

``That is good.''\par

``Then---''\par

``It is not enough.''\par

``With memory only, you could have denied me. With suspicion only, redirected me. So the proof was necessary.''\par

``For you,'' she said. ``Not for the Mountain.''\par

``If the chamber is lawful, why is proof of it fatal?''\par

``Because proof travels.''\par

\ornament

``A secret can be guarded,'' Va-Sheva said. ``A witness can be contained. Proof can be lost, stolen, copied, planted, or found in the wrong sleeve by a child looking for thread.''\par

``I kept it sealed.''\par

``You carried it through the Mountain to reach me.''\par

I had no answer.\par

She stepped closer and left the scrap where it was.\par

``Listen. You are Veil of Light --- which makes it worse. You do not only hold proof. You make it visible. Hold a mark, and it becomes a door in another's mind.''\par

``I came to you so that would not happen.''\par

``That is not a defence.''\par

I lowered the scrap slightly.\par

``I did not come to expose Shield, or to accuse before understanding.''\par

``I believe you.''\par

``If Shield can touch Outsiders under seal, there must be a sequence by which abuse is judged. If Ka-Xhian authorised something, someone must know whether it was lawful.''\par

``Someone does.''\par

``Who? Justice? Light? Faith?''\par

She kept silent to each, and then said, ``Stop.''\par

I stopped.\par

She reached at last and took the scrap from my hand. Her fingers kept clear of mine. She folded the paper back into its cloth without looking again at the words, then placed it inside a small dark case from her belt.\par

The case closed with a soft double click.\par

I felt lighter, and, without its weight against my skin, more exposed.\par

Va-Sheva took one step back.\par

``The offence is that you carried sight away,'' she said. ``Seeing was only the door.''\par

``Will you take me to Ka-Dhavar?''\par

``I will consult him. Without you. You do not answer yet.''\par

I studied her.\par

``Am I accused?''\par

``Not by me. That is the only answer I can give before my Keeper.''\par

Va-Sheva turned toward the door, then stopped. Her right hand moved.\par

It was a small gesture. Too small for alarm, too precise for accident. Two fingers folded beneath the thumb. Wrist turned inward. Hand cut once toward the floor. Palm opened downward.\par

The two wardens outside the door were meant to stay unseen. But the observation room's angle caught enough reflection in the opposite wall that I saw one of them shift. Then the second.\par

Both froze.\par

They looked at each other. The first warden's face changed with something close to disbelief. The second's hand moved halfway toward his chest and stopped.\par

Va-Sheva kept her eyes on me. She repeated the gesture.\par

The wardens ran. No question. No confirmation. No sound beyond the sudden hard rhythm of trained feet leaving their posts.\par

I turned back to her.\par

``What did you order?''\par

``A threshold drill,'' she said. ``Nothing that concerns Light.''\par

``Those men did not look as if they were training.''\par

``Then they need more of it.''\par

``Va-Sheva.''\par

She met my eyes.\par

``You have done very serious damage,'' she said.\par

``I will consult Ka-Dhavar. You will speak to no one else --- not Ka-Raedin, not Justice, not Faith, not any attendant or clerk or Candidate. You will not return to the chamber. You will remain where Shield places you until Shield has contained the matter.''\par

``Contained me?''\par

``The matter.''\par

``I came to the right door,'' I said.\par

``Yes.''\par

``And now the door closes.''\par

``Yes.''\par

If she had been cruel, I would have had something simple to push against. She was not cruel.\par

``Wait,'' she said. ``Let me handle it.''\par

``Until tomorrow?''\par

A hesitation so small only Light would see it.\par

``Until you are called.''\par

I considered refusal, without drama: asking for Ka-Raedin, demanding Justice witness, returning to the northern halls with the dignity of office. But every possible movement now carried the same danger she had named. If I challenged Shield here, in a threshold room, after giving them the scrap, I became exactly what procedure feared.\par

I had chosen the right door. To reject its first closure because it frightened me would make the choice meaningless.\par

``I will wait,'' I said.\par

The words felt like putting my own hand on the latch from the inside.\par

Something like grief moved through her and vanished before it could become personal.\par

``Good.''\par

She opened the door herself. No wardens stood beyond it now. The corridor was empty.\par

The room was far too comfortable for a place meant to hold me.\par

``Remain here.''\par

``For how long?''\par

``Until Shield returns.''\par

I almost smiled.\par

``That is a very Shield answer.''\par

``Yes.''\par

She turned to leave.\par

``Va-Sheva.''\par

She stopped.\par

``Did I do wrong by coming to you?''\par

Her back remained to me for a moment.\par

``No.''\par

Then she left and closed the door.\par

The lock stayed silent.\par

I sat, then stood and tested the door once.\par

It opened.\par

That frightened me.\par

I stayed where I was.\par

Shield had placed me in a chamber and inside my own agreement.\par

Then the first sound came through the stone: metal settling into place, a bar dropped where no public door stood.\par

I rose.\par

Another sound answered from another direction. Then a faint change in the air: pressure along the passage, the lamp flame bending toward the door and straightening.\par

The Mountain was changing state.\par

I opened the door.\par

The corridor beyond was no longer empty. Two Shield wardens stood at opposite ends, far enough to seem indifferent, near enough that the corridor had become a container. A Balance clerk moved with a slate, accompanied by a Justice witness carrying a narrow black tablet. They stopped at a family chamber three doors down.\par

``Bodies,'' the Balance clerk said.\par

A woman inside answered. ``Six.''\par

``Names.''\par

She gave them. One by one, voices answered from within. The Balance clerk marked. The Justice witness watched. A Shield warden placed a seal across the door's exterior latch, making exit visible as breach.\par

Water was counted, and tools --- a needle, a blade for cloth, two cooking hooks --- shown, witnessed, sealed. Then they moved to the next chamber.\par

I felt cold spread from my spine outward.\par

The Lesser Seal.\par

Faith entered from the far end of the corridor, two attendants in pale robes, speaking softly as they walked.\par

``Remain in place until release. Let no fear cross the threshold before the body does. Remain in place until release.''\par

No explanation. Only formula.\par

A Light attendant arrived behind them and dimmed three wall lamps in sequence. Frozen into low, stable flame.\par

I looked at the nearer warden. ``What is this?''\par

The warden gave me nothing.\par

The second deep sound came then, closer, followed by a chain-rattle swallowed in stone. Perhaps I had set it off.\par

\ornament

I stepped back into the side chamber and closed the door before anyone ordered me to.\par

Inside, the room looked exactly as before. Bench. Water jar. Lamp. Sealed niche.\par

I sat.\par

I studied the lamp. The flame stood too steady --- the air in the room had been altered along with the corridor, hidden vents closed or shifted. If I shouted, the sound might fail. If I spoke, who would hear?\par

Shield would. No one else who mattered.\par

The first hand of the Lesser Seal had gone to the Mountain's passages.\par

I waited.\par

Time became difficult. Whether one silence had passed or three, I had no way to tell. The sounds continued, deeper, farther, then nearer again.\par

No one would know I was here.\par

When Shield came for me, there was no dramatic arrival.\par

The lamp dimmed first. I rose at once. The door stayed shut.\par

The wall niche opened.\par

At the same moment, the door opened inward and two wardens entered, one low, one high. A fourth dropped from a ceiling seam behind my right shoulder.\par

I moved once. If I could place one body between myself and the lamp, I might see enough to avoid the first grip.\par

Shield had accounted for that.\par

The first hand went to my mouth --- cloth, pressure, a thumb beneath the jaw. My head was drawn back and fixed before sound could become speech. My knee was touched from behind, the floor came up quietly, and someone caught my shoulder before it hit stone.\par

No shout. No command. No anger.\par

I tried to draw breath through the gag and found I could.\par

They covered only the upper lamp, leaving the room in low amber shadow, enough sight to work. One warden opened my robe seam and searched the place where the scrap had been. Empty. Another checked wrists, belt, collar, sandal folds.\par

Va-Sheva entered last. Of course.\par

She stood in the doorway, clear of the physical restraint. Her face was pale and controlled, and a black line of seal-dye crossed the back of one hand.\par

I tried to speak her name through the cloth. It emerged as nothing. She heard it anyway.\par

``You asked Shield to handle it,'' she said.\par

No defence. No apology. No cruelty.\par

I struggled once, because the sentence had found the one place my composure was still young. The wardens absorbed the movement without tightening more than necessary. Pain appeared, clean and instructive. I stilled.\par

Va-Sheva came closer. ``I told you to wait,'' she said.\par

My eyes hardened above the gag.\par

``You did.'' Her voice lowered. ``That is why you are alive.''\par

Whether that was mercy, threat, or confession escaped me.\par

She stepped back. The wardens lifted me. My feet touched the floor only enough to seem to walk, my arms bound close beneath a dark outer cloth.\par

No one saw. The Lesser Seal had cleared the corridors of anyone who might have.\par

They took me through the wall niche. The passage behind it was narrow, steep, and already occupied by Shield. Bodies stood in darkness at intervals, turning aside only as the group reached them. Small covered lights glowed at floor level, enough for trained feet.\par

I knew within twenty paces that I had never been in this part of the Mountain, though it ran beside places I had used all my life.\par

Through stone I heard a family answering Balance count, and a child asking whether the drill would end before morning bread. A woman answered yes, quickly.\par

I tried to mark turns. Shield denied me that too. In the desert my Light training had let me read the hidden angle of a route. Here the passages had been built so that the angles told me nothing.\par

At one junction, a Justice witness waited. Someone other than Sa-Tavan. An older woman with a black tablet, white hair braided tight against the skull. She looked at me, the gag, the bindings, Va-Sheva, then marked the tablet.\par

``Body held,'' she said.\par

``Speech held,'' Va-Sheva answered.\par

``Proof recovered?''\par

``Recovered before seal.''\par

``Seal witnessed.''\par

They continued downward.\par

This room was made against sound. The walls stood in deep wedges, rank on rank, each faced with pressed felt of wool and hair, no plane square to another, so that a sound had nowhere to return from; the ceiling matched them, and a floor of fine mesh carried my weight over more of the same below. A hidden lamp gave low, sourceless light, and a water recess sat behind a narrow grille. My sandals fell without noise. When I shifted my weight, the small sound of it left me and never came back. There was no angle to use, no seam to study.\par

Faith had made the quiet and Shield had made the seal. Faith is given the Listening of Stone --- the prayer laid to its rhythm, the trembling of the Earth understood --- and Faith knows how to take a sound apart until none of it remains. Shield keeps what Faith has silenced.\par

The whole of it rested on nothing that met the Mountain. I felt the faint give underfoot --- a chamber hung clear of the rock, so that no tremor of the corridor and no far turn of Balance water could cross into it. They had cut a piece of the Mountain loose from the Mountain and filled it with the silence they had made.\par

They sat me on the ledge and removed the bindings from my arms first. Two wardens held position while a third untied the outer cloth. The gag remained. Va-Sheva stood before me.\par

``Listen,'' she said.\par

I did.\par

``You are under Shield containment by Lesser Seal. Justice has witnessed recovery of proof and holding of speech. Light will be informed through proper sequence. You will not be questioned until the Seal completes.''\par

I stared at her. She understood the question.\par

``Your family is not informed.''\par

I closed my eyes once despite myself.\par

``No public scandal,'' she said. ``No corridor rumour. No devotional panic. No one wakes to your name.''\par

She nodded to the warden. Only then was the gag removed.\par

I held my tongue. My jaw ached. My mouth was dry. Speech, once returned, felt like a dangerous tool set back in my hand while they watched.\par

When I did speak, my voice was rough but level.\par

``You did not wait until tomorrow.''\par

``No.''\par

``You never meant to.''\par

``No.''\par

``You used my agreement to keep me still.''\par

``Yes.''\par

I looked around the clean chamber. ``And if I had not agreed?''\par

``The same Seal. More force. More risk. Fewer mercies.''\par

``Mercies.''\par

``Yes.''\par

I laughed once, without humour. ``Shield has a difficult relationship with that word.''\par

``Shield has a difficult relationship with all words. That is why we prefer doors.''\par

``What happens now?'' I asked.\par

``A trial.''\par

``Before whom?''\par

``Those permitted.''\par

``On what charge?''\par

She took her time.\par

``Breach of Seal,'' she said at last. ``Bearing of forbidden proof. Unauthorized revelation.''\par

I looked at her for a long time.\par

``If I defend myself by speaking what I found?''\par

``Then you repeat the breach.''\par

``And if I do not?''\par

``Then you stand silent before a danger you cannot name.''\par

She turned to leave.\par

``Va-Sheva.''\par

She stopped at the invisible seam where the door would open.\par

``Did you choose death?''\par

Her face held, but something behind it closed.\par

``No.''\par

The answer was no comfort.\par

``Then what did you choose?''\par

She looked at me once more.\par

``Containment before understanding.''\par

Then, at the invisible seam where the door would open, the office slipped. ``\textit{Kay}, I am sorry" she said: only that, the nickname she may have had for me, secretly, stripped of every prefix her whole life had trained her to lay before it. ``I am so, so sorry'''' A tear stood at the rim of her eye, and grew.\par

And she left; and as she went the drop became a river, and a breath became a sob, and the sob became a flow of sound and wail and sorrow, carried back to me through stone that had been built to carry nothing. It broke my heart, and I did not want it to end. Then the door sealed behind her without visible line.\par

Then the silence the room was built for closed over me. The last of her went out of the stone, and after it came nothing: no corridor, no water, no far prayer, no breath of the vents. Into the empty place my own body rose. The knock of my heart came first, too loud. Then a thin sea-sound beneath it that was my own blood. Then the dry creak of a joint as I turned my head. With no sound to set me in the room, the floor let go of its certainty, and I put a hand to the felt to keep my feet. However long the Seal took, I would spend it here --- with the knock, and the sea, and the creak, and no other company at all.\par

\begin{room}

--- \ldots{} A room built to give a man back nothing but the sound of himself. Faith made the quiet and Shield made the seal, and between them they left one place in the whole Mountain where a man cannot get behind the size of what he has done. \ldots{} You went into it too, Sarah. Not that room --- but into the deep of it, past the wall, and you came out with your face in its drawer and your name in its ledger. So they had you, somewhere back there. In a room the sound could not leave, with one of Shield's own across the table and the long questions coming. That is where you learned to hate us cleanly enough to spend a life on it. Tell me I have it right.

--- You have it wrong, and I would have you mark exactly how wrong, because the whole of the difference between us is folded into it. Nobody dragged me anywhere. Nobody had to. I did not break into your Mountain, boy; I was invited through the front of it, with a lamp held low for my feet. And the man waiting on the other side asked me no question at all. He made me an offer. \ldots{} You want a soundless room? I will give you one past anything Shield ever sealed. I walked into mine on my own two feet, and I drew the door shut after me, and I turned the key from the inside, and I have been the one prisoner in it the better part of sixty years, and I called it my life's work. He had a face, the man who held it open for me. He had a name.

--- \ldots{} Say it.

--- Amastan.

\end{room}
