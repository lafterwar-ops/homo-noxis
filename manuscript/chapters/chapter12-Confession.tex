\typesetchapter{Chapter 12}{Confession}

I carried three things against my ribs for two days: the torn strip with its four entries, the photograph of Sarah Blackwood beside Amastan at the outer folds, and Va-Sheva's ribbon stiffened with old blood.\par

On the first morning I supervised two Candidates through an aperture exercise. Their measurements differed by the width of an eyelash. I made them strip both frames, and they found a grain of soot beneath one lower edge. They cleaned the wood and began again. The strip crackled inside the seam of my robe when I bent over their hands. The ribbon lay folded beside it; the photograph lay flat beneath both.\par

\begin{scripture}
Sarah Blackwood. Amastan. Ka-Xhian. Threshold Exception.
\end{scripture}

Through the first day I kept all three in separate folds of my robe. At every lamp I considered feeding paper, photograph and ribbon to the flame together. Their order, material and joined presence would have gone, leaving my memory to accuse whomever fear chose. I kept them.\par

By the second evening I could make the archive establish only contact beyond the Threshold, not its purpose. Every shelf had carried the same warning: \textit{harmless unless taught}. I had removed two records and begun supplying the lesson myself.\par

I tried to make one Kahir examine another without first crossing the boundary between them and could not. Each office alone held the facts by which its secret acts might be judged. Justice needed an accusation before it could receive them; an accusation needed those same facts. Whoever carried the first fact to Justice would commit the breach that allowed Justice to ask whether a breach had occurred. Giving Shield all three would also leave the office named by the evidence as its only custodian. I needed the law that governed the interval before any Keeper was accused. I slept in the robe.\par

At Second Silence the next day, Ka-Raedin was teaching four senior Candidates to recover a survey line after one of its floor points had shifted. Cords crossed the chamber from three fixed lamps to a black pin set in clay. One candidate held the last cord under tension while another read the error aloud. Ka-Raedin had let them carry the false line almost to its consequence.\par

``Stop,'' I said. ``Leave every cord where it stands and clear the chamber.''\par

The candidate at the clay pin looked to Ka-Raedin.\par

``They have one measure left,'' he said.\par

``My concern takes precedence.''\par

``Why?''\par

``Because a Veil of Light has just told you so.''\par

Ka-Raedin looked from my face to the white cloth at my wrist. ``Mark your positions. Touch nothing. Outside.''\par

The Candidates fixed their cords and withdrew. He waited until the last sandal had passed the turn and for me to speak.\par

``I have been thinking about Light doctrine, as you taught me to. Keeper, you keep what must remain dark. As Veil, I am the one who places the light. What happens if I place the light in front of something that must remain dark?''\par

``I can cover it. I cannot make you, myself or others unsee what it reveals.''\par

``Shield defends from the enemy without. Who defends against the enemy within?''\par

``The other four offices.''\par

``What if they cannot see through the enemy's seal?''\par

``Justice can hear an accusation.''\par

``Not if Justice is the accused.''\par

Ka-Raedin released the cord between the two pins. ``What trouble have you put yourself into?''\par

``\textit{Ray} this is serious, possibly existential. The Mountain may have carried a breach before any living Keeper took office.''\par

``A breach within? If it has a hand, that is treason. If it has none, defect. Which do you mean?''\par

``I cannot say.''\par

``How old?''\par

``I cannot say.''\par

``To protect me?''\par

``Partly. If Balance were emptying the stores, Justice destroying records or Faith altering scripture, the proof would belong to the office it accused. What then?''\par

``The offices keep one another by remaining divided. The office that finds the danger preserves what it has; a second witnesses it; the offices not accused establish standing. The office concerned answers before the others, never to itself alone.''\par

``How?''\par

``You have withheld the act, the accused and the proof. I cannot give you a road without inheriting all three.''\par

``Then the rule is enough.''\par

Before I left, Ka-Raedin said, ``Va-Kailan. Be very careful. I know of one Veil who carried a question like yours into a sealed hearing. He did not return to his office.''\par

``Then let me consult Light's record of it.''\par

``No. That record is sealed from Veils.''\par

I returned to the Veil's Chamber before the evening lamps. I looked at the image once more. Sarah Blackwood faced the hard light without narrowing her eyes. Amastan smiled beside her. The Mountain rose behind both of them, ordinary enough that an Outsider might have passed the photograph across a table and seen only two people before a rock. I turned it face down.\par

I laid my Light token beside the photograph. Its close rings held the old likeness of ripples of water spreading from a point, the figure by which Ka-Raedin had led me from the ray to interference. His refusal returned with it: the hearing record was sealed from Veils. If I found the Keeper's Chamber, perhaps I could reach it.\par

I wrapped the photograph in paper from the Light records, closed it with white wax and pressed my token's rings into the wax. On the outer fold I wrote my name and, beneath it:

\begin{scripture}
For the Veil who follows me. If I do not return to Light, do not open this alone.
\end{scripture}

The cradle lifted from its stem when turned against the thread of its fixing. Beneath it lay the spare aperture leaves every Veil was taught to keep dry and ordered. I placed the packet below them and returned the stem to the mark. I checked the fixing twice and wiped the place my thumb had darkened. The torn strip and ribbon remained against my ribs. I had been taught to surrender portions of judgment to offices beyond my own. That surrender, more than stone, made the Mountain possible. If I withdrew mine when its law first endangered me, I had never believed in the order at all. I could refuse Shield's single hand and still submit myself to the five.\par

On the third afternoon I sent Va-Sheva a route inquiry concerning a repeated exterior reflection near an outer ventilation seam. The courtesy was permitted between Veils. Every word in it was true. She named a narrow observation room between Light and Shield, with a wall slit facing a sealed passage and a bench beneath one high lamp.\par

I arrived first.\par

\ornament

Va-Sheva entered without an escort in sight. Dark duty leather covered her robe; her hair was bound high from her neck. She took the end of the bench nearest the door. Her eyes counted wall slit, lamp, floor, hands and door. They stopped at the seam of my robe.\par

``You called Shield over an irregularity of light?''\par

``Shield has changed you.''\par

``The irregularity.''\par

I gave her the answer she had given me at fifteen.\par

\begin{scripture}
And God called the desert the Threshold; \\
and He set heat upon it by day, \\
and cold upon it by night, \\
and mirage upon its face, \\
that no man should cross it lightly.\par
\end{scripture}

``Did you mean it,'' I asked, ``or did you lie?''\par

``What is this about?''\par

``How did you become a Veil? Were you tortured?''\par

``That is not for Light to know.''\par

``Is that how Shield took the girl who stole lamp-oil with me and blamed the smell on a kitchen cat?''\par

``\textit{Kay}. What is going on?'' she asked, and then ``Va-Kailan of Light, explain yourself.''\par

``Is the soundless room training for capture? So an Outsider cannot make a Veil of Shield reveal the Mountain?''\par

``No Outsider has questioned me. Tell me what you saw.''\par

``Before I answer, trust this much: I meant to protect the Mountain.''\par

``I will remember that you said so.''\par

``Promise me that if Shield takes me, I do not disappear.''\par

``If Shield takes you, Balance will count you living and Justice will witness the holding. I promise nothing beyond that.''\par

``Then whatever I am called after this, remember that I came to you.''\par

I drew Va-Sheva's ribbon from my robe and held it out to her. She took it. Her eyes found the colour, then the dried blood.\par

``It was you?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``What else did you take?''\par

``A torn record and a photograph.''\par

``What did the record say?''\par

``Sarah Blackwood. Amastan. Ka-Xhian. Threshold Exception. Did you know Ka-Xhian? Was Ka-Dhavar his Veil?''\par

``The succession of Shield is not yours to question. Where are they?''\par

``The record is here. The photograph is beneath my seal in Light.''\par

``Who did you tell?''\par

``No one. Ka-Raedin heard a question of law and no fact.''\par

``Give me the record.''\par

I brought out the folded strip. Va-Sheva drew a dark case from her belt, laid the ribbon inside and held the case beneath my hand. I set the strip beside it. She closed the lid with two soft clicks.\par

Va-Sheva stood, opened the door and stepped into the Shield corridor. A polished strip of stone opposite the wall slit returned the passage at an angle. In it I saw her dark figure and the two wardens waiting beyond her. She looked at the nearer man. Her right hand turned beside her thigh: two fingers beneath the thumb, wrist inward, one cut towards the floor, palm opened down.\par

One warden started towards the room. The other caught his arm. Both looked at Va-Sheva through the slit as if asking whether they had read her hand correctly.\par

She repeated the signal.\par

They ran. Their footfalls divided at the first junction and ended.\par

Va-Sheva returned to the doorway and fixed the case to her belt with a second strap. The northern turn lay six paces beyond her shoulder. From there I could reach the Light stair without crossing a Shield door. Ka-Raedin would receive me under my own name.\par

``What did you order?''\par

``What I promised. Remain here until the count reaches you.''\par

I looked once at the turn and stayed where I was.\par

She closed the door.\par

The first buried bar fell before her steps had ended. Another answered from below, then a chain moved behind the wall. Air pressed along the passage and the lamp bent towards the door. The Mountain was changing state.\par

When I opened the door, two Shield wardens stood at opposite ends of the corridor. Neither approached. A Balance clerk moved between the family chambers with a slate, accompanied by a Justice witness carrying a narrow black tablet. At each door the people inside gave their number, then their names. Each voice answered separately. Tools and water were shown. Shield sealed the latch after Justice marked it.\par

At the fifth door, one name received no answer. The mother said the boy was asleep and began explaining too quickly that he had worked the night water turn. The Justice witness cut the seal. Shield opened while a Faith attendant went in. A child's angry cry followed, then the attendant's voice naming breath, heat and waking. The boy answered his own name. Only then did Balance set the fifth mark and let the count move on.\par

Two Faith attendants came from the far corridor, repeating the Lesser Seal instruction as they walked.\par

``The Veil of Light is missing. Remain in your quarters for the count and until release. Let no fear cross the threshold before the body does.''\par

A Light attendant followed and dimmed three wall lamps in sequence. Each settled into a low, stable flame. The count, the witness, the held doors and the measured lights passed before me in their proper offices. I returned to the observation room and waited.\par

The latch remained free on my side. Beyond it, the Light turn was still six paces away. Crossing those six paces would turn the act into flight and leave only Shield's description of it. I sat beneath the lamp with both hands visible.\par

The wall niche opened when the count reached our section. Three wardens entered through it. I stood and held out my hands. One crossed my wrists, tested the cord and bound them beneath a dark outer cloth. Another searched the empty seams where the strip and ribbon had been, then the belt, collar and sandal folds. The third placed a folded gag between my teeth and fixed it behind my head. It held my jaw without closing my breath. Va-Sheva watched from the doorway with the sealed case against her belt. She gave no order until the search was complete.\par

They took me through the niche. The hidden passage was narrow and steep, its small covered lights reaching each foot placement and no higher. Shield wardens stood in darkness at intervals and turned aside as we passed. Through stone I heard a family answering Balance count and a child asking whether the doors would open before morning bread.\par

I tried to mark the turns. Each doubled beneath the last; the low lamps showed feet and withheld the walls. The cloth over my arms shifted whenever a warden changed grip, and the four of us had to shorten step together on the steepest stair. Once my heel missed an edge. Both wardens took my weight before the stumble could make a sound. At one junction an old Justice witness waited, white hair braided tight against her skull. She inspected my gag, bindings, Va-Sheva's case and the black line of Seal dye across her hand.\par

``Body held,'' the witness said.\par

``Speech held,'' Va-Sheva answered.\par

``Proof secured?''\par

``Two objects secured before Seal. One record declared beneath a Light seal.''\par

``Seal witnessed.''\par

They continued downward. I knew the last turn before the room appeared.\par

The wedges came first: ranks of blunt points faced with pressed wool and hair, thrust from walls and ceiling. At the edges an open metal lattice crossed above another field of points rising from below. The central stone island held the fixed seat, the wrist and ankle rings, the gutter and the black stain left in its seams. At the foot of the seat, a narrow break remained in the dried blood.\par

The wardens carried me over the lattice. Their footfalls ended beneath them. Nothing returned from the wedges to tell my body where wall or ceiling stood. The inner frame stood within a second frame, divided from the Mountain by felt and an empty hand's breadth. Under our joined weight the island moved by a fraction. My body mistook the movement for a larger fall and pushed against their arms. They placed me in the fixed seat and left the rings open. Two held position while a third removed my bindings and gag. The first swallow after the cloth came out sounded close enough to belong to another person. Low light reached the island, though I could see no lamp. Behind the seat, water stood beyond a narrow grille.\par

Va-Sheva remained before me. ``You are under Shield containment by Lesser Seal. Justice has witnessed two objects in Shield custody and one record declared beneath a Light seal. Balance has entered you living. Light has received your name. You will not be questioned until Justice summons you.''\par

``You counted me.''\par

``Balance did.''\par

``What happens when I am summoned?''\par

``Justice sets the charges.''\par

``For my breach alone?''\par

``For whatever has standing.''\par

``I brought you a breach and committed one by bringing it. Enter both.''\par

``Shield will enter yours. The other must obtain standing before the five.''\par

``Then I will ask Justice for it.''\par

She looked at the open rings beside my hands. ``You may have made your death more orderly.''\par

``I know. I did not divide the proof so judgment would spare me.''\par

``Why, then?''\par

``So judgment could not stop at me.''\par

Va-Sheva went to the invisible seam. At it she said, ``\textit{Kay}, I am so sorry.'' She turned before I could answer, and the door sealed behind her without a visible line.\par

Then the silence the room was built for closed over me. The last of her went out of the stone, and after it came nothing: no corridor, no water, no far prayer, no breath of the vents. Into the empty place my own body rose. The knock of my heart came first, too loud. Then a thin sea-sound beneath it that was my own blood. Then the dry creak of a joint as I turned my head. With no sound to set me in the room, the floor let go of its certainty, and I put a hand to the felt to keep my feet. I said my name once to prove that speech still existed. It returned from no wall; it remained inside my teeth. I did not speak again. Until Justice summoned me, I would wait here --- with the knock, and the sea, and the creak, and no other company at all.\par
