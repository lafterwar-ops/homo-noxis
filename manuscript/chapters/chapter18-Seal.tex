\typesetchapter{Chapter 18}{Seal}

They convened the five in the sealed hearing-chamber below the Hall, the round low room with the single lamp and the five seats cut into the living rock that I had never in my life expected to see, because a Veil does not see it; a Veil waits outside the door of it while the offices decide the things too large for offices. I saw it because I was the thing being decided. They brought me in between two Shield wardens with my hands bound before me, which was law, and they brought Paul and Sarah in unbound, which was not law, which was Va-Sheva's doing, and which told me before a word was said that Shield had already chosen to spend some of its own standing to keep my two Outsiders on their feet and speaking.\par

The chamber was smaller than the fear of it. All my life the sealed hearing had been a place the size of God in my imagining, and it was in truth a round room a strong man could have crossed in ten strides, and the smallness of it was the most frightening thing in it, because it meant that everything my whole world was, and had ever been, was decided by five people sitting close enough to touch, in a room you could have walked past in the dark. The lamp between them was the old kind, the token-flame that Faith keeps, and it threw their shadows up the curved wall so that behind each seated Keeper a great dark second Keeper leaned, and the shadows shifted whenever the Mountain was struck, which was every little while, a deep flat knock coming up through the floor into the soles of my bound feet, so that the whole hearing was conducted to the slow rhythm of the very thing we were gathered to decide what to do about.\par

The five were older than the last time I had stood before any of them, and one of them I had helped to make. Ka-Raedin of Light sat in the seat under the dark aperture that is Light's, and he was my Keeper, the man whose ascension I had watched from beneath the pillar four years before I broke, the man in whose office I had served until I pulled the cord I was told to leave. He would not look at me directly, and I understood the whole of his difficulty in that one refusal: his star witness against the end of the world was the greatest heretic his office had produced in living memory, and every word I spoke in that room made him weaker in it, and he needed my words more than any of them.\par

Ka-Orun of Justice spoke first, because in that chamber Justice always speaks first and last, and everything true happens between. ``You were sent out under seal,'' he said, ``to determine whether the Enemy Within was real. The council that sent you could not lawfully agree to end you, and so it agreed to use you, and it wrote you an errand, and set its seal to it, and put you out of the Threshold with the errand in your mouth. That errand still stands. It has never been revoked, because no one believed there would ever be a finding to revoke it for.'' He let that sit. ``Make your finding.''\par

I have thought a great deal, since, about the shape of that moment. They had invented a mission to be rid of me, a lie dressed in law, a nothing-errand about an enemy no one believed in, so that my exile would have a lawful face and their consciences a lawful floor. And now I stood in the one room that could not unsay its own seal, and the enemy the errand had invented was four weeks' walk become four hundred metres, and drilling. The lie had come true. I was, by their own hand and against all their intent, exactly the thing they had pretended to make me: a Veil sent out to find the Enemy Within, returned to report that the Enemy Within was at the gate.\par

\ornament

I made my finding. I kept it plain, the way Paul had taught me without meaning to over a week of watching him refuse to reach for grandeur, and I gave them the camp: the trucks that struck the ground, the rig and the hole going down, the men dug in on the ridges facing inward, the eye in the sky that owned it. I gave them the drovers' road that Shield had let fall from memory and that the enemy had come to straight, as though handed a map. I told them the shape of the danger without yet telling them the whole of its source, because the whole of its source was standing at my shoulder in Outsider's clothes and I wanted the Mountain to fear the thing before it hated the woman.\par

They made me give it twice, and the second time they made me give it in the words of measurement, because a Keeper does not move on a story, he moves on a quantity. How many men. I did not know; hundreds, and more coming. How deep was the hole. Sarah answered that one, from where she stood, to the metre, and the number went round the five faces and altered every one of them, because it was a number with a date fastened to it, the day the hole would reach the Hall, and I watched Ka-Leth of Balance do the arithmetic behind his eyes and arrive at the date and go entirely still. What could the eye in the sky see. Everything, I told them, that was not underground. And Ka-Dhavar of Shield said, low, that this was the first war in the Mountain's whole history in which the enemy could see the top of the board while the Mountain could see only the one square it was standing on; and no one answered him, because there was no answer to be had.\par

They received it as five offices receive a thing, which is to say five different ways at once, and the disagreement between them was not weakness; it was the machine working as it was built to work, five walled minds refusing to become one mind, even now, even with the walls shaking.\par

Ka-Dhavar of Shield believed me before I finished, because Shield had felt it for a season already, in the small wrong tremors, in the outer stations that had gone silent, in the sense every good watchman has of being watched in turn. ``I have said this table was under an eye for two years,'' he said, ``and I was told I was seeing shapes in the dark, which is Shield's disease. I take no pleasure in the shapes having faces now.'' He was the only one of the five who did not seem afraid, and it was because he had done his being-afraid in private, over years, alone, while the others slept behind the seal.\par

Ka-Raedin of Light would not take my finding on my word, and he was right not to, and it cost him to be right. ``Light does not act on a witness's fear,'' he said, still not looking at me. ``Light acts on a thing seen twice, by two, and verified. This man has given us a picture. A picture is a claim. I have built my whole office on the difference between a claim and a proof, and I will not spend the Mountain on the word of'' --- and here he stopped, because there was no end to the sentence that did not wound his own office, and he chose the wound --- ``on the word of the finest mind my office ever ruined. Show me the proof, and I am with you before Justice finishes speaking. Give me only his fear, and I am the last door that holds.''\par

Ka-Leth of Balance did not argue whether the enemy was real. Balance never argues whether; Balance argues how much, and how long. ``Say it is all true,'' he said, heavy and slow. ``Then I am already counting. If Shield seals the Mountain whole, I have stores for the population for a measured time and no longer, and air that must be turned and water that must be balanced between the living reserve and the other, and every day sealed is a day spent against a total I did not choose. Sealing is not safety. Sealing is a slower arithmetic with a worse end if help does not come, and no help is coming, because there is no one to come. I need to know not whether we can shut the door. I need to know what we eat behind it, and for how long, and what we become on the last day of the stores.''\par

And Ka-Elun of Faith, who was old now, older than the woman I had watched wounded at the throat when I was a boy of fifteen, read the whole thing through the one instrument Faith trusts, which is the Mountain itself. ``The stone is answering wrong,'' she said, and her voice was the same voice that had blessed a thousand mornings, gone thin. ``I have listened to this Mountain speak for sixty years. It has one long slow sentence it says, that I was taught to hear before I was taught to read, and for a season now there has been another voice under it, striking, and the stone flinching, and answering, and I told myself it was the Mountain settling, the way we all tell ourselves that.'' She looked at me, and she knew me, I saw her know me, the boy from under the pillar. ``You heard it too. As a child. You have the ear. You always had the ear, and Light took you instead of Faith, and I have wondered since whether that was the first mistake in all of this.''\par

For a while they argued, and I stood bound between the wardens and watched the Mountain disagree with itself the way it was built to. Ka-Raedin of Light pressed me for the one thing I could not give him, a proof seen twice by two, and I told him I had brought him two Outsiders, a machine, a photograph, and the drawings taken off his own dead, and he said that a heretic, an enemy, and a device were three reasons to doubt and not one reason to believe. He was right. It was the rightness of a man watching a flood climb his step and refusing to leave his house until someone can prove to him that the water is water. Only Shield and Faith spoke as though the thing were already upon us, because theirs were the two offices with an ear turned outward; the other three still held, for a few minutes more, the luxury of debating whether the roof was truly coming down while it came.\par

It was Ka-Leth of Balance who forced the first hard thing into the open, because Balance always must. ``Say we seal,'' he said. ``Then hear what sealing is, since no one else at this table will say it and I am the office that must. We have air for the whole of the population, turned and cleaned, for a long while, but not without end. The living water is deep, and it is not endless. And the other water, the great reserve, is not ours to drink; it is ballast; it is a counterweight; and the day we broach it for thirst is the day we unbalance the very thing it was laid down to balance. I have grain and oil and salt fish against the lean years, and I know them to the sack, and I can feed five thousand souls behind a shut door for a measured time, and at the end of that time, if no help has come, and none is coming, we do not open the door onto a rescued world. We open it, if we open it at all, onto whatever the outside has become while we lay buried, or we do not open it, and we are a tomb that took a long while to fill.'' He turned his heavy face to Justice. ``I do not argue against the Seal. I tell you only that the Seal is not the safe answer and the Cataclysm the terrible one. There is no safe answer left in this Mountain tonight. There is only the choice of which terrible thing we do on purpose, with our eyes open.''\par

It was Justice who turned the room toward Sarah, because Justice had heard, in my careful finding, the shape of the thing I was walking around.\par

``You have told us what is at the gate,'' Ka-Orun said. ``You have not told us how it found the gate. A thing eleven thousand years hidden is not stumbled upon by soldiers with a drill. Someone drew them the map. And you have brought into this chamber, unbound, against every law I hold, two people of the outside, and you have set one of them at your shoulder as though her word were worth a Veil's, and I will now ask the question you have been arranging the room to keep me from asking. Who is she.''\par

I could have answered. I had it ready, the whole of it, Reggane and the blind Keeper and the photograph and the forty years. But Paul spoke first, from where he stood with the little machine still turning in his coat, and he did the thing he had come all this way to do, which was to make certain the true thing was said by the one it belonged to.\par

``She will tell it herself,'' Paul said, ``or it is worth nothing. I have spent a week learning that a thing is only proof when it comes from the mouth that lived it. Ask her. And Keeper --- when she answers, listen the way you would listen to an enemy who has decided, for her own reasons, to stop lying. That is the most dangerous kind of honest there is, and it is the only honest you are going to get from her, and it is enough.''\par

So they asked her, and she told them, and I will not set it all down again, because you have had it from her already, in a warm room by a lake, in her own cold voice. But it was a different thing to watch her say it into that chamber, to the faces of the people whose secret she had spent her life stealing, with the walls flinching around us all the while. She did not perform it. Paul and I had to draw each admission from her by name, because left to herself she would have given them the architecture and spared them the appetite, and it was the appetite they needed to believe, so we made her say the appetite. That she had found them by listening. That a Keeper of Shield had let her in, once, and shown her a shape and kept her hungry. That she had built a forty-year machine to reach into the one place in the world made to keep any hand from reaching in. That she had told herself, the whole time, that she was building a wall.\par

``Tell them why the blind Keeper let you in,'' I said, ``and do not dress it as mercy to the Mountain.'' And she said, ``He let me in because Shield needed eyes on a world that had just learned to make a sun, and I could give him those eyes, and he was blind and out of better doors.'' ``And what did you take that he did not offer.'' ``Everything he did not nail down. And one or two things he did.'' ``And when you left him,'' Paul said, quiet and relentless, the honest instrument doing its one office, ``what did you want.'' She was silent a moment, in front of the five, with the walls flinching, and then she gave them the true thing, because it was the only thing that would move them. ``More,'' she said. ``I wanted more. I have wanted the whole of it every day of my life since, and built everything I am toward the having of it, and the single reason I am on your side of this table tonight is that a hand worse than mine closed on it first.''\par

Ka-Leth of Balance made a low sound in his chest. ``You let an Outsider say the word \emph{whole} in this chamber,'' he said to Justice, ``and did not have her struck down where she stands.'' ``I let her say it,'' Ka-Orun answered, ``because she is the finding. She is the thing the Beast was made against, sitting in our hearing under guard, telling us there is now a worse one at the gate. Strike her down and we blind ourselves to the only mind that has ever mapped the mind that hunts us. Justice keeps her breathing. Justice will reckon what she is owed on the day there is a world left to owe her in.''\par

Va-Sheva spoke then, once, from where she stood along the wall, because a Veil does not speak in the sealed chamber unless an office bids her, and Ka-Dhavar of Shield bid her. ``I brought them in,'' she said. ``The breach is mine, and I will answer for it after, if there is an after. I vouch for the hands of the Outsider they call Jean-Charles; I have worked beside them, against smugglers, on our own routes, and they did not once reach for the thing they stood next to. I do not vouch for the woman. I say only this of her: that everything she has told this chamber, I have set against what Shield already knew, in the dark, for a season, and it has not failed one test. And that is worse than if it had.'' She stepped back to the stone. It was the whole of her testimony, and it was enough, because Shield does not spend words, and every soul in that room knew it.\par

``And the men at the gate,'' Ka-Orun said, when she had finished. ``Are they your hand.''\par

``They were,'' she said. ``They are the loaf that grew from my crumbs. They have my grids, my methods, my notation, and the parts of Horizon that leaked, which is all old houses do. They are not under my control, and I will not insult this chamber by pretending I came here to save you out of the goodness of a heart I have never claimed to own. I came because they took the thing from me, and because I would rather it were sealed forever under this Mountain than held by cruder men than I am, and because a woman who has spent her life making certain no one hand could hold the whole cannot, at the end of it, bear to watch the whole taken by a hand worse than her own.'' She looked at the five of them, one to the next. ``Hate me at your leisure. You do not have the leisure now. I am the only person alive who can tell you what they will do next, because it is what I would do, and we are, God help us all, the same animal.''\par

The proof Light demanded came up out of the dark while she was still speaking, carried by a Shield warden and set on the stone before Ka-Orun: the flat hard case Jean-Charles had taken off the desert, and the black instrument for speaking across distance, and the single page with the mark in its corner.\par

Paul authenticated it, because Paul was the one among us whose whole trade was whether a thing is what it claims to be, and he did it slowly and without drama, showing them the marks a forgery does not carry, the wear, the countersignatures, the small stupid true details that no one inventing a thing thinks to invent. And Sarah read the operational vocabulary aloud, the flat cold words soldiers use to keep from hearing themselves, and translated each one into what it meant, and it was the translation that turned the chamber to stone.\par

There was a cell for the drilling, she said, and a separate cell for the reading of what the drilling struck, and a cell for the men on the ridges, and a cell kept walled from all the others for the handling of what the orders called the assets, and the assets were people, and the people were us. There was a schedule for the taking. There was a protocol, a whole careful page of it, for keeping taken persons alive and lucid under questioning. And there was a line she read to them twice, in the flat voice she had used all her life to keep her own hands clean, that set aside resources for the preservation of the primary control personnel, five in number, and a second line for the securing of the central asset intact and the prevention of its actuation by the incumbent operators. ``The incumbent operators,'' she said, and let it lie in the chamber a moment. ``That is what they have called you, in a room in a language none of you will ever hear. It is the phrase a man uses for a hand he intends to lift off a machine and replace with his own.''\par

The plan did not order the Mountain destroyed. That was the whole of the horror, and Ka-Orun of Justice saw it land and made certain the room saw it land, because it was the hinge his ruling would turn on. The plan ordered the five command authorities identified and taken alive and unharmed at any cost. It ordered the central mechanism located and secured intact. It ordered local activation prevented, which meant they knew there was an activation, and feared it, and meant to hold the thing in the one state where only they could work it. They had not come to break the Mountain. They had come to own it, whole, alive, with its keepers on the ring and its terrible engine under a new hand.\par

\ornament

Ka-Orun did not rule then, though the room leaned toward it. He set both hands flat on the stone and was quiet long enough that I understood he had walked up against the one thing his office could not do.\par

``I can find the condition met,'' he said at last. ``I cannot find it met alone, and I cannot find it met by five, and you will hear why, because it is the oldest wall in this Mountain and tonight it stands in my road. To rule the Beast lawful I must weigh the whole of it at once: what the enemy is and intends, and how long the stores would hold behind a Seal, and whether the engine can still be aimed and worked at all, and the law and sequence of the firing, and the timing that Faith alone can hear. Five things. And no one of us may lawfully hold more than his own. Shield may not be told Balance's count. Balance may not be shown Light's aiming. That is not custom; it is the founding law, the very thing the Beast was built to keep --- that no single hand ever hold the whole, not even to save it. I am asked, on the last night, to make the largest ruling the record has ever held, and the law forbids me the one vantage from which it could honestly be made.''\par

``Then the law has beaten us,'' Ka-Leth said, ``over nothing, at the end.''\par

``The law has a door in it, cut for exactly this, and opened only twice before my time.'' Ka-Orun's voice did not rise. ``The Threshold Exception. When a danger from outside cannot be met without knowledge that no office within may lawfully gather, the Mountain may admit a thing across the Threshold to hold what the offices may not: not a Keeper, not a Veil, not a sixth hand upon the machine --- a witness. One who belongs to no Kahir, holds no key, turns no valve, gives no order, and may therefore be told everything precisely because he can do nothing with it. A hand may act. A witness may only record. Shield opened that door once, a lifetime past, to let an Outsider map the sky above us.'' Now he looked at Sarah, and then away. ``Tonight I open it wider than it has ever been opened. I know the price of it. I open it anyway, because the alternative is to lose the world by procedure.''\par

And then Shield did a thing I had forgotten I had made possible. Va-Sheva crossed to Paul, took his right hand, and turned the palm up to the lamp; and there, under the grime of the crossing, was a small white scar her office knew and mine had never been taught --- the Shield sign a Threshold is built to answer, that I had pricked into him under a tree in another country and named a captive's game. ``A Veil of Light set this,'' she said, and it was not a question, and there was a whole scandal folded into the flatness of her voice, for a Veil of Light has no right to the sign, and I had carried it across a compartment and out a gate and set it into an Outsider years before there was a night that needed it. She let the hand go. ``The law says the Threshold opens to a mark set into the hand,'' she said to Justice. ``It is set. Not for the tunnels --- he came in by the seam like a thief, as we all did. For this hour, and no other: the one in which the law must decide whether an outsider may be let to hold us. He has carried our last door in his palm since before he believed we were real.'' And I watched Ka-Orun look from the scar to me, and understand that the Exception he had meant to force open by his own will had been answered once already, quietly, years ahead of him, by the one man among us who had never learned to leave a line uncrossed.\par

He turned to Paul, and the whole chamber turned with him. ``You. You belong to no office of mine; you hold no lever of this place and never shall; and you have spent a week doing the one thing my record was built to do and does badly, which is to take accounts that will not touch and lay them side by side until the truth stands up between them. I will have each of my Keepers tell you, alone, the one thing his office may tell no other. You will hold all five. You will tell me how they fit. And by your holding --- not by mine, for mine is forbidden --- I will be able to rule. You will act on none of it. You will decide nothing. You will carry the whole across the space between us, because you are the single creature in this room the law permits to carry it. Will you take it?''\par

Paul was quiet a moment --- the man who had come to a house by a lake to check whether one impossible person was real, asked now to become the only permitted memory of an impossible world. ``I will hold it,'' he said. ``I will touch nothing. But hear what you are making, Keeper, before you make it. The instant I hold your five, I am the most dangerous object in this Mountain, because I will be the only one in it who holds the whole --- the exact thing you have killed men for eleven thousand years to prevent. You are trusting the wall you built against your own hands to hold against a stranger's, on the night you can least afford to be wrong about it.'' ``I know what I am making,'' Ka-Orun said. ``I am making the thing my office was always going to need and was always forbidden to keep. Do it anyway.''\par

So they deposed to him, one at a time, the way the record takes a witness, in the flinching dark, and Paul held the small machine low and did not once look up. Shield gave him the enemy: the disposition above, the ring, the hour it would close. Balance gave him the count: the stores, the two waters, the last day of each. Light --- Ka-Raedin, who an hour before would spend nothing on my word --- gave him the deep surfaces, and whether the thing could still be aimed true through the roots of the world. Justice gave him the law of the firing, and the one abort, and the stroke past which there is no abort. And Ka-Elun of Faith, who was still alive then, and had perhaps a hundred breaths left in her though none of us knew it, gave him the last fragment and the strangest: the timing, the phase, the way the returning stroke must be heard and answered, the note beneath the note --- the whole of what Faith alone had ever been trusted to carry, set into the ear of an Outsider because there was, that night, no other ear the law would suffer it into. She gave it plainly, the way you hand a heavy thing to someone before your own arms fail. And Paul held all five, and the machine turned, and for the length of one held breath the thing the whole Mountain was built to make impossible existed in that room, lawfully, in the mind of a man who could do nothing with it but remember.\par

Then Ka-Orun asked him the one question --- how the five fit --- and Paul answered it in the flat plain voice he kept for true things, and the shape the five made together was a single sentence, and the sentence was that there was no road left out of that room that was not the Beast. Only then did Justice rule.\par

``There it is,'' Ka-Orun said, into the silence, and his voice had changed; it had the flatness a voice takes on when it has stopped weighing and begun to rule. ``Hear the distinction, all of you, because everything now turns on it, and Justice will state it once. An enemy who comes to destroy the Mountain is a thing Shield may fight. We have always been ready to be destroyed; the Book is half made of it; a Mountain that falls keeps its secret in the falling. But this enemy has not come to destroy us. This enemy has come to possess the whole. To take the five, and the mechanism, and the key, and to hold in a single hand the thing this entire world was built across eleven thousand years to make certain no single hand could ever hold. And an enemy who comes to possess the whole cannot be fought, and cannot be endured, and above all cannot be allowed to leave. There is only one lawful answer to a hand closing on the whole. It is the answer the Beast was made to be.''\par

Sa-Tavan of Justice, who had set my exile into the record with the same hand, took up the clay and set this down too, every word of it, the finding and the distinction and the condition, pressing the marks into the wet tablet that Justice keeps because Justice trusts no memory living, not even its own. And I stood bound and watched a man I had known write out, calmly, in a good and unhurried hand, the lawful reasons for the ending of the world, and I saw the whole of my people in that one sight. That was the thing they had truly built, under the weapon and behind it: not a bomb only, but a way for five decent people to do the one unforgivable thing in good order, by law, with the reasons filed and countersigned, so that no hand that did it would ever have to feel that it had chosen it. We had made the end of the world into an act of obedience. It was the most careful thing our whole careful civilisation had ever made, and I have never decided whether it was the summit of us or the proof that we should never have been trusted with a Mountain at all.\par

\ornament

The charge that killed Ka-Elun came while Justice was still speaking, and I have wondered ever since whether the enemy timed it, whether some reader in that camp had found an acoustic road into the Hall and fired down it precisely then, or whether it was only the blind arithmetic of the drilling arriving where it had always been going to arrive. I will never know. What I know is that there was a blow unlike the others, sharper and nearer and up, and a sound came down the Faith passage into the chamber that was not stone grinding on stone but stone letting go of stone along a line cut true a hundred generations before any of us, and then the roof of the passage above the old woman's seat came down.\par

Shield was moving before the dust, Va-Sheva first, throwing herself and two wardens at the collapse, and I was moving too, my bound hands useless, and we got stone off her, and it was not enough. Ka-Elun of Faith, who had blessed a thousand mornings, who had risen wounded at the throat before a boy who never forgot it, who had the ear that heard the enemy's voice under the Mountain's own, lay in the ruin of the road to her own Hall, and she was not afraid, and she looked for me in the dust, and found me, and I put my bound hands against the side of her face, and she said, so low that only I and the machine in Paul's coat could have caught it, ``You should have been Faith.'' And then she was gone, and the office of Faith went partway with her, because the Veil of Faith, who had been at her side, was dragged living from the same stone with the hearing gone out of both ears and his skull broken and his mind sunk somewhere it did not surface from in time, and there was, in that hour, in the whole Mountain, no one left who could do the thing Faith does, which is to listen to the stone and know the note and say it back.\par

I had watched Ka-Elun rise into her office when I was fifteen and believed my brightness made me good. I watched her leave it under a fallen roof with the outside pouring in through the wound. The Mountain had shown me its whole arc in one woman, and I had been a boy at the start of it and a bound heretic at the end, and both times I had been able to do nothing but stand under the weight of it and witness.\par

And Ka-Raedin of Light, who an hour before had asked me for a thing seen twice by two, stood in the dust of the fallen passage and looked up at the outside light coming down grey through the wound torn in his Mountain's roof, and said, to no one, in a voice with the whole of the office gone out of it, ``There is my second witness.'' He did not ask for another after that. The Mountain had proved the finding by breaking, and it had spent Faith to do it, and the price of the proof he had demanded was the one office none of the five could do without.\par

\ornament

Justice did not stop for grief. Justice cannot; that is its cruelty and its mercy both.\par

``Faith is down,'' Ka-Orun said, when the dust had thinned and the old woman had been covered. ``Hear what that means, and I will speak plainly because there is no longer time for the long forms. The Mountain is found. The enemy intends not our death but our possession. The five are named and hunted as a single system to be captured entire. Faith, the office that listens, is struck out of the five at the very hour we need it most, and cannot be restored in the time we have. Ordinary defence cannot give us back our secret; a secret once found by a state is not made unfound by killing the men in one desert, for the state remains, and the knowledge remains, and it will come again, and again, with better drills, until it has us. There is no wall we can raise that returns us to the world we had this morning, which was a world that did not know we were here.'' He rose, which in that chamber Justice does only once. ``Therefore I find the condition met. The whole is threatened with capture by a single hand. The lawful answer is the Cataclysm, and I rule it lawful, and I set the record of my ruling in Sa-Tavan's tablet, that whoever survives the world may know it was done by law and not by panic. Shield. Raise the Greater Seal.''\par

And Ka-Dhavar of Shield, who had believed longest and feared longest and taken no pleasure in being right, bowed his head once and gave the order that closes a world.\par

\ornament

I had never seen the Greater Seal and I pray the earth I never see its like again. It is not a door. It is the Mountain deciding to become solid, gates dropping in sequence down every route from the deep Hall to the far Threshold, each one a slab the height of three men falling into a throat cut to hold it, the sound of them going down running away from us into the dark like a giant walking away and pulling the world shut behind him. Shield had drilled it for eleven thousand years and never done it in earnest, and the wardens moved through it white and perfect, and Va-Sheva stood at the centre of the sequence calling the gates down in an order she had learned before she could read, and it was the most terrible competence I have ever watched, and I loved her for it, and I have never told anyone that until now.\par

And below us, through the closing galleries, the Mountain that did not know was learning. You cannot drop the gates of the Greater Seal in a living city and keep it quiet; the sound of it is the sound of the world's largest door, said over and over, coming nearer, and the people heard it, and did the things people do. Some ran toward the deep wards where the drills, the drills everyone had half-believed were only drills, had taught them to shelter. Some ran the wrong way, toward gates already down, and beat their hands against the cold slabs, and were gathered up by wardens and turned back weeping. A bell that had rung nothing but the hours for a thousand years began to ring a thing that was not the hours. And under all of it, in the common wards where the evening bread had been rising an hour before, five thousand people were coming to understand, in whatever words their teaching had given them, that the thing at the bottom of every prayer they had ever been made to say was no longer a story told to frighten children into obedience. The Beast was not a warning any more. It was a decision, and it had been taken, over their heads, in a sealed room, by five tired people, one of whom was already dead.\par

There was one gate they held. The far one, the last before the outer folds, and they held it past the moment the sequence called for it, and I did not understand why until I saw Jean-Charles come through it at a dead run out of the closing dark, alone, having gone back out into the breaking desert to do the last thing his network could do against the seizure cell and having cut it too fine on purpose, because that is the kind of man he is. He came through with a hand's breadth to spare and the great slab came down behind him and sealed on the desert and on the enemy and on the sky, and he stood there in the sudden stone silence with the dust settling on him and looked at me, and did not smile, and said, ``I am the last thing that will ever come in from out there. Whatever we are now, we are all of it there is going to be.''\par

Va-Sheva did not embrace him, because there was no time and because it was not the way of either of them, but she laid her hand flat on his chest for the length of one breath, over the heart, the way Shield counts a living man, and said, ``Then you are Mountain now, until you die. There is nothing else left to be.'' And Jean-Charles, who had bought sweets for a grieving woman and carried a heretic out under a cloth and turned a network against soldiers and come in beneath a falling gate, took that the way a man takes an office he did not ask for and cannot give back, and nodded once, and it was done.\par

Only Sarah, of all of us, could not look at the two of them, and I did not understand why until long after; she alone in that chamber already knew whose hand had unlatched the door the enemy had walked through, and she let Va-Sheva name him Mountain regardless, because there was a Mountain to save that night and no room left in it yet for what the naming would one day cost.\par

\ornament

We went up, after, to where the Hall opens and the Pendulum still swung, because there is one place in the Mountain from which you can see the last of the day, a high slot cut for the Festival light, and the offices went to it without speaking, the survivors of them, to watch the thing they had just chosen to leave.\par

The Seal had closed every route but not yet the crown, and through the crown, far up, there was still a line of the outside, a thin bright wound of evening sky over the desert, the last of the world we were built to hide from and had, this once, in the end, been found by. And as we watched, Shield closed the crown. It came across slow, a shield of stone drawn over the last slot the way you draw a lid, and the line of sky narrowed, and narrowed, gold going to a thread, a thread going to a thought of gold, and then the stone met the stone and there was no more outside at all, anywhere, for any of us, and there would not be for ninety days, and the Pendulum swung on through the dark exactly as it had swung that morning, patient, indifferent, drawing its slow circle on the floor as though nothing had happened, as though five thousand people had not just been sealed alive inside a bomb with the men who were about to fire it, as though the world above were not, even now, being made ready to end.\par

Paul's machine was still turning. I could hear it in the new and total silence, the small patient turning of the reel, the one outside thing we had sealed in here with us, drawing faithfully down onto its spool the sound of a Pendulum and five thousand held breaths and one bound heretic's, keeping the record of the first hour of the end for a reader we had no reason left to believe would ever exist. I found that I was glad of it. If we were to be the last of it all, I wanted there to be a witness, even a blind and turning one, even here, even now, even if the only eyes that ever read it were our own in the dark.\par
