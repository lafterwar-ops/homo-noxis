\typesetchapter{Chapter 6}{Festival}

\begin{room}

--- The twenty-fifth of November; quarter to nine; the fifth of our interviews. Shall we?

--- Not yet. No question has come to me worth the tape, and I should like to wait until your \textit{Civilisation} is finished before I put one.

--- The instruction and the physical training do not resume until tomorrow. We have the entire day.

--- Then let me put a smaller one. Who chooses them? The creatures; then the heavens; then the climb of your kind; and now your cathedrals. Someone set those in that order. A tutor does not build a week; he teaches the hour he is handed.

--- \ldots{} That is not mine to say.

--- No. But you have just said it. You asked me once about the Genesis gravure. Today your question is answered, in its proper place.

\end{room}

I kept arranging the five lessons. Reflection, shadow, focus, turning, threshold return --- I carried them everywhere; the order felt like a path.\par

Weeks passed. The lessons stopped. The season went on, and instruction became ordinary again, which I found almost insulting. Lamps were cleaned, mirrors adjusted, wicks trimmed, brackets inspected, old smoke scraped from the high vents. Twice I spent a whole morning learning how an unclean mirror could betray a sealed door by scattering what it should have returned whole.\par

``Boredom is useful,'' Va-Raedin said on the third such morning.\par

When no lesson was given, I searched where searching could be disguised as service. I studied the great gravure on the southern wall until it blurred --- Adam, Eve, the five children, the Mountain, the Oasis, the forest, the rivers, the desert Threshold. I watched how the lamps struck the carved hand of God differently at morning and at evening. I counted where shadows gathered and failed to move. I stood in the public tiers and tried to decide whether any line from the Light alcove might pass through Faith's bronze rail and down into the lower stone. The geometry refused me.\par

\ornament

Then Ka-Syphiron of Light summoned me.\par

The summons came through Va-Raedin at the end of a morning exercise so plain I had nearly fallen asleep standing. The other Candidates were dismissed; their footsteps withdrew into the northern passage; Va-Raedin waited until the room had emptied.\par

``Ka-Syphiron will receive you before the second meal.''\par

The Keeper of Light received me in a room with no view and almost no object: a low table, an unlit lamp, three polished stones, one chair for him and none for me. He stood when I entered.\par

He had the beauty of something preserved past kindness. Age had narrowed him without weakening the impression of design --- white hair, fine hands, a face calm enough to make feeling seem a defect in other people. He wore no ceremonial robe, only pale cloth with a thread of gold at the throat, and he looked more like the Keeper there than he ever had in the Hall.\par

``Sa-Kailan of Light,'' he said.\par

``Keeper.''\par

``Va-Raedin tells me you are quick.''\par

I wavered between accepting the word and defending against it. ``I try to learn.''\par

``Trying is common. Learning is less so. Understanding is rarer. Wisdom is rarer still.''\par

``Yes, Keeper.''\par

He gestured to a stone set before him --- to stand on, a place chosen for me by another man. I stepped onto it.\par

``Tell me what a Veil of Light is.''\par

The answer came easily, and therefore badly. ``A Veil learns who placed the light. And possibly, learns to measure a substance by its effect upon Light.''\par

``That is doctrine. I asked what a Veil is.''\par

I hesitated. ``The Keeper's successor.''\par

``Sometimes.''\par

``The one permitted deeper Light knowledge.''\par

``Too vague.''\par

``The one who has crossed beyond Candidate instruction.''\par

``By what act?''\par

I looked at the unlit lamp on the table. I already had the answer, and had forgotten it. ``By finding the Veil's Chamber.''\par

``Yes.''\par

The plainness of the word went into me like cold water.\par

``The title is not given for cleverness,'' he said. ``Not for age, not for loyalty, not for hunger, not for the admiration of one's teacher, not for the impatience of one's own mind. A Candidate of Light becomes Veil of Light by finding the Veil's Chamber. Until then he is Candidate, whatever he believes he has understood.''\par

I felt the words arrange themselves around me like bars. ``And if he never finds it?''\par

``Then he never becomes Veil.''\par

``Even if the Keeper dies?''\par

``The Mountain does not owe a man the office he failed to reach.''\par

I thought of Va-Raedin. He had found the chamber long ago; of course he had; that was what being Veil meant. But had Ka-Syphiron guided him there, or withheld from him the same mercy now being withheld from me? Had he stood on this stone once, in front of another Keeper, young, hiding his hunger?\par

``Does the Chamber teach the next path?'' I asked.\par

``You are not yet entitled to next paths.''\par

He crossed to the table and touched the unlit lamp, unhurried, the way a man tests a familiar instrument. ``You have the flaw of many gifted candidates,'' he said, and he said it almost warmly, as though it were a quality he had collected before. ``You think a locked door is an insult addressed personally to you. A door has no opinion of you, Sa-Kailan of Light; it was hung before you were named. Sometimes a locked door is only a mercy you have not yet earned the right to resent --- and sometimes it is a very patient kind of grave. From the outside the two look identical.''\par

``Look at me.'' I looked. ``You are seen,'' he said. ``Not celebrated. Seen. There is a difference. Va-Raedin sees speed in you. I see appetite. Speed may serve Light. Appetite must be disciplined before it convinces itself that hunger is revelation.''\par

The younger brother from the Book of Blood came into my mind without my calling him --- small in body, cunning in heart, watching a strength that others loved until \textit{envy entered him as a serpent entereth the grass}. The old story had become too useful against me.\par

``You may go,'' he said.\par

\ornament

And that was all. No clue, no riddle, no test. Only the rule, set down where I could no longer pretend ignorance of it: a Candidate of Light became Veil by finding the Veil's Chamber. I walked out worse off than I had walked in. He had done more than refuse me the way. He had closed every posture I had left: now that I knew the single law of my life and had been denied the keeping of it, stillness would convict me of the failure he had described, and motion would convict me of the appetite he had named. \par

I tried three times to follow Va-Raedin, and failed three times, each worse than the last. The first time he stopped without turning and told me I was making noise with my left sandal. The second, I trailed a glimpse of pale robe through two corridors and discovered, after twenty breathless minutes, that I had been stalking a hanging cloth left near a vent to dry. The third cured me. I waited near the old Light gallery where I had seen the five markings, certain he would return there after evening instruction, and held still until my legs ached and I was proud of my patience. Then a junior attendant walked in, handed me a folded note, and left without a word. The note read: \textit{A hidden watcher should not cast such a visible shadow.} After that I stopped following Va-Raedin.\par

For several days I stayed away from the Hall of Ceremony, to spare myself the admission that I went there to search rather than pray. Then I went anyway. The chamber received me with indifference; one restless Candidate weighed nothing against stone laid down before my great-grandfathers were named.\par

I began with the architecture and failed. Then I turned to scripture. Faith had preserved the oldest words. Faith kept chambers barred to Light, or claimed to. Faith's rites opened doors no lamp could open.\par

Ka-Elun of Faith was too high and too newly enthroned. I requested audience with Va-Maera of Faith instead, through the public petition line beside the Hall of Ceremony. She was older than Ka-Elun by several years, which made her position strange to anyone outside Faith and ordinary to everyone within it.\par

She received me in a narrow chamber outside the Hall of Prayers. \par

The room held no ornament but a covered bowl of water and a hanging cord of five knots. No lamp showed, yet the light came softly from somewhere above. She stood the whole time. She wore white, a heavier white than the attendants, layered and severe, her hair fully covered, her face unpainted, her eyes dark and steady. I had expected devotion and found administration sharpened by holiness.\par

``You request access to the Hall of Prayers,'' she said.\par

``Yes, Veil.''\par

``Why?''\par

``To study the Covenant.''\par

``The Covenant is read publicly.''\par

``Not all of it.''\par

``No.''\par

``I am Candidate of Light.''\par

``I see your robe.''\par

``There may be passages concerning Light that are not read in public.''\par

``There are passages concerning many things that are not read in public.''\par

``I seek the Veil's Chamber.''\par

The words sounded too naked the moment they were out. She kept her face still. ``Then seek it in Light.''\par

I had prepared careful sentences. In the corridors they had seemed subtle. ``Faith works closely with Light,'' I said. ``In ceremony. In mist. In timing. In the way certain signs are made visible. I have seen enough to know that what people call miracle may depend on arrangements not everyone understands.''\par

The air changed. The room became suddenly smaller, and I understood that I had misjudged the distance between allusion and threat.\par

She took one step toward me. ``Say that again.''\par

I kept silent.\par

``Good,'' she said. ``Some doors stay closed because the fool outside them still has one living instinct.''\par

``I did not mean---''\par

``You meant enough. You come to Faith wearing Light's new hunger, and because you have seen mist in a prayer hall, because a lamp and a valve once agreed in front of your clever eyes, you think you stand above the people who kneel? You think you have found a crack in Faith? You have found a crack in yourself.''\par

``I was asking for access,'' I said, and the sentence failed while I was still speaking it.\par

``No. You were suggesting that Faith should fear what you might know. Listen to me carefully, Sa-Kailan of Light. Such suggestions do not endanger Faith. They endanger you first. If danger follows those words, it will find your door long before it reaches mine.''\par

I felt cold under my robe. She came closer.\par

``You have learned small tricks of perception and mistaken them for height. Mist is not Faith. Timing is not Faith. A hidden channel is not Faith. Do you think the sacred is diminished because it consents to pass through matter? Do you think God is disproved by a valve? You are not the first bright child to discover that blessing may have a mechanism. You may be the first foolish enough to speak of it as leverage in a house of prayer.''\par

``I was not threatening.''\par

``No. You were too clumsy to threaten properly. That does not make you innocent.''\par

I lowered my eyes. I could feel my pulse in my throat. Her voice softened.\par

``We speak with archangels of obedience and demons of appetite. We keep names that may not be spoken where children sleep. We know prayers for birth, madness, treason, plague, thirst, deformity, despair, and the moment before a condemned man understands that mercy will not interrupt justice. We bless marriages and executions. We comfort mothers whose children have been given to roles that will consume them. We keep fasts that would break your pride before your body. We know punishments your little training rooms have never heard of, and consolations beyond your imagining. And you come to me with mist?''\par

I stayed where I stood.\par

``Look at me. You understand almost nothing,'' she said. ``That is not your sin. Your sin is that you have begun to suspect it and decided to compensate by becoming dangerous. You are making a chain of mistakes. If you continue, you will not become a god of hidden knowledge. You will become an example, spoken of carefully to other gifted boys.''\par

The terror was that she had made consequence feel larger than Shield, or Justice, or Ka-Elun of Faith. I believed, suddenly and completely, that Faith could destroy me without touching me --- that it could name my hunger wrongly in the right chamber, set my words in the wrong order before Justice, turn my discoveries into contagion and my cleverness into evidence and my ambition into a spiritual deformity. Whether any of that was true mattered nothing. It was true enough in the room.\par

I folded. ``I withdraw the request,'' I said.\par

``Of course you do.''\par

``I apologise, Veil.''\par

``No. You submit. Apology is not required. Apology is what people offer when they still hope to manage the injury.''\par

I could barely speak. ``I submit.''\par

She stepped back. ``You may listen in the Hall of Ceremony like any child, elder, worker, mourner, or sinner. You may hear what is read in public. You may pray where prayer is permitted. You may not enter the Hall of Prayers. You may not request private scripture. You may not speak again of Faith's arrangements unless Faith first asks you what you think you have seen.''\par

``Yes, Veil.''\par

``And Sa-Kailan?''\par

``Yes?''\par

``If you truly seek Light, learn first that not every brightness belongs to you.''\par

She dismissed me with a movement of two fingers.\par

I left that chamber shaking, and hated the shaking only after I had gone three corridors and found that no one had followed.\par

At first shame walked with me. I imagined Va-Sheva of Shield laughing, though I knew better. Worst of all I imagined Ka-Syphiron of Light looking at me with calm recognition, as though appetite had behaved exactly as he had predicted. But the Hall was public, and public devotion protected me from suspicion. My kind approved of anyone who drew nearer to God. An old woman touched my sleeve one morning and told me my mother must be proud. A Faith attendant nodded to me before a water thanksgiving. Children slid aside on the benches, because a Candidate of Light sitting through recitation looked serious rather than desperate. \par

Months passed that way. I heard ration prayers, birth blessings, judgement remembrances, harvest petitions, mourning chants, water thanks, lamp rites, and fragments of the Covenant repeated until repetition became a kind of excavation. Lines that had once seemed ornamental began to catch on hidden hooks in my mind.\par

Va-Sheva found me once in that season, in a corridor near the washing pools, and I was so glad of it that I had answered every question she asked before it occurred to me to wonder why Shield wanted a Candidate's account of who came and went from Faith's chambers. She thanked me. She said I had grown thin, and that I should eat, and that I had always been the quickest of us; and she was gone before the pleasure of it had finished arriving. I spent the remainder of that day composing what I would say to her when next we met. That season kept us apart.\par

The Book of Genesis came often in those months --- sometimes in fragments, sometimes nearly whole, and once, during the long preparation for the seasonal Festival, in full. Ka-Elun of Faith read it herself that day from beneath the central gravure. Her Keeper's veil fell cleanly around her face, covering the pilgrim scars that had once made me stare. The Hall was full, short of ceremonial-full; workers had been released in shifts, children brought in teaching groups, Balance clerks seated with tablets because even long scripture had to be scheduled against labour. I sat on the northern tier and listened the way a man searches a wall for a seam.\par

She began.\par

And the evening and the morning were the first day.

And God made the sky above, and the ground beneath, and the pit below the ground: and it was so.

\begin{scripture}

And God called the spring in the shadow of the Mountain Oasis: and God saw that it was good.

And God said unto the living, The Mountain is refuge, and the Oasis is water, and the forest is plenty, and the rivers are life; but beyond the forest thou shalt not wander, for beyond the forest lieth thirst, and the eye of Heaven, and the forgetting of My shadow.

The Hall answered with bowed heads. I remained still.\par

--- May I interrupt now? You said before not to interrupt scripture.

--- Yes. That is the whole of the first book of the Covenant, the Book of Genesis. And it is the whole of the Genesis gravure also, which you asked me of on the first morning: those same five days, cut into the southern wall in stone instead of spoken.

--- One moment please, let me fetch a book from the library.

\centerline{[\,---\,]}

--- Here, look.

\centerline{[\,---\,]}

--- You cannot read it, for it is in my tongue, English. But let me say it aloud in your tongue:

\begin{scripture}

In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.
And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.
And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.
And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness.

--- It starts almost exactly the same, but goes on for much longer. How many of these thin pages are there in your book? 

--- More than a thousand.

--- We only have \textit{The Five Books of the Covenant}, and whatever Faith keeps hidden in its chambers. Could you have it translated, just the \textit{Genesis} part? I should like to compare the texts.

--- This, in truth, would be only chapter one of \textit{Genesis}, which contains fifty. That one chapter I could translate myself, and give you when next we meet. Let us continue.

\end{room}

I had heard those lines before, scattered across childhood and ceremony, but never with that much attention. In childhood Genesis had been the first story, and first stories are too large to be questioned, because they arrive before questions. I searched the reading for the Veil's Chamber and found nothing useful.\par

More weeks passed. I learned that scripture keeps its locks however often it is heard; it grows more layered.\par

No one troubled a Candidate who sat through readings with lowered eyes. Faith approved of me; Balance approved of anyone who sat still without consuming resources; even Va-Raedin, if he knew, gave no sign. Perhaps that was the correction.\par

The five lessons stayed inside me like a sequence waiting for a location. Reflection, shadow, focus, turning, threshold return. The first three I saw everywhere; the Hall was full of them --- the gravure that accepted certain lamps and refused others, bronze rails that caught faces at the edge of sight, shadows in the upper vaults that hid seams and vents and old repairs and once, to my brief excitement, a service niche that led only to a soot chamber. But turning and threshold return stood outside ordinary prayer. They needed substance, angle, and precision: water placed where light had a reason to enter, a beam disciplined enough to be bent, a surface struck at exactly the right condition. The Hall had water in basins and bowls and ritual vessels, and none of it sat in the path of a serious beam. \par

Then came the Festival of Equal Light.\par

It fell in the season when day and night were said to stand before Justice and neither could accuse the other of theft. Balance used it to review the stores --- seed, water, births, deaths, illness, labour lost, animals increased, what could be spared for planting. Faith called it older than accounting: the day on which God's first division of light from darkness was remembered without fear of either. Shield treated the gathering as a density problem --- too many bodies in one Hall, too many exits, too much attention turned toward one place.\par

Light prepared the Hall that season in ways I had never been allowed to watch so closely. The upper apertures were cleaned with white cord. The polished inserts in the central gravure were uncovered and wiped with cloths counted before use and after. The lamps were lowered. The bronze lips beneath the carved waters were checked by two attendants and sealed again. Three old vents high above the gravure were opened under Justice's witness and marked in the record at once.\par

The hidden people spoke of the heavens in their own way. But they knew the seasons, because the seasons measured survival. The Festival was order.\par

\ornament

The Hall filled before the Third Measure. I sat on the eastern tier, where I had sat for my Ascension, though I no longer felt like the boy who had stood there with a new bandage and a new name. The tiers packed shoulder to shoulder, and the Hall grew warm with the heat of a thousand bodies long before a lamp was touched. A child fretted at the crush somewhere below me until a hand closed over its mouth; the worker crowded against my side still smelled of the tallow of the shift that had let him go, and shifted from one bare foot to the other through the wait. Va-Raedin stood below with the attendants of Light. Ka-Syphiron sat in the eastern alcove, remote and pale, his face unreadable above folded hands. Ka-Elun of Faith stood beneath the central gravure. Balance had set baskets of seed and preserved fruit and sealed jars before it; Faith had laid the Genesis bark beside the festival prayers; Shield held the upper doors; Justice sat with its tablets open.\par

The lamps were dimmed one by one. A murmur passed through the Hall --- anticipation disciplined by rite. Ka-Elun lifted both hands. \par

\begin{scripture}
God who divided the light from the darkness, measure us.
\end{scripture}

The Hall answered. ``Measure us in mercy.''\par

\begin{scripture}
God who set Earth between Heaven and Hell, steady us.
\end{scripture}

``Steady us in shadow.''\par

\begin{scripture}
God who hid the spring beneath the Mountain's hand, keep us.
\end{scripture}

``Keep us from wandering.''\par

The incantation continued, carried in a rhythm that made breath part of the architecture. Faith named the first day and the second, the waters beneath, the Mountain raised, the Oasis hidden, the forest of plenty, the desert Threshold, Adam and Eve, and the five children in the first shadow. I listened only partly.\par

Something in Light's preparations had begun to disturb me. The dimmed lamps. The unsealed upper aperture. The polished fragments inside the gravure. The old vents opened under witness. A ceremony about equal light, held in a Hall built to receive controlled darkness. \par

``\textit{Let there be light,}'' Ka-Elun of Faith said --- speaking the words now rather than reading them, as though the hour answered her voice rather than the turning sky. And at the Third hour, the Measure, the Mountain's noon, the sun entered.\par

At first it was only a pale line high on the wall above the central gravure, so thin I almost missed it. I turned to follow where it fell. It crossed the Hall and touched the carved division between Day and Night on the \textit{pillar of Light}. Ka-Elun of Faith went on speaking. The line brightened. And where it struck the pillar, a mirror I had never once seen took the beam and sent it straight down.\par

My breath stopped. A mirror. It was the \textit{first shadow}.\par

My mind arranged the five lessons so fast it frightened me. Reflection, shadow, focus, turning, threshold return. The Light from Heaven. A fixed beam. A rare day. A public rite. A Festival of Equal Light. Heaven itself had set the angle. Heaven held the source. The path showed itself on the right day alone, however clever the searcher. Months of failure had proved my timing wrong, and nothing more. \par

Unless one waited for the Festival. Unless one chased the beam. Now.\par

Ka-Elun's voice went on above me, solemn and beautiful, and I barely heard it. Va-Maera's warning returned --- \textit{not every brightness belongs to you}. Ka-Syphiron's rule returned --- the Chamber must be found. Va-Raedin's prohibitions returned --- no salt, no vessels, no threshold return outside instruction.\par

I rose before I had decided to rise. No one near me noticed at first. The Hall was bowed in prayer, the lamps were dim, every eye had been trained toward holiness rather than toward one Candidate on the eastern tier moving too carefully between bodies. I stepped down once, then again, keeping to the shadow between two bench lines. I knew only that the beam went down. But for the first time in months, the Mountain had shown me where to begin.\par
