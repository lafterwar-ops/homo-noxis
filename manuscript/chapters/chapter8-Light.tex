\typesetchapter{Chapter 8}{Light}

\ornament

They had warmed the Hall of Ceremony again, and lit it the way Light lights a thing it means to say something with, which is to say sparingly, and from angles no one standing in the crowd would think to trace. There were fewer flames than at the Festival, and each had been set where it was told. Narrow shafts were thrown up against plates of polished stone beneath the northern vault and broken there into threads, and the threads laid down across the dark so that a face came out of the black here, a bowl there, the edge of the old Keeper's robe, the white knot at a wrist. Between the lit things the Hall stood deeper than I had ever seen it, as though the stone had been given back the night it was quarried from.\par

Above the northern descent the Genesis gravure held what light it was allowed. The carved Mountain kept a low bronze warmth along its ridges; the Oasis lay in green shadow beneath it; Adam and Eve were half-shown; and the five children came and went as bodies shifted below them. Over all of it, in the carved sky, stood the one small point that the children of my people learn before they learn its meaning, steady and hard, and it did not move. The eye of Heaven. It is a chip of polished stone set to catch and throw back the light, and it caught the light now, and watched.\par

I had come in beneath the northern descent with the white cloth new at my wrist, changed before dawn, the knot of it provisional and sitting too near the pulse, so that every beat of me seemed to knock against it from within. A double succession in Light, Keeper and Veil in the one morning: the Mountain kept no memory of such a thing, and it had come out to see it. The old had been carried from their beds. Children stood between their parents' knees. The lower workers came with their hair still wet from the steam chambers, and the whole shadowed weight of the people banked up the tiers and breathed as one animal breathes. I stood in my single thread of light and was, God forgive me, glad; for I had wanted this since before I owned the word for wanting, and here it was, come at last to my wrist.\par

\ornament

Ka-Syphiron came in carrying the last strong lamp, and the lesser flames became servants to it. It was a small fire, gold-white, closed behind polished ribs and a narrow throat of glass, and it chose what it would show: his fingers, the lower fold of his robe, the long bone of his own face, the silver thread at Va-Raedin's cuff. Around that little circle of granted sight the Hall deepened again, and all that lay outside it agreed to wait.\par

He turned first to Va-Raedin. Va-Raedin knelt, and knelt stiffly, the way a man kneels who has already begun to hand himself across. Ka-Syphiron set both his hands upon the bowed head. There was no softness in the gesture, and the want of it had a grandeur to it that frightened me. ``Va-Raedin of Light,'' he said, and the name went out over the stone. ``You have stood before what was hidden. You have seen, and you have not held. You have learned the first obedience of the eye. Rise no longer as Veil. Rise as Keeper. Rise as Ka-Raedin of Light.'' Va-Raedin lifted his face, and there was fear plainly in it, and he rose into the fear as a man rises into cold water. Still the old robe stayed on Ka-Syphiron's shoulders. Still the lamp stayed in his hand.\par

I marked, and could not have said why I marked it, that the flame in his hand had grown smaller. No one had touched it. No oil had been drawn off, no wick pinched.\par

Then the new Keeper turned to me, and I knelt, and the floor was cold through my robe, and my mind ran, the way a mind runs at the lip of a large thing, to small ones: a nick in the stone, the smell of old oil, the grain of my own breath. Ka-Raedin laid his hands on my head, and they were colder than the old man's had been. ``Sa-Kailan of Light,'' he said, and the name I had carried all my life came back off the walls of the Hall changed. ``You have found what was hidden from you. You have opened what was not given. You have learned that the eye may find what the hand is forbidden to hold. Do you agree to be bound?''\par

My mouth had gone dry. I had rehearsed the moment a hundred times in the dark of my own cell, and every rehearsal had carried a brightness on it that the moment itself did not have. There was only the cold weight of his hands, and the old man standing by in the robe he had not yet given up, and the whole Mountain leaning to hear whether I knew the size of the thing I was reaching for. ``I agree to be bound by it,'' I said. His fingers closed once upon my skull, too slight for any eye but mine, and he named me. ``Then rise no longer as Candidate. Rise as Veil. Rise as Va-Kailan of Light.'' I stood, and the Hall drew close about me, and every surviving lamp felt like a rule I would answer to, and every face like a witness set to see whether I would keep it.\par

Then the Hall began to lose its light, and it lost it the way a tide goes out, without haste and without mercy. Somewhere behind the crowd the feeds of the lamps were being shut, one upon another, in an order I could not see. A high slit above the northern arch went dark. A reflector lost its line and a whole thread of the Hall was gone. Two low passages closed their throats of light at once. The children in the upper tiers passed out of the light and into their parents' arms and made no sound. The gravure dimmed by degrees: the Mountain lost its outer ridges, the Oasis sank, Adam and Eve went under, and the five children held one breath longer than the rest, pale in the last slant of it, and then they too were gone.\par

Only the eye of Heaven was left, one stubborn point above the drowned Mountain. Ka-Syphiron turned the lamp in his hand, and its narrowing flame found the carved sky and touched the little point, and for one instant the eye of Heaven burned harder than it ever burns, as though it had opened wider to see what came next. Then the last hidden slit was shut. The point went black. The eye of Heaven was gone with all the rest, and there remained only the one small fire in the old man's hand keeping the whole of the Mountain from the dark, and only just.\par

Ka-Syphiron turned to the new Keeper. ``What does a Keeper learn?''\par

And Ka-Raedin, looking only at him, answered, ``What must remain dark.''\par

\ornament

Then the ceiling answered.\par

It came as a sound so low the bone took it before the ear, and dust let go from a seam I had never seen, and the last lamp shivered in Ka-Syphiron's hand though he held it high, and up in the throat of the vault the great weights loosed themselves each after each and took up their command. And I understood then, with a lurch beneath the ribs, what the wrongness had been that I had walked in under that morning and set down to grief. The Thread was gone. The Pendulum, that hangs from the top of the vault over a drop no honest eye can measure, and swings its slow crossing, and closes one round in near three days, and is one of the three clocks by which the Mountain keeps its life --- the Pendulum had been stilled. The great disk was drawn to the western wall and lashed there with its face to the stone, its wire carried aside, so that the air above the forbidden circle at the heart of the Hall stood empty, and open, and waiting.\par

Into that emptiness the Blind Stone came down.\par

It came so slowly that the mind was given time to refuse it. A roof of old black rock, and four walls beneath it, each thicker than a man is broad, all of it cut from the same deep stone as the Hall itself, descending over the two men and the one lamp. It had no floor. The Mountain was to be its floor.\par

For the last moment before it closed, the lamp found the faces of it. Standing at the corner, I could see only two of its sides; the others were there by their weight alone. On the first was cut a fortress, and its guardian faced the wrong way: walls folded within walls, gates that stood open toward the outside and sealed toward the centre, and a figure setting his shield against a door that was already within the wall. On the second, and stranger, the accused stood almost free while the judgment-seat itself was bound. A wall of records rose behind a seated figure, tablets and cords and sealed rolls cut so close together that they were texture before they were writing; and out of that written wall came a chain, and passed through it, and wrapped first around the seat, and only after it had bound the seat did it cross the floor toward the accused --- and even then it caught no more than his shadow, and left the man himself near untouched, his shadow lying chained beneath him on the ground.\par

I could read neither of them. I knew only that they were old, older than the robe, older than the Hall as I had been taught to see it, and that they had waited in the ceiling the whole of my life for this one descent.\par

The stone met the floor. The sound of it was final. The two men were gone within it, and the lamp gone with them, and the Hall went dark.\par

\ornament

There is no seeing what the Blind Stone holds. No face, no robe, no gravure, no eye of Heaven; only breath, and stone, and the knowledge that Light had gone down into a place where none could follow. I stood in it, a Veil of Light one hour old, with the whole of my people, in the dark.\par

Then, from inside the stone, cloth moved. A breath. The scrape of a knee, it may have been, or the slow fall of a heavy robe drawn off one body to be laid across another. The Hall listened until the listening had itself become a kind of trespass.\par

Then the first sound of pain. It was small. It went into every person in that Hall and it stayed there. Ka-Syphiron made no other. Once the new Keeper's voice said a thing inside the stone, and the words did not reach me, and I knew the voice only because I had spent my whole life hearing it at my shoulder and ahead of me and correcting me: Raedin's voice, turned to a stranger's.\par

Then a second sound, shorter, and the word for it came to me before I had leave to understand it, and I understood it anyway, and hated the understanding. After it, silence, long enough for a body to be arranged, and a robe to be settled, and Light to have gone all the way down into the dark with the man who had carried it.\par

Then the Mountain took the weight of the stone back into itself, and the Blind Stone began to rise.\par

We heard it before we saw anything at all. Then a seam of gold opened along the floor, from inside, and widened under the black rock, and touched the feet of those nearest and made them real again from the ground upward --- sandals, hems, hands held too tightly, faces still missing --- and then the lower edge lifted higher, and the lamp came back into the world.\par

The old Keeper's flame had crossed the dark and come up held in another man's hand.\par

Ka-Raedin stood within the returning light in the Keeper's robe, and it sat wrong upon him, the shoulders of it made for a larger man, the old folds refusing him; and along the lower rim of the lamp, where his hand closed on it, there was blood. Behind him Ka-Syphiron lay upon the stone floor of the Hall in his underrobe alone, the office gone off him and the robe gone off him, his chest still and his face uncovered and, without the office upon it, smaller than any of us had known it to be. The new Keeper's own face was white. He held the lamp in both hands, and for one heartbeat I thought the weight of the robe would carry him down. Then he did not go down. He lifted the lamp.\par

And the Hall answered it, slowly, the way an old instrument answers a new hand. A plate above the northern arch caught the flame and gave back a pale line; the line struck the lower wall and woke a second lamp; the second found the plate above the gravure, and the carving came back by degrees --- stone, Mountain, Oasis, Adam, Eve, the five children --- and last of all, when he turned the lamp a hair upon its axis, the small hard point above the carved Mountain returned, and the eye of Heaven watched again.\par

Ka-Raedin looked out over the Hall, and no one knelt, because no one had been told to kneel, and no one spoke, because speech had not yet been given back to them; and the people stood between their new Keeper and their dead one, and held still. Then his eyes found me. I wanted, in that moment, one human thing of him --- one crack in the office, one sign that he remembered the small warm foolish room behind the old gallery --- and he gave me none of it, and it was right that he gave me none.\par

``Va-Kailan of Light,'' he said. ``Come forward.''\par

I came. And as I crossed the floor the Blind Stone was climbing back into the ceiling above us, unhurried, carrying its two carvings up out of the reach of the lamp --- the fortress that guarded against its own heart going first, and the bound seat after it, and the hard edge between them last --- until the stone had gone back up into the architecture I had walked beneath the whole of my life and believed I knew, and the ceiling closed over it, and there was no seam. Only the Hall. Only the body on the floor. Only the lamp in the living hand. And the office, still standing.\par

\ornament

I do not remember being released. I remember that the northern alcove kept its light the longest, as though Light were slow to give up its own dead; and that the one strong thread of it lay across the place where Ka-Syphiron had stood and went no farther; and that when I came at last to the mouth of the northern passage and looked back, the Hall lay behind me in its ordered light, restored, and the eye of Heaven burned in the carved sky as if it had never once gone out.\par

My name had gone into the record of my people. The white cloth was lawful now at my wrist. And on the stone behind me a man lay dead who, an hour before, had set his two hands upon a boy's head and given him a title, and then gone down into the dark to make it true. I walked out through the passages that my title now opened before me, and I did not look back again.\par
