\typesetchapter{Chapter 7}{Chain}

\begin{room}

--- The twenty-seventh of March; a little after nine; the sixth of our interviews. Before your account --- did you reach the end of \textit{Odyssey}? 

--- I finished watching it, yes. A man kept twenty years from one small hard island by the sea and the wrath of a god; wandering, and remembering it, and never let to come home. He is one of mine, Paul. I will give him back to you presently, with the mark on him. But I went past him. I went down into your beginnings, and I have slept very little, and I have found a thing, and I am going to set it in front of you; and you will tell me I am reading my own book into yours; and I will tell you it is yours that will not stop reading as mine.

--- Then set it down.

--- Start with your words --- the oldest, the ones you cannot think without. You have \textit{Logos}: the word, the account, the thing spoken and kept and recited. That is Faith entire, the kept Word, in one of your bricks. You have \textit{Kratos}: might, dominion, the hand on the gate and the sword. That is Shield. You have \textit{Kairos}: the right hour, the moment a thing may move and no sooner --- that is Justice to the letter, that no motion begin before its hour. You have \textit{Ananke}: necessity, the weight that must come down somewhere, the cost that must be paid --- that is Balance, which asks only where the weight may lie without tipping the world. And you have \textit{Aletheia}. You have put it into your tongue as \emph{truth}. It means the un-hidden: less brightness than the thing fetched up out of concealment. That is Light, Paul. That is my own office, standing in your mouth, and you have named it a virtue. Your people carry my five offices folded into the words you say before breakfast, and not one of you has ever stood them in a row and looked.

--- \ldots{} Go on. You have more than words.

--- I have your beginnings, and they are worse. A god steals fire and gives it whole to men --- undivided, to every hand at once --- and for the giving of the whole of it he is chained forever to a rock, and a bird opens him each day. That is my first law and my first crime in a single story: no one hand may hold the whole, and the one who looses it is bound in the deep without end. You have a monster shut in a pit beneath a mountain, and when he strains at the chain the ground splits and the mountains throw up fire. That is not a story to me. That is a report. That is the Beast, held under my own floor, and your people felt him move. And when the woman opens her jar, and every evil in it flies out into your world, one thing stays behind, sealed, and does not leave; and you call it Hope. We call it the Promise, and we keep it under the stone. There is a flood in you as well, and a few saved high on a mountain; there is a father who devours his own children for fear of them; I could go on until the lamp burns out. Your oldest telling even keeps two of my offices coiled together at the start of the world --- Time and Necessity, my Justice and my Balance --- one body, before anyone prised them apart.

--- \ldots{}

--- Do you see it. You are holding every book of my scripture --- Genesis, Blood, Beast, Cataclysm, Promise --- scattered through your fire and your monster and your flood and your jar; and you have held it three thousand years and longer, and never once laid the pieces side by side, because you cannot, because you are cursed to keep the pieces and forget the whole. That is the curse said back to you word for word: you remember the Mountain and cannot find it; you hear the Gospel and twist it into a hundred sweet hungers. Come now, Paul. You are the Outsiders. You are the murderer's line. He went out of the Oasis with the stolen daughter of Faith, into the open land, and you are what came of them, and every tale you have ever set before me is the broken memory of the house you were driven from. I am not laying my book over yours. I have set them side by side, the way you taught me, and yours will not stop confessing.

--- \ldots{} That is the most frightening thing I have watched a man do across this table, and it was beautiful, and you are to hear both of those words from me. Now here is a third. You laid two texts side by side, and you were magnificent, and you knew which of them would win before you set them down. Every seam you read, you read so that your Mountain was the house and mine the exile, yours the origin and mine the copy, yours the kept and mine the cursed. Turn one card the other way up and the whole of it runs backward --- a late people who built a scripture out of older songs, and dressed it as the source. I will not tell you which is the truth; I do not know it. But a man who cannot bear \emph{not} to know, and so decides, and thereafter reads only the seams that prove him --- that is not a Veil reading a text, Va-Kailan. That is a frightened man raising a wall and calling it the truth. I have sat across a table from men like that before. And I had hoped not to find them in you. \ldots{} I am almost disapointed, but not surprised. Enough of it for now. You had just come out of your Veil's Chamber, and they kept a night for you. Give me that night.

\end{room}

\ornament

My reward was a small blackened room that smelled of oil and dust and old paper and mistakes. The room was low-ceilinged and warm, its walls darkened by years of lamp smoke, its shelves so crowded they seemed to survive by mutual pressure. Lenses wrapped in cloth. Brass tubes, trays of powdered glass, a cracked bowl, lengths of wire, mirror shards, sealed jars, two blackened spoons. A small hand-lamp with its flame turned downward into water. And an object that resembled the skeleton of a bird built by someone who had never seen a bird but had heard disturbing rumours of one. \par

Va-Raedin stood near the table. Ka-Syphiron sat behind it on a low stool with one uneven leg, wearing no ceremonial outer robe, his sleeves rolled to the wrist. A dark mark crossed one finger: soot, or ink, or blood long dried. He looked like a man interrupted in the middle of ruining something interesting.\par

``Close the door,'' he said. \par

I obeyed. The latch fell into place with a small sound. For several breaths nobody spoke. Ka-Syphiron placed both hands flat on the table.\par

``Sa-Kailan of Light. Yesterday, by witness of Va-Raedin, you found the Veil's Chamber.''\par

``Yes, Keeper.''\par

``You entered by lesson, error, discipline, insight, and disobedience.''\par

``Yes, Keeper.''\par

``You did not break the path behind you.''\par

``No, Keeper.''\par

``A Candidate learns where light falls. And a Veil?''\par

``A Veil learns who placed it.''\par

``That is the public version. A Veil learns who placed \textit{him}. And tonight you should see that Va-Raedin placed you there as surely as any lamp. Tell me. By what condition does a Candidate of Light become Veil of Light?''\par

``By finding the Veil's Chamber.''\par

``And by what condition does a Veil of Light become Keeper of Light?''\par

I looked at Va-Raedin. No rescue came.\par

``By finding the Keeper's Chamber.''\par

``Incomplete. A Veil who finds the Keeper's Chamber has reached the upper path. He has not completed the chain. Light does not pass upward because one man climbs; it passes when the lower chamber is opened again by one he trained. Only then is the chain complete. Faith, not long ago, had two Veils --- two who had found the Veil's Chamber --- but none had found the Chamber of the Last Prayer until recently. When Va-Elun found it, she rose at once to Keeper, because there was already a Veil of Faith beneath her to complete the chain. You know her now as Ka-Elun.''\par

I turned toward Va-Raedin.\par

``And did you find it?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``When?''\par

``Six years ago.''\par

``Six years?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``And you waited.''\par

``I served.''\par

``Waiting for me?''\par

``Waiting for the chain to complete.''\par

I looked down. I had spent one day waiting and found it almost intolerable. He had spent six years ready --- correcting boys, holding only what the Mountain permitted, waiting for a candidate worthy of becoming Veil.\par

``Va-Raedin of Light found what he was required to find,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``He trained, withheld, corrected. He endured the indignity of being ready before the Mountain was ready to receive him.'' Va-Raedin said nothing. Then the old Keeper stood. He came around the table, the uneven stool complaining behind him, and placed one hand on my shoulder. The hand was warm, dry, and surprisingly heavy.\par

``Congratulations, Sa-Kailan. You have done a difficult thing without making it unbearable to praise you. That combination is rarer than genius.''\par

I tried to answer. Nothing came but the start of a smile. He set his other hand on Va-Raedin's shoulder, and the three of us stood linked beside the table: the Keeper about to be released, the Veil who had waited, the Candidate who had opened the lower door.\par

``Congratulations, Va-Raedin.''\par

``Keeper.''\par

``No. Not Keeper. Not tonight. Tonight we celebrate.''\par

He bowed his head.\par

``Thank you.''\par

The words came so quietly the room seemed to strain to catch them. Ka-Syphiron released us both and stepped back.\par

``There. The chain has now been congratulated in all its necessary links. If Justice complains tomorrow that joy was not recorded, we shall say the failure was theirs.''\par

I laughed once. It came out broken.\par

Va-Raedin looked at me. ``The Keeper has praised you. I will not add to it --- praise is oil, and it has drowned better boys than you. You did not fail. Take that instead.''\par

``Enough,'' Ka-Syphiron announced, to no one. ``Absolutely enough. If Light spends every important moment proving it can stand still, then Shield has already won civilisation.''\par

The solemnity fled like a startled bird. He crossed to a wall panel so blackened by smoke it seemed merely dirty until his fingers found three hidden catches. It opened with the reluctant sound of secrecy. Behind it was a hoard: two dark bottles stopped with wax, a tray of small white cups, dried fruit, nuts, a round cheese, bread, strips of salted meat, and small cakes pressed with seeds.\par

He pointed a soot-marked finger at me. ``Clear the table.'' Then, to Va-Raedin: ``Ray. Those, there.''\par

\emph{Ray}, I repeated in my head.\par

``Keeper,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``Do not Keeper me. I am arranging a feast.''\par

``You are impossible.''\par

``An excellent quality in a Keeper approaching replacement.''\par

The spread grew, and it was absurd, and it was magnificent --- more food than I had ever seen set before ten people without a festival, a funeral, or an inventory mistake.
Ka-Syphiron uncorked the first bottle and set a white cup before me. The smell of hydromel rose warm and sweet into the room. Va-Raedin's expression tightened. ``He is nineteen.''\par

``An age at which a man may discover that honey, water, time, and disobedience can produce civilisation.''\par

``He is nineteen,'' Va-Raedin said again --- looking now at the tray of cups. Ka-Syphiron followed his gaze. ``Ah.''\par

``Absolutely not.''\par

``Absolutely yes. These are for your first Light lesson as Veil.''\par

I looked from one man to the other. ``What is wrong with the cups? May I see?''\par

Both men paused to look at me. Va-Raedin's face said no with the force of a sealed corridor. Ka-Syphiron's brightened. He lifted one of the small cups and set it before me.\par

It was fine bone after all, or something like it --- white, thick-sided, faintly blue at the foot, worn by many hands. At the bottom, beneath the glaze, a cloudy disc waited like a blind eye. I took it before my courage could find a more careful course. It was heavier than it looked. I tilted it toward the lamp, and the dull pale circle beneath the glaze stirred into a smear of colour, too faint to name, then vanished in the shine.\par

``A flaw,'' I said. ``Or a mark beneath the eye, perhaps.''\par

Ka-Syphiron looked at Va-Raedin. ``You trained this?''\par

``Under difficult conditions.''\par

``I see.''\par

I turned the cup again, more carefully. ``Thick wall. Curved base. The glaze distorts whatever has been set beneath it.''\par

``Better.'' I had learned to distrust that word.\par

Ka-Syphiron lifted the bottle. ``Look again.''\par

``I am looking.''\par

``And now?''\par

The hydromel poured. It climbed the white inner wall, trembled, and settled over the rounded base. A figure appeared. I worked out the optics a breath before I saw what the picture was. The smear beneath the glaze gathered itself, through liquid and curvature, into a naked woman, one arm behind her head, the other arranged with a confidence no woman of the Mountain would ever have permitted to be drawn, owned, or used as instructional material before a newly recognised Veil.\par

``Oh,'' I said. And then, uselessly, ``Oh.''\par

My face burned so fast I felt betrayed by my own blood. Ka-Syphiron struck the table with his palm. ``There.''\par

Va-Raedin actually laughed --- a rare sight. I stared at the cup, horrified, and let myself be the target of a good joke. Then I pulled myself together.\par

``Inverse turning,'' I said. ``Instead of scrambling a source, we are rebuilding one.''\par

``Spectacular, is it not?''\par

``And old,'' Va-Raedin added.\par

``Older than several current moral prohibitions.'' Ka-Syphiron raised his cup. ``To Light, and all its mysteries.''\par

We drank. The hydromel was sweet first, then hot, then sharper than I expected. I tried to hold the cough and failed, which produced another round of laughter --- and this time even Va-Raedin retreated from it too slowly.\par

Ka-Syphiron broke the bread with both hands and gave the first piece to Va-Raedin. ``To six years.''\par

Va-Raedin closed his eyes. ``No.''\par

``To six years,'' I said.\par

Va-Raedin looked at me. ``You are learning dangerous habits early.''\par

``Good,'' said Ka-Syphiron. ``A Veil of Light must learn to disappoint his teacher without becoming useless.''\par

We ate. The abundance alone felt improper. I took bread, then cheese, then one dried fig, because restraint still seemed like something I ought to display. Ka-Syphiron saw the gesture and put three more figs on my plate.\par

``This is madness,'' I said.\par

His face lit. ``Yes.''\par

``That is not a recognised category of feast,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``It should be. A truly great feast is one at which Balance has not been invited, Justice has not been informed, Shield has not inspected the door, Faith has not decided whether the songs are appropriate, and Light has hidden the cups.''\par

``Then it is not a feast. It is institutional collapse.''\par

``Only if discovered.''\par

The evening loosened. Bread broke; cheese softened near the lamp; nuts wandered across the table; the hidden woman appeared and vanished in the cups. Ka-Syphiron told a story about an attendant who had once tried to classify an obscene cup as a devotional vessel. I laughed until my stomach hurt.\par

``To Va-Raedin,'' the old Keeper said suddenly, softening despite himself. ``To readiness, properly humiliated by time.''\par

Va-Raedin held his gaze, then lifted his cup. ``Yes.''\par

Ka-Syphiron cut three uneven pieces of cheese with a knife far too ceremonial for the task. ``You understand what begins now?''\par

``The office?'' I asked.\par

``The partnership.''\par

``He will remain junior,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``Obviously. Partnership, not equality. The northern halls, candidate sequences, dangerous apparatus, cross-Kahir requests --- all of it will begin to touch him.''\par

``You will still be corrected,'' he added to me.\par

``Relentlessly,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``There. You see the burden.''\par

I laughed again, and this time Va-Raedin punished it with decorum a breath late.\par

Ka-Syphiron opened the second bottle --- which Va-Raedin allowed without approving --- and I found the courage to ask what had troubled me since the previous day.\par

``Keeper, I have a question.''\par

``Worse.''\par

``It concerns Faith.''\par

``Catastrophic. Continue.''\par

I looked at the cup, where the woman blurred beneath the hydromel.\par

``Va-Maera said she knew about what she called \textit{our little training rooms}. She had never entered them, but she knew enough to press on them. And there are other things. Their prayers for despair. Their archangels. The way they speak of voices, silences, thresholds. Va-Maera frightened me because she seemed to know where to press. As if despair had architecture. As if a prayer could be a tool. As if a voice could enter rooms where hands cannot.'' Ka-Syphiron set down the bottle and leaned back.\par

``Cross-Kahir knowledge is a dangerous appetite. Too much, too quickly, convinces a young man that the Mountain is merely a puzzle discourteously divided before his arrival.''\par

``It is not?''\par

``No. It is a wound, a machine, a refuge, a prison, a weapon, and occasionally a badly ventilated experiment room with immoral cups.''\par

He pointed a strip of salted meat at Va-Raedin. ``Ray. Do you remember what Ka-Sarem showed you when you became Veil?''\par

Va-Raedin's expression altered. ``Yes.''\par

``What?''\par

``A wheel turning inside glass.''\par

``By?''\par

``Steam.''\par

``Steam?''\par

``A sealed vessel,'' Va-Raedin said. ``Water heated beneath it. A narrow tube. A wheel so light it looked useless until vapour touched it. I thought the lesson concerned motion.''\par

``It concerned pressure,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``And restraint.''\par

``A Balance device?'' I asked.\par

``Yes,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``Most people take them for bean-counters,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``Ledgers, grain, birth allotments, death registers, goat inventories.''\par

``They are that,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``Oh, entirely. Let us not rescue them from justice. But at their heart? Chemistry. Patience applied to matter. Balance knows what heat does to milk, what sealed darkness does to grain, what pressure does to vapour, and what poison becomes before medicine, dye, solvent, or regrettable experiment.''\par

``They can help Light?''\par

``They already do,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

Ka-Syphiron lifted a sealed jar from the shelf. Inside was a powder so dark it seemed to drink the flame.\par

``Rare dyes. Glass additives. Oils. Powders that bite light at one colour and spit it back at another. Vapours that reveal a beam because the air itself has been persuaded to misbehave.''\par

``Balance makes those?''\par

``Balance prepares the matter. Light studies what matter does when a condition changes. Neither office should confuse assistance with possession.''\par

``Many compounds useful to Light,'' Va-Raedin added, ``would kill an inattentive boy before teaching him anything.''\par

Ka-Syphiron smiled. ``You see? This is why we keep attentive boys.''\par

He lifted my cup and turned it slowly, so the woman at the bottom blurred, vanished, returned beneath the hydromel.\par

``This particular object, for instance, uses an almost pure Faith principle. It recombines where Light would decompose --- though Faith would probably call it resurrection.''\par

``Generous tonight, Keeper,'' Va-Raedin said, disapproving.\par

Ka-Syphiron tapped the cup on the table. ``Faith humiliates the ear as we humiliate the eye.'' Another tap. ``Echo.'' Another. ``Delay.'' Another. ``Stone.'' The room returned each sound differently.\par

``Faith listens to stone the way we look through glass. A corridor lengthens a note; a vault divides it; a chamber stores part and returns it late. A prayer may be words addressed to God --- I do not settle that tonight --- but it may also be a shaped sound moving through stone. A woman sings here; the Mountain answers elsewhere. Faith hears the alteration and reverses it, as you reversed the image under liquid.''\par

``So their prayers are mechanisms?''\par

``No. That is the foolish version of the clever answer. Do better.''\par

I thought. ``Their mechanisms pray.''\par

His eyes brightened. ``Closer. Have you never noticed that five voices may sound louder than five voices should? Move five singers two steps, and the room seems to join them.
The air does not add like jars, Sa-Kailan. Sometimes a chamber is waiting for exactly the rhythm that makes it confess its shape.''\par

``Resonance,'' Va-Raedin said quietly.\par

Ka-Syphiron looked at him. ``Look who is generous with knowledge now, Ray.''\par

He sat back suddenly. ``Speaking of the decomposition of sound and light --- aren't you supposed to give him something?''\par

Va-Raedin became still. ``It was not for tonight.''\par

``What a miserable phrase. No wonder you are nearly Keeper.''\par

Va-Raedin ignored him, reached into the inner fold of his robe, and drew out a small object wrapped in grey cloth. I took it carefully. Inside was a triangular piece of clear glass, no longer than my thumb, polished on three faces and chipped at one corner. When I turned it toward the lamp, the flame broke faintly along one edge into a thin wound of colour.\par

``What do you call this?'' I said.\par

``A prism.''\par

``What does it do?''\par

``The \emph{what} you will have worked out by morning. The unpierced mystery is the how, and the why.''\par

``For the ceremony?''\par

``No.'' His eyes held mine. ``For after.''\par

I closed my fingers around it.\par

``When a beam seems whole,'' Va-Raedin said, ``do not believe it too quickly.''\par

Ka-Syphiron looked away too slowly to hide that the line pleased him.\par

``For teaching,'' Va-Raedin went on. ``If you remain teachable after having been praised.''\par

``That is not guaranteed,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``Pride has ruined boys more definitively than hydromel.''\par

He turned my wrist until the lamp struck the prism more narrowly.\par

``You are right to wonder about Faith. Right to be unsettled by Balance. You will be tempted by Justice. But do not become drunk on other people's locked doors. Light has enough of its own. There are office mysteries: apparatus, ceremonies, petitions, the training of the young. And there are mysteries still unpierced. Like the memory of light. A question I have spent years failing to understand.''\par

``The memory of light?''\par

``A poor name for an unopened door. Something none of us has opened.''\par

``It may not be a door,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``Everything interesting denies being a door at first.'' Ka-Syphiron turned the lamp. ``Some lights are a sum --- they look single, but break them correctly and many colours are travelling together. Others are almost a single thing. A gas may filter out one colour from the world, and, struck correctly, give back just that one colour.''\par

``As if it remembers it?''\par

``As if it is permitted nothing else.''\par

Then he smiled. ``There. A useless gift. The best kind for a young man with too many useful hungers.''\par

He stood suddenly. ``But not all mysteries deserve solemn faces. A civilisation that never plays repeats its oldest fears --- only with better posture.''\par

He pointed toward the side curtain. ``There are office mysteries, unpierced mysteries, and fun mysteries --- which irresponsible men preserve until Shield confiscates them.''\par

Va-Raedin's eyes narrowed. ``No.''\par

``Ray, your objections have been received and placed beneath the Mountain.'' He was already moving. He disappeared through the curtain.\par

The room quieted behind him. For several breaths the old gallery entered: lamp hiss, cooling stone, distant water striking a channel below.\par

I looked at Va-Raedin. Without Ka-Syphiron's brilliance filling the room, the Veil looked tired, and closer to me than before.\par

``Six years,'' I said.\par

``Yes.''\par

``Why did you never tell me?''\par

``Because you would have made it part of your hunger. If I had told you, you would have tried to become the boy who completed my chain. That boy would not have found the chamber.''\par

``I thought you were withholding because you did not think me worthy.''\par

``I was withholding because worthiness is often damaged by being named too early.''
He paused. ``You did well.''\par

``I did not understand everything.''\par

``No.''\par

``I still do not.''\par

``Good. A Veil who thinks the chamber has been solved because it has been opened is unfit to stand near it.''\par

He finally ate the bread in his hand. ``You are no longer only my student. Not yet my equal. Do not look pleased.''\par

I smiled despite myself.\par

``You will not believe this,'' I said. ``Earlier today, I said \emph{acceptable} to a servant of Light.''\par

Va-Raedin became very still. ``With satisfaction?''\par

``Sadly, yes.''\par

``There. The chain closes onto itself. You are already becoming me.''\par

I made a sound of horror --- which was when Ka-Syphiron returned through the curtain, carrying enough contraband to make the room feel guilty.\par

Va-Raedin looked at the objects in his arms. ``I object to all of them.''\par

``Your objection has standing and may be ignored with respect.''\par

He set two objects on the table and kept a wooden box beneath it. The first was a small book bound in worn brown hide. The second was a black cylinder mounted on a wooden base. He opened the book. On the first page a drawn woman stood in a long robe; page by page a hand moved to her collar, the collar loosened, the robe slipped from one shoulder. On the fifth, I looked up too quickly.\par

``Observe.''\par

He held the pages beneath his thumb and released them fast. The woman undressed --- by suggestion, but unmistakably, drawn with the technical care of a serious Light study and none of the solemnity the Mountain usually demanded.\par

``This is historically important, and is kept for study'' Va-Raedin said. He lasted almost one breath before he failed, and Ka-Syphiron laughed.\par

``And morale,'' Ka-Syphiron added.\par

``For evidence,'' Va-Raedin corrected.\par

He set a third object on the table: a small brass shutter, circular, slotted, blackened near one edge.\par

``Before the cylinder --- a necessary scandal in smaller form.''\par

Va-Raedin's expression changed. ``The Upfall Lamp.''\par

``What is the Upfall Lamp?'' I asked.\par

``A falling thread of water,'' Va-Raedin said. ``A lamp broken into pulses by a turning shutter. If the drops fall at one rhythm and the light strikes them at another, the eye accuses them of slowing. Stopping. Sometimes climbing.''\par

``That is impossible.''\par

``So motion can be broken. Observed in fragments,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``Misassembled by the eye. Stilled when moving. Moved when still.''\par

He pushed the shutter aside and turned the black cylinder on its base. ``And this one is from five links above us. Look through the slits.''\par

At first I saw only interruptions: darkness, image, darkness, image. He turned the handle. The drum spun quicker, and the woman inside shattered into repeated glimpses, each broken by blackness. Then the drum found its rhythm. The woman moved. Cloth loosened. A shoulder appeared. The garment slipped. Then the sequence reversed and she dressed again --- condemned by Light to undress and redress forever inside a spinning cylinder.\par

I stepped back. ``This is obscene.''\par

``Yes,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``And mechanically excellent.''\par

``The second quality is the official reason for its preservation,'' Va-Raedin added.\par

``The first,'' said Ka-Syphiron, ``is why nobody misplaced it.''\par

Va-Raedin looked at me. ``Is he disappointed it is not Va-Sheva?''\par

I turned violently red. Ka-Syphiron leaned back in laughter as though struck. ``Oh. That was cleanly done.''\par

``He looks as though he is going to be sick,'' Ka-Syphiron said, delighted.\par

``Men are all sick in that regard,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

Ka-Syphiron lifted one finger. ``Speaking of archangels.''\par

``Archangels?'' I said at once, grateful for any path away from Va-Sheva.\par

``When embarrassed men have run out of things to say, they come into this world to suspend time in an awkward silence.''\par

``And the Beast?''\par

The room stopped breathing.\par

Va-Raedin kept his eyes elsewhere. ``I only meant---''\par

``No,'' Ka-Syphiron said softly. ``You meant exactly what a boy should mean after too much hydromel and not enough terror. That is not a crime. Only a condition.''\par

He leaned forward. His face stayed as it was.\par

``You ask whether I believe in the Beast. Scripture has a shape for it, and we have a story we tell the young. I am one of five who keep the thing itself prisoner beneath the Mountain, held tonight, while we drink, and it would not stay held one season if the five of us agreed to forget it. On your path to Keepership --- if the Mountain does not kill you first --- you are bound to make at least one chilling discovery: the difference between a mystery that invites the mind and a secret that burdens the hand. Everything you have laughed at tonight --- the divided offices, the walls, the portions no one hand may hold --- was built to keep it, and for no smaller reason. When your turn comes to carry your portion of that weight, you will speak of it like that no more.''\par

His gaze drifted, for a fraction too small to accuse, toward the stone beneath our feet. Through it. Then it returned.\par

``No office talk tonight.''\par

``But---''\par

``No. Especially not if the question begins with \emph{but}. But is the hinge on which young men swing themselves into locked rooms.''\par

The Beast remained in the room after the joke ended. Then he clapped both hands once.\par

``Nothing better to relax an atmosphere than Justice.''\par

Va-Raedin laughed in disbelief. ``You cannot be serious.''\par

``I am almost never serious in ways people recognise at the proper time.''\par

He produced the wooden box. Inside were cards: thin painted slips marked in suits and numbers.\par

``Is this permitted?'' I asked.\par

``By whom? Justice? Certainly not.''\par

``That is not true,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

Ka-Syphiron considered. ``No. It is worse than not true. It is incomplete.''\par

He dealt. The game was easy to learn. Each hand sought twenty-one without exceeding it. Aces shifted between one and eleven, which I found morally suspicious. Candidate, Veil, and Keeper cards were worth ten. The Mountain won what would elsewhere have been called a tie.\par

``The Mountain wins ties?''\par

``Justice dislikes ambiguity.''\par

``Justice invented ambiguity.''\par

``Which is why they know how to charge for it.''\par

We played. I won early and was punished almost immediately. Va-Raedin lasted longer. I was ruined before half the shoe had gone. Eventually he lost as well.\par

``The Mountain wins,'' I said.\par

``This time. And most times.''\par

``Then why play?''\par

``That is the correct question asked too early.''\par

He squared the cards. ``A Veil of Justice could double his token count during a shoe some of the time. A Keeper could play indefinitely, never ruin, and always accrue.''\par

``That is impossible.''\par

``You have had a busy evening abusing that word. \emph{Impossible} saves lazy men the trouble of distinguishing what cannot happen from what cannot happen to them.''\par

``How?''\par

``Every hand has a correct action under condition. Perfect play delays ruin. It does not abolish it.''\par

``Then how do they do it?''\par

He smiled, and touched the discard pile. ``I will give you a clue. The revealed cards are the past.'' He touched the shoe. ``The remainder is the future.''\par

``Extremely generous tonight, Keeper,'' Va-Raedin murmured.\par

I looked from one to the other.\par

``If the record of the past is kept perfectly,'' Ka-Syphiron said, ``it alters the decision the future requires --- by simple condition. What has already appeared changes what remains possible. Justice knows this so deeply they have made a morality of it.''\par

The cards sat between us like a small, silent tribunal.\par

He closed the box. ``And there. You have now accused Faith of trespassing, interrogated the Beast, lost money you did not have to Justice, and been given a clue too large for your current office.''\par

``I have no office yet.''\par

``Exactly. An excellent time to overburden you.''\par

The smile thinned. He rested one hand on the box.\par

``And you must not do the common young thing of stealing one idea from another Kahir and mistaking it for the whole doctrine. They teach in layers: lesson one may be true, and lesson three may be why lesson one kills men when applied alone.''\par

``That is not only a Justice warning,'' Va-Raedin added.\par

``Sa-Kailan,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``Be very, very careful.''\par

No joke followed.\par

``Knowledge divided is structure before it is jealousy. Always older than your impatience. If you look across a wall, know first whether the wall keeps you out, keeps something in, or prevents two truths from producing a third neither office could survive.''\par

The evening changed. The laughter withdrew to the edges of the room. Ka-Syphiron set one hand on the table, near the closed Justice box.\par

``Tomorrow, the Mountain will speak what is already true. You will stand where you are called. You will not reach for understanding faster than ceremony permits. Let the Hall be larger than your mind for one morning.''\par

``Yes, Keeper.''\par

``And when you are given cloth, lamp, word, silence, or wound, you will receive each as condition, not ornament.''\par

``Yes.''\par

Then the old mischief returned, more gently. ``And if you must be terrified, try not to do it with your shoulders. It makes the robe hang badly.''\par

I laughed once, helplessly.\par

Then Ka-Syphiron said, ``All right.'' The words closed something. ``Sa-Kailan, I have a few things to tell Va-Raedin.''\par

I had been brought near. Fed. Praised. Warned. Taught beyond my level. Allowed to laugh in the room where tomorrow had already begun. Some words still passed above me.\par

I rose. For a moment I wanted to say something worthy of the evening, but everything I found would have diminished it. So I bowed --- lower than I had bowed on entering.
When I straightened, Va-Raedin was watching me. The warmth had left his face but not the room. He let it reach me only in the guarded way he could manage.\par

I opened the door. The old gallery waited outside, dim and smoke-blackened. Behind me, the two men waited until the door had nearly closed. I heard one fragment --- Ka-Syphiron's voice, lower than before.\par

``Here. For tomorrow, and for---''\par

Then the latch caught, and the rest belonged to the men still waiting to be released or raised. Deeper in the rock the Mountain kept its ordinary hours. In a warm room the oil-press turned, and the cake left when the oil was gone went off in baskets to the lofts and the tanks. Beside it the bone-gatherers boiled their baskets down to broth and glue and needle-stock. The public ceremony began before the Hall filled. One moment I stood in the old gallery, carrying mysteries away. The next, attendants of Light were dressing me in pale robes. The folded white cloth was bound around my wrist. Va-Raedin led me through corridors that had emptied ahead of us. Shield had already closed the side routes. Balance had already marked warmth and lamp oil for the Hall. Justice had already opened the record. \par

The Mountain had begun to change around the fact.\par
