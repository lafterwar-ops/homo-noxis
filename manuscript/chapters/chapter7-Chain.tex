--- What is all this for?

--- Science, of course. Our quest for truth does not end with your interpretations of Homer. One moment. Two phone calls.

--- Is this the same exercise as last night with all those amber liquids and no reels?

\centerline{[\,---\,]}

\textit{In Noxius}\par

--- Who here speaks my tongue? Helga clearly does not.

--- Sunny until ten tonight. Then storms. Do not laugh. It is a never-ending cycle of war and peace. Changes every other day. It's exhausting. \ldots{} Paul, did you at least check the dates? Eleventh of April on both. Fine.

\centerline{[\,---\,]}

Then a god gives men fire whole --- undivided, in every hand --- and is chained to a rock, where a bird opens him each day. \ldots{} A monster is chained beneath a mountain; when it strains, the earth splits and the mountain burns. \ldots{} A woman opens a jar; every evil escapes but Hope remains sealed. \ldots{} A flood spares a few on a mountain. \ldots{} And about that toast, it is a great deal of one taste, arriving at once and refusing to leave. I think I am of that other kind.

\centerline{[\,---\,]}

--- Difficult. I cannot tell them apart. They are equally good.

--- See, Paul? No difference between your \textit{Royal} and \textit{Afternoon} blends. What did I tell you?

--- To you both I say:

\begin{scripture}

And the light shineth in darkness; and the darkness comprehended it not.

\end{scripture}

--- Do you not see it, Paul? You hold every book of my scripture in pieces --- monster, fire, flood and hope sealed in a jar --- and have never laid them side by side. You are cursed to keep the pieces and forget the whole: you remember the Mountain but cannot find it; you hear the Gospel and twist it. And you Urs with your war. You are the Outsiders, born of the murderer's line. I did not lay my book over yours. I set them side by side, as you taught me, and yours will not stop confessing.

--- You chose the winner before you began: yours first, mine the copy. Indulge me with a new Light doctrine sentence for you: resemblance is not inheritance. Turn one card over --- suppose the Mountain came later --- and every seam runs backwards: a late people dressed older songs up as the source. I do not know which is true and will not pretend to. But a man who decides before comparing reads only the seams that prove him right. That is not reading but a frightened man building a wall. 

--- Harsh Paul. So, my hidden friend, the eggs?

--- That one is sweet and nutty, almost like butter. Tempting. But this one, the one with the blue stamp. Earthy and deep; and its white is more complex. It is my favourite.

My reward was a small blackened room that smelled of oil and dust and old paper and mistakes. The room was low-ceilinged and warm, its walls darkened by years of lamp smoke, its shelves so crowded they seemed to survive by mutual pressure. Lenses wrapped in cloth. Brass tubes, trays of powdered glass, a cracked bowl, lengths of wire, mirror shards, sealed jars, two blackened spoons. A small hand-lamp with its flame turned downward into water. And an object that resembled the skeleton of a bird built by someone who had never seen a bird but had heard disturbing rumours of one. Va-Raedin stood near the table. Ka-Syphiron sat behind it on a low stool with one uneven leg, wearing no ceremonial outer robe, his sleeves rolled to the wrist. A dark mark crossed one finger: soot, or ink, or blood long dried. He looked like a man interrupted in the middle of ruining something interesting.\par

``Close the door,'' he said. \par

I obeyed. The latch fell into place with a small sound. Ka-Syphiron placed both hands flat on the table.\par

``Sa-Kailan of Light. Yesterday, by witness of Va-Raedin, you found the Veil's Chamber.''\par
``Yes, Keeper.''\par
``You entered by lesson, error, discipline, insight, and disobedience.''\par
``Yes, Keeper.''\par
``You did not break the path behind you.''\par
``No, Keeper.''\par
``A Candidate learns where light falls. And a Veil?''\par
``A Veil learns who placed it.''\par
``That is the public version. Intelligible to all. A Veil learns who placed \textit{him}. And tonight you should see that Va-Raedin placed you there as surely as any lamp. Tell me. By what condition does a Candidate of Light become Veil of Light?''\par
``By finding the Veil's Chamber.''\par
``And by what condition does a Veil of Light become Keeper of Light?''\par
I looked at Va-Raedin but no rescue came.\par
``By finding the Keeper's Chamber.''\par
``Incomplete. A Veil who finds the Keeper's Chamber has reached the upper path. He has not completed the chain. Light does not pass upward because one man climbs; it passes when the lower chamber is opened again by one he trained. Only then is the chain complete. Faith, not long ago, had two Veils --- two who had found the Veil's Chamber --- but none had found the Chamber of the Last Prayer until recently. When Va-Elun found it, she rose at once to Keeper, because there was already a Veil of Faith beneath her to complete the chain. You know her now as Ka-Elun.''\par

I turned toward Va-Raedin.\par
``And did you find it?''\par
``Yes.''\par
``When?''\par
``Six years ago.''\par
``Six years?''\par
``Yes.''\par
``And you waited.''\par
``I served.''\par
``Waiting for me?''\par
``Waiting for the chain to complete.''\par

I looked down. I had spent one day waiting and found it almost intolerable. He had spent six years ready.\par

``Va-Raedin of Light found what he was required to find,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``He trained, withheld, corrected. He endured the indignity of being ready before the Mountain was ready to receive him.'' Then the old Keeper stood. He came around the table, the uneven stool complaining behind him, and placed one hand on my shoulder. The hand was warm, dry, and surprisingly heavy.\par

``Congratulations, Sa-Kailan. You have done a difficult thing without making it unbearable to praise you. That combination is rarer than genius.''\par

I tried to answer. Nothing came but the start of an uncontrollable smile. He set his other hand on Va-Raedin's shoulder, and the three of us stood linked beside the table: the Keeper about to be released, the Veil who had waited, the Candidate who had opened the lower door.\par

``Congratulations, Va-Raedin.''\par
``Keeper.''\par
``No. Not Keeper. Not tonight. Tonight we celebrate.'' \par
He bowed his head.\par
``Thank you.''\par

Ka-Syphiron released us both and stepped back.\par

``There. The chain has now been congratulated in all its necessary links. If Justice complains tomorrow that joy was not recorded, we shall say the failure was theirs.''\par

I laughed once and it came out broken.\par

Va-Raedin looked at me. ``The Keeper has praised you. I will not add to it --- praise is oil, and it has drowned better boys than you. You did not fail. Take that instead.''\par

``Enough,'' Ka-Syphiron announced, to no one. ``Absolutely enough. If Light spends every important moment proving it can stand still, then Shield has already won civilisation.''\par

He crossed to a wall panel so blackened by smoke it seemed merely dirty until his fingers found three hidden catches. It opened with the reluctant sound of secrecy. Behind it was a hoard: two dark bottles stopped with wax, a tray of small white cups, dried fruit, nuts, a round cheese, bread, strips of salted meat, and small cakes pressed with seeds.\par

He pointed a soot-marked finger at me. ``Clear the table.'' Then, to Va-Raedin: ``Ray. Those, there.''\par
\textit{Ray}, I repeated in my head.\par
``Keeper,'' Va-Raedin said.\par
``Do not Keeper me. I am arranging a feast.''\par
``You are impossible.''\par
``An excellent quality in a Keeper approaching replacement.''\par

The spread grew, and it was absurd, and it was magnificent --- more food than I had ever seen set before ten people without a festival, a funeral, or an inventory mistake.
Ka-Syphiron uncorked the first bottle and set a white cup before me. The smell of hydromel rose warm and sweet into the room. Va-Raedin's expression tightened. ``He is nineteen.''\par
``An age at which a man may discover that honey, water, time, and disobedience can produce wonders.''\par
``He is nineteen,'' Va-Raedin said again --- looking now at the tray of cups. Ka-Syphiron followed his gaze. ``Ah.''\par
``Absolutely not.''\par
``Absolutely yes. These are for your first Light lesson as Veil.''\par
I looked from one man to the other. ``What is wrong with the cups? May I see?''\par

Both men paused to look at me. Va-Raedin's face said no with the force of a sealed corridor. Ka-Syphiron's brightened with exhilaration and impatience. He lifted one of the small cups and set it before me. It was fine bone after all, or something like it --- white, thick-sided, faintly blue at the foot, worn by many hands. At the bottom, beneath the glaze, a cloudy disc waited like a blind eye. I took it before my courage could find a more careful course. It was heavier than it looked. I tilted it toward the lamp, and the dull pale dome of glass beneath the glaze stirred into a smear of colour, too faint to name, then vanished in the shine.\par

``A flaw,'' I said. ``Or a mark beneath the eye, perhaps.''\par
Ka-Syphiron looked at Va-Raedin. ``You trained this?''\par
``Under difficult conditions.''\par
``I see.''\par
I turned the cup again, more carefully. ``Thick wall. Curved base. The glaze distorts whatever has been set beneath it.''\par
``There he is.''\par
Ka-Syphiron lifted the bottle. ``Look again.''\par
``I am looking.''\par
``And now?''\par

The hydromel poured. It climbed the white inner wall, trembled, and settled over the rounded base. A figure appeared. I worked out the optics a breath before I saw what the picture was. The smear beneath the glaze gathered itself, through liquid and curvature, into a naked woman, one arm behind her head, the other arranged with a confidence no woman of the Mountain would ever have permitted to be drawn, owned, or used as instructional material before a newly recognised Veil.\par

``Oh,'' I said. And then, uselessly, ``Oh!''\par

My face burned so fast I felt betrayed by my own blood. Ka-Syphiron struck the table with his palm. ``There.''\par

Va-Raedin laughed. I stared at the cup, horrified, until I pulled myself together.\par

``Inverse turning,'' I said. ``Instead of scrambling a source, we are rebuilding one.''\par
``Spectacular, is it not?''\par
``And old,'' Va-Raedin added.\par
``Older than several current moral prohibitions.'' Ka-Syphiron raised his cup. ``To Light, and all its mysteries.''\par

We drank. The hydromel was sweet first, then hot, then sharper than I expected. I tried not to cough and failed, which produced another round of laughter --- and this time even Va-Raedin retreated from it too slowly. Ka-Syphiron broke the bread with both hands and gave the first piece to Va-Raedin. \par

``To six years.''\par
Va-Raedin closed his eyes. ``No.''\par
``To six years,'' I said.\par
Va-Raedin looked at me. ``You have been a Veil for less than an hour.''\par
``Good,'' said Ka-Syphiron. ``A Veil of Light must learn to disappoint his teacher without becoming useless.''\par

We ate. I took bread, then cheese, then one dried fig, because restraint still seemed like something I ought to display. Ka-Syphiron saw the gesture and put three more figs on my plate.\par
``This is madness,'' I said.\par
His face lit. ``Yes.''\par
``That is not a recognised category of feast,'' Va-Raedin said.\par
``It should be. A truly great feast is one at which Balance has not been invited, Justice has not been informed, Shield has not inspected the door, Faith has not decided whether the songs are appropriate, and Light has hidden the cups.''\par
``Then it is not a feast. It is institutional collapse.''\par
``Only if discovered.''\par

The evening loosened. Bread broke; cheese softened near the lamp; nuts wandered across the table; the hidden woman appeared and vanished in the cups several times. Ka-Syphiron told a story about an attendant who had once tried to classify an obscene cup as a devotional vessel. I laughed until my stomach hurt.\par

``To Va-Raedin,'' the old Keeper said suddenly, softening despite himself. ``To readiness, properly humiliated by time.''\par
Va-Raedin held his gaze, then lifted his cup. ``Yes.''\par
Ka-Syphiron cut three uneven pieces of cheese with a knife far too ceremonial for the task. ``You understand what begins now?''\par
``The office?'' I asked.\par
``The partnership.''\par
``He will remain junior,'' Va-Raedin said.\par
``Obviously. Partnership, not equality. The northern halls, candidate sequences, dangerous apparatus, cross-Kahir requests --- all of it will begin to touch him.''\par
``You will still be corrected,'' he added to me.\par
``Relentlessly,'' Va-Raedin said.\par
``There. You see the burden.''\par

I laughed again, and this time Va-Raedin punished it with decorum a breath late.\par

``Laugh,'' Ka-Syphiron allowed, ``but keep one thing past me. Distrust the counsel whose cost is paid in another body. This Mountain is full of clean hands giving heavy advice.''\par

Ka-Syphiron opened the second bottle --- which Va-Raedin allowed without approving --- and I found the courage to ask what had troubled me since the previous day.\par
``Keeper, I have a question.''\par
``Worse.''\par
``It concerns Faith.''\par
``Catastrophic. Continue.''\par

I looked at the cup, where the woman blurred beneath the hydromel.\par

``Va-Maera said she knew about what she called \textit{our little training rooms}. She had never entered them. And there are other things. Their prayers for
despair. Their archangels. The way they speak of voices, silences, thresholds. Va-Maera frightened me because she seemed to know where to press. As if despair had architecture. As if a voice could enter rooms where hands cannot.'' Ka-Syphiron set down the bottle and leaned back.\par

``Cross-Kahir knowledge is a dangerous appetite. Too much, too quickly, convinces a young man that the Mountain is merely a puzzle discourteously divided before his arrival.''\par
``It is not?''\par
``No. It is a wound, a machine, a refuge, a prison, a weapon, and occasionally a badly ventilated experiment room with immoral cups. And about Faith: this particular object, for instance, uses an almost pure Faith principle. Not decomposition but recombination --- though Faith would probably call it resurrection.''\par

``Generous tonight, Keeper,'' Va-Raedin said, disapproving.\par

Ka-Syphiron tapped the cup on the table. ``Faith humiliates the ear as we humiliate the eye.'' Another tap. ``Echo.'' Another. ``Delay.'' Another. ``Stone.'' The room returned each sound differently. ``A corridor lengthens a note; a vault divides it; a chamber stores part and returns it late. A prayer may be words addressed to God --- I do not settle that tonight --- but it may also be a shaped sound moving through stone. A woman sings here; the Mountain answers elsewhere. Faith hears the alteration and reverses it. Have you never noticed that five voices may sound louder than five voices should? Move five singers two steps, and the room seems to join them. The air does not add like jars, Sa-Kailan. Sometimes a chamber is waiting for exactly the rhythm that makes it confess its shape.''\par

``Resonance,'' Va-Raedin said quietly.\par

Ka-Syphiron looked at him. ``Look who is generous with knowledge now, Ray.''\par

He set down his cup. ``Speaking of the decomposition of sound and light --- aren't you supposed to give him something?''\par

Va-Raedin became still. ``It was not for tonight.''\par

``What a miserable phrase. No wonder you are nearly Keeper.''\par

Va-Raedin ignored him, reached into the inner fold of his robe, and drew out a small object wrapped in grey cloth. I took it carefully. Inside was a triangular piece of clear glass, no longer than my thumb, polished on three faces and chipped at one corner. When I turned it toward the lamp, the flame broke faintly along one edge into a thin wound of colour.\par

``What do you call this?'' I said.\par
``A prism.''\par
``What does it do?''\par
``The \emph{what} you will have worked out by morning. The unpierced mystery is the how, and the why.''\par
``For the ceremony?''\par
``No.'' His eyes held mine. ``For after.''\par

I closed my fingers around it.\par
``When a beam seems whole,'' Va-Raedin said, ``do not believe it too quickly.''\par
Ka-Syphiron looked away too slowly to hide that the line pleased him.\par
``For teaching,'' Va-Raedin went on. ``If you remain teachable after having been praised.''\par
``That is not guaranteed,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``He has been praised twice and fed. We may have lost him already.''\par
He turned my wrist until the lamp struck the prism more narrowly.\par

``You are right to wonder about Faith. But do not become drunk on other people's locked doors. Light has enough of its own. There are office mysteries: apparatus, ceremonies, petitions, the training of the young. And there are mysteries still unpierced. Like the memory of light. A question I have spent years failing to understand.''\par
``The memory of light?''\par
``A poor name for an unopened door. Something none of us has opened.''\par
``It may not be a door,'' Va-Raedin said.\par
``Everything interesting denies being a door at first.'' Ka-Syphiron turned the lamp. ``Some lights are a sum --- they look single, but break them correctly and many colours are travelling together. Others are almost a single thing. A gas may filter out one colour from the world, and, struck correctly, give back just that one colour.''\par
``As if it remembers it?''\par
``As if it is permitted nothing else.''\par
``There. A useless gift. The best kind for a young man with too many useful hungers.''\par
He was on his feet. ``But not all mysteries deserve solemn faces.''\par
He pointed toward the side curtain. ``There are office mysteries, unpierced mysteries, and fun mysteries --- which irresponsible men preserve until Shield confiscates them.''\par

Va-Raedin's eyes narrowed. ``No.''\par
``Ray, your objections have been received and placed beneath the Mountain.'' He was already moving. He disappeared through the curtain.\par
The room quieted behind him. For several breaths the old gallery entered: lamp hiss, cooling stone, distant water striking a channel below.\par
I looked at Va-Raedin. Without Ka-Syphiron in the room, the Veil looked tired, and closer to me than before.\par
``Six years,'' I said.\par
``Yes.''\par
``Why did you never tell me?''\par
``Because you would have made it part of your hunger. If I had told you, you would have tried to become the boy who completed my chain. That boy would not have found the chamber.''\par
``I thought you were withholding because you did not think me worthy.''\par
``I was withholding because worthiness is often damaged by being named too early.''
He paused. ``You did well.''\par
``I did not understand everything.''\par
``No.''\par
``I still do not.''\par
``Good. A Veil who thinks the chamber has been solved because it has been opened is unfit to stand near it.''\par
He finally ate the bread in his hand. ``You are no longer only my student. Not yet my equal. Do not look pleased.''\par
I smiled despite myself.\par
``You will not believe this,'' I said. ``Earlier today, I said \textit{acceptable} to a servant of Light.''\par
``With satisfaction?''\par
``Sadly, yes.''\par

``There. The chain closes onto itself. You are already becoming me.''\par

I made a sound of horror --- which was when Ka-Syphiron returned through the curtain with a wooden box beneath one arm. He looked at my cup. \par

``Still hiding her?''\par

He poured another measure of hydromel. The woman gathered herself again beneath the liquid. Va-Raedin looked at Ka-Syphiron.

``Is he disappointed it is not Va-Sheva?''\par

I turned violently red. Ka-Syphiron leaned back in laughter as though struck. ``Oh. That was cleanly done.''\par

``He looks as though he is going to be sick,'' Ka-Syphiron said, delighted.\par

``Men are all sick in that regard,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``Hold what you can of that one, Sa-Kailan, while the holding is cheap. It does not always stay cheap,'' Ka-Syphiron said. 

After a long silence, Ka-Syphiron lifted one finger. ``Speaking of archangels.''\par

``Archangels?'' I said at once, grateful for any path away from Va-Sheva.\par

``When embarrassed men have run out of things to say, they come into this world to suspend time in an awkward silence.''\par

``And the Beast?'' I asked. The room went still.\par

``I only meant---''\par

``No,'' Ka-Syphiron said softly. ``You meant exactly what a boy should mean after too much hydromel and not enough terror. That is not a crime. Only a condition.''\par

He leaned forward. His face stayed as it was.\par

``You ask whether I believe in the Beast. Scripture has a shape for it, and we have a story we tell the young. I am one of five who keep the thing itself prisoner beneath the Mountain. We keep it tonight, while we drink, and it would not stay held one season if the five of us agreed to forget it. On your path to Keepership --- if the Mountain does not kill you first --- you are bound to make at least one discovery: the difference between a mystery that invites the mind and a secret that burdens the hand. Everything you have laughed at tonight --- the divided offices, the walls, the portions no one hand may hold --- was built to keep it, and for no smaller reason. When your turn comes to carry your portion of that weight, you will speak of it like that no more.''\par

His gaze drifted, for a fraction too small to accuse, toward the stone beneath our feet. Through it. Then it returned.\par

``No office talk tonight.''\par

``But---''\par

``No. Especially not if the question begins with \textit{but}. But is the hinge on which young men swing themselves into locked rooms.''\par

Then he clapped both hands once.\par

``Nothing better to relax an atmosphere than Justice.''\par

Va-Raedin laughed in disbelief. ``You cannot be serious.''\par

``I am almost never serious in ways people recognise at the proper time.''\par

He set the wooden box on the table. Inside were cards: thin painted slips marked in suits and numbers.\par

``Is this permitted?'' I asked.\par

``By whom? Justice? Certainly not.''\par

``That is not true,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

Ka-Syphiron considered. ``No. It is worse than not true. It is incomplete.''\par

He dealt. The game was easy to learn. Each hand sought twenty-one without exceeding it. Aces shifted between one and eleven, which I found morally suspicious. Candidate, Veil, and Keeper cards were worth ten. The Mountain won what would elsewhere have been called a tie.\par

``The Mountain wins ties?''\par

``Justice dislikes ambiguity.''\par

``Justice invented ambiguity.''\par

``Which is why they know how to charge for it.''\par

We played. I won early and was punished almost immediately. Va-Raedin lasted longer. I was ruined before half the shoe had gone. Eventually he lost as well.\par

``The Mountain wins,'' I said.\par

``This time. And most times.''\par

``Then why play?''\par

``Excellent question. Wrong time.''\par

He squared the cards. ``A Veil of Justice could double his token count during a shoe some of the time. A Keeper could play indefinitely, never ruin, and always accrue.''\par

``That is impossible.''\par

``\emph{Impossible} saves lazy men the trouble of distinguishing what cannot happen from what cannot happen to them.''\par

``How?''\par

``Every hand has a correct action under condition. Perfect play delays ruin. It does not abolish it.''\par

``Then how do they do it?''\par

He smiled, and touched the discard pile. ``I will give you a clue. The revealed cards are the past.'' He touched the shoe. ``The remainder is the future.''\par

``Extremely generous tonight, Keeper,'' Va-Raedin murmured.\par

I looked from one to the other.\par

``If the record of the past is kept perfectly,'' Ka-Syphiron said, ``it alters the decision the future requires --- by simple condition. Justice knows this so deeply they have made a morality of it.''\par

He closed the box. ``And there. You have now accused Faith of trespassing, interrogated the Beast, lost money you did not have to Justice, and been given a clue too large for your current office.''\par

``I have no office yet.''\par

``Exactly. An excellent time to overburden you.''\par

The smile thinned. He rested one hand on the box.\par

``And you must not do the common young thing of stealing one idea from another Kahir and mistaking it for the whole doctrine. Do not commit the ludic error: the world does not promise to keep the rules written on the table. This is why they teach in layers: lesson one may be true, and lesson three may be why lesson one kills men when applied alone.''\par

``That is not only a Justice warning,'' Va-Raedin added.\par

``Sa-Kailan,'' Ka-Syphiron said. ``Be very, very careful.''\par

``Knowledge divided is structure before it is jealousy. If you look across a wall, know first whether the wall keeps you out, keeps something in, or prevents two truths from producing a third neither office could survive.''\par

Ka-Syphiron set one hand on the table, near the closed Justice box.\par

``Tomorrow, the Mountain will speak what is already true. Let the Hall be larger than your mind for one morning.''\par

``Yes, Keeper.''\par

``And when you are given cloth, lamp, word, silence, or wound, you will receive each as condition, not ornament.''\par

``Yes.''\par

Then the old mischief returned, more gently. ``And if you must be terrified, try not to do it with your shoulders. It makes the robe hang badly.''\par

I laughed once, helplessly.\par

``All right,'' Ka-Syphiron said, and something in the room closed. ``Sa-Kailan, I have a few things to tell Va-Raedin.''\par

I rose. For a moment I wanted to say something worthy of the evening, but everything I found would have diminished it. So I bowed --- lower than I had bowed on entering.
When I straightened, Va-Raedin was watching me. The warmth had left his face; it stayed in the room. He let it reach me only in the guarded way he could manage.\par

I opened the door. The old gallery waited outside, dim and smoke-blackened. Behind me, the two men waited until the door had nearly closed. I heard one fragment --- Ka-Syphiron's voice, lower than before.\par

``Here. For tomorrow, and for---''\par

Then the latch caught, and the rest belonged to the men still waiting to be released or raised. Deeper in the rock the Mountain kept its ordinary hours. In a warm room the oil-press turned, and the cake left when the oil was gone went off in baskets to the lofts and the tanks. Beside it the bone-gatherers boiled their baskets down to broth and glue and needle-stock. The public ceremony began before the Hall filled. One moment I stood in the old gallery, carrying mysteries away. The next, attendants of Light were dressing me in pale robes. The folded white cloth was bound around my wrist. Va-Raedin led me through corridors that had emptied ahead of us. Shield had already closed the side routes. Balance had already marked warmth and lamp oil for the Hall. Justice had already opened the record. \par
 
\begin{room}

--- What comes next is the double Ascension of Light.

--- Good. Let's stop there. I hear what comes next during instruction is finally optics; you should enjoy it, for once.

--- You are taking the machine?

--- Yes.

--- The lawn?

--- The lawn.

\centerline{[\,---\,]}

--- Martinez?

--- Nothing has happened. But I have learnt something. And I brought you this.

--- A map?

--- Of this part of Africa. Here is Algeria. Below it, Niger. Libya here, Chad here. Your Mountain is somewhere along this line.

--- The pendulum, I should not have told you.

--- No.

\centerline{[\,---\,]}

--- Roger Michel Martinez. Born in Oran, here, in fifty-seven. He was five when Algeria ceased to be French. I lose him for nearly twenty years. Then he appears here --- Niamey, the capital of Niger. Early eighties. Niger has enormous uranium deposits; France depended heavily on them. Martinez is still in his twenties, with a modest title and rather immodest access.

--- Edward?

--- Not yet. That's what interested me. Then Chad, eighty-six. Libya had troops in the north; France and Chad were fighting them. In February French aircraft bombed a Libyan airstrip deep in the desert. Martinez was in Chad at the time, and I can find no uranium business that explains why.

--- So he worked for France.

--- Perhaps. France is everywhere around him. Nowhere I can attach Martinez himself to French intelligence without cheating.
