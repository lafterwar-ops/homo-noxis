\typesetchapter{Chapter 7}{Chamber}

\noindent \textit{Not every door left open was opened for you.}\par
\noindent \textit{--- Va-Maera, Veil of Faith}\par

\ornament

\noindent You wanted to know, Paul, what a boy does when he is finally left alone with a question. I can give you the hour. It was during a Festival, while the whole Mountain sang with one throat, and I walked away from all of them to follow a thread of light no one was meant to see.\par

I left the eastern tier while the Hall of Ceremony bowed around me.\par

The Festival had lowered every head toward the central dais, toward Ka-Elun beneath the Genesis gravure, toward the baskets of seed and the sealed jars, toward the ancient balance of day and night.
Faith had trained the people to look where the rite placed holiness.
Light had trained me to look where holiness disappeared.\par

The beam was gone.\par

Not gone.
Hidden.\par

I had seen only its last visible length: sun through the upper aperture, the polished cut in the gravure, carved waters, raised Mountain, five children lit as if the first morning had returned for them alone.
Then the beam had narrowed and vanished into the lower stone beneath the dais.\par

The Hall continued without me.\par

``God who set Earth between Heaven and Hell, steady us.''\par

The people answered.\par

``Steady us in shadow.''\par

I reached the side passage behind the eastern tier and nearly collided with a Faith attendant carrying a covered bowl.
She looked at my robe, then at my face.\par

``Candidate?''\par

``Light service,'' I said.\par

The lie was small.
It entered me anyway.\par

She stepped aside, because Light often moved during ceremony, and because the Mountain was built from permissions most people had no time to question.
I passed her, and Va-Maera's sentence returned with cruel clarity.\par

Not every door left open was opened for you.\par

I kept moving.\par

\ornament

\noindent He has stopped at the small lie --- the word he gave the Faith attendant to pass a door he had no leave to pass. He turns it over as though it still troubles him, thirty years and a life away. It is the first time tonight he has looked up from the account and looked instead at the room.\par

``This house,'' he says. ``Whose is it?''\par

The reels turn. I let them turn once before I answer, so the pause reads as courtesy and not as counting.\par

``The account first,'' I say. ``That is the house's way, not mine. You will have your questions when the tape is full, and I will answer what I am let to answer.''\par

``That is not an answer.''\par

``No. It is the order of things here.'' I give him a little, because a bare wall only invites the climb. ``The house is old --- older than the man who keeps it, older than the men who kept it before him. It was rich before I was born and will be rich after I am dust. That much costs the house nothing, so you may have it.''\par

He watches me the way he has just told me he watched the false line of gold across the corridor stone --- the path that pretends to be found.\par

``You answer like a man who has been told exactly how much to give,'' he says.\par

``I answer like a servant,'' I say. ``Go on. You had passed the attendant, and the light had gone into the wall. What does a boy do when he is finally alone with a question?''\par

\ornament

The first side corridor offered no beam.
Only ordinary lamps, ordinary stone, ordinary smoke-darkened brackets.
For one terrible moment I thought I had already lost it --- that the annual light had travelled through a sealed channel no person could follow, through shafts cut in an age when the Mountain was younger and less merciful, through apertures too narrow for eye or hand.\par

Then I saw the dust.\par

Not in the air.
On the floor.
A thin line of gold lay across the grey stone near the base of the wall, so narrow that a step would have broken it.
I dropped to one knee.
The line was not coming from the corridor lamps; those were too low and too warm.
I followed it with my fingers held just above the seam.
It ended at a dull bronze plate set beside an unused service niche.\par

The plate was blackened with age except for one edge, where a reflected thread trembled.\par

I leaned closer, then stopped.\par

Reflection.\par

Not the object but the danger. Sight thrown away from the body.\par

The line on the floor was not the path.
It was the path pretending to be found.
A mirror could show the place behind the eye; a route that appeared to lie forward might have come from behind.\par

I turned.\par

From the corridor entrance, the bronze edge aligned with a black aperture above the service niche.
The light had crossed the floor only because the dust had caught a false remainder.
I stood where the false line began and raised my hand behind the aperture.
A narrow warmth touched my knuckle.\par

There.\par

The beam was travelling above me.\par

I climbed the rim of the service niche, found a shallow foothold cut too cleanly to be accidental, and pulled myself up.
The aperture was no wider than two fingers, but behind it a second channel ran along the wall, angled downward into darkness.\par

I could not fit through.\par

Of course I could not fit through.
This was Light.
A body was not always invited to follow where the eye could go.\par

I searched the niche.
Dust.
Old plaster.
A cracked bracket.
A strip of dark glass set at knee height.
I crouched and looked through it.\par

The hidden channel appeared enlarged.\par

Focus.\par

The glass did not merely show the channel.
It chose a portion of it and made that portion important.
The far end widened under the lens into a pale oval, and within the oval a small white mark had been painted on the stone --- not a letter, not a symbol, only a dot placed where no ordinary worker would ever have reason to see it.\par

False importance by focus, I thought.\par

But if the lens was fixed, then perhaps the importance had been fixed too.\par

I adjusted my position until the white dot sharpened.
The enlarged channel revealed a second aperture high on the opposite wall.
Without the glass it was invisible.
With the glass it opened like an eye.\par

I climbed down and crossed to the opposite wall.
No seam.
No handle.
No door.
Only stone, smoke, and a lamp bracket whose flame had gone out long ago.\par

I almost cursed.\par

Then I saw what the wall did not show.\par

Shadow.\par

The bracket should have thrown a small hook-shaped shadow from the nearest lamp.
It did not.
Something interrupted the interruption.
A narrow blackness sat where the shadow should have fallen, too regular to be dirt, too flat to be depth.
I placed my hand into the false darkness and found an opening hidden inside the missing shadow.\par

My fingers closed around a cold ring.\par

I did not pull.\par

Va-Raedin's correction entered before my hand moved.\par

Seeing is not permission.
Finding is not permission.\par

I listened.\par

The Hall below answered in unison.
The Festival rhythm still held.
No footsteps in the side corridor.
No Shield command, no Balance clerk, no Justice witness.
Only Faith above me and Light in the wall.\par

If the ring opened a gate, I would have opened it alone.
If it released water, I would have released it without measure.
If it sounded an alarm, I would have turned a Festival into consequence.\par

I waited long enough to become ashamed of waiting.\par

Then I pulled, slowly.\par

The ring did not open a door.
It lowered a shutter inside the wall.\par

A new line of light struck the floor.\par

This one was stronger, colder, and cleaner than the first.
It crossed the corridor, entered a low crack beneath a stone bench, and vanished again.\par

I slid under the bench.\par

The crack opened into a crawl passage.\par

I hesitated only once.
Then I entered.\par

Stone scraped my shoulder.
Dust filled my mouth.
The passage descended by uneven cuts, then bent left, then right, then narrowed until I had to turn my head sideways to continue.
The beam ran ahead of me through a channel cut apart from the crawl, close enough to see in fragments, too far to touch.
It appeared and disappeared through inspection slits like a creature refusing capture.\par

The Mountain pressed against me.\par

I thought of the Oasis sky and suddenly hated the memory of its size.\par

At the end of the crawl, the passage opened into a dry basin chamber no larger than a storage cell.
The floor sloped toward a circular depression.
An empty jar stood in a wall recess, sealed with old wax.
Above the basin, the beam entered through a narrow cut and fell onto bare stone, missing the far aperture by half a hand.\par

I stared at it.\par

The beam had reached this chamber and failed.\par

No.
Not failed.
Waited.\par

Turning.\par

Light changing its path through substance.\par

The basin was dry, but it had not been built for dryness.
Its inner edge bore a mineral ring.
The floor's slope led to a small stopper at the centre.
The sealed jar had not been left as offering or ornament.
I took it from the recess and examined the wax.\par

Balance green.\par

Of course.\par

Curiosity is not free.\par

I broke the seal.\par

The jar held water thick with mineral.
Not enough to fill the basin.
Enough, perhaps, if the basin's shape mattered more than its volume.
I poured slowly.
The water entered the depression, spread along the curve, and settled beneath the beam.\par

The light bent.\par

Not enough.\par

I looked at the mineral trace inside the jar.
Salt.
Or something like salt --- the very thing Va-Raedin had forbidden me to trust.
I swirled the last drops, scraped them gently round the bottom, and poured again.
The heavier water bent the beam a little farther.\par

Still not enough.\par

I had no more water.\par

Panic rose, small and bright.
The Festival beam would not last.
The sun would move.
The path existed only while the year held the correct angle, and I was kneeling before a basin half-awake because some earlier hand had left only part of what the chamber required.\par

Then I saw the stopper.\par

The basin did not need more water.
It needed less depth at the centre and more at the rim.
I turned the small plug.
Water drained through a hidden channel --- not away, but outward beneath the stone lip.
The body of water changed its shape: drawn thin over the centre, banked deep against the wall, so that the beam passing through it was turned along a new path.\par

The beam bent cleanly and entered the far aperture.\par

A sound answered inside the wall.\par

Not a click.
A shift of weight.\par

The beam continued.\par

I put the empty jar back, because theft from Balance still counted after revelation.
Then I followed the opening panel into the next passage.\par

It was taller now.
Walkable.
Cold air moved through it, carrying the smell of old water and metal.
On the wall beside me, five shallow cuts had been made in sequence.
They were not the marks from the old Light gallery, but near enough to make my throat tighten.\par

A polished line, a swollen point, a missing shadow, a bent ray, a returning surface --- the five lessons reduced to marks.\par

Not decoration.
Not record only.\par

Instruction.\par

I continued.\par

The fifth chamber held water.\par

A long black trough ran from one wall to the other, filled to the lip and perfectly still.
The beam entered through an aperture beneath the surface and travelled inside the water itself, pale and trapped.
It struck the upper skin at too shallow an angle and turned back down, crossing itself, refusing to escape.\par

Threshold return.\par

The final lesson.\par

At the far end of the trough stood a bronze plate above the waterline.
A seam lay behind it, visible only because the beam passed beneath and made the plate's lower edge glow.\par

Make it leave, Va-Raedin had said.\par

I searched for salt, clear water, levers, a drain, a plug.
Nothing obvious.
That was the frightening part.
The Festival beam narrowed as the sun moved.
I could feel time in the chamber as a physical pressure.\par

The trough had three marks cut along its side.
High, middle, low.
The water sat at the high mark.
A small side channel entered from the wall, dry.
Another, near the bottom, carried a thread of water away.
I touched it.
Moving.
Slow, almost hidden.\par

The chamber was already changing.\par

Not waiting for me.
Operating.\par

The water level had been set to high before the Festival.
As the lower thread drained, the level would fall, and the angle at which the trapped beam struck the surface would change with it.
Too early, the light stayed imprisoned.
Too late, it would pass too low to matter.
At one exact level, the surface would let a single thread through.\par

This was not a door I opened.\par

It was an occasion I had to recognise.\par

I knelt beside the trough and watched.\par

The beam shifted by a hair.
The surface lowered.
The trapped light returned into the water, again and again, each return a refusal.
I held my breath.
The bronze plate waited above the line like a verdict.\par

The water touched the middle mark.\par

A single thread of light escaped the surface and struck the bronze plate.\par

The plate warmed.
A hidden resin line darkened.
The seam behind it opened by less than a finger.\par

I slid both hands into the gap and pulled.\par

The wall gave.\par

Darkness breathed out.\par

I entered the Veil's Chamber.\par

At first, disappointment came so swiftly that I almost laughed.\par

It was not grand.\par

No altar.
No carved angel.
No crown of lamps.
No hidden window onto Heaven.
The chamber was low, cold, and almost bare, its walls smoothed by hand but not polished.
A circular basin sat at the centre, dry and shallow, with five narrow channels leading into it from different heights.
Above it hung a black ring of metal pierced by holes so small they looked like flaws.
On the far wall were diagrams cut into stone and filled with white paste.\par

Lines. Waves. Circles crossing circles.\par

I stepped closer.\par

The diagrams were unlike the lessons.\par

The Candidate rooms had taught beams as paths: source to surface, surface to mark, mark to eye.
These marks did not behave like paths.
They spread.
They crossed.
They vanished where they met, and strengthened where they met, and bent around obstacles, and returned behind barriers that should have stopped them.\par

I raised my hand without touching.\par

One diagram showed a series of curved lines entering a narrow opening and spreading on the other side.
Another showed two openings side by side, each releasing curved lines that met and made alternating bands of strength and absence.
A third showed a circular disturbance breaking around a stone in water and re-forming behind it, weaker but still ordered.
A fourth showed five channels entering one basin at different heights, producing rings that crossed.\par

Water, I thought.\par

No.
Not only water.\par

Light also.\par

The thought arrived before I could defend myself against it.\par

I want you to understand, Paul, that everything after that day grew from this one --- and that I did not know it yet. I knew only that my mouth had gone dry.\par

A beam was not always a line.
A line was what a beam became when it had been narrowed enough to teach a Candidate obedience.
But the tremble at the metal edge, the bands beside the light shaft, the spreading after a slit --- those were not accidents.
Edges did something to light.
Openings did something.
Obstacles did something.
Water only showed what light hid too quickly for the eye.\par

The chamber did not teach me the answer.\par

It made the old answers insufficient.\par

I moved around the basin.
Each channel was marked with one of the five lessons --- Reflection, Focus, Shadow, Turning, Threshold --- not in words but in the old marks.
Each would admit water from a different height, perhaps at a different time.
The basin's floor was cut with faint rings, so fine I had to crouch to see them.
They were not decorative.
When water entered, the rings would raise waves; and when five waves crossed, the basin would become a visible calculation.\par

The Veil's Chamber was not a prize.\par

It was an accusation against the simplicity of everything I had learned.\par

I had thought Light bent, reflected, focused, hid, and revealed.
Here Light became something stranger: a thing that could spread, cancel, strengthen, return behind obstruction, and write patterns where direct sight failed.\par

Hiddenness was not merely what the stone concealed.\par

Hiddenness was what remained after a path broke and reassembled somewhere the eye had been trained not to expect.\par

A sound moved behind me.\par

I turned too quickly.\par

Va-Raedin stood in the doorway.\par

He was breathing harder than usual.
Not much.
Enough.
His robe carried dust at the shoulder where he had come through the crawl.
He looked at me, then at the central basin, then at the far diagrams.\par

For a few moments he said nothing.\par

The silence was worse than reprimand.
It opened too many possible futures.\par

Then he said, ``You found it.''\par

I heard the words and did not know what to do with my face.\par

``Yes.''\par

``You left the Hall during Festival.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``You lied to a Faith attendant.''\par

The shame returned in a clean line.\par

``Yes.''\par

``You broke a Balance seal.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``You pulled a hidden ring without witness.''\par

I swallowed.\par

``Yes.''\par

``You opened a chamber alone.''\par

``Yes.''\par

Va-Raedin looked back at the diagrams.\par

``And you reached the correct chamber.''\par

I said nothing.\par

The two truths did not cancel one another.
That was what made the room suddenly feel larger.\par

``I did what the rule required,'' I said.\par

``No.
You did what the rule measured.''\par

The distinction entered too quickly for me to understand, and then it stayed.\par

``A Candidate becomes Veil by finding the Veil's Chamber.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``Then I found it.''\par

``Yes.''\par

His voice had not softened.
If anything it had become colder.\par

``And now you are discovering that success is not innocence.''\par

I looked at the central basin, the empty channels, the wave diagrams.\par

``Was there another way?''\par

``There is always another way in memory.
Rarely in time.''\par

``That sounds like Justice.''\par

``It is older than Justice.''\par

He stepped fully into the chamber and closed the wall behind him.
The darkness accepted us.
Only a small remnant of the Festival beam still entered through the last aperture, fading now as the sun moved beyond its appointed line.\par

``You followed the five lessons,'' he said.\par

``Yes.''\par

``Name them as you used them.''\par

``Reflection showed me the path was behind the first path.
Focus made the hidden aperture visible.
Shadow showed me the ring by its interruption.
Turning bent the beam through the basin.
Threshold return kept the light inside the water until the level changed.''\par

He listened without interruption.\par

``And what did the route teach beyond the lessons?''\par

I looked at the diagrams again.\par

``That light is not only a line.''\par

His face changed by almost nothing.\par

``Continue.''\par

``It spreads when narrowed.
It changes at edges.
It can weaken itself or strengthen itself where paths cross.
Water can make the pattern slow enough to see.
The hidden side of an obstacle is not empty.''\par

The last sentence came from somewhere deeper than the rest.\par

Va-Raedin turned toward the third diagram: the waves re-forming behind the stone.\par

``No,'' he said quietly.
``It is not.''\par

The chamber seemed to hold the words after he spoke them.\par

I wanted to ask what the hidden side meant, where the waves led, why the basin had five channels, what Ka-Syphiron knew, whether Va-Raedin had stood here and said the same thing at fifteen or seventeen or twenty.
I wanted too much.
The wanting itself felt visible.\par

He saw it.\par

``You will not touch the basin.''\par

``I was not---''\par

``You were about to become curious with your hands.''\par

I closed my fingers.\par

``You will not open the channels.
You will not scrape the paste from the diagrams.
You will not try to reproduce this in a washing bowl.
You will not speak to Balance about mineral water.
You will not ask Faith whether their prayers use crossing sound.
You will not ask Shield what lies behind a barrier.
You will not ask Justice whether two causes cancel or compound one another.
Do you hear the shape of the prohibition?''\par

I did.\par

Each Kahir had suddenly become dangerous by relation.
Balance water.
Faith sound.
Shield barriers.
Justice causes.
Light waves.\par

The Chamber was not only deeper Light.
It pointed outward toward every other charge.\par

``Yes,'' I said.\par

``No,'' Va-Raedin said.\par

``But you agree.''\par

This time the old correction did not humiliate me.
It steadied me.\par

``I agree.''\par

He crossed to the basin.
From a hidden recess he drew a narrow tool and set it near the first channel.
A small stop clicked.
Then another.
He reset each of the five channels, wiped the mineral trace from the smaller basin outside the chamber, sealed the empty jar with new wax taken from a fold in his sleeve, and closed the chamber's first panel.
His movements were quick, practised, and without reverence.
That disturbed me almost more than the diagrams.\par

``You have done this before.''\par

``I found the Chamber before you.''\par

``That is not what I meant.''\par

``It is the only answer you receive.''\par

We left together.\par

The route out was not the route in.
Va-Raedin did not retrace the Festival path; he opened a narrow service way behind the fifth chamber and led me through a descent that smelled of cold metal, old water, and dust.
The Festival beam no longer mattered.
Once the Chamber had been opened, ordinary mechanisms could carry back those entitled to know it.\par

That made me uneasy.
Discovery had felt like a door.
Permission revealed itself as a corridor built long before my courage.\par

\ornament

\noindent A corridor built long before his courage. He says it as a sorrow and does not hear how near it passes to the room he is sitting in. I let it pass. It is not my task to teach a man where he is.\par

He is looking at me again. The account has given him the taste for the thing behind the thing, and four warm days have made him bold enough to turn the habit on his host.\par

``The men who sent you,'' he says. ``This Horizon. What is it?''\par

Here I can give him the truth, because the truth is the strongest wall I own. ``I am Horizon,'' I say. ``It is not a country and not an army. It is a few who remember what the Mountain is for, and keep the memory while the Mountain cannot keep itself. It sent me. I serve it. There is no lie in that.''\par

He nods slowly, and I watch the answer do its work --- watch it settle him, because it is true, and a man will rest on a true thing even when it has been laid in his road to stop him asking the next one.\par

``And who stands above it?'' he says. ``Who keeps the keeper? A house this rich does not warm itself for a handful of men with long memories.''\par

The boy is quick. Ka-Syphiron was right about him, across thirty years and a language he never learned. ``The account first,'' I say.\par

``That is the second time you have set that same wall before the same question.''\par

``Then you have found where the wall stands,'' I say. ``That is more than most of my guests learn in twice the days.'' I make it warm; warmth is cheaper than argument. ``We are fewer than you fear and safer than you hope, and none of it bears on a boy and a beam of light in a mountain. Go on.''\par

He goes on. But he has counted the wall now. I saw him count it.\par

By the time we reached the lower passage beneath the Hall, the Festival above had entered its final response.
The people were rising.
Footsteps and voices moved through the stone.
No one knew a Candidate had left the eastern tier and returned from a chamber that existed only when the year, the Hall, the gravure, the water, and the five lessons agreed.\par

Va-Raedin stopped at the base of the eastern stair.\par

``You will say nothing.''\par

``To anyone?''\par

``To anyone.''\par

``Not even Ka-Syphiron?''\par

``I will tell Ka-Syphiron.''\par

I disliked that more than I could justify.\par

``It is my discovery.''\par

``It is Light's Chamber.''\par

``I found it.''\par

``Yes.
And if you make discovery into possession, you will prove Va-Maera right about you.''\par

That silenced me.\par

His voice softened slightly, which made it more dangerous.\par

``You found what most never find, Sa-Kailan.
I will not call it well done.
Well done is not what ruins a boy who has done it --- being seen before the seeing is ready is that.
Do not need it.''\par

I looked up the stair toward the Hall.
The Festival had hidden me because everyone else had looked where Faith told them to look.
If I returned now, I would look like any other Candidate coming out of a side passage after duty.
The Mountain would not change around me.
Not yet.\par

``And the Chamber?''\par

``You will enter again when summoned.''\par

``When?''\par

``When the Chamber's existence no longer belongs to your appetite.''\par

I took the blow because I had earned it.\par

``And the waves?''\par

He looked at me for a long moment.\par

``You will think about them.''\par

``But I will not touch the devices.''\par

``Not alone.
Not yet.''\par

``I agree.''\par

He nodded once.\par

Then, almost too quietly to hear beneath the Festival's rising noise, he said, ``A Candidate learns where light falls.''\par

I answered by instinct.\par

``A Veil learns who placed it.''\par

``No,'' Va-Raedin said.
``That is the sentence boys repeat before they know what it costs.
A Veil learns that the path he found was placed before he was born, and that finding it binds him before it honours him.
A door like this one opens once, and only toward you.
What you have seen, you carry now --- named for it or not.
There is no Candidate left for you to go back to being.''\par

I felt the words settle around the chamber inside me.\par

Va-Raedin climbed toward the Hall.\par

I stayed below for several breaths, listening to the Festival end above me.\par

I should have felt triumph.\par

You will ask why I did not, Paul. I have had a lifetime to answer, and the answer is still the wave diagrams.\par

I had found the Veil's Chamber.
Ka-Syphiron's rule had been met.
The months of failure had not been failure.
Va-Maera had denied me scripture, and still scripture had led me to the day.
Va-Raedin had forbidden salt, and still mineral water had opened the path.
The five lessons had become a map beneath my hands.\par

And I could not forget the wave diagrams.\par

Where height met height, the water rose.
Where height met hollow, it fell.
Behind an obstacle, the pattern returned in a place the barrier should have kept still.
The hidden side was not empty.
It was written differently.\par

I did not know why that mattered.\par

Va-Raedin did.\par

I had seen it in the silence after I spoke, though I did not know what I had seen.
A line had touched a notch.
A beam had found a surface.
Somewhere inside Light, an answer had moved one step closer to a door I did not yet know existed.\par

Above me, the people gave the final answer of the Festival.\par

``Keep us in shadow until promise.''\par

I closed my hand around the place where the Light token rested inside my robe.\par

I was still a Candidate.\par

For now.\par

The Mountain had not yet changed around the fact.\par

But somewhere behind me --- behind stone, behind water, behind the eye that had finally admitted where it had been placed --- the Veil's Chamber existed.\par

And I had entered it.\par

I spent the day after the Veil's Chamber doing exactly what I had been told to do.
I reported to the eastern practice halls at the appointed hour and was given no lesson.
A senior attendant set me to copy lens inventories in a side alcove where no one else worked.
At midday I was sent to carry sealed lamp oil from the Balance stores to the old gallery.
At second meal I sat with the other candidates, though the talk around me thinned whenever my hand moved too near my cup.
No one asked where I had been the day before.
No one failed to know.
The Mountain had ways of becoming silent around a fact before the fact was spoken.\par

I waited --- for Va-Raedin, for Ka-Syphiron, for correction, explanation, punishment, elevation, dismissal, anything that would take the thing I had done and make it a sequence.
None came.
Men and women of Light crossed my path and adjusted by nearly nothing: a pause too short to name, a glance at my hands, a slight lowering of the voice after I had passed.
Once, near the mirror stores, two attendants stopped speaking when they saw me.
They did not look guilty.
They looked as though I had arrived before the proper line had been read.\par

By evening I had learned that waiting could be sharper than examination.\par

The summons came after the lamps had been reduced.\par

A girl no older than twelve stood at the entrance to the candidate quarters with a small black token on her open palm.
It bore no name, only the sign of Light cut through a circle so narrow that the aperture seemed almost a wound.\par

``Sa-Kailan of Light,'' she said.
``You are expected.''\par

``Where?''\par

``Behind the old gallery.''
Then she closed her fingers over the token, turned, and left.\par

Behind the old gallery.\par

The place was older than the polished eastern halls, and less obedient to the official dignity of Light.
The girl led me through corridors where the stone had darkened under years of oil and careless hands.
Mirrors hung where no teaching arrangement required them; some were cracked and kept anyway.
One passage held a copper frame that had been half-melted and then mounted on the wall, as if failure, properly preserved, became instruction.\par

The lamps were fewer here, but not poorer.
They had been altered, repaired, altered again.
One burned too blue.
Another breathed irregularly behind a hood of beaten tin.
A third showed no visible flame, yet threw a pale bar of light across the ceiling whenever I passed beneath it.\par

The old gallery itself was long and low, with shallow alcoves on one side and blackened panels on the other.
Generations of lamp smoke had climbed the walls and stayed.
The ceiling bore old stains in the shape of experiments no one had admitted aloud.
Sketches had been pinned straight into the stone with copper nails: lens paths, impossible staircases, hands holding lamps, circles cut by straight lines, and three drawings of a goat viewed through increasingly ridiculous arrangements of glass.\par

On a shelf near the end, a row of cracked lenses rested beside a sign written in an old hand:\par

\begin{scripture}
DO NOT DISCARD.
FAILURE STILL BENDS LIGHT.
\end{scripture}

The girl stopped before a narrow door half-hidden behind a hanging cloth so stained with soot that it had ceased pretending to be grey.\par

She knocked once and withdrew before anyone answered.\par

``Come in, Sa-Kailan,'' Ka-Syphiron called from inside.\par

\ornament

\noindent A door behind the gallery, and a keeper waiting in it who was never on the dais. He tells it plainly, the boy who found the room behind the room, and he does not yet hear that he has handed me the very shape of his own suspicion.\par

He hears it a moment later. I watch the thought arrive.\par

``There is always a room behind the room,'' he says, and he is not speaking of the Mountain now. ``A keeper they show you, and a keeper who sends him. For four days I have given my account to a servant who says he is only a servant.'' He leans toward the fire, and the light moves on his face. ``Who is behind you, Paul? Who is this tape for?''\par

It is the best question he has asked, and it is the same question he asked when he wanted the name of the house, and the same again when he wanted to know who keeps Horizon. Three shapes, one question --- and he knows now that it is one question. I can see that he knows.\par

``The account first,'' I say.\par

He almost laughs. ``Of course.''\par

``You are not angry,'' I say, because understanding him is my work. ``You have found my wall three times in one night, and each time you have handed me back the road to it.''\par

``I was taught to follow the light into the wall,'' he says, ``even knowing what following it would cost. You have listened long enough to know that much about me.'' He settles back. ``Ask your questions when the tape is full. I will still be talking.''\par

And that is the thing I did not expect, and have not learned how to send up the line: he sees the trap --- has seen it since the rich house first troubled him --- and walks in anyway, night after night, because the account is the one part of him no quiet room ever trained to stop. I let the fire go down. The reels turn. I do not tell him he is right.\par
