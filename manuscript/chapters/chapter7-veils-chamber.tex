\typesetchapter{Chapter 7}{Chamber}

The Hall bowed. I did not.\par

I came down off the northern tier while the Festival held every head in the Mountain at the same angle, and I have never since found a better cover than a thousand people being holy at once. Faith had spent an age teaching them where to put their eyes. Light had spent seven years teaching me to look where nobody had been told to.\par

A Faith attendant met me in the side passage behind the tier, a covered bowl against her hip.\par

``Candidate?''\par

``Light service,'' I said.\par

The lie was small. It entered me anyway, and it is still in there; you may set that down as the first thing the Chamber cost me, and it was not the largest.\par

She stepped aside, because Light moved during ceremony, and because the Mountain was built out of permissions nobody had time to examine.\par

\ornament

The pillar of Light stood three paces inside the arch, and the beam was in it.\par

I had watched it come down out of the upper aperture and strike the carved division of Day from Night, and I had thought, up on the tier, that some hand had set a mirror inside a pillar for the length of one morning. I was wrong about the morning. The mirror had been in that stone since before my great-grandfathers were named, waiting twice in the year for a sun to find it, and no one had ever thought to mention it to me, because every man who knew had come this way himself.\par

Reflection. The first of the five, and it was handed to me for nothing, in front of three thousand people, by a woman saying \textit{let there be light}.\par

I followed it down.\par

\ornament

The beam came out at the pillar's foot and fell on the floor of the passage, and I stopped as though I had been struck across the chest.\par

It did not make a spot.\par

It made rings.\par

A small disc at the centre; and around the disc, ring upon ring of light and dark, close-set, even, spreading outward and going faint at the rim --- the rings a dropped stone leaves on a still pool, and never moving.\par

Something went through me then that I did not have time to open, and I have wondered since what my life would have been if I had stopped and opened it. I had seen that shape before. I could not think where, and the sun was moving, and a man in a hurry will file a miracle for later.\par

So I did what I had been trained to do, which was to ask the plainest question in Light.\par

Why is there a pattern where there should be a beam?\par

Because something is in the way.\par

Shadow. Distrust absence.\par

I looked up.\par

There was an insert in the ceiling: a thin disc of the pale metal, held on a stem, set square in the beam's path a little above my head. It was in the light. It was casting all of it --- the rings, the dark, the whole trembling pool of it on the floor. That was the whole of the matter. There was no theft, no channel, no second room drinking off the top. A thing stood in a beam and threw a shadow, and the shadow had rings in it.\par

I reached up and took it out of its seat with a thumbnail.\par

It had been made to come out. The seat was worn smooth, and not by my thumb.\par

The rings went out of the world. What lay on the floor was a clean disc of white light with an edge you could have cut bread against, and the beam above it was whole from the pillar to the stone.\par

I want to be exact about what I felt, because it was not triumph.\par

It was insult.\par

Seven years of chambers. Mirrors on bronze stems, water in bulbs, salt weighed out by the grain, a man ending a morning because I had understood too quickly. And the first true door in my life had asked me for one thing: notice that a thing is standing in the light, and take it out.\par

The instruction had been harder than the test. I did not know yet whether that was mercy or contempt. I know now it was neither. It was the difference between a school and a road.\par

\ornament

The clean disc lay on bare stone and went no further.\par

I got down and put my palm flat on it. Stone. Cold, dry, solid, and the light dying on it.\par

I nearly walked away. I want that written too. The road had shown me a marvel and then stopped, and a Candidate's first instinct at a dead end is to decide that the marvel was the lesson and the stopping was the humility.\par

Then I saw that the stone beneath the disc was not the stone around it. The same grey. The same grain. And a seam no wider than a hair, running round it in a circle so true that only a made thing could hold it.\par

It was a lid.\par

I worked my nails under it and lifted, and it came up light --- far too light for its size, hollowed underneath --- and beneath it a shaft went down into the Mountain, and the beam went down with it, and I could not see the bottom.\par

There was a stair beside the shaft. Narrow, unlit, cut for one body and not for a procession.\par

I took it, and the dark closed over me, and from that moment until the end there was nothing in the world but the beam and what the beam touched. No lamp. No wick. No mercy. The Mountain does not light the road to its own chamber. A man who goes down there goes down carrying his eyes and nothing else, and everything he needs to find he must find in the one line of sun he brought with him.\par

\ornament

The first chamber below was a mirror and a hole and a table.\par

The beam came down the shaft and struck a mirror set at the floor, and the mirror stood it up horizontal again and sent it across the room at the height of my chest, and at the far wall there was a hole, and the hole was too small.\par

Not a little too small. A third of what it needed to be. The beam struck the wall around it in a broad clean ring and only a poor middle of it went through, and what went through went on into the dark beyond and was not enough to do anything with.\par

Focus. Distrust importance.\par

By the mirror stood a table, and on the table lay glass.\par

Discs. Bulbs. Plates thick at the middle and plates thin at the middle; some that swelled a thing you held behind them and some that shrank it; oil-filled cells and one long crystal bar. Twenty pieces, perhaps. No instruction. No marks. No order. Just glass on a table in the dark, in the last hour of a day that came twice a year.\par

I took up the first disc and put it in the beam and the light drew together and crossed itself a hand beyond the glass and opened out again wider than before, and struck the wall in a circle the size of my head, and less of it went through the hole than had gone through with no glass at all.\par

That is what a converging glass does, if you let it finish. It gathers, and it crosses, and then it throws the light away again on the other side, larger and thinner than it began, and a boy who stops at the crossing thinks he has made light stronger and a man who does not stop learns he has only moved the place where the trouble is.\par

So I put a second glass beyond the crossing --- one of the thin-waisted ones, the kind that spreads --- and caught the light while it was still opening and stood it up straight again.\par

And the beam came out of the pair narrow.\par

Narrow, and parallel, and going nowhere but forward.\par

I moved the second glass a thumb's width and the beam widened. A thumb's width back and it pinched and began to spread again beyond. There is one distance between two such glasses at which the light comes out neither opening nor closing, and it is not a distance you calculate in the dark with your heart going; it is a distance you walk the glass to, by hand, watching the far wall, until the ring around the hole thins and thins and goes out.\par

The ring around the hole went out.\par

The whole beam went through the hole.\par

I have built better instruments since, in rooms with clocks and rulers, and I have never once had that feeling again --- the feeling of a made thing agreeing with you in the dark.\par

\ornament

I opened the door under the hole and went through and shut it behind me, because a man who leaves a door open in Light has told the room he is coming back, and I did not know yet whether I was.\par

The beam came through with me, and it had changed.\par

It was the same light. Not one part of it had been thrown away; I had lost nothing to the glass but the little the glass always keeps. It was the same sun, the same quantity, the same everything --- and it was now the thickness of two fingers instead of a forearm, and where it fell it fell hard. The same amount of light in a smaller place is not the same light in the way that matters. It burns. I put my hand in it and took my hand out again.\par

That, and not the mirrors, is the first secret of Light, and the Mountain teaches it to no one under nineteen: you cannot make more of it. You can only decide where to put it.\par

\ornament

The second chamber turned.\par

The beam crossed to a vessel standing on a plinth in the middle of the floor: a tank of clear glass, and it was not square. It was three-sided. A wedge, the width of a man's chest, with its point turned away toward the right-hand wall.\par

Turning. Distrust the path.\par

It was empty, and there was a jar in the recess beside it, sealed with green wax, with the cup and the sealed jar pressed deep into it and a narrow black witness line laid across.\par

Of course. Curiosity is not free. It is invoiced.\par

I broke the seal, and felt it break somewhere in the record as well, and poured. The water went in thick with mineral and stood in the wedge, and the beam entered the first face and leaned, and crossed the water, and came out through the second face and leaned again --- and two small leanings, taken one after the other in the right order, put the light down on the right-hand wall a long way from where it had been going.\par

Not far enough.\par

I have never in my life wanted a number so badly. I had a wedge of glass and a jar of Balance's water and no measure at all, and the turn was short of the right-hand aperture by the width of a hand, and the sun was going.\par

So I did the forbidden thing, and I did it without a heartbeat of hesitation, and I have never been able to make that sound better than it was: I scraped the dried mineral off the rim with two fingers and stirred it in.\par

The turn opened. The light walked along the wall toward the aperture and stopped short.\par

More. Another scrape. It walked again.\par

Heavier water turns light further. I had been shown that in a warm room by a man who then took the bowl away from me before I could ask a single question about why, and had forbidden me salt, vessel, basin and bowl for the rest of my natural life; and here in the dark under the Hall his own people had left me a wedge and a jar and no instructions, and the only way through the room was the thing he had told me never to touch.\par

I do not think that is hypocrisy. I think it is the whole of what an office is. You forbid a boy the knife until the day the road requires it, and on that day you leave the knife on the table and go away and let him decide what he has become.\par

The beam went into the aperture, square and clean.\par

It turned the corner of the Mountain, and I followed it.\par

\ornament

The third chamber was the one that broke me open, and it is also the one I have the least right to describe, because for most of it I was on my knees in the dark with my hands in water and I could not see anything at all except a line of light doing something no one had told me light could do.\par

A mirror took the beam and threw it straight down at the floor. A second mirror, set in the floor, threw it straight back up. And rising, it went into the underside of a long tank of liquid --- and stayed in.\par

Threshold return. Distrust the surface.\par

It came up at the skin of the water from beneath and did not come out. It turned back down. It struck the mirror and came up and did not come out and turned back down. The tank held a bright pale thread crossing itself over and over inside its own water, going nowhere, in a black room, in silence, with the whole light of the year inside it and no way out.\par

I knew this. This was the trough. This was the last of the five and I had solved it once, and the way to solve it was salt.\par

So I remixed.\par

I want you to understand what that is, because I did not, and I had been taught it. It is not a door swinging. There is no moment of the light \textit{beginning} to leave. Below the condition, the surface is a mirror and gives you nothing --- not a glimmer, not a hint, nothing. It is perfectly closed. And then it is not. The very next thing that happens, the light is already out, and out flat, running away along the top of the water so nearly level with the surface that it is barely a departure at all.\par

That is the whole cruelty of it. There is no warning and there is no gradient. It is refusal, refusal, refusal, and then it is gone from you, sideways, at the last possible angle, and if you were not watching the exact place where it would first appear you have missed the only announcement you were going to get.\par

I missed it twice.\par

The third time I put my chin on the stone at the level of the water and did not blink, and I stirred with two fingers, and the surface gave up a thread flat along itself, and I nearly wept.\par

And then I understood the room, and the understanding is the reason I am telling you all of this.\par

I kept stirring.\par

The thread came up off the water. Not much. A finger's rise for a scrape of mineral. Another scrape and it rose again, and it was no longer running along the surface, it was leaving it, and climbing, and it went across that black room like something being born, and it climbed and it climbed --- and it struck a mirror on the far wall that I had never known was there, because until that instant there had been nothing in the universe to show it to me.\par

And the mirror threw it to another.\par

And that one threw it to another.\par

I got up off my knees.\par

They came out of the dark one after another as the light found them, five, six, high up, low down, across the whole width of that room, a road of them, each one lit for the half-breath it took to hand the beam on to the next, and the room drew itself for me out of nothing in the time it takes to say it --- and the last of them put the light through an aperture in the left-hand wall and the whole thing went dark again behind it, and everything I had just seen went back to being stone I could not see in a room I could not see.\par

I have been in the Hall of Ceremony under a thousand lamps. I have stood in front of the Mirror with every beam in the Mountain ending on the wall in front of me. Nothing my people ever built has done to me what that black room did in four seconds.\par

They had not hidden the road.\par

They had hung it, complete, in the dark, and left it for a man to \textit{raise} out of the water by hand, one grain at a time, and see once, and lose.\par

I went through the left-hand aperture.\par

\ornament

The Veil's Chamber was a disappointment. I will say that plainly, because everything that happened afterwards was built on the correction of it.\par

It was low and cold and there was nothing holy in it. No altar. No carved angel. No crown of lamps. No window onto Heaven. Walls smoothed by hand and never polished, and a floor worn in one path from the door.\par

The beam came in from the aperture and crossed the room and put its clean two-finger disc on the far wall.\par

And the far wall was carved.\par

Cut into the stone and filled with white paste, in a circle wider than my two arms: a small disc at the centre, and around the disc, ring upon ring, close-set, even, spreading out and going faint toward the rim.\par

The beam's disc sat exactly in the middle of it. Exactly. The centre of the light on the centre of the carving, and all those rings around it holding nothing at all, empty stone, a pattern with no light in it and no reason to exist.\par

I stood in front of that wall for a long time.\par

I had seen it on the floor of the passage upstairs an hour ago and filed it for later.\par

I had carried it against my ribs for seven years.\par

And there was a thing between me and the wall that I had not looked at twice, because it was not grand and I had come there for grandeur: a stem rising out of the floor to the height of my chest, and on the stem a shallow cradle of pale metal, empty, set directly under the beam's path.\par

I did not understand it. It held nothing. It was not an altar and it was not a lamp. It was a socket, waiting, and the whole room had been built around it, and it was the only object in the Veil's Chamber of Light.\par

\ornament

``You found it.''\par

He was in the doorway.\par

I do not know how long he had been there. His robe had dust at the shoulder. He was breathing harder than I had heard him breathe in seven years, and he did not look at me at all. He looked at the wall, the way a man looks at a thing he has decided not to be afraid of any more.\par

``Va-Raedin---''\par

``Be quiet a moment. I am going to say something to you badly, and I would rather say it badly than not at all.''\par

He came and stood beside me.\par

``This is going to be one of the important hours of your life. You should know that while it is happening, because almost no one ever does.'' He was still looking at the wall. ``You have come down a road that took me nine days to find and cost you a morning, and you did it cleanly, and you are standing there thinking that you have finally arrived at the bottom of Light. That you understand it now. Mirrors, shadow, glass, turning, threshold. The five. You think the five is the floor.''\par

``Veil---''\par

``Nothing prepares you for the next part. Nothing. I was not prepared, and I had a better teacher than you have, and I was older than you are. It has been a decade and I am not over it. I do not expect to get over it.'' Then he turned his head and looked at me, and I have thought about that look every year since. ``Are you ready?''\par

``I do not know.''\par

``Good. That is the only honest answer, and it will not help you.'' He nodded at the stem. ``The sun is going. You have perhaps forty breaths. Before the beam goes out --- put your token on the cradle.''\par

I had it in my fist. I had been holding it since the stair and I had not known.\par

I put it in.\par

It fitted the way a bone fits a socket.\par

\ornament

The wall came alive.\par

The beam went through the rings of the token and came out the other side and \textit{opened} --- not scattered, not spilled, not blurred: opened, in order, into a disc and a ring and a dark and a ring and a dark, spreading out across the stone, precise as an argument, and every ring of light lay down inside a ring that had been cut into that wall by a hand that has been dust for longer than my people have had a name.\par

Ring for ring.\par

Dark for dark.\par

Out to the rim.\par

It did not approximate. There was no gap anywhere in it wide enough to lay a hair in. Somebody had known --- exactly, to the width of a hair, in a room with no light in it --- what that piece of metal would do to a beam of the sun on a day two thousand years after they were buried, and they had cut the answer into the wall and gone away.\par

I do not remember sitting down. I remember the floor being cold.\par

Because I knew what a shadow was. A shadow is the shape of a thing that stands in the light. I had taken the disc out of the ceiling an hour before and the rings had gone out of the world with it, and I had thought: \textit{a thing stood in the beam and threw a shadow, and the shadow had rings in it.} And I had let that sentence go past me, because I was in a hurry, and because it is an absurd sentence, and because there is no room in a straight line for rings.\par

Light does not do this.\par

A line cannot do this. A line has a position. That is what a line is for --- it is why the Mirror works, it is why two hundred and fifty-six of them can tell an old man which passage a body crossed at which hour. Everything I had been taught, every lesson, every room, every correction, every proud small triumph of seven years, stood on light being a thing that goes from one place to another place and can be told where to be.\par

And here was the sun, coming through a hole and around an edge, and lying down on a wall in rings.\par

Like water.\par

The token was not a picture of water. That was the thing I had been getting wrong since I was fifteen, and every man in my office had been getting wrong with me, and the ones who had come down here and seen this had never said one word to correct me, because they were not permitted to.\par

It was not a picture of water.\par

It was a picture of \textit{light}, and light does what water does.\par

``There it is,'' Va-Raedin said, very quietly, somewhere above me. ``Yes. That is the face people make.''\par

\ornament

The beam went out while I was still on the floor.\par

It did not fade; the sun simply moved off the pillar seven floors above us and the room shut. He had a lamp, and lit it, and the little yellow flame made the carved wall look like a decoration again, which I found obscene.\par

He took the token out of the cradle himself and put it into my hand and closed my fingers on it.\par

``Now listen, because we have very little time and you are of no use to anybody in this condition.''\par

``It spreads,'' I said. ``When it is narrowed. It spreads.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``Two openings would cross. They would cross like the water in the bowl. There would be places with nothing---''\par

``Yes.''\par

``Behind an obstacle---''\par

``\textit{Yes.}'' It came out of him like a door slamming. Then, level again: ``And you will not test one word of it.''\par

I looked up at him.\par

``You will not touch the cradle again. You will not scrape the paste out of that wall. You will not reproduce any part of this in a washing bowl, a jar, a cup, a channel, or a puddle in the goat pen. You will not ask Balance for mineral water. You will not ask Faith whether their singing crosses. You will not ask Shield what stands behind a barrier. You will not ask Justice whether two causes may cancel one another. Do you hear the shape of the prohibition?''\par

I heard it.\par

Every other Kahir in the Mountain had just become dangerous by relation. Balance water. Faith sound. Shield barriers. Justice causes. Light waves. The Chamber was not the bottom of Light. It was Light standing in the middle of the Hall and pointing at every other charge in the building and saying: \textit{that too, and that too, and that too.}\par

``Yes,'' I said.\par

``No.''\par

``\ldots{} I agree.''\par

``Better.''\par

He was already moving --- resetting something at the base of the stem, quick and practised and without one moment of reverence, which after what I had just seen felt like a man wiping his boots on scripture. He sealed the Balance jar with new wax out of a fold in his sleeve. On the way up he put the disc back into its seat in the ceiling of the passage with his thumb, in the dark, without looking at it, and the rings came back onto the floor behind us as we walked away, and he did not turn his head.\par

``You have done this before.''\par

``I found the Chamber before you.''\par

``That is not what I meant.''\par

``It is the answer you receive.''\par

\ornament

The way out was not the way in. He did not take me back up the shaft. He opened a service way behind the third chamber and walked me up a stair that had lamps in it, and handrails, and a smell of cold metal and old water, and I understood that the road I had bled down had a corridor running beside it the whole way, for the people entitled to know it.\par

Discovery had felt like a door. Permission turned out to be a passage built long before my courage.\par

At the foot of the stair he stopped. Above us the Festival was giving its last answer and three thousand people were getting to their feet, and not one of them knew that the floor of their holiest hour had been open for an afternoon.\par

``You will say nothing.''\par

``To whom?''\par

``To anyone.''\par

``Ka-Syphiron---''\par

``I will speak to Ka-Syphiron.''\par

``It is mine,'' I said. ``I found it. I took the disc out of the ceiling and I built the glass and I raised the light out of the water with my own hands, and you were not there for any of it. Let me tell him. Let me be the one who---''\par

I stopped, because I could hear it.\par

``Finish the sentence,'' Va-Raedin said. ``No? Then I will. \textit{Let me be the one who is seen.}''\par

The wall of the passage was very close and very cold.\par

``You have had the best hour of your life,'' he said, ``and you have spent the last minute of it asking to be congratulated. That is not a small fault, Sa-Kailan. That is the fault. Everything they will one day do to you will come in through that door, and it will not even have to knock.''\par

``And what am I now?''\par

``Wait.''\par

``For how long?''\par

``Until the Chamber's existence no longer belongs to your appetite.''\par

He climbed two steps and stopped, and did not turn round.\par

``A Candidate learns where light falls,'' he said.\par

``A Veil learns who placed it,'' I answered, because I had been saying it since before I could reason.\par

``No. That is the sentence boys repeat before they know the price of it. A Veil learns that the path he found was laid before he was born, and that finding it binds him before it honours him. That door opens once, and only toward you. You are carrying it now, named for it or not. There is no Candidate left for you to go back to being.''\par

He went up into the noise.\par

\ornament

I spent the day after the Veil's Chamber doing exactly what I had been told to do.\par

I reported to the eastern practice halls at the appointed hour and was given no lesson. A senior attendant set me to copy lens inventories in a side alcove where no one else worked. At the Third hour I was sent to carry sealed lamp oil from the Balance stores to the old gallery. At second meal I sat with the other candidates, though the talk around me thinned whenever my hand moved too near my cup. No one asked where I had been the day before. No one failed to know. The Mountain had ways of becoming silent around a fact before the fact was spoken.\par

I waited --- for Va-Raedin, for Ka-Syphiron, for correction, explanation, punishment, elevation, dismissal, anything that would take the thing I had done and make it a sequence. None came. Men and women of Light crossed my path and adjusted by nearly nothing: a pause too short to name, a glance at my hands, a slight lowering of the voice after I had passed. Once, near the mirror stores, two attendants stopped speaking when they saw me. They did not look guilty. They looked as though I had arrived before the proper line had been read.\par

By evening I had learned that waiting could be sharper than examination.\par

The summons came after the lamps had been reduced.\par

A girl no older than twelve stood at the entrance to the candidate quarters with a small black token on her open palm. It bore no name, only the sign of Light cut through a circle so narrow that the aperture seemed almost a wound.\par

``Sa-Kailan of Light,'' she said. ``You are expected.''\par

``Where?''\par

``Behind the old gallery.'' Then she closed her fingers over the token, turned, and left.\par

Behind the old gallery.\par

The place was older than the polished eastern halls, and less obedient to the official dignity of Light. The girl led me through corridors where the stone had darkened under years of oil and careless hands. Mirrors hung where no teaching arrangement required them; some were cracked and kept anyway. One passage held a copper frame that had been half-melted and then mounted on the wall, as if failure, properly preserved, became instruction.\par

The lamps were fewer here, but not poorer. They had been altered, repaired, altered again. One burned too blue. Another breathed irregularly behind a hood of beaten tin. A third showed no visible flame, yet threw a pale bar of light across the ceiling whenever I passed beneath it.\par

The old gallery itself was long and low, with shallow alcoves on one side and blackened panels on the other. Generations of lamp smoke had climbed the walls and stayed. The ceiling bore old stains in the shape of experiments no one had admitted aloud. Sketches had been pinned straight into the stone with copper nails: lens paths, impossible staircases, hands holding lamps, circles cut by straight lines, and three drawings of a goat viewed through increasingly ridiculous arrangements of glass.\par

On a shelf near the end, a row of cracked lenses rested beside a sign written in an old hand:\par

\begin{scripture}
DO NOT DISCARD.
FAILURE STILL BENDS LIGHT.
\end{scripture}

The girl stopped before a narrow door half-hidden behind a hanging cloth so stained with soot that it had ceased pretending to be grey.\par

She knocked once and withdrew before anyone answered.\par

``Come in, Sa-Kailan,'' Ka-Syphiron called from inside.\par
