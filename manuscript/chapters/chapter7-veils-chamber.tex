\typesetchapter{Chapter 7}{Chamber}

The Hall bowed. I did not.\par

I came down off the northern tier while the Festival held every head in the Mountain at the same angle, and I have never since found a better cover than a thousand people being holy at once. Faith had spent an age teaching them where to put their eyes. Light had spent seven years teaching me to look where nobody had been told to.\par

A Faith attendant met me in the side passage behind the tier, a covered bowl against her hip.\par

``Candidate?''\par

``Light service,'' I said.\par

The lie was small. It entered me anyway, and it is still in there; you may set that down as the first thing the Chamber cost me, and it was not the largest.\par

She stepped aside, because Light moved during ceremony, and because the Mountain was built out of permissions nobody had time to examine.\par

\ornament

The pillar of Light stood three paces inside the arch, and the beam was in it.\par

I had watched it come down out of the upper aperture and strike the carved division of Day from Night, and I had thought, up on the tier, that some hand had set a mirror inside a pillar for the length of one morning. I was wrong about the morning. The mirror had been in that stone since before my great-grandfathers were named, waiting twice in the year for a sun to find it, and no one had ever thought to mention it to me, because every man who knew had come this way himself.\par

Something went through me then that I did not have time to open, and I have wondered since what my life would have been if I had stopped and opened it. I had seen that shape before. I could not think where, and the sun was moving, and a man in a hurry will file a miracle for later.\par

So I did what I had been trained to do, which was to ask the plainest question in Light.\par

Why is there a pattern where there should be a beam?\par

I looked up.\par

It was casting all of it --- the rings, the dark, the whole trembling pool of it on the floor. That was the whole of the matter. There was no theft, no channel, no second room drinking off the top. The rings went out of the world. What lay on the floor was a clean disc of white light with an edge you could have cut bread against, and the beam above it was whole from the pillar to the stone.\par

I want to be exact about what I felt, because it was not triumph.\par

It was insult.\par

Seven years of chambers. Mirrors on bronze stems, water in bulbs, salt weighed out by the grain, a man ending a morning because I had understood too quickly. And the first true door in my life had asked me for one thing: notice that a thing is standing in the light, and take it out.\par

The instruction had been harder than the test. I did not know yet whether that was mercy or contempt. I know now it was neither. It was the difference between a school and a road.\par

The clean disc lay on bare stone and went no further.\par

I got down and put my palm flat on it. Stone. Cold, dry, solid, and the light dying on it.\par

I nearly walked away. I want that written too. The road had shown me a marvel and then stopped, and a Candidate's first instinct at a dead end is to decide that the marvel was the lesson and the stopping was the humility.\par

Then I saw that the stone beneath the disc was not the stone around it. The same grey. The same grain. And a seam no wider than a hair, running round it in a circle so true that only a made thing could hold it.\par

It was a lid.\par

I worked my nails under it and lifted, and it came up light --- far too light for its size, hollowed underneath --- and beneath it a shaft went down into the Mountain, and the beam went down with it, and I could not see the bottom.\par

There was a stair beside the shaft. Narrow, unlit, cut for one body and not for a procession.\par

I took it, and the dark closed over me, and from that moment until the end there was nothing in the world but the beam and what the beam touched. No lamp. No wick. No mercy. The Mountain does not light the road to its own chamber. A man who goes down there goes down carrying his eyes and nothing else, and everything he needs to find he must find in the one line of sun he brought with him.\par

By the mirror stood a table, and on the table lay glass.\par

Discs. Bulbs. Plates thick at the middle and plates thin at the middle; some that swelled a thing you held behind them and some that shrank it; oil-filled cells and one long crystal bar. Twenty pieces, perhaps. No instruction. No marks. No order. Just glass on a table in the dark, in the last hour of a day that came twice a year.\par

I took up the first disc and put it in the beam and the light drew together and crossed itself a hand beyond the glass and opened out again wider than before, and struck the wall in a circle the size of my head, and less of it went through the hole than had gone through with no glass at all.\par

That is what a converging glass does, if you let it finish. It gathers, and it crosses, and then it throws the light away again on the other side, larger and thinner than it began, and a boy who stops at the crossing thinks he has made light stronger and a man who does not stop learns he has only moved the place where the trouble is.\par

So I put a second glass beyond the crossing --- one of the thin-waisted ones, the kind that spreads --- and caught the light while it was still opening and stood it up straight again.\par

And the beam came out of the pair narrow.\par

Narrow, and parallel, and going nowhere but forward.\par

I moved the second glass a thumb's width and the beam widened. A thumb's width back and it pinched and began to spread again beyond. There is one distance between two such glasses at which the light comes out neither opening nor closing, and it is not a distance you calculate in the dark with your heart going; it is a distance you walk the glass to, by hand, watching the far wall, until the ring around the hole thins and thins and goes out.\par

The ring around the hole went out.\par

The whole beam went through the hole.\par

I have built better instruments since, in rooms with clocks and rulers, and I have never once had that feeling again --- the feeling of a made thing agreeing with you in the dark.\par
