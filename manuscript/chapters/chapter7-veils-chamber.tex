\typesetchapter{Chapter 7}{Chamber}

The Hall bowed. I did not.\par

I came down off the northern tier while the Festival held every head in the Mountain at the same angle, and I have never since found a better cover than a thousand people being holy at once. Faith had spent an age teaching them where to put their eyes. Light had spent seven years teaching me to look where nobody had been told to.\par

A Faith attendant met me in the side passage behind the tier, a covered bowl against her hip.\par

``Candidate?''\par

``Light service,'' I said.\par

The lie was small. It entered me anyway, and it is still in there; you may set that down as the first thing the Chamber cost me, and it was not the largest.\par

She stepped aside, because Light moved during ceremony, and because the Mountain was built out of permissions nobody had time to examine.\par

\ornament

The pillar of Light stood three paces inside the arch, and the beam was in it.\par

I had watched it come down out of the upper aperture and strike the carved division of Day from Night, and I had thought, up on the tier, that some hand had set a mirror inside a pillar for the length of one morning. I was wrong about the morning. The mirror had been in that stone since before my great-grandfathers were named, waiting twice in the year for a sun to find it, and no one had ever thought to mention it to me, because every man who knew had come this way himself.\par

Reflection. The first of the five, and it had been handed to me for nothing, in front of three thousand people, by a woman saying \textit{let there be light}.\par

I followed it down.\par

The beam came out at the pillar's foot and fell on the floor of the passage, and I stopped as though I had been struck across the chest.\par

It did not make a spot.\par

It made rings.\par

A small disc at the centre; and around the disc, ring upon ring of light and dark, close-set, even, spreading outward and fading at the rim --- the rings a dropped stone leaves on a still pool, and never moving.\par

I knew the shape. I had carried it against my ribs for four years.\par

I got the token out of my robe with hands that had stopped belonging to me and held it down beside the thing on the floor. They were not similar. They were the same, ring for ring.\par

I had been given a picture of what light does, at fifteen, for opening a room; and told to keep it, and not to lose it, and not to show it; and I had spent four years asking the metal what it was for.\par

It was not a sign of Light.\par

It was a portrait of it.\par

\ornament

Then I looked up, because by then I was a man who looked up.\par

There was an insert in the ceiling of the passage: a polished plate the length of my forearm, set flush and set at an angle, exactly where the beam passed under it. It was doing the thing Light does everywhere in the Mountain and calls upkeep. It was taking a portion. A share of the one holy beam of the year went sideways out of that plate into stone I could not see, and what came down to the floor came down short.\par

Contraband light. I had named it at fifteen, in the dark, proud of myself, and I had never once thought to look for it here --- in the Hall's own throat, on the Hall's own day, off God's own beam.\par

The plate came out of its seat with a thumbnail. It had been made to come out. The seat was worn smooth, and not by my thumb.\par

Behind me the rings on the floor hardened and brightened, and I understood that whoever came this way was expected to lift the tax off before he went down; that the Mountain had built the theft into the road, and then built the undoing of the theft into the test, and that both of these had been done by the same patient people, and that I was not the first to stand under that ceiling with a plate in his hand and a decision in his mouth.\par

I put it in my sleeve.\par

\ornament

The rings lay on bare stone and went no further.\par

I got down and put my palm flat where the disc sat. Stone. Cold, dry, solid, and the light dying on it.\par

I nearly walked away. I want that written. The road had shown me a marvel and then stopped, and a Candidate's first instinct at a dead end is to decide that the marvel was the lesson and the ending was the humility.\par

Then I saw that the stone beneath the rings was not the stone around it. The same grey. The same grain. And a seam no wider than the hair-hole in my token, running round it in a circle so true that only a made thing could have held it.\par

It was a lid.\par

I worked my nails under it and lifted, and it came up light --- far too light for its size, hollowed underneath --- and beneath it the shaft went down into the Mountain, and the beam went down with it, and I could not see the bottom.\par

There was a stair beside the shaft. Narrow, unlit, cut for one body and not for a procession.\par

I took it.\par

\ornament

The first chamber below had nothing in it, and that was the trouble.\par

The beam crossed it at head height and struck a bronze door that had no handle, no hinge, no keyhole and no seam, and there it stopped. I searched that room until my hands were black and found only the door.\par

Shadow. Distrust absence.\par

Four brackets on the wall. Four shadows on the floor. And one of the shadows was wrong --- not missing, wrong: short by a hand, as though the bracket that threw it stood nearer the lamp than it did. Something was interrupting the interruption.\par

I put my hand into the false dark and the air kept going where the wall should have been. A recess, cut behind the shadow's own edge, invisible from any place a man would ever think to stand. Inside it, cold, a ring.\par

I did not pull it.\par

I would like the credit for that pause and I will not take it, because I know what the pause was. It was not discipline. It was a cord in a dark room, and a panel opening behind me with a hard click, and a man saying \textit{Shadow hid the cord; pride pulled it}, and the whole of my life since arranged so as never to be that boy in front of that man again.\par

I listened. Above me, three floors of stone away, the Mountain answered Faith in one voice: \textit{Keep us from wandering.}\par

Then I pulled, slowly.\par

The door did not open. A shutter went up inside the wall, and the beam went through the place the bronze had been standing in, and I saw that the door had never been a door at all. It was a stopper. It had been holding back the light and not me.\par

\ornament

The next chamber had the mark in it.\par

A white dot, no bigger than a seed, painted on black stone at the far end; and the beam falling a hand to the left of it, and not moving, because the sun does not negotiate.\par

Focus. Distrust importance.\par

There was no glass in that room. I searched it twice. No disc, no bulb, no oil jar, no plate, nothing hung on a bronze stem, nothing set in a niche at knee height for a clever boy to crouch at. And the light coming down the shaft had begun to go long and yellow, and I could feel the year turning under my feet.\par

So I stopped searching the room, because the room was not where the lens was.\par

I took the token out of my robe.\par

Rings, close and even, spreading to the rim. A pale metal that had never taken a breath of tarnish and that no file we owned could mark. And at the centre, where the disc sat solid, a hole no wider than a hair, pierced clean through, for which in four years I had never once found a reason.\par

I held it up into the beam.\par

The light went through the rings and came out the other side \textit{gathered}. Drawn to a point, hard and small and bright as a struck spark, a hand's breadth beyond the metal. I moved my arm and the spark walked across the black stone, and I set it down on the white dot, and the dot answered, and the wall opened.\par

I stood there with my arm still out.\par

Not for the wall. For the token.\par

It was not proof that I had opened a room. It was the thing that opened the next one. Every Candidate who had ever earned it had been handed the key to a door he had not been told existed, and told to keep it and not to show it, and never once told what it was. And Va-Raedin had said, \textit{it is not easily made, and I will not cut you another}, and I had heard a teacher being stern with a treasure.\par

He had been telling me the plain truth. They could not make another. They had never been able to make one. It was older than the office it marked, and older than the men who handed it on, and every one of them had passed it to a stranger the way a man passes on a word in a language nobody left alive can read.\par

\ornament

The passage dropped again, and the air came up at me smelling of old water and cold metal.\par

The fourth chamber was a basin: dry, shallow, a mineral ring dried round its inner edge, a small stopper at its lowest point. The beam came in through a cut above and fell on bare stone, missing the far aperture by half a hand.\par

Turning. Distrust the path.\par

In a recess stood a sealed jar. Balance green, and the cup and the sealed jar pressed deep into the wax, and a narrow black witness line laid across it.\par

Of course. Curiosity is not free. It is invoiced.\par

I broke the seal, and felt it break somewhere in the record as well, and poured. The water came out thick with mineral and there was not enough of it. It spread along the curve and lay under the beam, and the light leaned, and did not lean far enough.\par

I scraped the last of the mineral round the bottom with two fingers and poured that in too. The beam leaned further and stopped short of the aperture by the width of a nail.\par

Above me the Hall gave its third response. I had been longer under the floor than the rite had been on it.\par

I had no more water.\par

It was the stopper that saved me, and it saved me by insulting me. The basin did not want more water. It wanted a different shape of water. I turned the plug and let it drain --- not away: outward, under the stone lip --- and the body of it thinned over the centre and banked deep against the rim, and the beam crossed the thick part and turned, and went into the aperture as though it had always intended to.\par

Something shifted its weight inside the wall.\par

I put the empty jar back in its recess. Set that down too. I had just lied to Faith, robbed Balance, stripped a plate off a consecrated ceiling and opened the floor of the Hall of Ceremony during the holiest hour of my people's year --- and I stopped to put a jar back on a shelf. A man will keep any rule that costs him nothing. It is how he goes on believing he has not yet become something.\par

\ornament

The last chamber held water.\par

A black trough ran wall to wall, filled to the lip, so still that it read as a floor. The beam entered beneath the surface and travelled inside the water itself, pale and caught, striking the skin from below at too shallow an angle and turning back down, and crossing itself, and refusing to leave.\par

Threshold return. Distrust the surface.\par

At the far end, above the waterline, stood a bronze plate; and behind the plate a seam; and the seam was the last door; and the light could not get out of the water to touch it.\par

\textit{Make it leave}, he had said, in a warm room with a lever and a rod and a dish of coarse salt, a lifetime ago, when the answer had been mine to fetch.\par

There was no salt here. No lever, no dish, no side vessel. I went the length of that trough twice with my hands and found three marks cut into its side --- high, middle, low --- and the water standing at the high one; a dry channel entering from the wall; and at the bottom a thread of water leaving so slowly that I had to touch it to know that it moved.\par

And then I knelt down and did the only thing in that whole descent I am still willing to be proud of.\par

Nothing.\par

The chamber was not waiting for me. It was running. It had been set to the high mark before the Festival by a hand that knew the sun's business, and it was draining; and as it drained, the surface would fall, and the angle at which the trapped light struck it would open by a hair at a time. There would be one level --- not a level I chose; a level the year chose --- at which the water would let a single thread go.\par

Too early and the light stayed in. Too late and the sun would be off the pillar, and there would be nothing left in the water to release.\par

This was not a door I opened. It was an occasion I had to be present for.\par

I watched the water go down. I have sat through trials since with less in my chest.\par

It touched the middle mark.\par

One thread came up off the surface and struck the bronze. The plate warmed. A line of old resin darkened along the seam. The wall gave by less than a finger.\par

I put both hands into the gap and pulled, and the Mountain let me in.\par

\ornament

I will be honest about the first thing I felt, because everything after it grew out of the correction.\par

Disappointment.\par

It was a low cold room with nothing holy in it. No altar. No carved angel. No crown of lamps. No window onto Heaven. Walls smoothed by hand and never polished. A dry shallow basin at the centre, with five narrow channels running into it from five different heights. Above it, hung on nothing I could see, a black ring of metal pierced with holes so small they read as flaws in the casting.\par

And on the far wall, diagrams: cut into the stone and filled with white paste.\par

Lines. Waves. Circles crossing circles.\par

I went to them the way a man goes to a window.\par

The Candidate rooms had taught light as a path. Source to surface, surface to mark, mark to eye. A line --- a thing with a position, which is the whole reason a Mirror can be built out of two hundred and fifty-six of them and told where every body in the Mountain stands.\par

These did not behave like paths.\par

One showed curved lines entering a narrow opening and spreading on the far side, wider than the opening had any right to give them. One showed two openings set side by side, and what came out of them meeting, and making bands --- strong, absent, strong, absent --- where nothing whatever had been added or taken away. One showed a circular disturbance breaking around a stone set in water and closing again behind it, weaker and still ordered, in the very place the stone should have kept empty. And one showed five channels entering one basin at five heights, and the rings they raised crossing, and the crossing making a shape.\par

Rings.\par

I had the token in my fist. I had been holding it since the third chamber and had not noticed. I opened my hand and looked at the metal and at the wall in the same moment, and four years came apart in me.\par

The rings on the token were not a picture of water.\par

They were a picture of light.\par

\textit{A mark of Light which looked so like water would have set the wrong question moving in the wrong heads.} That was why it hung at no Keeper's throat and stood above no door and appeared in no gravure in the Mountain; why it went only into the hands that had opened the rooms, and was shown to nobody else.\par

Because it is not a sign.\par

It is the answer, cut in metal, and hung at the belt of every boy too young to read it.\par

I stood in that cold room and the floor of my education went out from under me. A beam is not a line. A line is only what a beam becomes after a Candidate has been taught to narrow it until it obeys. Edges do something. Openings do something. Water does not make light strange; water makes light slow enough to be caught at it.\par

And the hidden side of an obstacle is not empty.\par

I did not know what that meant. I knew that my mouth had gone dry, and that everything I had been given in four years had been given to me in a shape small enough to carry and too small to be true.\par

\ornament

``You found it.''\par

He was in the doorway. He had come up behind me through my own road, and there was dust on his shoulder, and he was breathing harder than I had heard him breathe in four years.\par

``Yes.''\par

``You left the Hall during Festival.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``You lied to a Faith attendant.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``You took a plate off a consecrated ceiling.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``You broke a Balance seal.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``You pulled a hidden ring, alone, with no witness.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``You opened this chamber during the holiest hour of the year, with three thousand of your people standing on the stone above your head.''\par

``Yes.''\par

He looked past me at the diagrams for a long moment.\par

``And you reached the correct chamber.''\par

The two truths stood in the room together and did not cancel. I have never forgotten that they did not cancel.\par

``I did what the rule required,'' I said.\par

``No. You did what the rule measures.''\par

The distinction went in too fast to be understood, and stayed.\par

``A Candidate becomes Veil by finding the Veil's Chamber.''\par

``Yes.''\par

``Then I have found it.''\par

``Yes.'' He did not move. ``And now you are learning that success is not innocence.''\par

``Was there another way?''\par

``There is always another way in memory. Rarely in time.''\par

He came in and closed the wall behind us, and the last thread of the Festival went thin in the aperture and gave out, and the room was black until he lit a lamp he had carried down and not troubled to light before.\par

``Name them as you used them.''\par

``Reflection, in the pillar. Shadow, for the ring behind the false dark. Focus'' --- and I stopped, because the next word was the token, and the token was in my hand, and I did not know whether I was reporting or confessing.\par

``Say it.''\par

``Focus. With this.''\par

He looked at the metal in my palm. He did not take it.\par

``Turning, in the basin, with Balance's water. Threshold return, in the trough.'' I heard my own voice go careful. ``I did not open the last one. I waited for it.''\par

Something crossed his face and was gone before I could read it.\par

``That is the only part of this morning I will call well done.''\par

\ornament

``You will not touch the basin. You will not open the channels. You will not scrape the paste out of the diagrams. You will not reproduce any of this in a washing bowl. You will not ask Balance about mineral water. You will not ask Faith whether their singing crosses. You will not ask Shield what stands behind a barrier. You will not ask Justice whether two causes may cancel each other. Do you hear the shape of the prohibition?''\par

I heard it.\par

Every other Kahir had just become dangerous by relation. Balance water. Faith sound. Shield barriers. Justice causes. Light waves.\par

The Chamber was not deeper Light. It was Light pointing across the Hall at every other charge in the Mountain and saying: that too, and that too, and that too.\par

``Yes,'' I said.\par

``No.''\par

``I agree.''\par

He reset the five channels with a narrow tool out of a recess --- quick, practised, and without one moment of reverence, which frightened me more than the diagrams had. He sealed the jar with new wax from a fold in his sleeve. On the way up he set the plate back into the ceiling of the passage with his thumb, in the dark, without looking at it.\par

``You have done this before.''\par

``I found the Chamber before you.''\par

``That is not what I meant.''\par

``It is the answer you receive.''\par

\ornament

The way out was not the way in. He did not take me back up the shaft. He opened a service way behind the trough and walked me up a stair that had lamps in it, and handrails, and a smell of cold metal and old water, and I understood that the road I had bled to find had a corridor running beside it the whole way for the people entitled to know it.\par

Discovery had felt like a door. Permission turned out to be a passage built long before my courage.\par

At the foot of the eastern stair he stopped. Above us the Festival was giving its last answer and the people were getting to their feet, and not one of them knew that a Candidate had gone down through the floor of their holiest hour and come back up through a staircase with handrails.\par

``You will say nothing.''\par

``Not even to Ka-Syphiron?''\par

``I will tell Ka-Syphiron.''\par

``It is my discovery.''\par

``It is Light's Chamber. And if you make finding into owning, you will prove Va-Maera right about you, and she will not have to lift a finger to do it.''\par

That closed my mouth.\par

``A Candidate learns where light falls,'' he said.\par

``A Veil learns who placed it,'' I answered, because I had been saying it since before I could reason.\par

``No. That is the sentence boys repeat before they know the price of it.'' He went up two steps, and stopped, and did not turn round. ``A Veil learns that the path he found was laid before he was born, and that finding it binds him before it honours him. A door like that one opens once, and only toward you. You are carrying it now, named for it or not.''\par

``Then what am I?''\par

``There is no Candidate left for you to go back to being.''\par

He went up into the noise.\par

I stayed at the bottom of the stair in the dark with the token shut in my fist, listening to three thousand people give thanks for the equal light, and not one of them had seen where it went.\par

I thought that meant it had been shown to me.\par
