\typesetchapter{Chapter 23}{Loss}

\begin{founder}
A man tells the truth only at the bottom of the world,\\
where the dark and the deep have taken every ear but his.\\
I have come down to that bottom. Let it be set down here\\
by the one whose name no book that comes after him will keep:\\
\medskip

this I did not inherit; this I did, with my two hands.\\
A mind once stood here, and stood well, and now is gone.\\
We were the summit of the earth, the warm land and the cold;\\
we had named the lights of heaven, and set them in our songs.\\
\medskip

Our dead went down with the small things they had loved.\\
Our tools were older than any man's own memory.\\
We wrote little; we kept the whole world alive in men;\\
and in that keeping lay the one flaw of all of us:\\
\medskip

a house of loyal skulls stays loyal only while they last.\\
We were losing, and I was the first to say the word.\\
No war had come: no blade, no siege, no king thrown down.\\
No one struck us; we were undone by an open door.\\
\medskip

For there is a warmer kind of man than we had been,\\
more numerous, and lower, and their fires never go out;\\
and a people is not held by its walls, nor by its gods,\\
but by whether its daughters stay, and whose the children are.\\
\medskip

And ours would not stay. They went at dusk to the low fires,\\
to a wider room, where a woman might be more than one thing;\\
no one drove them, no one carried them; they went in gladness,\\
and bred into the warm kind, and our summit thinned in theirs.\\
\medskip

Whole cities, enough to fill a sea, have burned to the ground\\
over one choice the chooser could not, after, explain;\\
and we would sooner blame a goddess than name it a whim.\\
And there is a mercy that has forgotten how to count:\\
\medskip

it spends the last warmth of a house on the very guest\\
who came in from the cold to burn it; and we had that mercy,\\
and we called it goodness, and it was the shape of our end.\\
A virtue that cannot survive being practised is no virtue;\\
\medskip

it is only a soft name we give our own disappearance.\\
And restraint is not a virtue either: it is a fuel;\\
a people that will not burn it grows cold, and is taken;\\
and we would not burn it, and we grew cold, and were taken.
\medskip

My father saw it first, and gave the older answer:\\
a people never lives by being loved; break them.\\
He held the metal and the men, the gold to do it,\\
and had feared longer than I; long fear turns to iron.\\
\medskip

I said no: their slaughter would be forever our stain,\\
and a thing so bloodily won is never worth the winning.\\
We argued the years away across one table,\\
and I won cleanly, and it did no good at all.\\
\medskip

Her name was Ithe. I set it against my own rule,\\
for a record missing the true thing is the lie twice.\\
She was of my rank, my people, raised as I was raised,\\
the most complete soul I have known, and never praised.\\
\medskip

She carried my child. My house would go on through her.\\
And an enemy of mine wanted that line ended.\\
They reached her unhappy: bought, or frightened, or lied to.\\
There was a woman of the old craft, who kept the poisons,\\
\medskip

who knew a body's seasons, and the drawing-out.\\
Ithe went to her. The child was ended in a night.\\
Did she choose it, hating the door-less room I built?\\
Or was she a blameless lamp they bore through the dark?\\
\medskip

I had the means that morning to learn the truth of her,\\
whether she wept in the choosing, or only in the cold.\\
I had the means to know. I did not ask. Here is why.\\
Desire is the one power large enough to end an age,\\
\medskip

and it comes to us already wearing the face of fate,\\
so that no one, ever, is made to answer for it;\\
and I would not be the one to make her answer for it.\\
For if she chose, hating the door-less room I built,\\
\medskip

then the rot was in my own house, my own bed, and mine;\\
but if she never chose, if love had walked her down,\\
then the fault was one loose word of an enemy's, and small,\\
and the warm and mine might yet lie down and go on;\\
\medskip

and I would argue it all again, alone, my father dead,\\
and change nothing. So I did not ask. I let her stand\\
guilty; I asked her nothing; and all the world said guilt.
\medskip
The enemy who reached my child had reached my father:\\
they killed him in a well-loved pass, lightly guarded.\\
I stood above his body and said only: you were right.\\
And under the grief, deep in it, was a relief also,\\
\medskip

mine alone, that the war he wanted died there with him.\\
I had Ithe put to death. I gave the word, and watched.\\
I told myself I killed the guilt. I killed the question.\\
For the dead cannot be asked, and I had chosen not to;\\
\medskip

and killing her I sealed the not-asking forever.\\
It was not enough. It never is. The thing was done.\\
So I turned to the one I could still reach: the craft-woman.\\
I burned her, and it warmed me, and it was not enough.\\
\medskip

Then every woman of that craft, in every valley,\\
every keeper of the poison-craft, I took and burned.\\
It was the first fire I set, and I set it in her name.\\
And from the burning I learned the thing my life was built on:\\
\medskip

you cannot burn a knowing from the world; it is taught again;\\
another woman learns it, another fire, another year.\\
What cannot be destroyed can still be broken up,\\
and kept so no one hand may hold the whole again.
\medskip
He was dead, and all he held came to me alone:\\
the metal and the men, the buried knowledge, the estate.\\
I, who refused his war, now held all he would have used;\\
and I raised no army; an army, in the end, loses.\\
\medskip

His answer had been a war. Mine would be a story;\\
and a story, told long enough, outlasts every army.\\
I gathered those who knew the stone, and those who knew the springs,\\
and those who knew the lights, and the making of hard things;\\
\medskip

and soldiers too, for a work so dear must wall its mouth;\\
and I emptied my father's house, and turned it south.\\
And I took children. Set that down. It is the worst seed.\\
A place that must outlast its own making must be young,\\
\medskip

peopled with those who can be told a thing was always so.\\
I said they were being saved, and they were; and also this:\\
they were the first of a new kind of people on the earth,\\
raised to tend a thing they must never grasp, and kneel to it.\\
\medskip

The road south took the better part of a year; I lost them,\\
to the heat, to the sand, to the slow leak of a dead land.\\
Each loss I entered in a reckoning, and did not mourn.\\
We passed the last green, the last water mine had drunk,\\
\medskip

and the last mark of a people, and went into the waste,\\
a waste beyond the wanting of any living thing.\\
And a man swallowed whole never comes back the one who went down:\\
the deep keeps a part of him, and hands the rest a purpose\\
\medskip

he did not choose. The waste swallowed me, and gave me back\\
a founder, with a task in his hands he had not asked to hold.\\
My engineers began to tell me, in frightened voices,\\
that no such place was on the earth, in any land.\\
\medskip

And I answered with a cold thing I had long held true:\\
that a closed world holds, in the end, but two kinds of road ---\\
the loop a man may draw shut to a single point,\\
and the loop that rides a hole forever, and never shuts.\\
\medskip

I drew mine tighter across the dead land, and could not tell\\
whether it would close at last on the place I sought,\\
or catch on a hole in the world where I had set my hope.\\
I hunted a thing that might not be on the whole earth,\\
\medskip

and would not turn; I had gone blind to every road\\
that bent, or ended, or came back short of itself.\\
I went into the desert believing I would save them;\\
and in that I told no lie: my people were undone.\\
\medskip

And someone, truly, had reached into my house and killed.\\
Both were true. And there the ruin of me put down roots:\\
the wound had learned to speak in sentences, and each was true;\\
and a grief that tells you only true things, you follow.
\medskip

We found it the third year out; I knew before my men.\\
For I had left off hunting ground that men desire,\\
and turned to ground no man goes near; the earth gave in.\\
It offered nothing, and its nothing was first defence:\\
\medskip

no green, no game, no road, no reason, a shoulder of stone,\\
and a killing width of distance on every side.\\
It lay far out in a waste that murders men by breadth,\\
and gave nothing to warm folk going south, who never turned.\\
\medskip

Then, under the nothing, season on season, my water-men\\
laid slender rods against the stone, and the stone gave up\\
its buried secrets; the first was water: so much water\\
my chief sat on the rock, put his face in his hands, made no sound.\\
\medskip

More water than a siege could drink, enough to hold a people;\\
years passed before I said aloud what the water was for.\\
And heat beneath it, the old deep heat, shallow enough to reach,\\
a patient fire never wants feeding; sealed folk would not freeze.\\
\medskip

And ways: there were faults, and voids, and the promise of more,\\
of more that could be cut, till a new world hung down the rock.\\
Three years on a road that killed my people, called me mad,\\
and at the end I had the thing, glad past all I dared want.\\
\medskip

We laughed like boys in cool shade, and every rod said yes;\\
a man the world lays tools before feels, in his bones, chosen.\\
And nothing in my long life since has equalled that one year.\\
I saw the other thing as well, deep at a shaft, by lamplight.\\
\medskip

The deep fire never dies; water flashed on it turns to steam;\\
and steam sent down a throat clear through the world becomes a hand,\\
a flat hand laid on that turning world's beating heart, to press it.\\
My engineer, unknowing of my mind, told the force he'd raise;\\
\medskip

and that, though neither of us knew, was the beginning of all.\\
The ground gave up my people's living; and in the same grey stone,\\
the means to do to all the warm earth the thing done to mine,\\
and worse, all at once, and hidden from every watching eye.\\
\medskip

And I told myself, that bright year, that the hand I now could lay\\
upon the warm world would only be a thing of defence.
\medskip

Do not take the building for a wonder; those who came after\\
called it the masonry of God; that is the first lie.\\
It was labour, and mine: the greatest labour raised,\\
gathered in one place, done by living people, one by one,\\
\medskip

brought down that long, killing road across most of my life.\\
It cost; and I paid in the one coin such things are bought with,\\
the lives of others; I kept the account; it is long.\\
We learned the work by failing at it; the first great press,\\
\medskip

built to prove the deep hand answered, killed its builders.\\
They died for what none yet knew: what water does, flashed to steam\\
inside a shut, airless place; I stood at the gallery mouth,\\
watched the broken rest borne out, kept my face still, said nothing.\\
\medskip

I did not halt the work; I went and fixed the drawings:\\
a stair cut near a fault took off a hundred in an hour;\\
a wall let go one night, a shift drowned, set us back ten years.\\
We never learned to meter water till enough men died;\\
\medskip

I did not wait on their dying; each gain was bought, I stood firm.\\
You will want a single number; I have one, for I kept count,\\
kept it true; the one plain virtue I claim with no lie in it.\\
More than forty thousand came down that road while I breathed,\\
\medskip

and fewer than a third would climb it up into the day;\\
the difference between those numbers, done, is a kind of death.\\
Some twenty-seven thousand stayed in the rock they cut,\\
in flood, fall, steam, and the slow grind of a land wanting nothing.\\
\medskip

I built the greatest refuge, to keep my people whole,\\
and built it on a floor made of their bones, and did not slow.\\
When a chief came white with word of another hundred lost,\\
I asked him first whether the work they died on held.\\
\medskip

I watched the hour his love died, let him cease, said nothing,\\
turned my eyes back to the cold drawings; the man went; I stayed.\\
Then I did the thing that let them work to that end, never rise:\\
I saw to it that not one of them could wholly understand.\\
\medskip

And here the burning came back; I had learned it in her name,\\
and I turned it on the work as I had turned it on her:\\
I divided the knowledge, gave no team more than one piece:\\
the water-men knew water, thought they sank a well against siege;\\
\medskip

the light-men knew their bending glass, thought they drew the sun down;\\
those who cut the deep hot throat thought it drainage, theirs alone.\\
The men who raised falling gates thought them against armies;\\
no team of the hundred was let to hold two pieces at once.\\
\medskip

Only my few knew the well, the sun-ways, the drainage, gates\\
were one engine, the engine a weapon aimed through Earth's core.\\
I did not first divide it against an enemy outside;\\
I divided it against the hands within, so that no man,\\
\medskip

alone, should hold enough at once to look on what he made,\\
and, understanding it entire, set it down, turn, refuse.

Five works there were, five teams, and over each a head;\\
the whole split among the five, ungathered, unspoken.\\
I could see, even then, that I would die, and the thing must run\\
long past me, longer than a name is kept; so, before my end,\\
\medskip

I did what turns a day's work to a people, past its founder:\\
I ceased to call the teams by teams; I made them the lines.\\
The team of water, stone, and heat and measure, keeping accounts,\\
I taught to hand it down the years, master to child, in portions,\\
\medskip

with secret signs and pride and jealous keeping, till this team's work\\
grown hard as \emph{Balance}: a name, a blood, a calling, whence none knew.\\
Those who heard the deep return, and knew the timing of strokes,\\
I made them keepers of the holy hour: God in the stone, \emph{Faith}.\\
\medskip

Light-men, who set the shot and aim, I made mystery's keepers,\\
a hidden mystery of alignment, which I named \emph{Light}.\\
And those who held my books, and alone might say a thing may pass,\\
and kept the order of acts none recall, I named \emph{Justice}.\\
\medskip

And the men of gates, containment, secrecy, force at need,\\
I named the men of \emph{Shield}; they never felt it, kept silence.\\
A man does one thing unchanged until the end of ages,\\
if you make him believe the narrow task he does is himself.\\
\medskip

To each line I gave a discipline to mask its true labour;\\
and the discipline grew real knowledge, freely taught, deep, all true.\\
To Balance, honest measure, so the best builder of his age\\
might live and die in his craft, not see the weapon on the page.\\
\medskip

To Faith I gave the stone's deep art, rhythm, resonance, return,\\
and called it a prayer, so the best ear in a hundred years\\
would spend itself hearing God, not knowing it heard a weapon.\\
To Light I gave the wave bent to one burning point of fire,\\
\medskip

holy was its glinting sheath; to Justice I gave the record,\\
the sequence, the weighing if a thing may lawfully proceed;\\
and folded deep in Justice, I hid the leave a firing needs.\\
Truths, the whole, and each separate piece a naked truth;\\
\medskip

I set the truths in a ring round a hole I taught was God.\\
At the lip of it I taught the kneeling soul a holy dread,\\
which I built into the teaching for one purpose, no other:\\
so a curious mind would flinch from the one question, at the hour\\
\medskip

it ripened, fit to ask; I taught them well to flinch, and true.\\
Not one of them, in all the long line, has known he was doing it;\\
of all I made and hid in this Mountain, that is my proudest.
\medskip
Then I did the thing that beats beneath all the rest;\\
I did it for a sound reason, and the soundness is the worst;\\
The machine I made had one deep flaw, the flaw of all my kind:\\
it must live by living persons; and a person turns to dust.\\
\medskip

Call five uncertain hands a throne, that throne a waiting weapon;\\
give them forty quiet years; five afraid and praying priests,\\
who forgot they were engineers, above an engine none runs.\\
I built a test, and hid it in deep devotion, made it law.\\
\medskip

At each succession, when a Keeper died and a new one rose,\\
the people saw the five draw close, in secret, to do a thing\\
I dressed for them as prayer, and they called it covenant.\\
Beneath the words of prayer there was a firing: one small firing,\\
\medskip

large enough to prove the engine still answered when told,\\
and small enough to leave the world above unhurt, unknowing.\\
A few drops of water on the deep fire, done five times,\\
the long slow wait, its due; it knows one measure, no other;\\
\medskip

one stroke off its measure proves a listener nothing.\\
The world, struck five even times in its slow interval,\\
would answer one wait later, one wait behind the falling fifth;\\
that returning note would climb the deep stone to the waiting ear,\\
\medskip

say, to the one ear that knew to hear, the covenant still held.\\
I made it law, and secret too, and holy, all at once,\\
frightened, faithful folk would keep it forever, perfectly,\\
never let it slip past the last who knew the strokes' purpose.\\
\medskip

I called it readiness; told them God confirms His Keeper,\\
and said it did nothing more; in secret truth, the one hour,\\
in which my patient engine reached down, confirmed itself.\\
The first time, in my life, to prove it, I stood in the Hall\\
\medskip

I built to hold and shape that toll, and heard the Earth give it back:\\
five even, heavy blows, a long soft roll behind them, trailing,\\
like a struck world turning over once, heavy, slow, in its sleep.\\
It was the most beautiful, worst thing I made, and wholly mine;\\
\medskip

and I knew, that hour, I gave my cold machine a beating heart,\\
one beat to each generation, sounding on beneath the sand\\
as long as the Mountain kept its place; I knew, standing there,\\
that some far ear, fine enough, would find the body it beat in.

The machine and all its offices were the easy part;\\
the hard part was the thing no later people could invent,\\
being the offspring. A machine in trained and living hands\\
will last few lives, no more; an age on, the trained hands have\\
\medskip

forgotten what the training was for, and the whole is gone.\\
I had not paid my father's end, forty thousand dead in rock,\\
to raise a thing dead in six lives; I set to stop the clock.\\
One method only: I built a people to be the keeping;\\
\medskip

I built their selves to be the keeping, so they could not stop.\\
The children I took were of three kinds: our own kept seed,\\
some bought, and some gathered up off the failing land;\\
and I raised them, every one of them, inside a lie:\\
\medskip

the Mountain always was where I built it; offices holy,\\
where I merely wrote a duty, a roster of plain men;\\
the engine a living god, where my hands made a weapon.\\
I wrote a past, aged it, so the newest child, turning,\\
\medskip

would find more evidence that all was old past any counting,\\
never doubt his days a late leaf on a tree past reckoning.\\
I set among the first some actors who knew the whole truth,\\
who agreed, for love, fear, promise, to spend lives feigning faith;\\
\medskip

next generation, watching the first, believed unfeigned,\\
came to true faith; and by the third, no pretence was left alive,\\
and they knew it wholly, the easy way a child knows the sky.\\
I carved the Genesis alone, in the build's worst nights;\\
\medskip

the aim was plain: the whole outside made a horror to them;\\
it came more easily than I could wish; so no child of mine\\
should want the world outside, I took one true thing, turned it once,\\
turned it a single quarter, the one turn a good lie needs.\\
\medskip

The one true thing was a murder; I kept it, for a real thing\\
anchors each lie; I set the murder in the oldest age,\\
I made the murdered man a brother, loved and envied;\\
I gave the killer's line the open land for inheritance,\\
\medskip

cursed, and many, hating the cool green shade they never find.\\
I gave them a woman too: taken as spoil when the man fled,\\
blameless as a stolen lamp, a thing done to her, not by her.\\
So I cut her in the stone; and what I filed from the marble\\
was the true thing beneath her, which I have set down above:\\
\medskip

that she went, or was led; that I did not ask, and could have;\\
that I killed her for it, and burned the craft in her name.\\
None of that is in the stone. A people dreads a murderer;\\
no people was ever taught to dread a self-brought wound,\\
\medskip

nor a grief revenged on the guiltless to spare one question.\\
So in the carving I let it go; I let her stand in the stone\\
a pure and simple loss; I drowned her choosing in the grave,\\
the grave my father lay in; and I have watched the Keepers\\
\medskip

read her to trembling boys as the whole world's word, in an evening
I will not explain each stone; later people bowed to these,\\
and made a fetish of them; I'll not feed it; a few I give,\\
for you knelt to them, are owed. The great disk swings in the Hall,\\
my late children take for God's working face at weight on the world,\\
\medskip

is a clock, free of god, belonging wholly to the trigger.\\
Its slow round, three days back to where it stood, times the firing,\\
disguised: the strokes' count, slack and holy, a devotion\\
paid to the passing hour; so men who tend the engine below\\
\medskip

take their timing in the open, before the kneeling people,\\
and no suspicion touch the holiest object in the room,\\
which is a lit, slow-burnt fuse, dressed to look like a prayer.\\
The cleared centre of the Hall, the empty ground no foot crosses,\\
\medskip

held most sacred of all, is where a man who stands must die.\\
Kept empty because a man who stood upon that throat of stone\\
when the great stroke came would be destroyed; I'd take no worshipper\\
down into white flame. So I made its danger a holy thing;\\
\medskip

I called the killing-ground a sanctuary; they knelt round it,\\
and worshipped the one thing in the Hall they ought most to flee,\\
and the long worshipping, year on year, is what kept them alive.\\
Two black gravures face each other; the people kneel, penned\\
\medskip

between pictured dawn and undoing; the first stone is the lie.\\
The second, the Cataclysm, is the true thing I let them hold,\\
an honest picture of the work, hid in plain open sight,\\
which people read as prophecy, and leave its diagram unread;\\
\medskip

warning worshipped, year on year, is a warning never read.\\
This deep room, the clock, the ledgers, one door only firing frees,\\
I built it last of all, and told no soul, not even the five;\\
for by degrees, three lives, it sank beneath the offices'\\
\medskip

failing memory, and grew a thing that Justice, to this hour,\\
knows not it once knew; a hidden heart in a hidden body,\\
kept in patient dark, an age, for you. Few stones I read plain;\\
I leave the rest of the heap unspoken, and keep my silence.
\medskip
Why keep a written record, when what I was lay hidden?\\
I have wondered at it, and more than one answer is true,\\
and the true answers I find give each other the lie.\\
I kept it, first, wishing one day to be known and seen;\\
\medskip

and it is the least of reasons, the one that shames me most;\\
for I had made certain no soul within the work would know\\
that a mind had once been there. And then I found I could not,\\
endure to vanish in the work, and leave no heir behind;\\
\medskip

and I wanted one to see the hand; vanity outlasts grief;\\
and across the unbridged abyss of ages, here you are.\\
And I kept it to defend myself; when first I wrote,\\
I still supposed a reader who had seen all I had seen\\
\medskip

would find, at the account's end, I had done what a great man,\\
so placed, could do, and would, in reading, rise and clear my name.\\
I believe that no longer; I wrote too honestly for that;\\
and the clear-eyed reader coming at last to this door will be\\
\medskip

of the kind who uses the door to cast me off; I have found\\
that I mind its coming less than I had long thought I would.\\
And last, the reason I could not own till the two were written:\\
under it all, I wanted a door. Beneath the lord and plan,\\
\medskip

beneath the old wound, a man who knew a thing that never can\\
be stopped should never have been made; a man who could not\\
quite bring himself to build an engine with no way back out.\\
And so I hid a way out; I laid the truth at the bottom,\\
\medskip

behind a door that only firing the weapon can unlock,\\
so it falls to a far age that used the weapon in need;\\
among them, only to an eye trained to see what was set,\\
on purpose, in the dark, the eye my system was designed\\
\medskip

to breed out, and could not. I am an age dead as you read this;\\
and yet I built the door; whatever else you settle of me,\\
hold hard to this one thing: here, at the deep bottom of the worst\\
and darkest thing made, its maker left a door open for you.
\medskip
The end of the long build was cruellest, and I did it cleanly;\\
for I had grown old, and lost the power to feel what I spent,\\
and had gone wholly cold. The tens of thousands who cut it out\\
could not be loosed to bear its pieces out into the world;\\
\medskip

Divided knowledge is safe only kept divided, the hands,\\
scattered wide, grown old apart, will meet, drink, and slowly set\\
the kept-apart pieces back together into one whole.\\
What I did to stop that meeting made me tremble; I won't write.\\
\medskip

Some went from there: sworn hard, scattered across the land, watched;\\
and some stayed, first to labour in the sealed world, and were told\\
that they had always, always lived below. Some did not go.\\
That account is long; I kept it, like the rest; and it remains\\
\medskip

the worst and longest page I own, and I have set it apart\\
from this one; I do not ask you to read it; leave it alone.\\
Believe this much, only this: I could not require the doing\\
of my hand; in my quailing, did it through other men's hands,\\
\medskip

and turned my face away. They were gone, one way or other,\\
the great gates shut, at last, against the world; though the sealing\\
would wait an age beyond mine. Inside was a people, a few\\
thousand souls: kept seed, raised children, and ageing actors;\\
\medskip

on the first grey morning of the sealed world, they woke, and did\\
their offices, said prayers, knelt round the empty forbidden ground.\\
And children born below from then never knew a world\\
stood above them in the light, or be found by any walker;\\
\medskip

and they, in turn, would teach their children so. I stood among them,\\
old, finished, I did it, and watched the lie draw first breath;\\
and it was good; God, God forgive me,\\
it was the most beautiful thing I ever saw:\\
\medskip

a whole new world at dawn, believing it had always been.\\
And I knew it would hold; and that not one soul would thank me,\\
and none would know I had once been real, and walked among them.
\medskip

And yet I would not believe it until I had seen it work:\\
seen the weapon speak, the world go under, the clock reset,\\
the room I built bring the Mountain back up out of its own ash.\\
So I did what no keeper is permitted to know I did.\\
\medskip

I woke the Beast myself, once, with my own hand, and ended\\
the world above, to be certain that it could be ended;\\
and the sky went to ash, and the sealed few rode the long cold out,\\
and the reset ran as I had written it, and did not fail.\\
\medskip

My liturgy came to pass. The children woke to the day\\
I had made for them, and were told the world had always been so.\\
And the great Archive, at the last, was only what I had built:\\
a room, a clock, a way back; nothing holy; and it held.\\
\medskip

Then I knew it would run without me, and would need me no more,\\
forever; and I did the last thing, that none will believe:\\
I left. I walked out of the mouth of the Mountain, alone,\\
into the world I had ended, on my own two feet, to see\\
\medskip

with my own eyes what I had done, and go down and face the deep.\\
Set this here, so that whoever comes will know it of me:\\
the maker did not stay to be worshipped in his own dark.\\
He went out into the ash he made, and did not look away.\\
\medskip

I was Zero, first of us, the maker of you all;\\
whose father the outside killed, whose child the outside took,\\
through the hand of the one I loved, and never asked, and killed;\\
who chose certainty, and would, so wounded, choose it again.\\
\medskip

Go a great deal slower than you mean to, with your cold verdict;\\
for I have walked a long road in the ruin since, and learned\\
the one thing I built the whole of the Mountain never to say.\\
The Beast is not the fire, nor the water, nor the stone.\\
\medskip

I made it in my own image; it is me; and it is loosed;\\
and I go down now to walk the ruin of it, as what I am.\\
I am the Beast.
\end{founder}
