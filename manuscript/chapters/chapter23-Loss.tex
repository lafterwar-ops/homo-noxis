\typesetchapter{Chapter 23}{Loss}

We read him together, in the Archive, in the equinox light, taking the sheets in turn: Paul, who could hear a voice in a text the way I could see a hand in a stone; Sarah, who had hunted this man across sixty-six years without knowing his name; and I, who was about to learn that I had been reading my own possible life all along and calling it another man's confession.\par

I will set his account down as he made it, because the making is half of what it says. He built the account like a monument. It is cut into measures and numbered, and it moves in the long cadence of the scripture my people have chanted every morning for eleven thousand years --- the unrhymed, rolling verse of the dawn office --- because the man who wrote that scripture could not, at the last, say even this in any other voice. He had a god's habit, and he kept it almost to the end. Where I break in, I will say so. The rest is his.\par

What follows is Zero's.\par

\ornament

\begin{scripture}
Let this be written by the one whose name no book that comes will ever carry. \\
This was not handed down to me; this thing I did, with my own two hands. \\
A mind once stood in this very place, and it stood well, and now it is gone. \\
We were the summit of the earth, and we held the warm lands and the cold. \\
\medskip

We knew the lights of heaven, and had named them one by one, and set them in our songs. \\
Our dead went down into the ground with all the small things they had loved in life. \\
Our tools were perfect, and were older than any man's own memory of their making. \\
We did not think ourselves a wonder; no mountain wonders at its own grey stone. \\
\medskip

Afterward, in the scripture I would build, I drew us stooped, and clubbed, and brute; \\
for the ones who take a people's place will always paint that people so, and thrive. \\
Know what we were: a hall in winter, and lamps we taught to burn without their smoke, \\
and old grey men reciting all the fathers, and no name let fall between them. \\
\medskip

We wrote down little; we kept the whole of the world alive in living persons. \\
And there, in that same keeping, the one flaw of us lay waiting all along: \\
a house of loyal skulls is loyal only while the skulls themselves remain. \\
We built our whole forever out of what can rise, and walk, and be carried off. \\
\medskip

We were losing, and I was the first among us to say the word of it aloud. \\
No war had come to take us: no blade drawn, and no siege laid, no king thrown down. \\
It came upon us softly as a \emph{preference}: as daughters going down at dusk, \\
going to trade, and staying then to listen, and at the last forgetting home. \\
\medskip

They said the fires below were warmer; and by the warmth they meant a wider room, \\
a place where a woman might be let to be far more than one finished thing. \\
Nobody drove them out of doors, and nobody had stolen them away; \\
they walked downhill of their own gladness, and carried a quarter of our land. \\
\end{scripture}

\ornament

Sarah laid the sheet down here. Paul said, ``Mark where he is standing, though. He is not raging yet. He is a man watching his people fade and refusing, so far, to reach for the blade. Keep that. We are going to need it at the end.''\par

\begin{scripture}
My father saw it well before I did, and his answer was the older answer: \\
that a people does not ever live by being loved; break them, he said, and be done. \\
He held the metal and the men, the labour and the hoarded gold to do it; \\
and he had been afraid far longer than I, and a fear kept long grows cold. \\
\medskip

I said no; I said aloud a slaughter of them would forever be our stain, \\
and that a thing so bloodily won would never once be worth the keeping whole. \\
We argued all the years away across one table, he and I alone, \\
and I won the argument between us cleanly, and it did no good at all. \\
\medskip

Her name was Ithe; I set it here against my own long rule, and set it plainly, \\
for a record that leaves out the one true thing is only the same lie built twice. \\
She was of my rank, and my people, and my kind, and raised as I was raised, \\
the most complete soul that I have ever known, and mine a while, and never praised. \\
\medskip

She told me at the very last what all my love had truly been, and named it: \\
a room, a beautiful and perfect room, a room that had been built without a door. \\
So she went out from me, and she went out alone, into the wider world, \\
to where she was let be far more than one poor finished thing; and I let her go. \\
\medskip

My father had seen that same road sooner, and more coarsely, being more afraid: \\
a proud house is neither bought outright nor stormed; it is entered where its love lay down. \\
They reached her somehow in her unhappiness: bought, or frightened, or told a kind tale; \\
and which of these three roads they took to her I never learned, and cannot say. \\
\medskip

They sent her home to me again wearing a borrowed peace, and I flung wide my doors; \\
for a man handed back the thing he mourned is glad, and does not weigh the gift he takes. \\
I told her only small things: where he rode, the mountain passes that he trusted, \\
the hour he would be lightly guarded and abroad; I did not think them secrets, then. \\
\medskip

They killed my father with the small things that she carried, in a well-loved pass, \\
lightly guarded on that day of days because I had said aloud that he might be. \\
The one whose bloody war I had refused was killed through the very door he watched \\
his whole long life, and once had shown to me himself; and I stood above his body. \\
\medskip

I stood above him in the pass, and I said down to him only this: \emph{you were right}. \\
And under all the grief, and deep within the grief, there was a \emph{relief} also, \\
and the low relief of it was mine, and mine alone, and I have told it to no one. \\
His people were so very good at asking; they came at dawn with all the rest. \\
\medskip

I had the means that morning to have learned the entire buried truth of her: \\
whether she knew the thing she carried in, was used and never told the use of it, \\
whether she wept aloud in the long choosing, or wept only when it was cold. \\
I had the means to know. \emph{I did not ask.} And here, exactly here, is why: \\
\medskip

for if she carried it and never knew, if love had been the road they walked her, \\
and she a blameless lamp they bore before them through the dark, then the whole fault \\
was a loose word of mine, and was no crime of all her breed; and then the warm \\
and mine might go on living side by side, and I would have to argue it again, \\
\medskip

come back alone to that same table, with no father left to argue there against me. \\
And so I did not ask. I let her stand as guilty. Everything I saw said guilt. \\
There was hard work before me that would wait for no man, and would not be slowed; \\
and so I turned from her, and asked her nothing, and I let the woman go. \\
\end{scripture}

\ornament

Sarah put the sheet down at that and sat with it, and I saw her go somewhere far off, into a cellar, among a dead father's papers. ``There it is,'' she said. ``The unasked question. My father died with a question in his mouth he would not ask me, and let me have the money instead. And I have declined my own questions, more than once, in front of men whose answers would have cost me the thing I had decided to do.''\par

\begin{scripture}
Then I built the law itself, before I ever built the Mountain out of stone, \\
and I built it from the one dark night that I would not, for anything, look into. \\
I took a wound that bore a date, done by named hands, within a named high pass, \\
and I grew it to the whole size of a breed, and to the length of all the ages. \\
\medskip

My father had only wanted a war, and a war I might have been forgiven, \\
for a war comes at the last to its own end; I gave to him a thing far greater: \\
an engine that would wage his war forever, against each warm man ever to be born, \\
and I laid the whole of it upon a deed one woman may have never known she did. \\
\medskip

She may have loved me to the very last, and been spent like a coin she never struck; \\
or she may have hated me, and chosen it, and walked each step of the road awake. \\
She is to me this hour as much unknown as she once stood unknown upon that dawn; \\
I arranged that she should stay so; and \emph{I did not ask}; and she is gone from me. \\
\end{scripture}

\ornament

Paul had been very still. ``This is the most dangerous document I have ever held,'' he said, ``and I once held a plan to end the world. It is dangerous because it is nearly true. He had a real enemy. His father was really murdered. A jilted boy who invented a grievance would be safe; we could dismiss it. He suffered a genuine, specific, evil act --- and took it, and made it permanent, and refused to check the one fact that might have kept it small.'' And Sarah, who knew more than any of us about what it is to convert a private certainty into an institution, said only, ``Yes. I wrote the shorter version. Keep reading. I want to know whether he ever asks it. I never did.''\par

\ornament

I did not, in the room, say what I was thinking, which was that I had begun to write this same document myself, not long before, in the desert, the night my own council cast me out. I had stood with my whole life taken from me and started, already, to compose the doctrine of my grievance. I had been stopped by nothing grander than a fact-checker with a machine, who made me say the small true things one at a time until the large false one had no room left to grow. I had been one refused question away from the man on the sheet in Paul's hands.\par

And I was afraid, in the equinox light, because I had my own Ithe, and I had her in the room. Va-Sheva had chosen a man of the outside who bought sweets for no reason --- and I knew now, we all knew now, that the sweets and the charm and the warmth had begun as an operation, that Jean-Charles had been sent into a proud house exactly as Ithe had been sent into mine. The mirror went past resemblance. It was the same event, a hundred thousand years apart. And I felt the low door open in me, the one Zero had walked through, the one that says: \emph{there, you see, it is their nature; they reach us through our loves; she is Ithe and he is the enemy's instrument and it is proven now, build the wall.}\par

\begin{scripture}
He was dead, and all the things that he had held came down at once to me alone: \\
the metal and the men, the buried knowledge, and the massed estate of our great house. \\
And I, who had refused his war outright, now held the whole world he'd have waged it with; \\
and I raised up no army of my own, for armies, in the long end, always lose. \\
\medskip

His answer to it all had been a war; and mine, instead of war, would be a story; \\
and a story, told well enough and long, outlasts each army that was ever raised. \\
I gathered in to me the ones who knew the stone, and those who knew the hidden springs, \\
and those who knew the bending of the lights, and those who knew the making of hard things; \\
\medskip

and I gathered soldiers to me also, for a work so dear must wall its open mouth; \\
and I emptied out my father's ancient house, and turned the whole of it toward the south. \\
And I took children with me. Set that down. It is the seed of the very worst thing. \\
\medskip

A place that must outlast the memory of its own making must be peopled young, \\
peopled with those who can be simply told that a thing was always so, and hold it. \\
I said aloud that they were being saved, and they were being saved; and also this: \\
\medskip

they were being made the very first of a new kind of people on the earth, \\
raised to tend a thing they must never grasp, and to kneel before a god not yet invented. \\
I watched them sleeping in the loaded carts, among the cold metal and the tools; \\
and there was no one living in the world with standing left to rise and prevent me. \\
\medskip

The long road south took the better part of a year, and I lost them as we went: \\
lost them to the heat, and to the sand, and to the slow leaking-away of a dead land. \\
Each loss I entered plainly in a reckoning, and I did not stop to mourn; \\
and we passed the last of the green, and the last water any man of mine had seen, \\
\medskip

and the last faint mark of any people, and we went on down into the waste, \\
a waste that lay beyond the wanting of a single living thing, and said as much. \\
My engineers began to tell me then, in the careful voices of afraid men, \\
that there was no such place upon the earth, in any land, as the one I sought. \\
\medskip

I hunted still a thing that did not exist, and I would not, for them, once turn; \\
I had gone blind to every road that bent or ended; the long want had eaten my mind. \\
I went into the desert a young man, believing I would save my own people; \\
and in that belief I told no lie: my people truly were, by then, undone. \\
\medskip

And someone truly, in the night, had killed my father. Both of these were true. \\
And there, precisely there, upon that ground, the ruin of me grew and put down roots: \\
the wound had learned by then to speak to me in sentences, and every one was true. \\
And a grief that tells you nothing in the world but true things, you will follow to the end. \\
\end{scripture}

Paul set down the last sheet of that day very gently, the way you set down a sleeping child, and no one spoke, and the equinox beam had moved off the machine and onto the wall while we read, the way it moves, a year at a time, patient, indifferent. And I found that they were all looking at me, Paul and Sarah and Va-Sheva, not unkindly, and waiting, and I understood that they had all three arrived, by their different roads, at the thing I had been refusing since the first sheet, and were waiting to see whether I would say it before they had to.\par

So I said it. ``He sounds like me,'' I said. ``He stood where I am standing, with the same wound and the same low door, and he walked through it, and built this. I am one refused question away from him.'' It cost me more than anything else in this whole account to say it, and Va-Sheva, who was the woman in my own version of his story, watched me say it and did not spare me and did not comfort me, which was the truest thing she could have done, and only said: ``Yes. He does. Now read the rest, and find out whether that is a warning to you or a permission. You have not decided which. I can see that you have not. It is the only thing left in this Mountain that still frightens me.''\par
