\typesetchapter{Chapter 4}{Mountain}

\begin{room}

--- The nineteenth of November; nine o'clock; the third of our interviews. And by my own reckoning we are a day behind on your account already, for all the heavens and the animals we keep stopping to weigh. They gave you the heavens yesterday, I am told. You look no better rested for them than you did for the beasts.

--- I am not rested. He showed me the whole of it first, Paul, in one turning year: the making of the world, the first fire, the long dark, the wandering rocks; and my people arriving in the last breath of the last night of that year, a little before its midnight. Each second of his year, five hundred of mine. That I could hold. It was the one kindness in the whole afternoon: he gave me a measure I could stand inside.

--- The Cosmic Calendar.

--- And then he would not let me rest in it. He showed me a man who measured the round earth with a stick and its shadow and the noon sun, and asked no god's leave to do it. Eratosthenes. He would have been Light, Paul. He did the whole of Light's work in an afternoon, in the open, with no office to grant him the shadow. The voice on the light asked whether I should like to repeat the man's experiment, and I found that I did not. I knew it to be true. That frightened me more than any doubt: that I could look at a stick and a shadow and simply know.

--- You asked me, in this room, whether your people knew the earth for a sphere. You said you did, and did not; that it was complicated. Was the stick your answer?

--- Part of it. The rest keeps for its place. And it was beautiful, the whole of it, and cruel in the same breath, the way your kind is both at once. A man named Kepler gave his life to prove that five perfect shapes ruled the spacing of the worlds; he was wrong; he was himself the one who proved it wrong; and he went on loving the splendour of the thing to his death regardless. I do not know how a man bears a beauty he cannot trust. And that is the whole of my trouble, and why I have not slept. In that moving light, sometimes the thing was real, and sometimes it was men dressed and lit to play at what had once happened, and sometimes I could not tell the one from the other. I had a hundred questions yesterday. I have a million this morning. And I have understood that I cannot walk out and settle each of them with my own eyes, the way I settled your pond. A man could spend three lives on the questions of one afternoon. So I must do the thing I was never taught to do: decide which of them to trust without checking. And I do not know how.

\end{room}

\begin{room}

--- Where I was raised, I never had to choose. Scripture chose; the office chose. A warden told me, you agree, understanding is not required; and I agreed, and the not-understanding was a kind of rest. Out here there is no scripture to lean on, and you name that freedom, and you say the word as though it were a gift. But if I am free, then I must choose whom to believe with nothing standing behind the choice but myself. How do you bear it, Paul? If not God, then what? How does a free man decide where to put his trust?

--- Carefully, and never all at once. I trust nothing because it comforts me, nor because a man in a robe has said it, nor because I should like it to be so. I trust what has been made to stand against a test and did not fall: a claim that could be checked, and was, and could have failed and did not. That earns a little of my weight, and only the weight the test will bear, and I take it back the hour a better test throws it down. It is a slow way to live, and a lonely one, and I have found nothing that beats it.

--- Then you and I trust the same thing, and I did not know it until this morning. What survives the test. Remember that you said it. I may hold you to it.

\end{room}

\begin{room}

--- You are meant to be handing me a mountain, not a philosophy of doubt. Yesterday I left you on a beam running east, out of the room with the wall you call the Mirror. Va-Raedin had given you one thread of light of two hundred and fifty-five, to learn until you could walk it blind. Give it to me as it came.

--- Then take it as it came.

\end{room}

\noindent Light did not let a boy keep a revelation overnight and call it his own. The morning after the Mirror, before First Light had well filled the practice halls, Va-Raedin gave me one point off the wall and set me to learn it.\par

Not the wall. One point on it, low and toward the eastern side, steadier than most and given to a slow stir near Second Silence. ``That one is yours until you can walk it in the dark,'' he said. ``Source to fall, fall to source; every mirror it touches, every throat it passes, and whatever stands near its road that ought not to stand there. When you can draw me the whole of it with your eyes shut, you will be given a second. There are two hundred and fifty-five behind that one. Do not hurry. Hurry is how a candidate loses a beam and lights a wrong room looking for it.''\par

I was not sent alone. Two servants of Light were set to walk the beam with me --- not to guard it, for Light did not believe a beam wanted guarding, but because a Candidate fresh out of the Hall holds the doctrine of a beam and none of its habits, and a servant holds every habit and not one word of the doctrine, and the Mountain meant the two to grind together until something true came off.\par

The elder was Oren, a man of some twenty years, thick across the shoulders from a life of ladders, carrying the flat patience of one who has cleaned the same glass long enough to stop resenting it. He had walked beams since before I could read, and he taught as such men teach: by letting me be wrong first, and cheaply. The younger was a girl of fourteen, Sela, who bore the cloths and the little oil and said almost nothing. When Oren corrected me she watched to see how I would take it --- not unkindly; the way the young study a grown thing to learn what it is made of.\par

Two days had passed since my Ascension, and the Mountain had not yet finished paying for it. A ceremony spends order and does not replace it; for days afterward the whole body of the place labours to earn it back. Those who had stood too long in the Hall now had to carry, cook, count, wash, teach, bless, guard and record, out of turn and short of hands. Faith had new instruction to send out under Ka-Elun. Justice had the transfer to reconcile against old tablets. And somewhere, because Light had taken a servant off some other task to walk a beam with a new Candidate, Balance was adjusting a ledger. Balance always adjusted the ledger. That word is a soft thing where I sit now, a near-compliment; in the Mountain \textit{noticing} was an office, and it wore a green robe, and it did not forgive.\par

\ornament

\noindent We began at the fall, in the low room itself, before the ordinary hours let anyone else near it. Oren held two fingers a breath from the wall, beneath my point, and did not touch the stone. ``This end you have been shown,'' he said. ``It is the easy end. Any fool can stand where a beam dies. The craft is walking it back up to where it was born, and knowing at every turn that it is still your beam under your hand, and not its neighbour wearing its coat.'' He ran the order the other way from the one Va-Raedin had set in me --- fall, surface, throat, source --- and added the things a Veil never mentions. ``At every surface you ask three questions, always the same, always in this order, or you will teach yourself to trust a beam that is lying to you. Is the glass clean. Is the glass square. Is there a second light near enough to marry this one and give you their child in place of your beam.'' Sela lifted a small square of polished bronze from her satchel without being asked. ``She carries the test-glass because your eye will fail where the doctrine says use the glass, and a man who trusts his eye there is the man who lights the wrong room. Ask the three. Every surface. Even the ones you cleaned yourself an hour ago. \textit{Especially} those.''\par

I had thought the Mirror was the lesson. Following my one thread of light out of that room, I understood that the Mirror had only been the promise of it. The lesson was this: a beam is patient and a man is not, and every place they disagree, the man is wrong.\par

\begin{room}

--- You keep saying it: source to fall, and back to the source. You walk a beam home to where it is born. Tell me, then, what is born there. A mirror moves light; it does not make it. What is the source, at the top of all these threads, under a mountain, in the dark?

--- The sun. What else would it be? The Mountain bores shafts up to the sky, narrow as a needle and cut truer and longer than you would believe a man could cut them, and drinks what little Heaven lets fall down them. Everything after that is only the careful spending of that little: every mirror, every throat, the whole of the wall you call the Mirror. We make no light. We divide the sun.

\end{room}

\ornament

\noindent We did not get far before Shield stopped us. A warden raised one hand at the first turn and held back three workers, two children, and the three of us together. ``South service way is closed,'' he said. A woman with folded cloth on her hip frowned. ``Since when?'' ``Since it was closed.'' ``I have cloth due in the herb rooms.'' ``Then it will arrive by another passage.'' ``That adds half a descent.'' The warden looked at the bundles, not the woman. ``Then the cloth will be tired.''\par

The children laughed until their father quieted them. The woman did not laugh; she shifted the cloth and turned to go the long way, irritated and unsurprised. No one asked why the way was shut. In the Mountain a closure did not owe anyone a reason. It owed everyone an adjustment. I have thought since that this is the truest thing I could tell a stranger about the place --- not the darkness, but the way a shut door was answered with a shrug and a longer walk and never once with a question. And as the warden stepped aside, a woman in Balance green passed straight through the closed way with a sealed slate under her arm and no one to stop her. I watched her go. Permission, I understood then, was not a wall. It was a sorting mechanism: the same door that turned back cloth let green through without a word. I was proud of the thought. I did not yet know how much of my life it would cost me to finish it.\par

\ornament

\noindent The beam did not keep to Light. Few of them did, Oren said; that was half the reason to walk them. Mine climbed a service throat, crossed above a washing gallery through a slot no wider than a wrist, and came down again into the trafficked stone where Balance kept the water. The first queue had already formed along a shallow channel, watched by two clerks in green and one Faith attendant. Each household bore a marked vessel; a copper rod lay across the channel, and the clerks walked it from notch to notch, reading allowance by household mark, by work, by illness, by the unborn, by yesterday's draw. The Faith attendant blessed each jar before the measure was let go. Her prayer did not slow the work. It timed it.\par

``Half measure,'' a clerk said, to a woman who had already opened her mouth. ``Yesterday was half.'' ``Yesterday was second descent. This is third. Your house drew early, after the heat of the Ascension.'' The woman's jaw set, then let go, and she took the half-filled jar. I watched the water settle in it. The surface caught a high lamp and bent along the curve of the clay, so the jar showed fuller from one side than the other. I knew it for half measure only because Balance had said half measure. Without the mark, the eye would have argued. I was fifteen, standing over a stranger's water, and I understood a thing I would spend the rest of my life failing to unlearn: that the eye is a liar the moment a record is not standing beside it.\par

My beam ran on above the channel, and I nearly lost it there, caught for a breath by a brighter fleck off a wet patch on the wall. I said, ``There,'' and reached, and Oren let me put my whole certainty into the wrong light before he said, quietly, ``Ask the three.'' The glass showed my error at once. My beam was the duller one, higher, married for an instant to the wet-wall shine and now parting from it. The bright thing I had wanted was somebody's spill. ``You lost it because you liked the look of the other one better,'' Oren said. ``A beam does not care to be liked.''\par

\begin{room}

--- And out here, Paul. A week in this house and I have not seen a jar carried, nor a queue, nor a clerk at a channel. How do you keep yourselves alive? Where is your water counted?

--- We do not count water. It runs in the walls, in pipe, much as your Mountain runs it, only into every room, and warm as readily as cold. A man turns a handle and takes as much as he likes, and no one counts it.

--- Every room. Warm. And no measure. You are laying a picture of it on the table now: a fall of water stopped dead in the air. I have not decided whether I am looking at wealth or at waste. In the Mountain they are one word.

\end{room}

\ornament

\noindent Beyond the water the kitchens opened in a run of low, heated chambers, and the air thickened with steam and yeast and boiled grain and the sour-sweet breath of things left to ferment. The workers were mostly Balance, but Faith was in all their timing --- short prayers to seal a jar, longer ones to open stored grain, work-chants to keep hands moving together when the heat made speech unpleasant.\par

\begin{scripture}
Let every fruit be gathered. Let every vessel be sealed. Let no waste enter the Oasis.
\end{scripture}

They did not sing it beautifully. Some did not sing it at all. One woman sang with plain devotion; another used the lines to count her kneading; a boy mouthed them only when an elder's eye came round. Faith, I saw, did not require the same belief from every throat. It required the rhythm to hold. That was a more useful thing to know than most of what I had been taught to believe, and it unsettled me that I could see it so nakedly and still mean to kneel that evening as though I had not.\par

Two girls dusted trays with powdered shell. Calcium, I remembered from a childhood lesson --- belonging to no romance and no myth and no prayer, only to bone and tooth and poultice and mortar and the quiet insistence of bodies. Above a locked hatch three goats were painted on a stone panel, and beneath them a line stood cut in Balance script and copied below in common hand:\par

\begin{scripture}
THE OUTSIDE FEEDS ONLY WHAT THE MOUNTAIN CAN COUNT.
\end{scripture}

I slowed at the word. \textit{Outside.} It struck differently now than it had a week before. A kitchen supervisor caught me staring. ``You want goat duty, Light?'' A few workers laughed. ``No,'' I said. ``Then keep moving. Even Light casts shadows.'' I moved.\par

\ornament

\noindent It was in the wash passage below the kitchens that Sela undid me, without meaning to. I had tangled the beam a second time, under a run of brackets, and could not find it. She did not say she had it. She touched my sleeve and lifted her chin toward a fleck riding a bracket I had walked past twice, and when I saw it, and she saw me see it, something opened in her face --- a plain, unguarded gladness, the pleasure of a child who has shown a grown thing where the bird is hiding. It was there and gone. She turned back to the cloths.\par

I have no gentler way to set it down. I had known a girl my whole life. I had chased her through the service tunnels and laughed and wept with her and stood across a Hall from her two days before and felt hope stand up in me at the wrong moment. And in all of it I had never once seen her look at anything the way this stranger of fourteen had just looked at a bracket. I did not understand, that morning, why so small a thing should lie in my chest like a stone. I would understand it long afterward, when I saw the very same light cross a face I had loved my whole life --- and found that it was not turned toward me, and never had been, and was not going to be.\par

\ornament

\noindent Near the tool wash a man was recording where no water ran. Sa-Tavan, with a slate under one arm and the new black cord at his wrist, narrow and composed. ``You are far from the east passage,'' he said. ``So are you.'' ``I have a permission mark. It is not an honour. It is a route allowance.'' ``What are you recording?'' ``A disputed sequence in the second water queue. Justice records the order while the memory is still warm.'' ``And if you record it wrongly?'' His face changed, not to fear but to something graver. ``Then a correction becomes a wound.''\par

It was not the answer I had expected from a boy I had thought dry. A measure misrecorded became hunger; a sequence altered became guilt where none belonged. Justice did not wait because it loved delay. It waited because an act without its sequence could change the meaning of everything that came after it. I did not know, that morning, that I was speaking with the one who would one day sit in a chamber and record the sequence of me. He turned to go, then stopped. ``Your thumb is bleeding through. Have it witnessed, so responsibility is not confused between a ritual wound and an instruction wound.'' And he was gone before I could answer. I decided not to have it witnessed. I have wondered since whether that was the first small crime of my life, or only the first I noticed.\par

\ornament

\noindent I passed the child halls, cut into warmer stone near the family districts. Children sat on woven mats while a Faith teacher moved among them with a cord of five knots.\par

\begin{scripture}
The Mountain is refuge. \\
The Oasis is water. \\
The forest was plenty. \\
Beyond the forest thou shalt not wander.
\end{scripture}

The teacher touched the fifth knot. ``And if the forest is gone?'' The youngest faltered; the older answered.\par

\begin{scripture}
The Covenant remains.
\end{scripture}

The forest was gone. The Covenant remained. If a child did not know what had been lost, the line comforted; if an adult knew, it instructed grief to behave. I have found no cleaner definition of scripture than that: a line that tells grief how to behave.\par

\begin{room}

--- The children's third line. Say it to me again.

--- The forest was plenty.

--- A forest. Not the Oasis, not a garden: a forest, where you are, in the deep desert, taught to the children as a thing that once was and is gone.

--- Water everywhere once, the old lines say. Then water in one place only. Then less than that. The forest was the first of it to go.

--- You understand what you have just handed me. A forest does not become a desert in a lifetime, nor in ten.

--- No. It does not. I have always known the Mountain to be very old. It is one of the few things I was ever permitted to know.

\end{room}

\ornament

\noindent The beam ran east along the lamp corridors, and there Light stopped being ceremonial and became necessary. Oren stopped a young servant with a cold lamp and made me watch him teach it. ``Wick first, trimmed even. Then the oil, filled at the foot, never at the top, because oil on a high step is a fall waiting for the next servant who cannot see it. The mirror last, and with the cloth, never the hand. Wick, oil, glass. In that order, every lamp.'' The boy went off holding it like a rule. ``That is the whole of what we give a servant,'' Oren said, low. ``The order of a thing. What we do not give him is the reason --- which doors the light is set to leave dark. The reason is the Veil's, and the reason is the part that carries a price.''\par

\begin{room}

--- May I say a thing that is not a question. You have done it all morning: you take a lamp, a jar, a beam, and turn it in your hands until it has become a verdict upon your whole people. It is remarkable. It is also, and you must forgive me, a little exhausting to a plain man with a machine to fill.

--- I know. I hear myself. It is what Light does to you. Teach a child that nothing is only itself, that behind each lamp there is a hand and behind the hand a price, and he will never again let a thing be merely a thing. So let me give you one clean of it, in fairness: the lamp corridors were warm, and they smelled of oil and soot, and after a cold morning I liked them. There is nothing under it. Only warm.

\end{room}

\ornament

\noindent Where the beam crossed the repair halls, no Kahir owned the ground, and a low argument ran under procedure. A Balance man turned a pipe section. ``It will hold.'' A Light woman: ``It reflects into the side slit.'' ``Then dull it.'' ``Dull it and it corrodes sooner.'' An old Justice recorder said without looking up, ``State the consequence of each choice. Time to failure?'' ``Unknown.'' ``Then it is not yet a choice. It is two fears.'' And both workers stopped. Past the repair halls, in the medical rooms, a child sat still while a healer worked paste into a burn, a Balance clerk noting what the wound would cost. ``He will need extra oil in the dressing, three days,'' the clerk said, and the mother flinched at \textit{extra} until he added, ``recorded under injury allowance,'' and her relief was so plain I was ashamed to have seen it. Mercy here was not a warmth in the chest. It was a category.\par

\ornament

\noindent The beam ran at last to the edge of the storehouses: three gates, the first open, the second asking a mark, the third watched by Shield and witnessed by Justice --- and my thread of light ran along the lintel of the second and in under the third. ``Sa-Kailan.'' Ka-Leth, Keeper of Balance, broad and grave, her hands stained faintly green. ``It runs \textit{to} my third gate, and stops at my line, as you will. A beam is Light's to follow. What is behind that gate is Balance's to keep, and the line is where those two truths are made to agree. You may see where your light goes in. You may not go in after it.'' I had the doctrine half out of my mouth, and Oren went so still that I closed it. ``Then I will note the gate as its end.'' ``Better. Revelation is not free. Curiosity least of all. Light forgets that, being the one office that pays in another's coin.''\par

I stepped back. Shield opened the third gate for her, and in the moment it stood wide I saw past her --- not much, and not for long. A low chamber. Two wardens, and a third figure between them who did not stand like a warden. A shape under grey cloth on a table too small to be stores. And along the near wall, where the lamp found them, a row of small dark objects I had no word for, each one ringed and cold, that were not oil, and were not grain, and were not anything Balance counts. Then the gate closed, and it was only a gate again. I understood nothing, and it matters that it is honest: I understood nothing. I had looked because looking was the whole of my new craft, and I had seen a fragment I could not read. Oren was looking at the closed gate with the particular flatness of a man taking care not to look at it.\par

\begin{room}

--- Go back. The things along the wall. Ringed, and cold, and not oil, not grain. What were they.

--- I told you: I have no word for them that I would trust.

--- You have a word. You are choosing not to give it to me.

--- Now you sound like a man who wants the shape of the place. Leave the gate, Paul. It keeps. Everything in me that is still a Keeper is telling me that it keeps, and that I must not hand it across a table to the one man in the world whose whole purpose is to carry it out of this house. You may have the girl, and the water, and the warm corridors. You may not yet have the gate.

\end{room}

\begin{room}

--- Then a smaller thing. You have said east and south to me a dozen times this week, the way I would say left. But you were under a mountain. No sun, no stars, no horizon. How does a boy in a sealed hall know east from west?

--- Because the Mountain knows. It was built knowing. The halls are cut to it. A child learns the directions as he learns the corridors; they are the one lesson.

--- This needle I am setting on the table lies down of itself, north and south, in any cellar with the door shut. A small lodestone; the earth a very great one; the small lies down along the pull of the great. Your people keep the direction in the walls. Mine keep it in a box.

--- Then you have made a small piece of my Mountain a man may carry in his pocket, and we would both find it charming. And you would set the bearing down on your reel and believe I had not seen you want it. I know what you are, Paul. I know what you are for. You want the shape of the place, and the road to it, and I have not yet decided how much of my home to put into the hands of a man I have known five days. However much I have begun to like him.

\end{room}

\noindent I noted the gate as the end of the beam, and I did not sleep on it, and that was the beginning of everything they would one day try me for --- though it looked, that afternoon, like nothing more than a good boy doing his sums. Near the eastern stair a child recited before a fist-sized lamp while a Faith attendant mended his rhythm.\par

\begin{scripture}
And God said, Keep thou the Mountain, and keep thou the Oasis, and keep thou the Temple.
Keep the secrets apart, and let no one hand hold the whole.
\end{scripture}

I stopped just past the alcove. I had spoken those words myself, in the Hall, two days before. Now they followed me differently. \textit{The Temple.} I had said it a thousand times and never once asked where it stood, or what it kept. And \textit{keep the secrets apart} --- not as poetry now, but as design, as law, as threat. It was the most beautiful sentence my people ever made, and it was the door to everything that was done to me, and I would come, in the end, to hold the key to both.\par

\ornament

\noindent I came back at last to the candidate quarters half empty, and sat on the edge of my mat, and took the Light token from the pouch at my belt, and held it toward the lamp. It did not show me more. It showed me less, with greater force. The law had held everywhere I walked that day: each office kept one portion, and the whole existed only where the portions were forced to meet. It should have comforted me. But the comfort curdled. If no single hand held the whole, then every soul in the Mountain lived inside arrangements it could not see. And the thing I had learned to see, I understood with a discomfort very near awe, had a record open on me too, and had been keeping it, patiently, the whole time I imagined myself the one doing the looking. I was fifteen, and I thought being seen was the same as being known. It is not.\par

\begin{room}

--- You have set that sentence down carefully, twice now: keep the secrets apart, let no one hand hold the whole. Why do you keep it.

--- Because it is well made. Your people built a good sentence, and I collect good sentences. A thing that cannot be surrendered whole, because no one of you holds it whole: the traitor, the captive, the fool with the loose tongue each carry only a fragment, and the thing outlives the weakest man who keeps it. It is sound engineering in the dress of scripture, and sound engineering is true whether or not a syllable of the account around it is.

--- You say that as a man who would very much like to hold the whole himself.

--- Every man in my trade would. That is exactly why your people were wise to see to it that none of us can.

\end{room}

\begin{room}

--- Enough of the Mountain for one morning. I want to give you a thing, and to ask you a thing, and I would rather do neither in this chair. For three days you have tried to get me out of doors for my health, and I have refused you like a sulking child. Take me out now. I should like to stand under the open sky and show you that I can, I who could not lift my eyes to it at fifteen. Count it your victory. Tell the physician he may have it.

--- Gladly. It is a better use of a cold morning than a beam I am forbidden to finish. Come. There is a lawn on the south side, past the glass doors, that no one crosses in this weather.

\end{room}

\begin{room}

--- There. You see? I am standing under the whole enormous nothing of it, and I have not fallen down. Now walk with me. Away from the doors, to the middle, where the grass is longest. Good.

--- You did not bring me out for the sky.

--- No. I brought you out for the one thing this ground has that your room does not: no walls. A wall can be given an ear; grass cannot. Out here there is nothing that hears me but you, and that machine on your shoulder, and I have decided that I trust the machine, because it is yours, and because it does not lie about what it has heard. So. Will you trust me for a moment, and play a small game with me?

--- That depends upon the game.

--- It is a scientist's game. You told me this morning that you trust what survives a test. I have a test. This afternoon, when you go to your work: you told me you spend your afternoons alone in your room, turning the reel. Do not, this once. Go instead and stand behind the glass of the room where you set me down each day. And look. And when we next meet, tell me whether anyone was there.

--- Behind the glass.

--- You told me no one watched me; that only the tutor sat with me. I believe that you believe it. But a man kept apart, as you are kept apart, does not know what stands on the far side of his own wall. So go and see. If the place is empty, you will have proved me a frightened fool, and I will play your football, and never trouble you with it again. And if it is not empty, then I was right; and a man ought to stand by the one who was right.

--- And that is the whole of it.

--- That is the whole of it. If I am proven correct, if there are men behind that glass, then you are mine, Paul. Not the house's. Mine. Your loyalty comes to me.

--- That is a large thing to swear on a lawn.

--- It is the smallest thing in the world, if I am wrong; and I am so often wrong. And understand me clearly: I do not ask it against Sarah. I have never met her. You say she is good, and busy, and set high above us both, and I believe you. I ask it for her. Are we even certain that it is Sarah's own people watching Sarah's house, and not some other eye set upon Sarah herself? A woman who heads what she heads: would there truly be no one in all the world with cause to watch her?

--- She runs a great deal. Ships; metals; men who matter. It is not impossible that she is watched.

--- Then we are agreed that it is possible. And if it is possible, two men who wish her well had better know. Go behind the glass, Paul. For her, if not for me. And swear me the small thing, that survives its test, or does not.

--- Very well. If there are men behind that glass, which there will not be, then I am your man. There: it costs me nothing to promise what cannot come to pass. Now put your face straight before we go in. You look like a boy who has got away with something.

\end{room}

\begin{room}

--- One thing more, and then we go in and are proper again. You keep saying the house, and them, as though you and I stood upon the two sides of a wall. We do not. Did no one tell you? I have a name here too, now: one of your kind's names. Sa-Kilian, of Horizon. Ka-Sarah brought me in; a woman named Va-Elise did the writing; I was filed and stamped like a crate of your metals. So when you serve Horizon, you serve the house that owns me. We are colleagues, you and I. That is why I may ask you what I ask. And that is why it ought to trouble you, a very little, that I, the crate, the specimen, seem to know the shape of our shared master better than you do.

--- I did not know they had given you a designation.

--- No. You would not. You are kept in your cell as I am kept in mine. Come in out of the cold. I have a physician to satisfy.

\end{room}

--- So. The water.

--- I did it, Paul, and it was worse than any football, and I have the bruises of a man who fought the water and lost. And I have decided that I want more of it. Tell the physician he has beaten me: I will swim, and I will learn to go under, and to do that I must rest this body I have punished and then drive it further, and that is two days at the least. We will not meet tomorrow. We will meet the day after.

--- Two days, then. The physician will be insufferable; his errand has succeeded where the tutor's did not. I was told, you know. That you went at last into the water. It is the first good news I have carried up out of this room in a week.

--- Then carry it up, and be pleased. Two days, Paul.

\end{room}
