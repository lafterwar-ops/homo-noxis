\typesetchapter{Chapter 16}{Horizon}


We went back up the Labyrinth together, the five Keepers, Va-Sheva and I, with Sa-Tavan following Justice. We had collected him in the circular room beyond the bridge, his tablet still wrapped against his chest. Ka-Elun sang at the divisions. On the steeper turns I put a hand against the wall and let the person ahead draw away before I followed.\par

At the carved prayer Ka-Elun raised her lamp. The stone opened onto the Hall of Prayers, and Ka-Raedin retrieved the lamp he had left there. Sa-Tavan went with Justice; the other Keepers took their own ways. I watched Ka-Raedin until the turn hid him. Va-Sheva waited beside me.\par

I had never seen the Mountain this empty. The doors along the gallery were shut, including the little service door people usually propped with a bread basket. Our feet sounded all the way to the next turn.\par

``May I pray before the Pendulum? One last time, please.''\par

She looked down the gallery, then led me to the Hall of Ceremony.\par

The Pendulum passed beneath us. I knelt at the rail where I had knelt among hundreds of others and said the prayer my father had taught me. With each return the Beast's face caught the light. When I had finished, I stayed on my knees for another passage. Va-Sheva waited until I got up.\par

She took a lamp from its bracket by a service door on Shield's side of the Hall and led me down into the passage towards the second ridge. At a division I recognised the slope leading towards the archive. The opening with Faith and Shield joined above it was there too. I stopped short of it.\par

Va-Sheva held the lamp farther along the passage. The door ahead bore three Shield seals, cut one over another, the oldest sunk deep into the stone. I had turned away from it on my first visit. She drew a long iron key from inside her belt, Shield's mark cut into its bow. She fitted it beneath the seals and turned it; bolts scraped back inside the stone. She recovered the key and pushed the door open far enough for us to pass.\par

Beyond it the passage widened into a room with four pillars on either side. Two stone benches faced each other across a low lamp-holder. High above them, a round hole in the roof held a few stars. The corridor continued beyond the opposite bench.\par

``Who sits here?'' I asked.\par

``Ka-Xhian received the woman in your photograph here. His Veil stood behind those pillars.''\par

I looked at the bench she indicated. Va-Sheva set the lamp in its holder and opened a wall cupboard. She brought out two packs, water skins and sand wraps. Before she let me put them on, she searched the folds of my robe, the stitching at my cuffs and the soles of my sandals. Ka-Raedin already held my Light token; her fingers checked its empty place.\par

``Nothing for your household?''\par

My mother's face came before me with the ordinary exactness of a meal-place: her thumb smoothing a cracked bowl, the small dark mark beside one nail. ``Nothing.''\par

Va-Sheva covered the white knot at my wrist with dark cloth, bound it with a cord and tied my belt again. I watched her fit Justice's packet inside her own pack.\par

``They are still counting?'' I asked.\par

``The doors stay shut until you are out. Then they will be told you were seen entering the Labyrinth. Faith will send pilgrims to look for you.''\par

``And on the fourth day.''\par

``Yes.''\par

She lifted the pack onto my shoulders. I pulled the straps even and followed her into the opposite corridor. Three sealed crossings stood along it, the mark of Shield joining the stone leaves at each. Beyond the last, the passage ran straight into darkness.\par

At its end she covered the lamp and knocked against the rock. The opening came slowly, bringing cold air across my face. A young man waited outside, his head and mouth wrapped against the cold.\par

``Amastan,'' Va-Sheva said.\par

He gave me a brief look, took the lamp from her and put it inside the opening. Then he helped her draw the stone shut.\par

\ornament

Three camels knelt in a hollow below the entrance. Amastan tightened a strap, moved a water skin from one load to another and beckoned me to the smallest animal. I climbed on as he showed me. When it rose I pitched forward over its neck; Va-Sheva caught my pack and pushed me back into the saddle.\par

For a while I could see the Mountain whenever I turned. Then we went down between two ridges and I lost it. I twisted farther, trying to see over the rise, until the camel stumbled and I had to catch the saddle. Amastan clicked his tongue and led us on.\par

We travelled at night. By the first dawn I could scarcely close my knees. Under an overhang Amastan spread a desert-coloured cloth across the opening and pinned it with stones; I lay down before he had finished. At dusk Va-Sheva woke me with a water skin against my cheek. The next night an engine passed beyond a ridge. Amastan led the animals into a hollow and we waited there until he was willing to move.\par

Before the third dawn we reached a dry well, its lip cracked and its rope gone. A dead thorn tree leaned over fallen stones. Amastan unloaded the camels and took them round the ridge to feed. Va-Sheva sat against the well and loosened the wrapping at her neck. I lowered myself beside her, slowly enough to spare my knees.\par

``I made you stand against your own Keeper.''\par

``Kay, I sat where the sequence required me and scarcely spoke. He has heard me disagree before. We both put the Mountain's safety first. I will survive this.''\par

For a while we watched the light grow along the ridge. She had folded her wraps beneath her head and closed her eyes. I moved a stone that was pressing into my leg.\par

``Will I see you again?''\par

``Your reports come through me.''\par

``That is not what I asked.''\par

She opened her eyes. ``I have to get you to them first.''\par

``You chose to bring me yourself.''\par

``Kay. You were entrusted to me.''\par

``I wanted you to choose me.''\par

She pulled the wraps from behind her head and began folding them. I waited for her to answer. Before she had finished, an engine sounded beyond the ridge. She got up with the cloth still in her hands.\par

The Toyota came over the rise slowly, large tyres working through the loose stones. Two people sat behind its glass. The man beside the driver saw Va-Sheva and raised a hand. She raised hers before the vehicle had stopped.\par

``Jean-Charles,'' she said.\par

He climbed out, a sun-lined man in close-fitting clothes, with a short blade at his belt and a row of closed pockets down his coat. Va-Sheva dropped her wraps and ran past me. She jumped into his arms, locked her legs round his waist and kissed him, her hands behind his head. He caught her under the thighs and stumbled back against the vehicle. She was laughing when she drew away to look at him; he tried to speak, and she kissed him again.\par

I went back to the well for her wraps. I would not ask her to choose me again.\par

When he set her down, she kept a palm flat against his chest. He covered it with his own and held it there before reaching into a pocket for a bright, flat box tied with pink ribbon.\par

``Pistachio and rose. They travelled badly. Eat them tonight or they die.''\par

Va-Sheva took it with both hands. The driver's door opened, and she put the box inside her coat. I held out her wraps. She took them without looking at me.\par

The woman who stepped out had short pale hair and a grey jacket without an office sign. A narrow cord disappeared into her collar. Va-Sheva called her Va-Elise. They spoke together beside the vehicle while Jean-Charles loaded our packs.\par

I went towards the camels. Jean-Charles caught my shoulder with his right hand and turned me back towards the vehicle.\par

``With us. Leave the animals to him.''\par

He spoke my language with unfamiliar stresses. His hand was gone before I had decided whether to resist it. Va-Sheva came to me, loosened the dark cord at my wrist and drew it away without disturbing the cloth over the white knot. She laid it on Justice's sealed packet and handed both to Va-Elise, who put them in her bag.\par

``Get in,'' Va-Elise said. ``I will show you the belt.''\par

From the back seat I could see Va-Sheva in the little mirror on the door. She had taken Jean-Charles a few paces away. I heard the engine and Va-Elise moving things beside me; their words did not reach the vehicle.\par

Va-Sheva caught his hand as he began to speak and brought it low against her belly. She held it there, looking into his face, then nodded. He stared at her. His mouth opened into a smile so sudden that she laughed and caught his other hand. He bent to kiss her; she met him, then drew back and looked towards the ridge beyond which lay the Mountain.\par

She turned her hand in his, showing the black line of Seal dye. His smile faltered. For a moment he kept both hands closed around hers. She said something else, and when he answered she put a finger against his lips. He kissed it. She was crying now, still smiling, wiping her cheek with the hand he had released.\par

``What has he said to her?'' I asked.\par

Va-Elise leaned across me and drew the belt out. ``Lift your arm.''\par

I did, and lost the mirror behind her shoulder. By the time she had fastened it, Va-Sheva was walking towards Amastan. Va-Elise returned to the wheel. Jean-Charles watched Va-Sheva go, then took the front passenger seat. I moved closer to the opposite side of the seat.\par

``You are safer than you feel,'' he said.\par

The engine deepened. In the mirror I watched Amastan hold a camel steady for her. She turned once towards the vehicle, but we were moving by then and I could not see her face.\par

An hour after the well, Jean-Charles raised a black instrument to his eye and adjusted it with his thumb.\par

``One vehicle. About three kilometres. Coming towards us.''\par

``Shit. Who the fuck is that?'' Va-Elise asked.\par

He watched a little longer. ``Stop the car.''\par

The Toyota stopped so hard that the belt caught me across the chest and drove into my shoulder. I gripped it, trying to pull enough slack to breathe.\par

``If there's one in front, there may be another behind us. Turn round.''\par

Va-Elise swung the Toyota across the flat. Jean-Charles reached down beside his seat and brought a long case across his knees. As he opened it, she pointed through the front glass.\par

``There. Dust.''\par

A pale column rose ahead of us, on the ground we had just crossed. Behind us, the first vehicle was now gaining on us. A man stood in its open back with his hands on a mounted gun.\par

Jean-Charles took a rifle with a glass tube above it from the case. He passed me two padded cups joined by a band and touched his ears. I pulled them over mine. Something struck the rear door with a hard metallic crack.\par

He turned on the passenger seat, one knee pressed into its cushion, and brought the rifle past my face. ``Careful. This will be loud.''\par

The shot broke the glass behind us. I flinched as wind struck the back of my head. Jean-Charles caught my upper arm and settled the rifle across my shoulder.\par

``Stay still. Breathe in, then hold it when I say now.''\par

I drew air in. His cheek was close to mine.\par

``Now.''\par

The rifle jolted against my shoulder. I held my breath until he squeezed my arm and told me I could let it out. Twice more he said ``Now'', and twice more I held still while he fired. Va-Elise kept her eyes ahead.\par

He lifted the rifle from my shoulder and watched through its glass. ``All three dead. Turn round.''\par

The belt pulled across the sore place as Va-Elise turned us back. Through the broken window I saw the second vehicle veer away across the flat. Its dust grew fainter.\par

We passed the first vehicle on our way back along our original track. It stood crooked on its wheels. Two heads hung forward in the cab; a third man lay across the open back beneath the gun. A red band circled his head wrap. His jacket and trousers were different colours of camouflage.\par

``Who were they?'' I asked.\par

Jean-Charles lowered the rifle across his knees. ``Kidnappers.''\par

``For whom?''\par

``Whoever pays. \textit{Daesh} buys captives across the border.''\par

Glass had gathered in my lap. I tried to brush it away and he caught my wrist.\par

``Leave it. Are you hurt?''\par

I shook my head. He took off the padded cups. My ears rang, and blood was running from one knuckle.\par

We drove until the sun was high. At a halt Jean-Charles shook out my robe and bound my hand while Va-Elise watched the empty ground behind us. He tightened the dressing and waited for me to get back in. Va-Elise drove on.\par

By late afternoon we reached a road lined with houses. People stood in their doorways. A man pushed a wheeled bin past our window and never looked up. I turned to watch him until the vehicle carried us round a corner.\par

Va-Elise drove through a metal gate into a yard. Jean-Charles closed it behind us and began clearing the broken glass while she let me into the house, locked the door and put her bag on a table. The lights were small glass bulbs fixed to the wall. I held my palm near one while she went through the cupboards for clothes.\par

``You can look later,'' she said. ``Wash first.''\par

They gave me water in a room where I could close the door. I washed the dust from my face and arms. It gathered in the bottom of the basin; when Jean-Charles showed me the release, it went away through a hole too small for my smallest finger.\par

The shirt closed with discs pushed through slits in the cloth. I missed one and had to undo the others to put the edges level. The trousers showed each leg separately. I turned twice before the metal wall, searching the folded things for an outer wrapping.\par

When I returned, Jean-Charles pulled the back of my collar free where I had folded it under. Va-Elise handed me a pair of spectacles.\par

``My sight is good.''\par

``The lenses are clear. We want people to remember an ordinary young man with glasses.''\par

She sat me in front of a camera, then took the spectacles off again. On the table were a telephone, a world map spread wider than my arms and a passport waiting for my image.\par

``Outside, your name is Kylian. Say it.''\par

I said it. She corrected the sound, took the picture and wrote the name in the outside hand. I copied the letters until she covered her version and I could make them without it.\par

I put down the pen. Va-Elise was putting the photograph against the passport. She gave me back the spectacles when she had finished, then named the places on the map: Switzerland, Geneva, Saanen. I followed her finger across the printed water.\par

``Someone may take this from you and look at your face,'' she said, closing the passport. ``Let him. Stay beside me. If he speaks, wait for me to answer.''\par

Jean-Charles lifted the lid from a dish of chicken, its skin browned and shining with spice. Steam rose from yellow rice heaped with soft onions. Va-Elise spooned the cooking juices over my plate; beside her waited a tray of small pastries glossy with honey, their ends packed with crushed almonds.\par

Jean-Charles took his glass and raised it a little.\par

``Measured. Witnessed'' he said, grinning.\par

Va-Elise looked at him. ``Do not.''\par

``Sorry bad joke'' he added, and set it down. "We don't make a ceremony of supper, so eat while it's hot."\par

I laughed, and had to put down my glass. Va-Elise shook her head and went on serving.\par

``You did well in the car,'' he said. ``Kept your head. You're safe now.''\par

The skin cracked between my teeth. Beneath it the meat came away wet with lemon and the juices that had soaked into the rice. I ate until Va-Elise reached for my plate, then held it out for more. Jean-Charles was still on his first helping.\par

He pushed the pastries towards me. Honey ran over my thumb when I bit into one; the thin layers broke against my lips, releasing the almonds. I licked my thumb and picked up the flakes from my plate.\par

After the meal Va-Elise cleared a place beside my glass and set a book there. ``We were told you might enjoy this. It's about how people out here have studied light.''\par

I cleaned the honey from my fingers. The cover showed a beam bending through a block of glass. I followed its passage with my finger. Below the title, a strip of paper bore the words in careful Mountain hand.\par

\textit{Light --- David Burnie.}\par

I read the title twice. I could feel myself grinning, and then my eyes filled. Va-Elise waited while I wiped them on my sleeve.\par

``Thank you,'' I said.\par

I opened it at the beginning. Thin sheets of handwriting had been bound between its printed pages. There were photographs of objects with their names and descriptions. I recognised the candle and the mirror, and lingered over instruments with polished tubes and small wheels. Each label had its translation.\par

``You translated all of it?''\par

``The whole book. We kept the English beside it. Your tutor will help you with that.''\par

I began with \textit{Light, myth, and magic}. The opening paragraph asked what would happen if the sun failed to rise: water would freeze, plants and animals would die. I read on to its assurance that this could not happen, then turned the page.\par

On the next spread, Prometheus carried fire down from the mountain where the gods lived. The caption said he had stolen it for men. I read the paragraph again.\par

I took a loose thread from the edge of my cuff and laid it beneath his name. ``May I take it with me?''\par

``It's yours, Kylian.''\par

Va-Elise took the telephone into the next room and shut the door. Jean-Charles sat opposite me and asked about the road out of the Mountain. He wanted the marks on the door I had left unopened, the soundless room's position beside it, whether the Mountain had stayed under seal until I was out. I answered with the book on my knees. He waited when I stopped, then asked about the next crossing.\par

Near the end he said, ``No need to trouble Elise with any of that. She writes everything down, and a man should keep some of his own weather.''\par

I nodded and kept my hand on the book. When Va-Elise returned, I carried it to bed myself and lay down with my shoes still on. She woke me before it was light again. The Toyota had a new rear window. Va-Elise took the wheel; Jean-Charles sat beside her.\par

At the airfield Va-Elise handed papers through a small window. A man came out and opened a gate; our vehicle stopped beside a white aircraft with a gold cross dividing green and black on its tail.\par

``Jamaican diplomatic flight,'' Va-Elise said. ``To Geneva. Stay with me.''\par

The captain came down the steps to meet us, a lean, dark-skinned man with his hair tied high behind his head and gold bars on the shoulders of his white shirt. He shook Jean-Charles's hand and kept it while they laughed. Then he offered me his.\par

``Capt'n Billy.''\par

I gave him the name Va-Elise had told me to use. He repeated it while Jean-Charles lifted our bags from the vehicle and handed them to one of the crew.\par

``Back to the desert for me,'' Jean-Charles said. ``Stay with Elise.''\par

He got behind the wheel. Billy led Va-Elise and me up the steps; from the top I watched the vehicle turn towards the gate. Wide leather seats faced one another across a table. Va-Elise put her papers on it. Billy rested a hand on the back of my seat while she translated between us, his English into Mountain speech, my answers back to him.\par

``Next time you come to Jamaica. You wanna go snorkelling? You wanna eat lobster? You wanna smoke weed? You can do all dees tings. Just let Capt'n Billy know.''\par

Va-Elise repeated the English slowly before giving me each part. I asked what snorkelling was. She put two fingers beside her eyes, pointed downward and said that I could breathe while I looked under the sea. Billy watched the demonstration and nodded.\par

Va-Elise looked up from her papers. ``Geneva first, Billy.''\par

``Yes, ma'am.'' He pulled once on my fastened belt, then went forward to the other pilot and shut the door.\par

The floor tilted beneath my feet; pressure closed my ears. The earth withdrew below the window.\par

``Do not look down until I say,'' Va-Elise told me.\par

I watched the fastener of my belt. A loose end of the strap lifted each time I breathed. When I looked up, the line of land beyond her shoulder lay far below us. Water reached from it to the edge of the window. She put her papers down and talked while I kept my eyes on her.\par

Somewhere over that water Va-Elise put a cup beside my hand and returned to her papers. I waited for the drink to spill. Its surface trembled, settled, and stayed almost level while the land passed slowly below us. I drank without taking my eyes from the rim. After a while I put the cup down and let go of the seat.\par

I opened the book at the thread and asked Va-Elise to read the English beneath Prometheus. She read it slowly, then again as I followed the printed words with my finger.\par

The next chapter was \textit{Shadows}. A caption about an eclipse named Jamaica. Opposite, beside a shadow theatre, I found a portrait and asked her the man's name.\par

``Johannes Kepler.''\par

She read me the short account of his work on light and shadows. I asked her to say his name again.\par

``Is Jamaica across this sea?'' I asked.\par

``Across the Atlantic. Much farther.''\par

She took out her telephone and showed me a photograph of a small boat in clear blue water. I could see its shadow on the sand beneath it.\par

``Could we go there?''\par

``Geneva first, Kylian.''\par

She left the picture open beside my book.\par

At Geneva a vehicle met us beside the aircraft and took us to a helicopter. ``The family's,'' Va-Elise said. She held my sleeve against its beating wind until I reached the door.\par

From it I saw mountains with roads cut across their faces, houses standing on the slopes and water gathered between them. Church towers rose above the roofs. A boat crossed the lake below us, leaving two lines that spread until I could no longer distinguish them. We landed on a helipad beside this house above the water. The air smelled of pine, metal and bread.\par
