\begin{center}\small\itshape Mountain - inside the Greater Seal\end{center}

\begin{room}

\textit{In Noxius}

(Paul): --- I need your account of the hearing. The sealed chamber, the five, the ruling. Will you begin with where you stood?

(Kailan): --- No. Put the thing away. It fell three days since. It is falling still, Paul, on people who never heard our names. Who is left above to read how carefully we decided?

(Paul): --- I cannot answer that. The reel keeps what the five said. It does not keep what you could see from the wall.

(Kailan): --- You were in the room. Your machine ran the whole while. Ask the Keepers. Ask Sarah, ask Va-Sheva --- they spoke. I stood with my hands bound and said almost nothing.

(Paul): --- Va-Sheva refuses me; Sarah turns away; the Keepers are turning air and counting fish. I have asked them. At present I have my account, and I was occupied with the machine for much of it.

(Kailan): --- I have a shift on the vents. Before it, I need to lie still. There is nothing in me to give you.

\centerline{[\,---\,]}

(Paul): --- Then leave the hearing. The road from Blackwood. Could you give me that?

(Kailan): --- \ldots{} You kept the record. Give them the road yourself. I will take it up inside the Mountain. Paul. Let me rest.

\end{room}

I am Paul Morgan. This is my account of the return. My training is in strata, middens, worked flint and bone laid down in silt forty thousand years ago. The dates and intervals here come from the reel boxes and my notebooks. Where I stopped recording, I have used my memory; where I could not understand a speaker, I name the person who supplied the words.\par

At dusk I came down the steps of Blackwood with one bag holding my notebooks, interview reels and travelling recorder. The dressing Kailan had put round my right hand caught under the strap. I freed it as Va-Elise waved me toward the helicopter. Behind her, men carried cases through doors I had never seen open. A sheet of paper crossed the lawn, rose into the rotor wind and vanished over the roof.\par

Kailan waited at the door while a crewman loaded Sarah's chair. She was still giving orders when we lifted. Her right thumb shook against the headset until she closed it beneath her left hand.\par

She called the outer stations separately. Two calls ended with her spoken acknowledgment. During the third she repeated a number, waited and marked a cross in her book. She began the fourth with the pencil still against the page. Kailan sat opposite me; at each bank I drew the bag back against my ankles.\par

At Geneva, Urs stopped at the hangar's red line and we boarded the private aircraft. There were beds and a shower. Food arrived through a door I failed to notice, and I ate all of it. Kailan watched the window. Sarah ate half a piece of toast and slept upright. Once she opened her eyes. ``Finish your meal, Mr Morgan. I do not know when any of us will eat properly again.''\par

Va-Elise spread her maps at the table. Where the road ended, she pointed out our meeting place with Amastan. ``Beyond this, his ground.''\par

``If he cannot reach it, keep them under cover,'' Sarah said. ``Nobody goes looking. Keep enough fuel to take the drivers back.''\par

I lay down. We flew through the night; I woke as we began our descent.\par

\ornament

At dawn we came down onto old colonial tarmac. Four massive black tactical 4x4s stood beyond the lamps, engines turning, windows dark above the high slabs of their doors. Jean-Charles came from the second to meet us. Kailan spoke his name and raised a hand. Va-Elise was already giving the drivers their maps.\par

A tall blonde woman stood beside the driver's door, her hair cropped above her ears, a fitted black shirt tucked into her field trousers. She handed Va-Elise a roll of satellite prints.\par

Men loaded water and medical cases while Sarah refused the chair. Kailan stayed one step below her until she reached the tarmac. She stood there to recover her breath, watching the baggage go in. Jean-Charles checked a long weapon case and slid it beneath his seat.\par

``Alice. Do you remember the route to the first stop?'' Va-Elise asked.\par

``My name is Alice, and I remember everything.''\par

Jean-Charles laughed. ``She's been saving that one.''\par

Alice clipped the map beside her wheel. ``When the vehicles turn back, you stay with us,'' Sarah told her.\par

Va-Elise drove the lead. Alice took the second, Jean-Charles beside her; Sarah, Kailan and I were strapped into the row behind. Two more crews followed. The sun cleared the horizon as all four swung onto the tar.\par

The acceleration pinned the bag against my stomach. Road markers came at us too fast to read. A rise lifted my weight out of the seat; the belt caught me and the road struck back through the floor. Sarah gripped the handle above her head. Jean-Charles watched the mirrors.\par

The tar ended. Va-Elise left it at speed and a wall of dust rose behind her. Alice drove along its edge, the front wheels hammering through ruts I saw as they vanished beneath us. Once the lead vehicle came wholly clear of the ground. It landed in a burst of sand and kept going. Through our rear glass the other two appeared and disappeared in the enormous brown cloud rolling across the pan.\par

A knot of vehicles stood far to the east. Men beside them turned to watch. I had seen convoys like ours outside ministries; nobody came onto the track. Jean-Charles kept his hand on the closed weapon case until they were gone behind the dust.\par

At a fork Alice followed Va-Elise's single flash of brake light. We crossed the wash so hard that my teeth met. Beyond it the track narrowed between rocks and the engines dropped a note. I tried to ask how much farther. The next rut took the words.\par

\ornament

Near the end of the track stood one of Sarah's stations, its door open to the desert. Jean-Charles went in and called us after him. Sarah stopped with her hand against the frame.\par

``They had orders to keep this shut. Direct clearance for every visitor.''\par

Three enamel cups stood beside a kettle. Water ran from the tap. Blood had dried on the floor and the table lip; there were no bodies. A duty board bore three names. On the back of a stores request someone had written \emph{door opened with}. The next letter was a downward stroke. I read the words into the recorder.\par

Sarah tried the door with its key. The frame, hinges and lock were undamaged. Va-Elise went out to examine the approach while Jean-Charles searched the rear rooms. Beside the drum, a broken chair held the dark print of a hand.\par

The drum kept turning. Five strokes came up from the ground beneath us and drew themselves on its paper. Kailan stood before them without touching the sheet. They were evenly spaced, with the same rise. When the fifth ended, the stylus went back to its thin, irregular line. Sarah watched the interval and checked her wristwatch.\par

``Again in forty minutes,'' she said. ``These are the same five strokes. Same height, same spacing. They are sounding on a programme.''\par

A record box had burned in the corner. I lifted a blackened leaf with my forceps; beneath it, fuel and water entries remained readable. The stores request reported the same five-stroke signal. Va-Elise returned with photographs of tyre marks beyond the rise. Outside the door she showed us two sets of prints and a scuff that became parallel heel cuts.\par

``Taken, probably,'' Sarah said. ``Three people who can read my notation and show where these lines are buried. Elise, I want them back.''\par

She read the names into my recorder with their offices and years of service. At the third she looked back at the board. Va-Elise found the route drawer unlocked and empty.\par

``A visitor they admitted?'' I asked.\par

``Possibly. Or a key, or a threat. We have no witness.''\par

She opened her station book and crossed six call signs. Beside them stood hours and dead-drop times. Va-Elise copied the six entries.\par

``They share outside support with this post,'' Sarah said. ``Recall each separately through the central office, by its cleared channel. No resupply on these roads. Bring out the people; break what they cannot carry. Nobody returns to Blackwood.''\par

She put the station key into Va-Elise's palm. ``Open the files before you burn them.''\par

Elise went out to the drivers. Sarah corrected the third man's years of service for my recorder. When we carried the water out, the next five strokes began. The kettle answered against the table. Sarah counted the interval aloud and told us to keep moving.\par

\ornament

The vehicles stopped where the track entered folded rock. A young man waited there with two others and eight animals, his face bound against the glare. Sarah called him Amastan.\par

He counted us and our loads before he answered her. He rejected two water tins whose caps rang against the handles and made me wrap my bag's metal catches in felt. His companions repacked the animals in the shade of the folds. When nothing metal could strike metal, he uncovered his face.\par

``The man you knew has been thirty years in the ground,'' he said. ``Ka-Dhavar chose me to carry the name.'' He drew the last strap through its ring. ``Your officers put it in their treaties long before either of us. They spelled it wrong.''\par

Sarah told him about the emptied station and the missing three. He looked toward the folds. ``They searched the tents at the western well yesterday. My wife and children are at the last hide. I take you to Shield if they come in with us.''\par

``I cannot grant that,'' Sarah said.\par

Kailan came round the animal. ``Sheva can hear you at the seam.''\par

``Before any of you cross it.''\par

``Yes.''\par

Va-Elise opened a map on the bonnet of the lead vehicle. Amastan looked once, drew a different line in the dust with the butt of his switch and put three stones across the route she had marked.\par

``That road was safe when I took over. It has tyre cuts now. Men watched it two nights ago.'' He moved the line west through a blank place on her map. ``We go here.''\par

Amastan made Jean-Charles repeat the turns, corrected the third and erased the drawing with his foot. Va-Elise gave Jean-Charles his tactical packet: this sector and two abort points. ``For the rest, ask him.''\par

She collected the drivers' maps and took the instruction-camera case back to the lead vehicle. A spare driver from the fourth vehicle took Alice's wheel. Alice put her water and rolled prints into a saddlebag. Elise led the four vehicles out; beyond the folds two went north, one east and one toward the old tarmac. Amastan waited until their dust had thinned, shifted Sarah's saddle one hole and gave the word to move.\par

We set out at night and lay under ground-coloured cloth by day. Amastan chose the hours, the wells and the order of drinking. The animals drank before us.\par

Amastan walked ahead without a light; his last companion brushed the places where a hoof slipped. Jean-Charles twice offered a bearing. Amastan continued along his own line. On the second night he raised a closed hand and every buckle stilled. An engine reached us through the soles of my boots, became audible beyond the ridge and passed out of hearing before he lowered his hand.\par

We reached a well before dawn, a crack at the back of a cleft. Amastan removed six fitted slabs, drew water with a skin narrow enough to pass between them, watered the animals and replaced the slabs in reverse order. His companion brushed the rock with a branch. When they had finished, I could not find the opening from three paces away.\par

``Does the Mountain keep this well?'' I asked.\par

``My people keep it.''\par

Sarah rode in a sling they fitted around the saddle. At ninety-three she could still mount on the first night. By the third, Amastan and Kailan had to lift her. She allowed it without speaking. At noon her hand shook against the rim of her cup. I set the machine between my knees and held the cup while she drank. When it was empty she said, ``Mr Morgan, if that appears in your account, at least spell my name correctly.''\par

``I always do.''\par

``One useful quality.''\par

Amastan shortened the next passage. He moved the longest climb into the coldest hour and put Kailan at the left side of Sarah's animal, where the ground fell away. Sarah caught the guide's sleeve before he could go ahead.\par

``We have already lost half a day.''\par

He looked down at her hand. ``You could not lift your foot into the sling.''\par

``I have crossed this country since before you were born.''\par

``Then let him hold you on the climb.'' Amastan waited. Sarah released his sleeve and put her hand on the saddle.\par

We left at the hour he had chosen. On the third night he stopped at a cairn and counted its stones with his switch. ``One has been added,'' he said. He left it standing and took us six hours around.\par

At the next halt Kailan called the folds the Mountain's outer wall, the last country no state had crossed. Amastan had the end of a load strap between his teeth. He drew it tight, fastened it and came round the animal to face Kailan.\par

``Whose men changed that cairn?'' he asked.\par

Kailan looked back along the fold. ``I do not know.''\par

``Nor do I. That is why we walked around.'' Amastan picked up his switch. ``Your Mountain may call this its wall. Ask it who keeps the wells.''\par

Kailan moved back to the left of Sarah's animal. I recorded speech in the daylight hides and a few minutes at each change of ground: hoof-fall on sand, iron striking buried stone. I marked the unrecorded intervals in my notebook. Amastan held the ground-cloth over my hands while I changed batteries.\par

On the fourth night the five strokes reached us without paper or instrument. I felt the first through my feet. Sarah's animal raised its head. Four more followed at equal distances, too low to hear and distinct through the ground. Amastan put his fingers to the rock. Sarah counted. We covered the metal fittings, gave the animals the final water and went forward on foot.\par

\ornament

In the last dark of the fourth night we reached the high cleft. Amastan's wife waited under a cloth with two children. Two more animals stood tethered behind her; filled water skins lay beside their packs. She spoke to Kailan in the Mountain's language. He asked which quarter she had come from.\par

``We are of your people,'' she said. ``Both born outside, among the Kel Ad. We trained in Dhavar's rooms. These two have never been under stone.'' The smaller child held the edge of her sleeve.\par

Amastan told her what Kailan had agreed. ``The seam is below their posts,'' he said.\par

``I have water for two nights. After that I must take them past the western well.'' She pulled a small bag from beneath the cloth. ``I am coming with you.''\par

His companions took charge of the animals. His wife stayed with the children while Amastan brought us up through a dry wash to the camp overlook and stopped us below its rim. He pressed a palm to the ground.\par

``From there onward, their instruments can hear a stone roll.''\par

He took Jean-Charles forward on his belly. When they returned, Jean-Charles asked him to repeat the passage between two listening posts. Amastan traced the first turns against the rock.\par

``At the last bend you go alone. If they see me, they will watch the way I came.''\par

Jean-Charles removed his coat, covered the pale skin of his hands and face, and tightened the cloth at his collar. He saw me watching.\par

``Still taking notes on me, professor?''\par

``Your questions at the safe house are on the record. So is your request that Kailan keep them from Elise.''\par

He pulled the cloth across his mouth. ``And Sarah heard him tell it. She still asked me to get her through their posts.''\par

``That is on the record too.''\par

``Good. Keep the machine dry.''\par

Amastan led him over the rim and came back alone. I heard nothing from Jean-Charles before the light came.\par

Grey light entered the pan while we lay against cold rock. Dishes, tents and parked vehicles separated from the ground. Beyond them stood the drilling rig. Two pale flecks crossed the sky without sound. Alice watched them, checked her watch and gave Amastan the next times she expected overhead coverage. A dish beside the command tents turned slowly and stopped. I kept my face below the rim.\par

Through the glass I saw men on the ridges facing inward, with tripods among them pointed into the rock. Survey stakes made straight red lines across the pan. Alice spread her latest print beneath the glass and marked two posts absent from it. Sarah named a western truck's position as the northern mouth of an old drovers' track to the Oasis. Shield had allowed it to pass out of use, she said; no Outsider was supposed to know where that mouth lay.\par

``They were told,'' Kailan said.\par

``I think so.'' Sarah pointed below the rig. ``West of your Hall. What lies deeper along that line?''\par

``The reserve.''\par

``Water?''\par

He nodded.\par

A double ring of tents occupied the camp's centre. Generator housings stood beside it, and men were loading stretchers into long vehicles. I watched an orderly carry a folded blanket past the inner cordon. Armed men at its entrance kept a truck waiting while its driver showed a paper. I handed the glass back.\par

Sarah kept her eye to it. ``A good half of what you see was written in my hand.'' She followed the orderly until he went inside a tent.\par

``Some part of me wanted the Mountain found. I wanted not to be the last one holding it.'' She lowered the glass into the space between us.\par

We waited through the daylight under cloth Amastan weighted below the rim. Whenever he brought Sarah water, she asked whether Jean-Charles had returned. Alice kept the glass on the wash. Men changed at the posts; vehicles moved between the rig and stacked pipe. Heat shook their outlines. When the shadow reached our ledge again, Sarah sat up without help.\par

We heard no shot. Late in the day, Jean-Charles appeared in the wash with a man bent in front of him. Amastan went down to meet them, placing their steps as they climbed. The prisoner wore the camp's grey clothes. His wrists were bound; a gag held his mouth. Jean-Charles carried a flat hard case and a black field radio.\par

``Post nine,'' he said. ``The seam is open beyond it. Not for long.''\par

He set the case beside my recorder and said he had taken it from a locked drawer in the command tent. I had not seen him enter it. Sarah opened the case with the lid toward the camp, shielding the white paper. Consecutive issue marks ran through the leaves; corrections impressed the sheets beneath them. I matched two impressions against the light.\par

``The leaves were together when these corrections were made,'' I said. ``I cannot certify their contents.''\par

Sarah touched the mark on the first sheet. ``French strategic service. The mineral company is a cover. This is a state operation.''\par

She translated into the Mountain's language: bore depths and dates, capture medicine, water control, approach denial. The personnel annex counted three thousand and eighty-four people, including three hundred and eighty-six French service numbers. I checked the subtotals.\par

``The Algerian concessions are genuine,'' she said. ``But the central coordinates are withheld from their copies. Each office can show its permits without seeing what we have here.''\par

Kailan leaned across the case. ``What do they know of the mechanism?''\par

``That there is one. These are orders to reach it. My grid, my methods.'' She held the page at its lower corners. ``They have not written down what they expect it to do.''\par

Three leaves into the logistics annex, I found the local name Sarah had used for the northern track. It stood among forty routes. Sarah translated the entry beside it: \emph{source: internal, reliability high}.\par

``After Martinez was shut out, who held that corridor?''\par

``One channel.'' She kept the page on the rock between us.\par

``Jean-Charles?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``The entry does not name him. And he brought us the case.''\par

She folded the page once. ``Keep that qualification in your account. I am keeping this.''\par

She put it inside her coat. Jean-Charles watched the wash beside the prisoner. He had asked no question about the papers.\par

The field radio lit. A young voice spoke French. Jean-Charles covered the set until the light died. Sarah translated: post nine had missed its check; if Farid had crawled behind a rock to sleep again, he could answer to the sergeant.\par

Amastan cut the gag. The prisoner raised his head when Sarah repeated Farid's name. Amastan held the water skin for him because his wrists were tied behind his back. Water ran into the hollow above his collar; when Amastan lowered the skin, Farid bent after it. Amastan let him drink again.\par

Sarah put our questions to him in French and gave us his replies. He said he guarded a mineral survey. He knew his own watch and the two posts beside it. He had been told there were ``primitives'' underground, a lost tribe to be handled \emph{without loss of life}. Sappers were to preserve what they found intact.\par

Kailan crouched where Farid could see his face. Sarah stayed beside them to translate.\par

``Who told you to take no life?'' Kailan asked.\par

``A medical officer. I do not know his name.''\par

``The same officer who sets the charges?''\par

``No.''\par

``Who can stop the digging?''\par

``Operations. Not our command.''\par

``Who among you may speak to the men on the western ridge?''\par

Farid looked toward it. ``No one at my post. They have their own command.''\par

Kailan drew the case toward his knees. Sarah found the live-capture orders and translated the five priorities, each coded by task with the name omitted. He stopped her at three and repeated them in his own words: the one who reads the deep water; the one who keeps the hours; the one who holds the seal. Sarah pointed to the other two entries and gave him their wording again.\par

``Read those to him,'' he said.\par

Sarah did. Farid shook his head at each. He tried to lean away from the case; his bound hands caught against the rock.\par

``Five functions,'' Kailan said to Sarah. ``They want all five alive. Put them together under guard and make them work it.''\par

``Can they?'' Sarah asked.\par

Kailan kept the page between his hands. ``That is what the division is meant to prevent.''\par

Farid looked from Kailan to Sarah. She had stopped translating. He pulled his wrists against the binding and looked again at the water skin.\par

The radio spoke again, in a lower voice. ``The sergeant,'' Sarah said. Lights came on at two posts.\par

``The road is open,'' Jean-Charles said. ``Amastan knows a seam beyond post nine their instruments do not yet cover. Farid's missed check has shortened our hour to minutes.''\par

One of Amastan's companions moved Farid's bindings to the front and led him into the high cleft. He carried the water skin with them. ``We keep him until your road is shut,'' Amastan said. ``Then he goes.''\par

Kailan put the page back and closed the case. Jean-Charles took its handle, put it into Sarah's hand and gave me the field radio. ``We go now.''\par

We went back into the high cleft. Amastan's wife had fastened the small bag high on her back. She gave each child water, drank from the same skin and handed it to one of the wellkeepers. The older child put a hand on her belt. Amastan slung a long carrying cloth against his back and lifted the smaller one.\par

Sarah drew Alice beside the animals. ``Go out with them. Keep a way back while it is safe, then withdraw to Elise. Tell her to bring out the other posts. Do not wait for us if this road closes.''\par

Alice checked her map with the nearer wellkeeper. He pointed beyond the cleft. ``We leave by the west cut. You follow me.''\par

``I have Elise's next call,'' Alice said. She folded the map and put it away.\par

We left the animals and blankets with them. I bound my bag high against my ribs. Sarah tried to crawl with the station book inside her coat; its spine held her elbow out. She tore out the crossed leaf with its call signs, hours and dead-drop timings, folded it with the annex inside her sleeve and left the book under a stone.\par

Amastan led us to the rim. Three lights moved along the wash Jean-Charles had used; another swept the ridge above post nine. Men called in pairs. The drilling continued beneath them, a regular iron turning that covered smaller sounds and then stopped without warning. Each time it stopped, we stopped with it.\par

At the first turn Amastan placed our feet in his own marks. At the second his wife took the smaller child while he helped Sarah across a slab that sounded hollow when my knee touched it. At the third he pointed to a black cut no wider than a doorway.\par

``That cut reaches the old seam,'' he said.\par

Sarah gripped his wrist. ``Close the northern well.''\par

``It was closed before you came.''\par

``The other two?''\par

``The wellkeepers have the warning. One to each.''\par

He looked at Kailan. ``Shield will have to ask us before sending anyone that way again. I will tell Dhavar myself.''\par

Jean-Charles entered the cut first, Sarah behind him, Kailan behind her, then me. Amastan's wife followed with the children between her and their father. We moved on elbows where the roof came down. The radio against my ribs gave one burst of French through its cloth. Kailan's foot stopped beside my hand. Ahead, Sarah whispered, ``Eight through twelve. Close the western arc. Find Farid.''\par

I passed the set forward. Jean-Charles silenced it and sent it back through Sarah and Kailan.\par

``Could you answer them?'' Sarah whispered.\par

``I do not know the man's alarm word.''\par

I wrapped the radio with its control facing away from me. Sarah pushed the case flat and crawled after it.\par

The cut opened above a slope of broken shale. Across it stood post nine: two men, a low tent and a tripod turning by degrees. Beyond the post, the ground met the Mountain in an unbroken face. I could see no seam. A white lamp moved along the base of the rock, withdrew and returned.\par

Jean-Charles counted the tripod's turns with one finger. On the fifth he took us across. For twelve paces the shale shifted under every knee. We placed each stone in the space the hand ahead had left. Sarah's breath became audible. Kailan stopped and drew her arm across his shoulders. I looked back as Amastan's wife passed the smaller child to him and took the older one's wrist. The tripod turned away. We moved again.\par

A voice challenged from the post. Jean-Charles put us flat and answered in French. I have no French, and he was not loud. The voice asked a second question. He gave a shorter reply. A man stepped beyond the tent and raised his lamp toward us. At that moment the drilling rig began again. Jean-Charles pulled Sarah to her feet and ran bent beneath her arm. Kailan took her other side. I went after them with the recorder striking my ribs. When I looked back, the lamp crossed the place where we had lain. Small stones were still rolling down from it.\par

The rock face remained whole until Jean-Charles put his hand into a shadow below it. His arm disappeared to the wrist. Kailan knelt, cleared three handfuls of gravel and uncovered a narrow bronze plate with five shallow cups. He pressed the outer two, the centre, then the outer two again. Nothing moved.\par

Jean-Charles kept his shoulder at the stone. ``Nothing.''\par

``They have changed the return,'' Amastan said. He passed the child to his wife and knelt beside Kailan.\par

Sarah drew her feet clear of his knees. ``You have room. Try.''\par

Amastan set another pattern and held the centre cup. Jean-Charles leaned harder against the stone. A line opened without sound. Cold air came through it. Two hands reached from the dark. Amastan called into the opening. ``My household first. Will Shield receive us?''\par

Va-Sheva's voice came through the opening. ``All four of you have shelter. Send the children.''\par

He took the smaller child from his wife and passed it into the hands. She lifted the older one through, then followed. Amastan went after her. Sarah passed the case to Kailan and took the hands; they pulled her inside. He passed the case through and followed it. Jean-Charles caught my coat at the shoulder and pushed me after him.\par

The white lamp found the bronze plate as he entered. A shot struck the rock above the opening. The hands inside pulled him clear; the seam closed on a fall of dust. No weapon answered from within.\par

For several breaths I heard our breathing and the recorder turning inside my bag. Someone close to me coughed. Then a lamp was uncovered.\par

\ornament

Va-Sheva stood in Shield grey between two wardens. Her belt lay above the swelling of her pregnancy. One warden held the seam; the other searched us. Jean-Charles laid down his weapons. Amastan unrolled the carrying cloth and put down a rifle and two small radios. Sarah showed her folded pages and put down the captured case; I set the field radio beside it and opened my bag to show the recorder. Amastan's wife held both children against her while the warden searched her bag.\par

Va-Sheva pointed at the machine. ``You and that will answer to Justice. Open no reel before then.''\par

The warden wrapped the captured case and field radio for a stone recess. He laid Amastan's cloth and equipment beside Jean-Charles's weapons. Sarah returned her pages to her sleeve. Blood from the shale had soaked through one knee of her trousers. Va-Sheva pointed to it.\par

``After the hearing,'' Sarah said.\par

``Bind it while she speaks,'' Va-Sheva told the warden.\par

The warden knelt to bind Sarah's knee. Va-Sheva faced Kailan across the lamp.\par

``You were to report through me. Your errand did not authorise you to bring Outsiders and our recorded speech through that seam.'' Her hand passed from Kailan to Sarah, Jean-Charles and me. ``By the law I hold, I should kill you here and close the stone over the four of you.''\par

Kailan looked at the warden by the seam. ``Then hear the finding before you carry out the law. You are the ear they gave me.''\par

``I am listening.''\par

``The men outside mean to take the five alive. Sarah has their orders. The bore is aimed below the Hall. Hear her measure.''\par

Sarah held her leg still under the warden's hands. ``My methods brought them this far. I can show Shield what they are doing with them.''\par

Va-Sheva faced her. ``How deep?''\par

Sarah gave the measure. Va-Sheva asked the angle, the number of rigs and the interval between the five strokes. Sarah answered each. The warden pulled the bandage tight; she stopped on a number, then gave it again.\par

Va-Sheva turned to Jean-Charles. ``You brought them through the outer posts?''\par

``Yes. They have challenged us. The seam was seen.''\par

``I have worked beside you. I trust your hands.'' She sent the inner warden to summon Shield and Justice.\par

``Under necessity of Shield, I admit the four of you as far as the sealed floor and no farther. Justice will decide whether the five hear you. The seam you used is dead from this hour. I answer for the admission.''\par

The warden drove a bronze pin into the five-cup plate. Three descending blows sounded inside the wall. Va-Sheva called him away from the closed seam. ``Take the mother and children to the lower ward. Bring Amastan to Ka-Dhavar. He will want the roads before he goes to Justice.''\par

The warden returned her bag. She drew the older child's hand back to her belt and followed him. Amastan went with them. Va-Sheva lifted the lamp. Jean-Charles and Kailan took Sarah between them; I followed through the inner door.\par

At the crossings Va-Sheva sent wardens ahead for Stone's survey and back for the captured case. Sarah gave her the three bores, one deep and two shallow, and the date the orders named for reaching the Hall.\par

``I cannot certify their progress,'' she said. ``I need your survey. You may be able to collapse a throat ahead of the bit, if you can lose the chambers beyond.''\par

With a warden's marking stone she drew the rig and its angle on the passage wall. Kailan placed the Hall.\par

``The reserve is deeper,'' he said. ``I cannot give you its depth.''\par

Va-Sheva studied the marks and wiped them away with her sleeve. ``Justice will want what you saw, what she measured and what the papers say. Keep those separate.''\par

Where the stair turned, Sarah and Jean-Charles had gone several steps ahead. Va-Sheva lowered her voice. I was one step behind Kailan with the recorder against my ribs.\par

``Elise wrote that you did not speak for eleven days at the house. Is it true?''\par

``Yes.''\par

``Could you drink?''\par

``When she put the cup in my hands.''\par

``Sleep?''\par

``Not enough.''\par

Va-Sheva raised the lamp to see the next step. ``I would have ordered the containment again.''\par

Kailan stayed below her. She lowered the lamp a little.\par

``Do not ask me for more tonight,'' she said. ``I need to keep you alive long enough to answer them.''\par

She climbed after the others. Kailan waited until the space of one step had opened between them, then followed.\par

\ornament

The route crossed an open stair above the common wards. Lamps burned over food stalls. A woman walked twelve children in pairs; the smallest held the back of the child's tunic ahead of him and dragged one sandal. A seller weighed roots in a hanging pan while two men disputed his weights. Someone beat dust from a mat over a rail and earned a shout from the level beneath. Water ran in a channel cut through the centre of the floor.\par

No one looked upward as we came onto the stair. I had spent two years comparing tool marks, copied signs and the mineral residue on objects Sarah put before me. Below us a man mended a pot with wire. He held its broken halves between his knees and worked the edges together until the painted band beneath the rim joined across the break. He passed the wire through a drilled hole, drew it tight and turned the pot to reach the other side. His cup stood outside the circle of fragments at his feet. I wanted to ask what he used to seal the holes.\par

At a table under three lamps, a baker pressed his thumb into rounds of dough, turned each one and pressed again. The evening bread reached us before we crossed the centre of the stair: yeast, warm grain and the scorched flour at the edge of an oven. I knew charred cakes from sites nine thousand years old. I knew the pits in which the grain had been stored, the querns on which it had been broken and the changes in human bone after people began to eat it every day. The baker struck flour from his hands and called to a child to carry six loaves home.\par

The recorder knocked against the rail. Several faces lifted. Va-Sheva caught my sleeve before it struck again.\par

``They do not know,'' she said. ``They will not learn it from you on a stair. Walk.''\par

The child with the six loaves had stopped beneath us. One loaf slipped against his chest; he trapped it with his chin and laughed. I drew the machine close under my coat and walked. At the upper turn the stalls disappeared. The smell remained for twelve steps more.\par

\ornament

The first blow took the stair sideways.\par

Stone struck my shoulder. The recorder drove into my ribs beneath my coat. A lamp went out below; another rolled in a yellow circle until a hand caught it. Then the sound arrived through the floor: one flat concussion, a single force driven into the Mountain without the patient interval of the five-stroke sounding. Dust fell from the joints overhead. Far inside the rock, worked stone ground against worked stone.\par

Sarah put both hands on the wall. ``A charge. Down a shallow bore, close enough to break a void or test what breaks around it. North-west, near the surface. What lies there?''\par

Va-Sheva had turned toward the sound. She stood with the lamp held clear of the wall.\par

``What lies there?'' Sarah repeated.\par

``Faith,'' Va-Sheva said. ``A Faith passage. The road to the Hall.''\par

She ran. Jean-Charles went with her and the nearest warden. Sarah called for a lamp, a plan of the north-western arc and the keeper of Stone. The ward children below had begun to cry. The seller's hanging scale knocked against its post with each movement underfoot.\par

Kailan put his palm to the wall. I put mine beside it. A tremor travelled through the stone, stopped, then returned deeper and slower. Grit moved under my fingers. From beyond the turn came a sharper failure and Va-Sheva shouting for the passage to be cleared.\par

I lifted the bag toward the grinding and felt the recorder's motor through its flap. A warden set his lamp beside Kailan and ran ahead. Kailan took it. We ran after the others.\par
