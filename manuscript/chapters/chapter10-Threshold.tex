\typesetchapter{Chapter 10}{Threshold}

\begin{room}

--- She has given us a door in that desert, and the man who kept it. Sa-Kailan: you were raised inside the thing on the other side of that door, and taught that no outsider had ever stood within a hundred miles of it. Did your people keep any record at all of hands like hers?

--- \ldots{} Ask it the way you mean it, Paul. You are asking whether I was lied to.

--- I am asking what you found, and when you found it.

--- Then set the machine where I can watch it turn, and I will tell you about the night I became a criminal.

\end{room}

Everything I did in the Tomb, I did with my hands. The crime began on a single night, the night I carried something out of a chamber. What the Mountain drills into a child of Light I have already set down --- that the eye is innocent and the hand is guilty; that a man may gaze on anything so long as he leaves it where it lies. I gazed a long time in that chamber. Then I carried something out.\par

\ornament

For several days after the martyrdom, I waited for the Mountain to feel different.\par

It stayed the same.\par

Water still came by measure.
Children still recited badly in the teaching halls and were corrected by voices patient enough to become cruel.
Shield still closed passages without explaining itself.
Balance still marked loss and use with the same green dye.
Faith still spoke over jars and wounds and dead bees and frightened mothers and old men who had begun to forget the names of their own children.
Justice still sat where people wished no one were sitting.\par

The Mountain had changed my name, recorded my sequence, bound white cloth to my wrist, and placed me beneath Ka-Raedin in the chain of Light.
Then it had gone on living.\par

A boy might have resented it. The old Sa-Kailan would have. I could feel that old hunger sometimes, though shame had pressed it into a quieter shape. It rose when younger Candidates stopped speaking as I entered and looked at me with the startled obedience given to an office, no friendship left in it. It rose when Ka-Raedin dismissed me from a chamber before I had asked the question burning behind my teeth.\par

But what I had watched Ka-Syphiron do had changed something in me that pride could not argue with.\par

Who would sprint toward that? The next chamber. The next rule. The secret Ka-Raedin had crossed into with a clue I had given him without knowing it. A few months earlier I would have burned myself trying to follow --- watched corridors, misread reflections, risked humiliation for one stolen angle of my teacher's path, and thought myself brave for it.\par

Now, when the impulse stirred, I saw Ka-Syphiron opening his robe below the ribs. I saw the blade drawn from the lamp. I saw an old man give his body so that a link could be finished.\par

No.\par

If I rushed toward Keeper, I would be misreading what I had just watched the last Keeper do.\par

So I took my time. I learned the Veil's Chamber the way a man learns a room in which someone has died, though no body had ever lain there.\par

Permission had changed the room. It no longer pulled at me the way a forbidden place had; it only gave me work to do. I cleaned channels, copied markings under supervision, maintained the dry basins, checked the hidden apertures for dust, and stood in silence before the wave diagrams that had made Ka-Raedin Keeper. Lines spreading. Lines crossing. Lines broken around obstacles and returning where direct sight failed.\par

The hidden side held something.\par

On the sixth morning after the ceremony, while I was cleaning the rim of the small black basin, Ka-Raedin said,\par

``You have not asked me how I found it.''\par

I kept the cloth moving until the circle was clean.\par

``No, Keeper.''\par

``Why?''\par

``Because you would not answer.''\par

``That has not always stopped you.''\par

``No.''\par

He waited.\par

I set the cloth aside slowly. I wanted it down properly before I said anything dangerous.\par

``Before the ceremony, I would have thought every answer withheld from me was a door shut against my worth. Now I think some doors are shut because the person outside them has not yet learned what opening them costs.''\par

Ka-Raedin looked at me long enough that the silence became part of the answer.\par

``Ka-Syphiron did not die to make you timid.''\par

``No, Keeper.''\par

``Then what did he die to make you?''\par

``Sequenced.''\par

Ka-Raedin crossed to the wave wall and touched the edge of one of the old carvings with the back of his finger.\par

``You will be tempted,'' he said, ``to confuse patience with obedience.''\par

``I already am.''\par

``And?''\par

``They are not the same. Obedience waits because it is told to wait. Patience waits because the thing would be damaged by being taken too soon.''\par

He turned back to me.\par

``That sentence is too good for you.''\par

``Then I must have heard it somewhere.''\par

``No. That is what worries me.''\par

It was the closest thing to amusement he ever offered, and I received it without smiling. Once I would have stored such a line like stolen honey; now I let it pass.\par

``I will not chase your chamber,'' I said.\par

``My chamber?''\par

``The Keeper's Chamber. The path you found.''\par

``Why?''\par

``Because if I reach it before I understand why I should not desire it, I will arrive as the wrong man.''\par

His face stilled. Ka-Raedin had blood on that knowledge. I had seen it spill.\par

``Good,'' he said --- without sting this time, and without indulgence either.\par

Then, because he was Ka-Raedin, he added, ``Now clean the basin again. You left oil near the lower groove.''\par

I looked down. I had.\par

I cleaned it again.\par

I stayed curious. The hunger had not left me. But I no longer pressed at Faith's doors or followed Ka-Raedin through reflections like a child trying to catch an adult's shadow.\par

Instead, I watched.\par

And waiting changed the Mountain.\par

I began to see Light at the edges of Light. Small leaks. Accidental glints. Exterior light entering in wrong seasons; internal lamp-lines escaping through vents and returning from stone beyond the official paths.\par

At first I dismissed them as maintenance defects --- a tarnished plate, a wet patch catching more than it should. Yet certain wrongnesses repeated. A faint white flash beyond a high ventilation slot in the north-east service descent, visible only as the night lamps were trimmed. A reflected thread along the upper edge of a sealed Shield route, present three nights and absent the fourth. A pale return from far outside the Mountain when internal lamps burned late and the air beyond the Oasis was dry.\par

I let the first glint pass. I recorded nothing. I spoke to no one. But I placed the observations inside myself in order. The old Sa-Kailan would have gone after the first brightness with a stolen lamp and a heroic justification. I watched for twelve nights.\par

I learned that the flash failed when high dust crossed the moon. I learned that it depended on a deeper glow from a maintenance shaft used after evening work. I learned that it appeared only from one position in the old vent gallery, and only if I lowered my head enough to see along the line where air entered before it became Mountain air.\par

Then memory supplied the missing shape.\par

As Sa-Kailan, Candidate of Light, with dust in my sandals and Va-Sheva beside me saying softly: \textit{Do not point.}\par

Va-Sheva closing possibilities before danger had a name.\par

A flash may be nothing. A turned marker may be nothing. An old woman out of place may be nothing. Three nothings close together become a shape.\par

Shield had built the world so that small wrongnesses could be read before they became deaths. It placed markers, watchers, angles, hidden bodies, and silent signals.\par

Light sees what can be shown.\par

Shield sees what must be stopped before it shows itself.\par

What had once been said to me as a rebuke now sounded like an invitation, and I distrusted it.\par

For several more nights I did nothing but make myself useful. I supervised younger Candidates in the practice halls. I spared them the teaching Va-Raedin had given me. I was still too young to make cruelty look like skill. When one boy rushed a false door and struck his shoulder, I waited until the exercise ended and said, privately,\par

``The room did not defeat you. Your first answer did.''\par

The boy looked at me with a resentment so familiar that I almost smiled.\par

Former peers avoided my gaze. Older Light attendants tested my authority with small omissions --- a report handed late, a lamp plate left unpolished, a route question phrased too casually. I did not answer with anger, and I did not soften it either.\par

But in the empty hours of the night, I returned to the glint.\par

I chose the route only after I could close my eyes and walk it in memory. From the eastern lower spine to the old vent gallery. Down the maintenance stair past the disused oil shelves. Through the narrow inspection slit that overlooked the sealed north passage. Pause for the third Shield change. Across the dark water service, where my robe would take damp if I brushed the left wall. Then the old air chamber above the cedar descent, where outside breath moved through stone before becoming Mountain air.\par

Deeper still lay the compost dark, where the Mountain took back what it had spent: kitchen scrap and night-soil and spoiled straw, turned and turned again by men working half-naked in the heat, the worms running thick under their forks.\par

The cistern water here stood green and half-alive, and a keeper drew a measure of the weed from it each day.\par

So far I had only looked.\par

On the night I chose, the Mountain was quiet after a minor mourning rite. Faith had held the lower Hall late for a child who had died of fever before naming. Such deaths were common enough to pass without shock, but they always altered its sound. The night lamps burned low. The air was dry. No dust cloud veiled the moon beyond the hidden openings. The glint appeared twice before second-night silence, faint and precise.\par

I waited until it appeared a third time.\par

Then I went.\par

I carried no blade. A blade would have made me a frightened Veil, pretending the desert was something metal could bargain with.\par

The passage outward was new to me. I had watched two maintenance workers remove its inner grille once, months earlier, under Shield supervision, and they had replaced it badly or deliberately. Either way it yielded to pressure applied where the lower stone had been shaved thinner.\par

Beyond it, the Mountain became less human.\par

Shield had touched it. Someone had cut handholds into the stone and worn them smooth with use. Someone had carved a line that fingers could find and low light hid. Yet the passage lacked Light's interpretive softness and Balance's measured signs. It existed because some men had to move through places the rest were forbidden even to picture.\par

Cold air came through cracks ahead.\par

I paused before the final opening. The subtler fear was worse than any curse out of the Book of Blood: that what I was calling wisdom, here at the boundary, was hunger. I had an answer ready --- \textit{Light must follow what is revealed} --- and I distrusted it, because it sounded exactly like something I would say to excuse myself.\par

Still, I opened the stone and stepped out.\par

The night desert was another country from the one I had seen by day --- larger, because it showed me almost nothing. The Mountain rose behind me as blackness with weight. The sky above was crowded with stars I had no training to name. I wrapped the mouth cloth against the instinct to breathe too quickly. The air tasted of stone, salt, and distance.\par

I kept low in the Mountain's shadow until the ground fell away toward the outer folds. The Oasis lay out of sight below another ridge, hidden even from me.\par

Once I found a line of small stones that might have been natural, except their flatter faces all turned the same way. I circled wider, losing time and confidence.\par

I was Light, and the desert made that plain quickly. My sandals, made for stone, slipped on loose gravel; the cold stiffened my fingers; the water skin knocked against my side until I tied it higher. I misjudged distance again and again --- a rock that seemed close took a hundred breaths to reach. I had memorised the glint's direction from within the Mountain, but without walls my eye had too much to take in, and it kept fooling me.\par

I stopped at the first low ridge and looked back.\par

From there the Mountain looked like a sleeping beast, vast and black, with a few hidden breaths of light where the vents opened under shadow. The land folded around it. The Mountain's shoulder cut the sightlines. A man could die out here within sight of the Oasis, only because the land never gave him the angle to see it.\par

--- Stop there a moment. You had never been outside the Mountain at night.

--- Once, as a child, before I was taken in. I do not trust the memory.

--- What did you make of the sky?

--- That it was crowded, and that I had no names for any of it. \ldots{} You have names for them.

--- Some. It is no secret of my century. Those lights are other suns, unthinkably far off; men have drawn shapes between them and hung old stories on the shapes; and one of them stands almost still, near the north, and has brought travellers home for longer than your Mountain has kept records.

--- \ldots{} A fixed light in the north.

--- \ldots{} You were keeping to the Mountain's shadow. Go on.

\end{room}

The glint appeared again while I was crossing the second wash --- once, from far ahead and slightly higher than I expected. Too clean. Too brief. A white point, then gone. I froze, crouched, and waited. Nothing. I shifted my head by a finger's width. Nothing.\par

I almost smiled. Then I remembered Va-Sheva saying: \textit{Do not start believing the outside speaks to you.}\par

The final approach took longer than I expected. The ground rose in broken shelves, each one stealing the next from sight. Twice I nearly turned back: the return was also a route to be preserved. I marked three stones with dust rubs invisible from a distance but plain to my trained eye. I counted steps between turns. I drank once, sparingly. The water tasted of leather and guilt.\par

The outcrop, when I reached it, looked natural at first.\par

From below the glint stayed dark. I moved around the stone slowly, searching for wrongness. A surface too smooth. A crack too straight. A place where the dust failed to gather. Shield would build a place so that no one would think to look for a door at all.\par

I found the polished mineral only after kneeling.\par

It sat in a shallow hollow no wider than my hand, hidden behind a lip of stone. A natural crystal face or mineral plate, polished by human effort and angled toward the Mountain. In daylight it would vanish among other pale scratches. At night, under the right internal lamp or moon angle, it caught and returned a single point: a signal or warning that could be read only by one placed to read it.\par

Beside it, almost worn away, was a mark cut into the stone.\par

A threshold line, broken twice, with a small inward notch.\par

Shield.\par

My mouth went dry.\par

The outcrop was Shield. A point beyond the permitted prayer ground, aligned with Mountain light, invisible to ordinary eyes, reachable only by a dangerous route.\par

I felt no triumph. The things I knew slid into new places.\par

Light had led me sideways, into another Kahir's secret.\par

I should leave. I stayed still and let the thought finish itself.\par

I should return, report to Ka-Raedin that a Shield mark and an exterior glint had been found by Light observation, and then allow Keeper-to-Keeper sequence. That was the proper structure. It was also a way to ensure Shield sealed the site before I knew what I had seen.\par

A second thought followed: if I entered, I might commit breach. A third: if I withdrew, Light would surrender what had been revealed.\par

I waited until the three thoughts stopped competing and became a single condition.\par

I chose to go in and know, whatever it would cost.\par

The mechanism resisted silently, then gave with a thickness that spoke of long maintenance. Air breathed out from within. Dry. Old. Mineral.\par

Full.\par

I slipped inside and pulled the stone nearly closed behind me, leaving a finger's width for air and for return.\par

The passage sloped downward. It was narrow at first, forcing me sideways, then widened into a cut corridor whose walls bore neither Light markings nor Faith text. Dust lay thin over the floor, thin enough to show where feet had disturbed it, though few had passed lately. I held my lamp low and moved slowly.\par

After twenty paces the corridor divided. One branch descended deeper into darkness. The other angled inward toward the Mountain.\par

I chose neither immediately.\par

No entry mark, no return mark, no prayer, no measure, no instruction. Only stone and the smell of preserved air.\par

\textit{Quarantine,} I thought.\par

Then another word came, less precise and more terrible.\par

\textit{Tomb.}\par

I took the descending branch.\par

It ended in a chamber whose first impression was emptiness. Then my lamp found the nearest shelf.\par

Objects.\par

At first only a few: stones, broken blades, small bowls, fragments of painted surface, a corroded shape that might once have been a tool or an idol. Then the flame widened and more shelves appeared, rising along the walls, tier after tier, receding into darkness. A second chamber opened beyond the first, then a third, each cut lower and lined with recesses. No bodies lay there. No bones arranged for burial. Yet death was in it all the same.\par

Civilisations had entered here as fragments.\par

They had stayed.\par

I lifted the lamp to the nearest shelf. Every object was tagged with containment marks. Shield sign at the top. Justice witness line beneath. Balance preservation notch. Sometimes a Faith refusal mark. Sometimes a Light observation point. Each tag stated what the object was believed to be, how it had entered, who had handled it, and whether it could be touched, decay, or carry doctrinal danger.\par

Archive, then. Quarantine and memory together.\par

The first object I truly saw was a small bronze figure of a man holding a weapon shaped like three prongs. The body had gone green with age, the beard set in formal curls. Around the base, faint wave marks had been scratched or cast. The Shield tag gave no name I knew. Faith had marked it: \textit{water-lord corruption.} Balance had added: \textit{metal stable; salts present; do not wash.}\par

A water god. A man-shaped water power standing proud with a weapon in his hand. Outsiders had taken the memory of deep water and made it into a lord of appetite --- the desire to command water without counting it.\par

Next came a dark figure with the head of an animal on the body of a man. Long snout, upright ears, narrow limbs. Black surface, gold traces, eyes shaped with an unsettling calm. Beneath it lay a cloth fragment marked with faded symbols and a small carving of a wrapped human shape. The tag bore a Faith warning and a Justice witness mark older than the handwriting around it.\par

Death.\par

Outsiders had given death posture, office, beauty. Faith taught that the dead returned under God's keeping. This figure looked as if death itself had been made a Keeper.\par

I looked away first, then forced myself to look back.\par

The next chamber changed material. Painted stone. Carved panels. A fragment of image preserved behind cloudy glass. I held the lamp high and saw a man with a blade raised over a bound youth. The youth lay on something close to an altar. Above the man's hand, a sign of Heaven: lines descending from cloud or fire. The father's face had been damaged, but the blade remained clear.\par

For several breaths the horror escaped me.\par

Then the Book of Blood did it for me. Brother against brother. Father against son. Hunger made law. A broken piece of Gospel called whole.\par

Outsiders had carved a holy story in which a father bound his own son and prepared to kill him while the animal stood spared beside them. They had made obedience into a willingness to murder the child before the goat --- authority coming down on someone helpless, a blade raised by nothing but the right of being the father.\par

The tag below it was more complicated. Shield danger. Faith contamination. Justice: \textit{retained for comparative doctrine.} Light: \textit{image damaged; blade line clear.}\par

Comparative doctrine. A phrase too calm for what it held.\par

The next image was worse, because the people in it looked tender, and what they were gathered around was cruel. A small metal cross, then a larger one of wood darkened by age. Then a painted fragment of people kneeling before a tortured man fixed upon crossed beams. His head drooped. His ribs showed. Wounds marked hands, feet, side. Around him the worshippers lifted their faces in reverence. Some wept. Some reached toward him.\par

I stared.\par

The Mountain had martyrdom --- I granted that now. I had seen Ka-Syphiron die. But this was different, or seemed so. Ka-Syphiron had cut himself when the link was complete. This man had been fixed, displayed, adored in his suffering. The Outsiders, I thought, had turned pain itself into an altar and then knelt to it.\par

I slowed myself. \textit{Veil, not boy. Interpret after seeing.}\par

I looked again. The worshippers' faces were grieving. Something lived in the tortured man's face that the artifact failed to carry across time, because I lacked the language for it. To the Outsiders, holiness seemed to go in through torture and come out wearing gold.\par

Beyond it, on a lower shelf, stood a figure of a woman unlike any gravure in the Mountain. Many arms spread from her body. Many breasts, or ornaments like breasts, covered her chest. Her face was serene and excessive at once. Around her were small animal shapes, fruits, weapons, and open hands.\par

Fertility, perhaps. Abundance. Or greed dressed up as a goddess.\par

I thought of the Mountain's half measure, its injury allowance, its waste counted, its honey given in amounts so small that sweetness became an event --- and I looked back at the many-breasted figure and saw Outsider appetite given a mother's shape.\par

I moved through the older sections slowly, forcing each object into observation before doctrine could seize it.\par

A false water lord. A death keeper. A father's blade over a son. Worship before torture. Abundance without count.\par

Each artifact might have meant something else to the people who made it. But whatever reached me came damaged.\par

The later sections grew stranger. Coins with faces in profile, some crowned with leaves, some with hard noses and a godlike arrogance. Metal helmets eaten thin by rust. A broken eagle standard. Fragments of cloth with crescent shapes and angular letters. Curved blades wrapped in oilskin. Small glass bottles. A prayer charm. A piece of parchment with a map drawn in brown ink, the Mountain unnamed but hinted by lines and old riverbeds.\par

Each shelf had its chain: object, source, risk, witness, preservation. \textit{Object recovered beyond Threshold. Object acquired by exchange. Object removed from a dead caravan. Object seized after breach attempt. Object received under exception. Object retained by Shield only. Object forbidden to Faith novices. Object harmless unless taught.}\par

Harmless unless taught.\par

A brass-and-glass instrument lay in a fitted case, its purpose unclear, though its lenses told me it belonged to sight or measurement. Beneath it, a Shield translation had copied one outsider word from the original label: \textit{French.} Whether it named a people, a place, or a dead authority, I had no way to tell. Nearby were coins marked under another translated name: \textit{Algerian.} Later, sharper coins, stamped with faces and signs carried into the archive only as sound and category. Cloth badges. Shell casings. Surveying rods. A cracked compass whose needle trembled uselessly when I moved the lamp close.\par

Time became material. Stone became bronze, bronze became iron, iron became glass, glass became paper, paper became photograph.\par

The chamber widened at the modern section. Here the shelves became cabinets, some locked, some open. The air was drier, kept by hidden vents. The labels were newer and more dangerous --- Shield classification, Justice witness number, Balance preservation instruction, Light observation angle, Faith doctrinal restriction. The handwriting varied. The system held.\par

That was where I felt the danger.\par

Old corrupted gods could be explained --- Shield guarded the threshold, Light was meant to know how error looked. I could accept that, uneasily.\par

The first showed a woman under hard light. Her hair was tied back. Another place, near the Mountain's outer folds.\par

The lower white border carried marks in an outsider hand. Beneath them, in careful Shield script, someone had copied two names.\par

Sarah Blackwood.\par

Amastan.\par

A woman. Recent enough that the colours still held. Recent enough that the Mountain's current elders might have been breathing when she was.\par

Sarah Blackwood. I had never heard the name, and finding it here, in a Shield drawer, was worse than if I had.\par

The second photograph showed no face. Only desert, distance, and a terrible flower of light rising from the horizon, too large to be flame and too ordered to be storm. The sky around it had been bleached almost empty. On the lower border, in the same unfamiliar hand, were marks I had never learned to read. One sequence had been copied beneath in Shield script:\par

Reggane, 1960.\par

The word and the numbers meant nothing to me. A place, perhaps. A date, perhaps. A wound recorded by Outsiders because even they had been frightened by what their own hands had made.\par

What that flower was lay beyond me. Others would --- boys in countries of clean mountains who saw it carried in their newspapers, who had a word for it, and a number, and later a treaty. I had none of those things. All I knew was that it was too large to leave.\par

A third photograph lay partly hidden behind the second. It showed equipment on metal legs, men standing at a distance, a line of wire crossing pale ground, and the same awful emptiness of desert after violence. The Shield label refused doctrine. It read only:\par

\textit{Outer fire. Witnessed beyond range. Do not expose to untrained Faith.}\par

Do not expose to untrained Faith.\par

I set the photographs down as if they might accuse me of touching them.\par

The cabinet below contained maps. Several layers, some drawn in outsider lines, some annotated in Shield marks. Mountain folds. Dry washes. Old approach paths. Observation points. The Oasis hollow indicated by absence --- a blank space around which the routes bent. Glint points. Hidden sightlines. And a route leading from the outcrop inward, then splitting toward the Labyrinth and, impossibly, toward service routes near the Hall of Ceremony.\par

My breath slowed.\par

I unfolded another map. This one bore names in scripts beyond my reading, but three had been copied in Mountain hand.\par

Amastan. Sarah. Ka-Xhian.\par

Ka. A Keeper.\par

Shield or Justice had recorded the contact with a title.\par

Lines connected the names to dates, routes, and small boxed terms. \textit{Breach. Containment. Exchange. Loss. Sealed. Limited disclosure. Threshold Exception.}\par

Exchange.\par

I read the word three times.\par

Inside the next drawer were objects wrapped in cloth and tagged. A small metal cylinder. A folded paper covered in dense outsider writing. A tool with glass at one end and knobs along the side. A strip of black material that reflected my lamp with a dead sheen. A dried flower pressed between two sheets. Several pages had been cut out, copied, translated, and marked by Justice as incomplete.\par

One of the cut pages had kept its Shield tag whole, and on it, copied in the careful hand the archive used for sounds it could not weigh, stood two words in outsider letters: \textit{Noxius Zero.} I read them as I had read \textit{French} and \textit{Algerian} --- noise the outside had made and Shield had thought worth keeping --- and could no more say what they named than I could say it of the coins. I set the page back and went on.\par

At the back of the drawer lay a Shield record tablet.\par

I lifted it with both hands. The script was compressed, official, closed to public reading. It preserved a sequence, and that was more dangerous.\par

\textit{Contact maintained under Threshold Exception.}\par

\textit{Outsider asset contained beyond full breach.}\par

\textit{Amastan intermediary confirmed.}\par

\textit{Sarah Blackwood retained under observation.}\par

\textit{Ka-Xhian authorised limited disclosure.}\par

\textit{Horizon note sealed.}\par

Horizon.\par

The word meant nothing in any lesson I had been given. Yet it had the feel of a word kept empty on purpose, one you were not given until you had already crossed some line. Horizon: the place where Heaven touched Earth but could never be reached.\par

I set the tablet down carefully, because my hands had begun to shake.\par

I arranged the facts.\par

The chamber existed, hidden by Shield beyond permitted routes, and it ran past old proof into recent contact. Shield had preserved records of Sarah Blackwood and Amastan. A Keeper, Ka-Xhian, had authorised limited disclosure under something called Threshold Exception. Maps showed routes and stations and knowledge beyond anything the hidden people were taught. Justice had witnessed enough that the records bore sequence, not rumour. And none of it had ever been spoken in Genesis, Blood, Beast, Cataclysm, Covenant, ceremony, lesson, or public record.\par

I forced myself to slow further. What did this prove?\par

Less than treason, perhaps. It proved divided knowledge --- but divided knowledge was law. It proved an exception --- but an exception might be needed in the rare place where following the ordinary law would wreck the very thing the law was there to guard.\par

Then I looked again at the older artifacts.\par

They had prepared my mind, a lens I had been trained to correct for. Yet even correcting for doctrine left the unbearable central fact standing.\par

Shield had stood before the people as the threshold against Outsiders while keeping channels to Outsiders. Shield had watched children kneel before Blood and Beast while holding photographs of the children of the murderer in secret drawers.\par

And Va-Sheva belonged to Shield.\par

This lay beyond Va-Sheva. She was Veil, not Keeper --- young for the office she held. She might know nothing of this chamber. Or she might know everything, and have looked at me in the Oasis while standing inside a lie so large it had become her natural air.\par

I closed the drawer. The sound was soft, and even so it seemed too final.\par

Beyond the modern cabinets, a narrow inner passage led back toward the Mountain. I followed it with the lamp held low. After thirty paces it reached a sealed door marked with three signs.\par

Shield. Justice. Light.\par

Light.\par

I stood before it for a long time. The sign was older than Ka-Raedin's tenure, perhaps older than Ka-Syphiron's. Some earlier Light office had known of this route, or been bound to silence by it. The Justice mark meant sequence had entered; the Shield mark, threshold danger; the Light mark, that what lay behind the door had been seen, or needed someone trained to know how sight itself could be corrupted.\par

I left the door sealed.\par

The old Sa-Kailan would have pressed, searched, scratched, risked the alarms, and told himself that holding back was cowardice and forcing the door was his duty. The proof I already held was more dangerous than anything I could force before dawn.\par

I had spent enough for one night.\par

I returned to the modern cabinet, took nothing at first, then reconsidered. If I stole evidence without going through sequence, I would be committing breach. I needed something small enough to conceal, strong enough to anchor memory, and slight enough that its absence would pass.\par

The photograph was too obvious, the tablet too dangerous. A tagged object would change the archive's count.\par

I chose a loose copy fragment from the back of the map bundle --- a scrap bearing four entries in Mountain hand:\par

Sarah Blackwood. Amastan. Ka-Xhian. Threshold Exception.\par

I folded it once and placed it inside the inner seam of my robe. Then I stood in the Tomb --- or the Chamber of Thresholds, or whatever Shield named this place when it dared name it at all --- and let the full weight of it enter me.\par

It was the name that undid me. Chamber of Thresholds. I had crossed one to stand in it, and I understood at last what the whole Mountain was, above in the light and down here in the dark alike: an engine for the governing of crossings, and this room its hidden heart. My people had let all of it in --- the water-lord, the death-keeper, the father with the blade, the adored and tortured man --- and had kept each one by forbidding it to mean anything. The statue was admitted; its god was held at the door. Take in the body, and quarantine the meaning. Let a beam pass a wall, so long as no man passed with it. Let a boy walk down into the Oasis, if the water could wash the desert off him at the gate.\par

And kneeling there I saw why the art must fail, and had perhaps already failed in me. A threshold runs one way. A man may cross it; he cannot cross back into the one who had not. The dust washes off. The flash does not. They had been right to fear every object on these shelves, because the one thing no gate holds at the door is what has already gone in through an eye. I had come to learn what this room kept, and in the learning I had become the thing it was built to hold out --- a place inside the Mountain where the outside had entered and could never again be made to mean nothing. The scrap folded in my seam was a courtesy to my own memory. I was already the breach.\par

I had found part of a truth. Whoever had built this place kept the rest of it back on purpose, some of it for good reason and some of it for a monstrous one. But the part I had was solid enough to bruise my hand.\par

The Mountain's official story was false in a way children could never imagine. The Outsiders had remembered broken pieces. Blood had drawn its warnings from something real.\par

If the doctrine had simply been false, I could have thrown it out. But it was true, and it was being used to cover an exception, and there was no clean place to stand in that.\par

I lifted the lamp and looked once more at Sarah Blackwood's face. The Outsider woman stood under desert light, half squinting. She looked ordinary. I had been taught that the dangerous ones rarely looked dangerous at first.\par

I lowered the lamp.\par

I would hold my rage. I would keep away from Va-Sheva, the one my anger wanted first. I would keep the evidence in my hands. I would not spill it in front of Ka-Raedin like a child demanding the Mountain rearrange itself around his outrage. I would think. Sequence mattered. Witness mattered. A word in the wrong place could let them call it all a breach before the truth in it had a chance to hold.\par

Va-Sheva's refusal to answer had reached past rank. Shield's silence had a structure behind it, and now I had stood inside its walls.\par

I left as carefully as I had entered.\par

The return through the passage felt longer. The sky had paled at its lowest edge --- a warning before sunrise. I moved faster than I should have and slower than fear wanted. The marks I had made on the stones brought me back across the washes. Twice I stopped to restore disturbed dust with the side of my sandal. Nothing came. Or something came and stayed hidden.\par

When I reached the hidden vent above the old air chamber, my hands were numb, my mouth dry, and the water skin nearly empty. The Mountain received me without welcome. Stone closed behind me. Warmth returned gradually --- first as smell, then as pressure, then as accusation.\par

I replaced the grille, checked the seams, brushed grit from the lower ledge, and stood in the darkness until my breathing no longer sounded like a confession.\par

Inside my robe, the scrap touched my skin.\par

Sarah Blackwood. Amastan. Ka-Xhian. Threshold Exception.\par

I left it folded. Every word of it was already in me.\par

By the First hour I stood in the eastern chamber before Ka-Raedin arrived --- washed, robed, composed, no dust on my sandals and no sign that the world beneath the Mountain had opened sideways in the night. When the Keeper entered, his eyes moved once over my face, then down to my hands, then to the floor around my feet.\par

``You slept poorly,'' he said.\par

``Yes, Keeper.''\par

``Why?''\par

I looked at him. There had been a time when the question would have frightened me into cleverness. Now I let the silence hold.\par

``Because the title is heavier in the dark,'' I said.\par

He studied me.\par

``Good.''\par

He turned toward the basins.\par

``Then we will begin with weight.''\par

I followed.\par

I had found something badly wrong, and I had not yet earned the standing to say so aloud.\par

\textit{Horizon.} It stayed where the chamber had placed it: far, unreachable, trembling at the edge of sight.\par
