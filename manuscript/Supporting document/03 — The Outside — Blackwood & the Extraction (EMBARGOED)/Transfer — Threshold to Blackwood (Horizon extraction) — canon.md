# Transfer — The Threshold to the Blackwood Estate (Horizon extraction) — Canon

*Purpose: fix the route, timing, cover, and condition of Kailan's journey out of the Mountain to the Blackwood Estate, so any chapter that touches the extraction (the 2026 interview frame, the prologue, Ch13's airfield glimpse) stops drifting. This is a side-side-side plot. On the page we leave **breadcrumbs, not explanations**; this doc holds the full truth so the breadcrumbs stay consistent and a curious reader can reconstruct the how — but the manuscript never over-explains it. What matters dramatically is only that K arrives safely at Blackwood, days out, composed over authority and raw beneath.*

---

## 1. The governing principle — breadcrumbs, not exposition

The transfer is scaffolding, not story. The reader should be able to *reconstruct* it if they care (Jamaica → alumina → the 1980s → a Blackwood/Rich-type trader), and should never be *told* it. We reveal just enough to be searched and found; we do not narrate the logistics. If a passage starts explaining the route, cut it.

---

## 2. The parties

- **Horizon** — the outside operation that receives K at the threshold and moves him out. (Fuller definition lives in the "Paul Fully Horizon" material; here it is simply the field capability.) Paul is Horizon: a sincere recruit, the doubting eye.
- **The Blackwood Foundation** — funds and owns the operation; the estate is its ground; the debt in §5 is its leverage.
- **Jamaica** — provides the diplomatic flight. Motive is an old debt of national survival (§5), not friendship.

---

## 3. The route (truth — almost entirely off-page)

- **Origin: the Mountain's threshold** — the *south* door/gate (per the Geography lock: gates open south, the Genesis gravure at the door-face). He leaves the only world he knows through the same threshold the equinox light enters. Location: central Sahara, ≈ 21.3° N, ≈ 0° E, the Tanezrouft; Reggane ~4–5 weeks' walk due north, the Richat due west.
- **Leg 1 — overland, by night, sedated.** Horizon moves him from the threshold across the desert to a remote airstrip. He is kept under (§4) so he cannot retrace the way: the Mountain's location is the crown secret, and not even his own memory may carry it out cleanly. He sleeps through it.
- **Leg 2 — light aircraft**, desert strip → a staging field out of the deep interior (unspecified; never named on the page). A short hop.
- **Leg 3 — the Jamaican diplomatic flight**, long-haul into **Geneva (GVA/LSGG)**. Diplomatic status = no customs, no manifest anyone can pull, an unconscious passenger across borders unremarked; and Geneva's constant diplomatic traffic makes a Jamaican state flight completely unremarkable.
- **Leg 4 — helicopter**, Geneva → the **Blackwood Estate** helipad, a side-valley of the Saanenland above Gstaad. **Locked final leg.** It is *safer and cleaner* than the road: no police stop, no refuelling, the aircraft already pre-positioned at Geneva to meet the diplomatic flight, and — decisively — **the Blackwood clan flies it constantly**, so a chopper coming home over the Alps is the least remarkable thing in the valley. (Heavy/long-haul air legs always terminate at Geneva, never at the small local field — see the Estate & airfield canon.) The **helipad is visible from the interview room** — so in Ch13 Paul can simply tilt his head at the pad below the window: *that one.* The machine that carried K, sitting there the whole time.

---

## 4. Condition and sedation

- K is **sedated for the desert legs and much of the flight** — "a few days under the influence," sleeping through transport. Rationale, threefold: route secrecy (he must not map the way out), humane handling of a man leaving his entire world, and medical caution. He surfaces only intermittently; the crossing is a fog to him, which is why he can testify to everything about the Mountain and almost nothing about the journey.
- **On arrival: ~4 days of remise en forme** at the estate — medical clearance, rest, acclimation — until the doctors judge him "sufficiently rested" (the prologue's own words) and the interviews may begin.

---

## 5. The Jamaica favour (the debt — breadcrumb only)

- **Blackwood kept the lights on in Kingston in the 1980s when no one else would**: a Rich-type alumina-for-oil rescue — emergency oil delivered on trust the night the central bank was hours from collapse, repaid with a long alumina offtake. A debt of national survival.
- By 2026 it is a decades-old favour, but exactly the kind of debt a small state settles quietly — one diplomatic flight, no questions asked. **On the page: a single breadcrumb** (alumina; the eighties; "when no one else would"). Never the ledger.
- **Real-world source of truth:** the Marc Rich / Jamaica alumina–oil story as told in *The World for Sale* (Blas & Farchy). Blackwood stands in that archetype's shoes. (Historical colour, kept off-page: the same trader famously bankrolled the 1988 Jamaican bobsled team — *Cool Runnings / Rasta Rocket.* Useful to know; never to say.)

## 6. Timeline lock

- **T0** — K leaves the Mountain at the threshold, handed to Horizon.
- **T0 → T+ ~4–8 days** — transfer (mostly sedated) + remise en forme at the estate.
- **2026-11-17, 09:00** — the first interview begins (prologue dateline). So the extraction falls in **the second week of November 2026**.
- Cadence thereafter: **mornings = interviews** (the account set down); **afternoons = luncheon + instruction "with another."**

## 7. Where this surfaces on the page (and how lightly)

- **Prologue:** the self-writing tablet, "sufficiently rested," Paul's five years learning the tongue, the doubting eye. No route.
- **Ch13:** two frame beats, one motion, both sprung by the sight of an aircraft.
  - First, K sees an aeroplane on a landing strip and connects it to the diagram of *"a bird from somebody who had heard disturbing rumours"* — the outside world's aircraft rhyming, from inside the story, with a Mountain artefact.
  - That sight then prompts the question **"so how did I arrive here?"**, and Paul answers *vaguely, and deliberately so*: instruction has not begun, so the full answer cannot land yet — but in short, friends on a diplomatic visit to Algeria lent a plane into Geneva, and then **the helicopter on the pad below the window** — the Blackwoods' own, flown constantly, unremarkable — brought him up over the Alps; good friends who ask no questions because they owe us, from years ago — Kingston. He tells K to **write it down** — *it's an island; the answer will come.*
  - Two things this beat carries: a straight **payoff of the prologue's deferred-question mechanic** (write it down, answered in its turn), and a quiet irony — **Horizon partitions knowledge to K exactly as the Mountain partitioned it to its candidates** (a breadcrumb now, understanding later), which K, of all people, would recognize. Breadcrumbs, never the ledger.
- **Anywhere else** the flight or estate is touched: breadcrumbs only.

## 10. The shadow on Horizon (the double game)

- The same breadcrumbs that let a reader **place Blackwood exactly** (Jamaica → alumina → the 1980s → a Rich-type trader) also invite a darker reading. Look at the real Marc Rich archetype — sanctions run around, states kept solvent on a Friday-night phone call, kingmakers who answer to no flag — and these are precisely the men who might fancy themselves **gods upon the earth.** So Horizon/Blackwood is **not a clean rescuer**: it plays a double game, and K may have been moved from one temple of would-be gods to another.
- **Paul does not know it.** He is sincere; the doubting eye is real. But by the end of Book 1 (the Ch13 conversation) the reader has been handed enough to place Blackwood *and* to recognize the archetype — so the **suspicion forms behind Paul's innocence**, and the reader gets ahead of the narrator. That is the intended dread: the crumbs that locate Blackwood are the crumbs that make us uneasy about it.
- **Handling:** never stated. It rides entirely on the same breadcrumbs (Rich, alumina, Kingston, the trader's god-complex). The suspicion is the reader's to form; Paul's sincerity is the screen it forms behind. (Cross-ref the "Paul Fully Horizon" material for exactly what Paul does and does not know.)
