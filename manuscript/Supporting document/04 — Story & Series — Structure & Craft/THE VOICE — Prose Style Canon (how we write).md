# THE VOICE — Prose Style Canon (how we write)

*The standing law of the prose. Read this before drafting or editing any chapter. Its purpose is that we never re-derive the voice again: we draft **in** it, not loose-then-purge. Everything below was earned across the manuscript's passes and is now identity, not preference.*

---

## 0. The credo (read first)

**This book is told by a man giving an account. He states what happened and what a thing was, in cool, exact, physical language — and he stops. He does not tell you what it meant, what he learned, when it first happened, or what it "really" was. The meaning is the reader's to make.**

All the interpreting — the philosophy, the epigram, the wisdom, the "lesson" — lives in the **mouths of the characters** (the interview room, in-account speech) and in **scripture**. It never lives in the narration. The narrator's intelligence shows in *what he chooses to put in the frame and what he leaves in the dark*, not in his commentary on it.

If you remember one sentence: **In narration, show the thing and get out of the way.**

---

## 1. The three zones (the operational backbone)

Every rule below turns on *which zone a sentence is in.* Get this right first, every time.

| Zone | What it is | Governed by the bans? |
|---|---|---|
| **NARRATION** | All unquoted narrative prose, whoever currently owns the telling: K's past-tense account, Paul's present-tense interviewer frame (*"He stops to drink…", "I say…"*), Sarah's recorded prose testimony, and any later ordering hand. | **YES — fully.** |
| **DIALOGUE** | Anything inside `\begin{room}…\end{room}` turn exchanges; **and** in-account quoted speech in `` … '' spoken by a character within the account. | **NO — free.** |
| **SCRIPTURE / FORMAL** | `\begin{scripture}`, `\begin{declaration}`, and italic attributed sayings/epigraphs (`\textit{…}` credited to a character). | **NO — free / formal register.** |

**The disambiguation rule:** if it is the current teller's own unquoted voice describing, reflecting, or explaining — Kailan, Paul, Sarah, or another ordering hand; past or present tense — it is **narration** and it is governed. A character telling a long account does not turn prose paragraphs into dialogue merely because a recorder is running. Only words physically enclosed as speech, room turns, or formal/scriptural environments enter the free zones.

**Why the split exists:** characters are *allowed* to be aphoristic, antithetical, interpretive, prophetic, philosophical — that is *them*, and it's often the best writing in the book (Va-Raedin's maxims, Ka-Syphiron's jokes, the Covenant's cadence, Paul granting a point). The ban is on the **narrator doing it in his own voice.**

## 2. THE BANS (narration only)

### 2A. The narrator-interpretation purge — PRIMARY LAW

The narrator must never step out of the event to gloss it. Purge all six, in **any** wording (they are one habit in six costumes):

1. **Antithesis / "not X, but Y"** — in every dress: `rather than`, `less…than`, `instead of`, `sooner than`, `only`, `the least of`, `X; no, Y`, `expected X — instead Y`.
   - ✗ *"assigned rather than left to gather where it pleased"* → ✓ *"kept in its allotted places."*
   - ✗ *"That did not make me happy. It made me careful."* → ✓ *"It made me careful."*

2. **"Appears X, is really Y" / the unmasking reveal** — `seemed X, was really Y`; `looked like X, it was Y`; `I had taken it for X, it was Y`.
   - ✗ *"I had thought I was standing in a garden. All along I had been standing inside a defensive line."* → ✓ *[show it, or state the fact once, no appears/really frame].*

3. **Significance / "first time" markers** — `that was the first time…`, `for the first time…`, `the first thing I ever…`.
   - ✗ *"It was the first thing I had ever been left free to choose."* → ✓ *[deleted]*. (Literal/temporal uses in dialogue, or *"she looked at me for the first time"* as a plain action, are fine.)

4. **Epiphany / retrospective-comprehension markers** — `I understood then…`, `I knew then…`, `I saw then…`, `I realised…`, `I would learn…`, `I did not yet know…`, `that was when I…`.
   - ✗ *"Standing there, I understood why the children were told the Oasis was hidden."* → ✓ *"The Oasis was hidden from every eye."*

5. **The show-then-gloss DOUBLE-BEAT** *(the deepest; ungreppable — you must READ for it)* — a concrete sentence followed by a second sentence that states its meaning or significance.
   - ✗ *"Then it had gone on living. That too was instruction."* → ✓ *"Then it had gone on living."*
   - **Rule:** whenever a sentence exists only to tell the reader what the previous sentence *meant*, delete it.

6. **"The lesson was…" / theme-labeling / grand definitional pronouncement** — `the lesson was…`, `That was the shape of…`, `It was X: the Y that Z`, `That was the thing that…`, `That is this chapter.`
   - ✗ *"Each office would keep its own version… and that disagreement was the Mountain itself."* → ✓ *"Each office kept its own account, and no two agreed."*

**Treatment, in order of preference:** (1) **delete** — most of these add no fact, only a gloss, and the prose strengthens when cut; (2) if a marker is welded to a real event, keep the event and strip the frame; (3) never swap one tic for another, never add a new gloss.

### 2B. The aphorism ban ("carved above a door")

No narration sentence may arrive as its own final inscription. Beyond §2A this also forbids: the **bare maxim** (*"A machine against change."*), **chiasmus** (*"I kept things, she let them go."*), the **balanced-pair-with-a-twist**, and the **tricolon that resolves into a maxim** (*"refusal to waste, refusal to romanticise, refusal to…"*).

### 2C. Prolepsis / flash-forward — near-zero

The account is told in sequence. Do not lean forward (*"I would come to learn…," "that would change," "little did I know"*). If a genuine flash-forward is precious, it is a rare exception and must be argued, not defaulted to.

### 2D. The "not" rule — reconciled

The literal word **"not" is permitted** in narration in its plain, non-antithetical uses (*"the door did not open," "I felt no triumph," "none of them consumed"*). What is banned is the **antithesis** (§2A.1), in every costume. **Never dodge "not" by manufacturing a `rather than` / `less…than` / `only` construction — a plain "not" beats an aphoristic dodge.** *(This supersedes the earlier absolute no-"not" rule, which was a blunt proxy for this same goal. If the author wants zero "not" again, say so and it reinstates.)*

## 3. THE KEEPS — do NOT over-strip

Cool is not dead. These are craft, and they are explicitly **out of scope** of the bans:

- **Abstraction made physical.** ✓ *"Light had made the Hall into a body and given one side of it fever."* This is the good writing. Keep it.
- **A whole thing compressed into one image.** ✓ *"It was the Mountain and its many floors, laid on a wall in points of light."* Keep.
- **Concrete similes.** ✓ *"entered me like a mechanism engaging"; "worn smooth as a river stone."*
- **Plain emotion beats.** ✓ *"That frightened me." "It was heartbreaking."* These report feeling, not a stated intellectual meaning. Keep.
- **Genuine in-scene reasoning / tactical thought** that resolves nothing and states no theme (K arranging the sequence; Paul weighing whether a claim will hold). Keep — that is interiority, not gloss.

The failure mode on the other side is **flattening into robotic subject-verb-object monotony.** Plain ≠ choppy. Vary sentence length; let some sentences run and breathe; earn vividness through concrete specifics.

---

## 4. Positive craft — the dos

- **Concrete, sensory, physical, specific.** Name the thing. Vividness comes from the exact detail, never from commentary.
- **Science is destiny.** Physical phenomena carry the drama — the mechanism IS the ordeal (mirror → shadow → focus → refraction → total internal reflection → diffraction). Never stop the story to *explain* an idea; let the explanation *be* the ordeal.
- **Physics must be correct.** This book's trust rests on it. Diffraction needs the red-glass monochromator; total internal reflection is digital; the pendulum is 2.75 days; the mass-energy ledger closes. **Do not contradict the World & Engines canon** (Geography lock, Mass-Energy ledger, Pendulum, Veil's Chamber optics).
- **Rhythm.** Alternate long and short. Use deliberate fragments for pressure ("*No. Stone.*"), lists for gathering, but never let the page go airless (aphorism-dense) or robotic (SVO-choppy).
- **The narrator's intelligence** is in the *selection* — what enters the frame, what is left in the dark, which two facts are set beside each other. Trust the reader to connect them.

---

## 5. The living Mountain — world-texture craft

The world is present everywhere and lectured nowhere. When seeding survival/world-life texture:

- **Everywhere and nowhere at once.** Each survival dimension (water, salt, grain, fuel, air, waste, light, fish, mushroom, bees…) appears **once**, in the chapter where it has a natural hook. The sum is the whole; no two intersect.
- **Glimpse, never diagram.** Show a worker doing a thing and move on. Do not explain the loop; let one clause imply it. *"crickets, fed on the grain-husk the cooks swept down to them."*
- **Hero-out.** K (or Paul) *notices in passing* and is not the subject. Plotless, no-lesson, relief beats.
- **Metabolism, not list.** Prefer transformations that connect (fire → heat → cook → soot → ink; water → salt → preserve; sun → grow → oxygen). Connected texture reads as one organism; a list reads as inventory.
- **Density discipline.** The failure mode is *"every paragraph a survival mechanism."* When a chapter clogs, cut where the **least unique dimension** is lost — trim the duplicate, keep one vivid instance of each. Let the story breathe between the systems.
- **No mysticism.** Concrete and mechanical. "Explaining without explaining": say just enough that a skeptical reader stops doubting the place, in the plainest terms, then stop.

## 6. The room (dialogue) mechanics

Inside `\begin{room}…\end{room}` (the 2026 interview frame, Paul Morgan ↔ K):

- **Turn marker:** each speech begins `--- ` (space after). No speaker attributions, no quotation marks around turns.
- **Paul uses NO contractions.** Ever. ("do not," "I am," "you are.")
- **Paul never speaks K's name.** K may name Paul freely.
- **No mid-sentence em-dash inside a room turn.** Use colons and semicolons. (Em-dashes are fine in *narration*.)
- **K's pauses:** `\ldots{}`.
- **In-room beat / silent action:** `\centerline{[\,---\,]}`.
- **In-account quoted speech** (a character speaking inside K's narrated past): `` … '' (double-backtick / double-quote).
- **Language cues** where used: `\textit{In English}` / `\textit{In Noxius}`.
- Dialogue is a FREE zone — characters may be aphoristic, antithetical, philosophical. That is where the book's wisdom is allowed to live.

## 7. LaTeX / file mechanics

- One `.tex` per chapter, `\typesetchapter{Chapter N}{Title}` at the top.
- Environments: `room`, `scripture`, `declaration`. Preserve them exactly; never let a ban touch inside them.
- Paragraph breaks are `\par`; scene breaks are `\ornament`. Preserve both; never leave doubled blank lines.
- **Never edit manuscript `.tex` files through the shell** — use the file tools (Read/Edit/Write). Shell reads can be stale; shell writes can clobber.

## 8. Why the bad habits recur (the diagnostic — internalise this)

We fixed the same problem three times because we kept treating a **symptom** as the disease:

- Banning the **word** "not" did nothing — the antithesis simply moved into `rather than`, `less…than`, `only`.
- Banning the **aphorism** (the ornate closed line) did nothing to `I understood then…` or the show-then-gloss double-beat — those are the *same interpretive reflex* in plainer clothes.

**The disease is a narrator who narrates by interpreting** — who cannot let an image land without telling you the lesson. It is a matter of **stance**, not vocabulary. When drafting or reviewing, hunt the *stance*, not the phrase: at every sentence ask, **"Is the narrator stating the thing, or telling me what it means?"** If the latter, cut to the thing.

You cannot grep a stance. The double-beat and the appears/really reveal have no keyword; they are caught only by reading every sentence with that question in hand.

## 9. Drafting checklist (use while writing new prose, and as a self-review pass)

Per sentence, in narration:

1. Is this a **statement of event/image**, or a **gloss on meaning**? → if gloss, cut to the event.
2. Any **"first time / I understood / the lesson was / that was the…"**? → cut.
3. A concrete sentence followed by an **explaining sentence**? → delete the explainer.
4. Any **antithesis** (`not X but Y`, `rather than`, `less…than`, `only`, `instead`, `sooner than`)? → make it plain.
5. Any **appears-X-really-Y**? → state Y, or show the seeing.
6. Any **bare maxim / chiasmus / carved line**? → dissolve into plain statement.
7. Is it **prolepsis**? → return to sequence.
8. Did I flatten into **choppy SVO**? → vary the rhythm; let one sentence breathe.
9. World-texture: **glimpsed, hero-out, one dimension once, connected** — or clogging?
10. Physics/number: does it **agree with canon**?

If it survives all ten, it belongs to the voice.

---

## 10. One-paragraph summary (pin this)

Cool, exact, physical first-person. State and stop. The narrator shows; the characters interpret. No epigrams, no "I understood then," no show-then-gloss, no antithesis, no prolepsis — in narration. Keep abstraction-made-physical, keep emotion beats, keep vivid specifics; do not flatten into robotic. The world lives in glimpses, one dimension at a time, connected as a metabolism, never lectured. Physics is correct and canon is law. Dialogue and scripture are free zones — that is where wisdom is allowed to speak.
