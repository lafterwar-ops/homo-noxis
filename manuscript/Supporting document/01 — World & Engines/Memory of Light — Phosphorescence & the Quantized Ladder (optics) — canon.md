## The two experiments — do not conflate

### Experiment A — the MEMORY of light (phosphorescence). *No prism. No bars.*
- A phosphor (glow-in-the-dark board: zinc sulfide, or strontium aluminate) absorbs light of a shorter wavelength and **re-emits it at a longer wavelength** (a Stokes shift), and — unlike fluorescence — **delayed**, continuing to glow *after the source is removed*. That delayed give-back is the "memory."
- **Persistent phosphorescence** (the long glow): a high-energy photon frees an electron; the electron is **trapped in a lattice defect** (a vacancy), held there, and only released later by a random **spike of thermal energy**, after which it falls back and emits a photon. Trap depth ≈ 0.6–0.7 eV for a good room-temperature glow. Timescale: seconds to hours.
- **What the eye sees:** a soft colored **glow** (green for common phosphors), fading slowly. It is effectively one broad emission colour — **NOT a set of bars**. No spectroscope is used or needed.
- **The shadow-print is real.** Wikipedia (*Phosphorescence*, "shadow wall"): *"A shadow wall is created when a light flashes upon a person or object in front of a phosphorescent screen which temporarily captures the shadow."* Documented museum exhibit (Exploratorium "Shadow Box"). Where the object blocked the charging light, that patch stores nothing and stays dark; the silhouette persists on the glow and lifts on its own as the charge spends. ✔ This is exactly the Ch8 hand-print beat.

### Experiment B — the BARS (emission line spectrum). *Needs a prism/grating AND an excited gas.*
- Wikipedia (*Emission spectrum*): *"An emission spectrum is formed when an excited gas is viewed directly through a spectroscope."* And: *"The spectrum appears in a series of lines called the line spectrum... The production of line spectra by the atoms of an element indicate that an atom can radiate only a certain amount of energy. This leads to the conclusion that bound electrons cannot have just any amount of energy but only a certain, specific amount of energy."*
- **This is the demonstration that makes quantization visible** — discrete bright bars separated by darkness, direct proof that the atom gives light only in fixed amounts.
- **Requires:** (1) an **excited gas / flame** source — a gas-discharge tube (hydrogen shows the Balmer bars: red, cyan, blue-violet; neon; sodium lamp), or the low-tech **flame test** (a pinch of salt in a flame: sodium → amber-yellow, strontium → red, copper → green); AND (2) a **prism or diffraction grating** to disperse the light.
- **A phosphor glow board will NOT show clean bars.** Do not stage bars off the board.
- **Reading the stars belongs HERE, not to the board.** Astronomical spectroscopy identifies a star's composition from its **line spectrum** (Fraunhofer absorption lines; Kirchhoff & Bunsen, 1859). It is Experiment B's principle, viewed through a prism/grating — never a property of the glow board.

**Underlying unity (say it, but keep it as the shared cause, not a shared picture):** in both, an electron sits only on discrete levels ("steps"), climbs by swallowing a photon of exactly the gap's worth, and on falling emits a photon of that same worth (colour set by the gap). A→ the fall is *delayed* (trapped/forbidden transition) so light is *stored*. B→ the many possible fixed gaps, dispersed by a prism, appear as *separate bars*.

## Period plausibility (all fine for the Swiss-house era)
- Flame tests: Melvill 1756; Bunsen & Kirchhoff 1859. Prism dispersion: Fraunhofer 1821; diffraction grating earlier still. Discharge/spectral lamps: 19th–20th c. Phosphors (ZnS) in safety products since the 1930s; strontium aluminate 1993 (use ZnS-era framing to be safe).

- **Very small:** the electron steps (both experiments).
- **Very large:** starlight often from fires long dead ("the sky is a room full of the past"), and a star's makeup is knowable only because its light carries the **same fixed lines** (Experiment B) — the infinitely small explaining the greatest.
