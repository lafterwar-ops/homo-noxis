# THE MOUNTAIN AS ORGANISM — World-Life canon (Book One)

**The one law:** *The Mountain was alive before Kailan. It will remain alive after him.*
Every addition reinforces that. Nothing added turns on the hero; everything leaks past him. By the end of Book One the reader should be quietly homesick for a place that never existed, and should believe the Mountain could survive without Kailan entirely.

---

## CRAFT RULES (obey on every addition — these ARE the point)

1. **Ambient, plot-irrelevant, discovered — never written.** These beats carry no lesson, no payoff, no "finished insight," no interpretive closer. If a reader could be quizzed on it later, cut it. Their job is to prove that *not every paragraph must be remembered.*
2. **Show consequence, hide mechanism.** Name a system chapters before (often instead of) explaining it. Let a profession exist for many pages before the reader learns what it does. Trust the reader; mystery is part of scale.
3. **Life independent of K.** The best beats intersect his story nowhere. He steps around them and goes on. *"They had no business with me, and I had none with them."*
4. **`not`-free narration** (book-wide rule): no "not"/"cannot" in narration; use no, never, nothing, without, positive verbs. Dialogue may use "not".
5. **Length:** two or three sentences glimpsed in passing, up to ~half a page for a full "ordinary-life moment." Never longer.
6. **Draw names, loops and sayings from THIS file** so the book stays one world.

---

## 0. SCALE — ~5,000 souls (target 4,500–5,500)

Never stated as exposition; **inferred.** Let the reader feel it through: districts never visited, workshops never entered, professions never needed, people whose lives never cross his, queues, apprentices, old masters, deliveries, maintenance, inventories, repairs, arguments over ordinary things.

**Canon action:** Ch4 currently says *"a Mountain of some three thousand."* Raise to *"some five thousand"* (or soften to "thousands upon thousands") so the felt scale and the stated number agree. This is the only place the number is spoken; keep it that way.

**District map** (a handful visited, most only glimpsed):
- *Visited:* the Hall of Ceremony; Light's practice halls & old gallery; the family quarters (by Kahir); the food galleries; the washing pools; the child halls; the salt-house; the kitchens/bakeries; the lower cells; the Oasis and its pens (outside).
- *Only ever glimpsed / named / never entered:* the deep waterworks; the fungus caverns; the fish tanks; the worm beds; the ash-fields; the coal seams; the reed beds; the bone-kitchens; the drying lofts; the loom-halls; the insect-lofts; the deep cold-stores. Kailan lives and dies never needing most of these.

## 1. THE LOOPS — nothing has one life

The Mountain has spent centuries learning to waste almost nothing. Whenever a thing is used, ask *what happens next?* Several orthogonal loops (invent more freely; never lecture them — glimpse them):

- **WATER:** spring → drink → wash → gardens → fish tanks → reed-bed filtration → distilled at the salt-house (its trace weighed to the grain, per Ch4) → back to drink & gardens. Water leaves the Mountain only as a debt repaid, never as breath.
- **ASH:** everything burnt → ash weighed by Ashkeepers → fields & Oasis, plus lye for soap and glaze for the potters. *"Ash is the last harvest."*
- **CLOTH:** robe → mended → work-rag → bandage → paper pulp → lamp-spill fuel → ash → field.
- **BONE:** goat & fish bone → broth → needle/button/tool → boiled to glue → ash.
- **WOOD** (precious, scarce): repair → tool → charcoal → ash → field. A splinter is accounted for.
- **ROT:** night-soil + spent grain + goat bedding → compost → fungus caverns (food & medicine) → worm beds → fish feed → fish → people → back to the start.
- **GOAT:** milk → cheese & whey; hair → cord & felt; hide → leather; dung → compost & fuel; bone → above. The whole animal is spent; nothing of it is buried.

The cumulative feel: *nothing truly disappears.* Never say so. Let the reader arrive at it.

---

## 2. FOOD — orthogonal by design (survive partial failure)

Each source solves a *different* engineering problem, so a failure of one is a hungry season, never a death:
- **Carbohydrate:** barley, millet, sorghum, root tubers.
- **Protein:** lentils, beans, chickpeas, peas; crickets & mealworms bred in the dark on spent grain and press-cake; mushrooms; fish; goat dairy.
- **Fat:** pressed oil-seeds (the press-cake feeds the insects and fish); goat fat; a few Oasis nuts.
- **Micronutrient:** herb & leaf gardens; fermented crocks (people argue over the year's ferment); algae sheets; Oasis fruit in season.
- **Oxygen:** algae tanks, aquatic plants, and the underground gardens breathe for the sealed deep.

No monoculture anywhere. Balance can lose a whole system and limp along on the rest — and everyone half-knows it, which is why Balance's word is law.

---

## 3. THE MOUNTAIN BREATHES — ventilation as a living organ

The great upward hollow of the Hall is a chimney; the furnaces, kilns, bakeries and salt-house are its bellows. Heat climbs, cold sinks. Whole districts depend on it.
- **Winter draft** (cold pouring down the low ways); **summer draft** (heat pulled up and out the high vents). Vent shafts opened and shut by season, under Justice's witness (as in the Festival, Ch6). Corridors near the kilns run warm; certain staircases are always cold; some passages are water-cooled; the Mountain is aired at night.
- **Folk knowledge:** children know exactly where the warm air sleeps and choose the cold corridors in the hottest weeks; old people rage at a wrong door left open.
- **Sayings** (use verbatim, sparingly): *"Close that door — you are stealing tomorrow's warmth." "The winter air climbs faster than you think." "Leave the lower door open; let the Mountain breathe before sunset."*
- Tended by the **Draft keepers / Vent keepers.** A wrong door is a small, real crime.

## 4. ELEVATE THE UNDIGNIFIED — the custodian culture

No necessary task is low. Each has centuries of pride, inherited gesture, and its own sayings. Children admire these masters; old masters are honoured; nobody laughs. A working menu (each with a gesture + a line):

- **Ashkeepers** — weigh and route the ash; touch the scale-beam before a weighing. *"Ash is the last harvest."*
- **Water listeners** — press an ear to the channel-stone to hear the spring's health; a hum is sweet water, a knock is water turning. A boy with a slate marks once at a nod, twice at a frown.
- **Draft / Vent keepers** — open and shut the Mountain's lungs by the season.
- **Compost tenders, Mushroom tenders** (forever arguing humidity), **Worm-bed keepers.**
- **Mirror polishers** — Light's own servants; they gossip; *"an eye, not a mirror."*
- **Salt & coal measurers** — record today's take though they believe the seam endless.
- **Lamp cleaners; Gong keepers** (strike the day's five marks — see the pendulum-clock canon); **Reed-tenders; Bone-cooks; Robe menders; Fish keepers** (who listen to the water); **Goatherds** (who discuss births endlessly).

Faith regards them as custodians, not servants. A saying or gesture from this list may recur across chapters like a folk memory.

## 5. THE ORDINARY-MOMENT MENU — drop-in seeds (~½ page, no lesson)

Roughly one every chapter or two. Nothing important happens. Draw from these; invent freely in the same key. Each must read as *overheard life*, not doctrine.

- **Ch1 (Ascension, packed Hall):** a Draft-keeper easing a low door shut with her foot without looking; an old man muttering that they opened the high vents too soon; a child counting the pendulum's marks on the floor to pass the wait.
- **Ch2 (into Light):** mirror-polishers gossiping over a rag; a staircase that is always cold; a delivery of lamp-oil filing past on someone's shoulder; an apprentice being corrected at a wall the reader never sees again.
- **Ch3 (Oasis):** two goatherds arguing a difficult birth; a reed-tender cutting to a rhythm; a Balance child sprinkling the snail trays "with the seriousness of a priest" (already present — a keeper).
- **Ch4 (the working day):** a Water listener at the channel with her slate-boy; Ashkeepers weighing broken pottery; the insect-lofts glimpsed through a slot; a household grumbling at a half-measure of water; the salt-house boy who reads figures he will never be told the meaning of (present — build the world around him).
- **Ch5/6 (Festival & feast):** a baker testing bread by sound before the rite; someone complaining that this year's algae tastes different; robe-menders repairing ceremonial cloth; an elderly couple sitting where the warm draft gathers in winter.
- **Ch7+ (the back half):** deliveries and repairs continuing during a crisis; a queue that forms even on the worst day; coal measurers recording the day's take though the world is ending; the Mountain airing itself at dusk regardless of what has happened to Kailan.

---

## 6. GUARDRAILS

- These beats are **relief from the book's intelligence.** They must feel plainer than everything around them. If a beat starts explaining itself, it has failed.
- Keep them `not`-free. Keep them short. Keep the hero out of them wherever possible.
- Recurring texture (a saying, a profession, a smell of a district) is good — it makes the world feel remembered rather than described.
