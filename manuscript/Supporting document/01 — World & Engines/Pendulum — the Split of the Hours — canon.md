# Pendulum — the Split of the Hours — canon (Book One)

*Purpose: answer the one mechanical objection a careful reader raises at the Pendulum — how a swing-plane that takes **~2.75 days** to turn once can give **five named hours every single day**. This doc fixes that, and only that. It sits **under** the Geography lock (which owns the numbers: latitude 21.6° N, Foucault Long Return ≈ 66 h). Where they touch, Geography wins.*

---

## 0. The two numbers that must live together

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Beat** (one back-and-forth swing) | ≈ 22 s | length 125 m → T = 2π√(L/g) ≈ 22.4 s |
| **Long Return** (plane turns once, 360°) | ≈ 66 h ≈ **2.75 days** | Geography lock (P = 23.93 h ÷ sin 21.3°) |
| **Day** (dawn to dawn) | 24 h | the sun they are forbidden to see |
| **Named hours per day** | **5** | First Light · Second Silence · Third Measure (noon) · Fourth Record · Fifth Blade |

The reader's trap: *five marks around a circle the plane crosses once every 2.75 days would ring five gongs per 2.75 days, not five per day.* Correct — **if there were only five marks.** There are not.

---

## 1. The resolution, in one sentence

**The floor-ring is not a day-dial with five marks. It is the whole precession dial, and Faith has written almost three days of the five-hour scheme around it at once — about fourteen struck positions — so that one turn of the plane rings ~five gongs a day for ~2.75 days.**

That is the "multiple days written around the circle." The five *names* recur two-and-two-thirds times around the ring; **First Light**, **Third Measure** and the rest each appear more than once, at different points of the circle, one set per day of the sweep.

---

## 2. The mechanics (numbers that close)

- Plane turns 360° in 66 h → bearing advances **5.45° per hour**.
- Want a gong every **24 h ÷ 5 = 4.8 h** → marks spaced **4.8 h × 5.45°/h ≈ 26.2°** apart.
- Marks needed to fill 360°: **360 ÷ 26.2 ≈ 13.7** → call it **~14 struck positions** around the ring.
- One Long Return (66 h) therefore rings **~13–14 gongs = five a day × 2.75 days.** The arithmetic closes.

**Grouping (what Faith paints):** three arcs — *first turning, second turning, third turning* — of five named marks each (the third arc only ~two-thirds finished, ~3–4 marks). A resident does not read the arc; he only hears the gong and its name. The arcs are Faith's bookkeeping, not the public's.

---

## 3. The two honest complications (and how the Mountain answers each)

**(a) The plane is a line, not an arrow.** The bob swings along a diameter, so the plane lies across the ring in *both* directions at once and meets a mark and the mark opposite it together. Nothing in the geometry alone tells First Light from the hour ~33 h later on the opposite radial.
→ **Answer: Faith holds the count.** The gong is struck *by hand, by an attendant who knows the sequence* (already in Ch2). The plane says *"a mark is reached"*; the human says *which* one. This is why the gong is manual and why the office matters — the count is the thing a machine cannot keep. *(This replaces the earlier "two-faced God/Beast disk tells the ends apart" idea, now retired: Ch1 is rich enough without it.)*

**(b) 2.75 is not a whole number.** 66 h is **not** a clean multiple of 24 h, so the marks and the true solar day drift against each other; the ring can never perfectly repeat. This is already in the text — *"Measure was sometimes noon, most days near it, now and then an hour adrift."*
→ **Answer: continuous nudging + a hard reset.**
  - *Nudging:* as the seasons lengthen or shorten the day, and as the ~0.75-day remainder accumulates, Faith shifts the marks a little to keep First Light near true dawn. This is Faith's endless sacred labour — a clock kept honest by hand.
  - *Reset:* the **Festival of Equal Light** (equinox sun admitted, twice a year — see Geography lock §4) re-pegs the ring to a real dawn, zeroing all accumulated drift. Between the two festivals the Pendulum is *"the only clock we had, and the only one we were allowed to trust"* — a **maintained approximation**, never a perfect automaton.

**Thematic payoff:** the civilisation built to make *"tomorrow exactly today"* is, at its very clock, fighting the drift of an incommensurate cosmos, and can hold the line only by unbroken human attention. The one machine they trust needs a person awake beside it forever.

---

## 4. The fine beat (below the gong)

The **22-second swing** is the sub-division inside a mark. Anything needing timing finer than ~4.8 h (a Faith rhythm, a measured prayer, a Balance process) is counted in **swings** by an attendant. Coarse dial = the ring; fine metronome = the beat.

---

## 5. What is spoken vs. what is background

- **Spoken (may appear in text):** the five names; the gong struck by Faith; the marks moved with the season; Measure = noon-ish; the Festival resets the clock; the plane takes ~2.75 days / ~66 h to come round; the ring carries "more than one day" of marks.
- **Background (do not lecture):** the 26.2° spacing, the ~14 count, the line-vs-arrow point, the drift arithmetic. Glimpse the labour (an attendant nudging a mark; a boy asked to count swings); never diagram it in the prose.
