# Author's Note — historical sources

*Supplied with the submission package. Not bound into the novel.*

---

*Homo Noxius* is a work of fiction built beside a real history, and the reader should know where the boundary runs.

France detonated its first nuclear weapon at Reggane, in the Algerian Sahara, on 13 February 1960, and conducted seventeen tests in Algeria before testing moved to the Pacific. The people living in and moving through that region — in the Touat, along the salt roads, at the wells — were not consulted, not evacuated, and not counted. Much of what was measured afterward remains closed. France's 2010 compensation law approved a small fraction of the Algerian claims brought under it, and the handover of the archives on the tests' consequences and on waste buried on site is still an open demand between the two states.

Nothing in this novel is intended to fictionalise those deaths or to stand in for them. Sarah Blackwood is an invention, and so is the machine she spends her life pursuing; the desert she calls empty is not empty, and the book knows it because the historical record says so.

Moussa Belkacem and Amastan ag Malik are invented characters. Their names are common ones, and they are also, together, the name of Moussa ag Amastan (1867–1920), amenokal of the Kel Ahaggar, who negotiated with and resisted the French occupation of the Hoggar. That echo is deliberate. The Kel Tamasheq — called Tuareg on maps drawn elsewhere — are a living people with their own language, law and historical memory, and the hidden civilisation in these pages is emphatically not them, nor any ancestor of theirs. The Mountain is an invention with an invented language, and the genetic premise attached to it belongs to that invention alone.

Where the novel is accurate — the dates, the test site, the uranium economy, the structure of colonial cartography, the contested records — it is accurate on purpose. Where it invents, it invents openly. Any error that remains is mine.
