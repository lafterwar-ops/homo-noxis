\typeprologue

\begin{center}\small\itshape Blackwood Estate --- the Swiss Alps\end{center}

\begin{room}

--- The seventeenth of November, nineteen ninety-eight; nine o'clock; the first of our interviews. The doctors are content that you are sufficiently rested, and that we may begin. We shall sit together each morning, so that your account may be set down; the afternoons are given over to luncheon, and to instruction, with another. Should a question occur to you in the course of these sessions, you would do well to write it down, and it shall be answered in its turn.\par

--- You speak my tongue very well.\par

--- You are very kind. I have had the learning of it these five years.\par

--- Who are you?\par

--- I am Mister Paul Morgan. I read geology, archaeology and anthropology at Oxford for three years, a seat of learning you will not have heard of; and, upon a scholarship from the Blackwood Foundation, three years more. I was invited to join the household here two years ago.\par

--- Mister. Is that a rank? Should I address you as Mister Paul?\par

--- Just Paul.\par

--- Paul. Paul, what does this do?\par

--- It keeps a record of what we say; your kind keep theirs upon tablets; this is the same, only swifter: a tablet that writes itself. You need not mind it. You need not perform for it. It is there so that my memory should not betray the record. And I ought to say, before we begin, that a week ago I did not believe your kind existed. Not truly. And I shall be quite candid: I believe it rather less than I should, for all the years I have spent studying it. That, in some part, is why the task or retranscription has fallen to me: a fresh and doubting eye, as they were pleased to call it.\par

--- Paul, you do not believe the Mountain is real?\par

--- What about you: when you dwelt within the Mountain, did you believe in the Outsiders?\par

--- Not fully, not at the start anyway.\par

--- Then we are even. Begin where it begins.\par

\end{room}
