\typeprologue

\begin{center}\small\itshape Blackwood Estate --- the Swiss Alps --- 17 November 1998\end{center}

\ornament

The reels are turning. I have told him so, because I am required to tell him, and he has watched them the way other men watch weather --- with a mild, exhausted attention, as though they might do something he should be ready for.\par

He is not what the file promised. Four days ago they brought him in asleep; the kindest way, they said, to carry a man across a world he was raised to dread. 
Since then he has been measured, and walked in the gardens under the larches, and fed, and let to sleep as long as he liked. 
The doctors have signed him over. 
Now the room is mine, and my instructions are the oldest there are: learn everything, surrender nothing, and never let him feel he is anywhere but home.\par

He believes he is at Horizon. That is the whole of the arrangement. 
To him I am Horizon's own --- the one who keeps the record, sent by his Keeper to take the account every returning man is asked to give. 
It is a clean legend. 
It sits on me lightly, because the only false thing in it is the name carved over the door.\par

We are alone in it --- he, and I, and the machine turning quietly between us. That too is arranged: a man speaks more truly to one listener than to a room. The reels are a patient listener. For this hour they are the only other ears in the world, and whatever he gives me, they keep.\par

``Begin wherever it begins,'' I tell him.\par

He does not begin at once. He looks at the window, at the white shoulders of mountains that are not his mountain, and asks me a question instead.\par

\begin{room}
``That word,'' he says. ``\emph{Alps.} I read it, in the days I was waiting. It is the name of these?''\par

``It is.''\par

``They have a name.'' He turns it in his mouth like a coin of unfamiliar weight. 
``And there are many of them, each one small enough to need a name, to be told from the others.
'' Something in this troubles him more than anything the doctors did to him. 
``Ours has no name. 
It is only the Mountain. 
I did not know a mountain could be a small thing.''\par
\end{room}

I say nothing that matters, and let him take me for a harmless man with a machine. 
It is the second time I have asked him to begin. 
I have learned that the second asking is the one that works --- a man far from home, fed and watched by strangers who smile, 
will walk a long way toward the only voice that seems to want him to keep talking.\par

``It begins with a boy who was told he had been chosen,'' he says at last, ``and believed it, the way you believe the floor under your feet.''\par

And he begins.\par
